\begin{frame}{The modal solver --- architecture}
  \note{\textasciitilde 11 min mark. What it does and why it's the right tool for this problem. Then land the three hard problems.}

  \small\textbf{Approach.} For PDEs with constant spatial coefficients, Fourier-transform: each mode $k$ evolves under matrix $M(k)$. Diagonalise once; $y(t,k)=e^{M(k)t}y_0(k)$. Machine-precision ($|dE/E|\sim10^{-14}$), no CFL, cost independent of $t_\mathrm{end}$.\normalsize

  \vspace{0.25em}
  \begin{columns}[T,onlytextwidth]
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \textbf{Why it fits this problem:}
      \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
        \item Every theory in the scan linearises to constant-coefficient PDEs in flat space $+$ uniform $B_0$ --- the modal eligibility criteria match exactly.
        \item Signals span $>10^{12}$: time-stepping errors swamp $P < 10^{-8}$; modal solver has no accumulation error.
        \item $\sim\!10^5$ solver calls per inference run --- $100\times$ speedup over CVODE makes the campaign tractable.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.49\textwidth}
      \textbf{Hard problems solved for PGT:}
      \begin{itemize}\scriptsize
        \item \textbf{Rank-deficient mass matrices} (PGT constraint fields carry no time derivatives) $\Rightarrow$ Schur-complement elimination. Same structure as Dirac--Bergmann: naive Legendre transform gives $\max|dE/E|=1.37$.
        \item \textbf{Massive eigenvalue degeneracy} $\Rightarrow$ Krylov \texttt{expm\_multiply} throughout --- computes $e^{Mt}v$ without eigendecomposition; unusual for a spectral solver.
        \item \textbf{Nyquist-mode energy leak} $\Rightarrow$ zero one Fourier coefficient: $|dE/E|:\;10^{-5}\to10^{-14}$.
      \end{itemize}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}
