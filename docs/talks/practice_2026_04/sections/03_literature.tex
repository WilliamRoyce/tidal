\begin{frame}{Fundamental weakness}
  \textbf{Planck-scale suppression:}
  \[
    P \;\sim\; \left(\frac{B_0 D}{M_{\rm Pl}}\right)^{\!2}, \qquad M_{\rm Pl}\sim 10^{19}\,\text{GeV}
  \]
  Even for a magnetar ($B_0\sim 10^{15}$\,G over $\sim\!10$\,km), $B_0 D / M_{\rm Pl} \sim 10^{-5}$, giving $P\sim 10^{-10}$.

  \vspace{0.15em}
  \textbf{What the literature established:}
  \begin{itemize}\small
    \item \textbf{Gertsenshtein 1962} --- original effect; conversion immediately recognised as hopelessly small.
    \item \textbf{Boccaletti et al.\ 1970} --- full analytic solution for a localised $B$-field region.
    \item \textbf{Raffelt \& Stodolsky 1988} --- embedded in the axion--photon mixing context.
  \end{itemize}

  \vspace{0.15em}
  The formula is exact in linearised Einstein--Maxwell, suppressed by the Planck scale. Any amplification requires new gravitational physics.
\end{frame}
