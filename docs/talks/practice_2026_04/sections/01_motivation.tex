\begin{frame}{Motivation}
  \note{\textasciitilde 1 min mark. The Gertsenshtein effect is too weak to ever observe. Frame this as the question that motivates the whole project.}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The standard Gertsenshtein effect (graviton $\leftrightarrow$ photon conversion in a $B$-field) is a textbook prediction of Einstein--Maxwell theory --- and it is \emph{astrophysically useless}.
      \begin{itemize}\small
        \item Magnetar field $B\sim 10^{15}$\,G over $\sim 10$\,km: $P\sim 10^{-10}$.
        \item Laboratory $B\sim 10$\,T over $1$\,m: $P\sim 10^{-40}$. %TODO: cite source for universe lifetime comparison
      \end{itemize}
    \item For any usable signal, new physics is required. The question is: \emph{which?}
    \item This project's approach is unapologetically phenomenological --- systematically scan the space of quadratic curvature and torsion extensions of GR, and ask which subsectors amplify the conversion.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
