\begin{frame}{Why graviton--photon conversion?}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item GW astronomy spans $\sim$12 decades in frequency, but no coherent detector reaches the high-frequency ($\gtrsim$ MHz) band.  Hypothesised sources --- early-universe processes, primordial black-hole mergers, evaporating PBHs --- would sit there if they exist.

    \item The \textbf{Gertsenshtein effect} (graviton $\leftrightarrow$ photon conversion in a magnetic field) is the proposed mechanism behind several existing detection-route reanalyses --- of astrophysical photon backgrounds (galactic / extragalactic $B$-field as converter), and of dark-matter direct-detection cavities (microwave resonators in strong $B$-fields, where the same field that would convert axion-DM to photons would also convert incoming gravitons).

    \item In bare Einstein--Maxwell, the mixing length is $\sim 10^{6}$\,km even at magnetar $B$-fields: too long for any plausible astrophysical source.  No confirmed detection has been reported.

    \item \textbf{Gap.}  \emph{BSM gravity} --- extensions of GR with extra interaction structure --- could amplify the conversion.  This had not been computed in any theory beyond Einstein--Maxwell.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
