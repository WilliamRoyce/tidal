\section{Gertsenshtein Conversion Formula}
\label{sec:gertsenshtein-formula}

\subsection{Summary}

The graviton-photon conversion probability in a uniform magnetic field $\Bzero$ over propagation distance $D$ is:
\begin{equation}
  \Pconv(\text{graviton} \to \text{photon}) = \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\kappa \Bzero D}{2}\right)
  \label{eq:gertsenshtein-P}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa^2 = 16\pi G$ (standard gravitational coupling). Equivalently:
\begin{equation}
  \Pconv = \sin^2\!\left(\sqrt{4\pi G}\, \Bzero\, D\right)
  \label{eq:gertsenshtein-P-alt}
\end{equation}

This is derived from the Lagrangian $\lag = \frac{1}{\kappa^2}R - \frac{1}{4}F^2$ via the TIDAL symbolic pipeline and confirmed numerically (RMS $< 0.015$ across a 40-point $\Bzero$ sweep at $N=1024$, corrected for graviton effective mass). It is independently confirmed by Lella et al.~\cite{lella2024constraining}.

\textbf{Palessandro \& Rothman~(2023) quote $P = \sin^2(\sqrt{G}\,\Bzero\,D)$, which is incorrect by a factor of $\sqrt{4\pi} = 2\sqrt{\pi} \approx 3.54$ in the argument.} Hwang \& Noh~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton} independently criticise P\&R's linearisation procedure.


\subsection{Conventions: $\kappa$ in TIDAL vs.\ in the Literature}
\label{sec:kappa-conventions}

The symbol $\kappa$ is used in the gravity literature for objects that look
similar but are placed differently in the Lagrangian. Confusing them produces
wrong numerical factors in mixing amplitudes — this is the origin of several
discrepancies in the Gertsenshtein literature, including the Palessandro--Rothman
factor-of-$\sqrt{4\pi}$ error noted above. This block fixes TIDAL's convention
explicitly so it is never re-confused.

\paragraph*{What TIDAL actually does.}
TIDAL expands the metric as $g_{\mu\nu} = \etamunu + h_{\mu\nu}$ (the raw
perturbation, with \emph{no} $\kappa$ factor in the field redefinition). The
action is
\begin{equation*}
    \lag = \frac{1}{\kappa^2} R - \frac{1}{4} F_{\mu\nu} F^{\mu\nu},
    \qquad
    \kappa^2 = 16\pi G,
    \qquad
    [\kappa] = [\text{Mass}]^{-1}.
\end{equation*}
Numerically, $\kappa$ takes the value $\sqrt{16\pi G}$ — the same constant that
Lella et al.\ (2406.17853, formerly mis-cited in this document as ``Lella et al.'')
call the ``vertex coupling''. The earlier reference to ``Domcke 2301.02072'' on
this line was an attribution error: arxiv 2301.02072 is Palessandro \& Rothman
(\emph{Phys. Dark Univ.} 40, 101187, 2023), and they use a different,
non-canonical normalisation $\sqrt{G}\Bzero$ that is wrong by $\sqrt{4\pi}$
in the conversion-formula argument (see~\cref{sec:conventions}).
The $\kappa$ enters the equations of motion through the LHS-normalisation step
in the TIDAL pipeline: dividing the $h$ equation by its kinetic prefactor
$1/\kappa^2$ multiplies the photon coupling by $\kappa^2$, giving an
asymmetric mixing matrix whose beat frequency is $\Bzero\kappa$ (geometric
mean — see derivation below).

\paragraph*{Two equivalent ways to write the same physics.}
TIDAL's choice ($g = \eta + h$, action prefactor $1/\kappa^2$) is one of two
equivalent presentations of canonically-normalised linearised gravity. The
other is the canonical-graviton form used by Domcke and Dandoy:
\begin{equation*}
    g_{\mu\nu} = \etamunu + \kappa\, \tilde{h}_{\mu\nu},
    \qquad
    \lag = \tfrac{1}{2}(\partial \tilde{h})^2 - \tfrac{1}{4} F^2 + \dots,
\end{equation*}
related to the TIDAL form by the rescaling $\tilde{h} = h/\kappa$. Both choices
yield the same physical conversion probability $\Pconv = \sin^2(\kappa\Bzero
D/2)$ with the \emph{same} numerical $\kappa = \sqrt{16\pi G}$. They differ
only in where the $\kappa$ symbol appears: in TIDAL, it sits in the action
prefactor; in the canonical form, it sits in the metric expansion. The two are
related by a field redefinition, not a different convention.

\paragraph*{A genuinely different convention: the Einstein constant
$\kappa_\text{E}$ (NOT used by TIDAL).}
Some papers (e.g.\ BHL 2303.11094, 2406.12826) reserve the symbol $\kappa$ for
the prefactor of the Einstein--Hilbert action in a different normalisation:
\begin{equation*}
    S_\text{EH} = -\frac{1}{2\kappa_\text{E}}\!\int\! R\,\sqrt{-g}\, d^4x,
    \qquad
    \kappa_\text{E} = 8\pi G,
    \qquad
    [\kappa_\text{E}] = [\text{Mass}]^{-2}.
\end{equation*}
This $\kappa_\text{E}$ is \emph{not} the same as TIDAL's $\kappa$: it is
dimensionally distinct ($[\text{Mass}]^{-2}$ vs.\ $[\text{Mass}]^{-1}$) and is
related to the reduced Planck mass by $\kappa_\text{E} = 1/M_\text{P}^2$ where
$M_\text{P}^2 = 1/(8\pi G)$.

\paragraph*{Relation to the reduced Planck mass.}
The two conventions are connected by
\begin{equation*}
    \kappa^2_\text{TIDAL} = 2\kappa_\text{E} = 16\pi G,
    \qquad
    \kappa_\text{TIDAL} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{M_\text{P}},
    \qquad
    M_\text{P} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\kappa_\text{TIDAL}}.
\end{equation*}
The factor of $2$ is the canonical-normalisation factor; consistent with
TIDAL's prefactor $1/\kappa^2 = M_\text{P}^2/2$, which matches the BHL action
$-\tfrac{1}{2}M_\text{P}^2 R$ when written with explicit Planck-mass prefactor.

\paragraph*{How to tell which $\kappa$ a paper is using.}
Always check the dimensions and where $\kappa$ sits in the Lagrangian:
\begin{itemize}
  \item If $\kappa$ appears as $1/\kappa^2$ in front of $R$, or as $\kappa\Bzero D$
        inside a sine, or in $g = \eta + \kappa h$ for canonical $h$, it is
        the TIDAL/Domcke convention ($[\kappa] = [\text{Mass}]^{-1}$,
        $\kappa^2 = 16\pi G$).
  \item If $1/(2\kappa)$ is the prefactor of $R$ in the action, it is the
        Einstein-constant convention ($[\kappa_\text{E}] = [\text{Mass}]^{-2}$,
        $\kappa_\text{E} = 8\pi G$).
\end{itemize}
TIDAL's macros.tex and all docs/tex files use the first convention. The MSci
report should write the action with explicit $M_\text{P}^2$ prefactor (BHL
joint-paper style) and reserve the $\kappa$ symbol for the Gertsenshtein
formula and any place where the linearised vertex coupling is needed — this
choice avoids both ambiguities at once and is consistent with the macros.

\subsection{Derivation from the TIDAL Equations}

\subsubsection{Starting Point}

The Lagrangian:
\begin{equation}
  \lag = \frac{1}{\kappa^2}\, R - \frac{1}{4}\, F_{\mu\nu}\, F^{\mu\nu}
  \label{eq:lagrangian}
\end{equation}
with $\kappa^2 = 16\pi G$, background metric $\etamunu = \text{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$, and uniform background magnetic field $\Bzero$ along $x$ generated by $\bar{A}_y = -\Bzero z$.

After:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Perturbation expansion: $g = \eta + \varepsilon h$, $A = \bar{A} + \varepsilon a$
  \item TT gauge on $h$, Lorenz gauge on $a$
  \item Plane-wave reduction ($\ddx = \ddy = 0$, propagation along $z$)
  \item Constraint elimination (traceless $h_4 = -h_7$; transverse $h_6 = h_8 = 0$)
\end{itemize}
the Gertsenshtein channel ($h_7 \leftrightarrow a_2$) satisfies:
\begin{align}
  \ddt^2 h_7 &= \ddz^2 h_7 - \Bzero^2 \kappa^2\, h_7 - \Bzero \kappa^2\, \ddz a_2 \label{eq:eom-h7} \\
  \ddt^2 a_2 &= \ddz^2 a_2 + \Bzero\, \ddz h_7 \label{eq:eom-a2}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Why the Coupling Is Asymmetric}

The $h_7$ equation has coupling coefficient $\Bzero\kappa^2$ while the $a_2$ equation has $\Bzero$. This arises from the kinetic normalisation step in the pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{VarD} (EulerLagrange.wl) computes $\delta\lag/\delta h = 0$, giving: $(1/\kappa^2)\,\partial^2 h + \text{coupling} = 0$
  \item \textbf{ExtractLHSCoefficient} (ExportJSON.wl) identifies $\text{lhsCoeff} = 1/\kappa^2$
  \item \textbf{LHS normalisation} (ExportJSON.wl): $\text{rhs} = -\text{rhs}/\text{lhsCoeff}$ divides by $1/\kappa^2$
\end{enumerate}
This multiplies the coupling by $\kappa^2$: the raw coupling $\Bzero$ becomes $\Bzero\kappa^2$ in the $h$ equation. The $a$ equation has $\text{lhsCoeff} = 1$ (Maxwell kinetic term), so its coupling $\Bzero$ is unchanged.

\subsubsection{Eigenmode Analysis}

For a plane-wave mode $e^{i(kz - \omega t)}$, substitute $\ddz \to ik$ into \cref{eq:eom-h7,eq:eom-a2}:
\begin{align}
  \omega^2 H &= (k^2 + \Bzero^2\kappa^2)\,H + ik\,\Bzero\kappa^2\, A \\
  \omega^2 A &= k^2\, A - ik\,\Bzero\, H
\end{align}
giving the mixing matrix:
\begin{equation}
  M = \begin{pmatrix}
    k^2 + \Bzero^2\kappa^2 & ik\,\Bzero\kappa^2 \\
    -ik\,\Bzero & k^2
  \end{pmatrix}
  \label{eq:mixing-matrix}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Eigenvalues:} $\det(M) = k^4$, $\text{Tr}(M) = 2k^2 + \Bzero^2\kappa^2$, giving:
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_\pm = \frac{2k^2 + \Bzero^2\kappa^2 \pm \Bzero\kappa\sqrt{4k^2 + \Bzero^2\kappa^2}}{2}
\end{equation}

\textbf{For $k \gg \Bzero\kappa$} (massless dispersion limit):
\begin{equation}
  \omega_\pm \approx k \pm \frac{\Bzero\kappa}{2}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Beat frequency:} $\Delta\omega = \omega_+ - \omega_- = \Bzero\kappa$. The conversion probability is therefore:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\Pconv(t) = \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\Delta\omega\, t}{2}\right) = \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\kappa\,\Bzero\, t}{2}\right)}
  \label{eq:P-final}
\end{equation}

Note: the beat frequency $\Bzero\kappa$ is the geometric mean of the two asymmetric couplings: $\sqrt{\Bzero\kappa^2 \cdot \Bzero} = \Bzero\kappa$. This is a general result for coupled oscillators with asymmetric coupling strengths.

\subsubsection{Algebraic Equivalence: TIDAL = Lella}

TIDAL uses $\kappa^2 = 16\pi G$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\kappa}{2} = \frac{\sqrt{16\pi G}}{2} = \sqrt{4\pi G}
\end{equation}
Lella use the reduced Planck mass $M_P = 1/\sqrt{8\pi G}$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\Bzero}{\sqrt{2}\,M_P} = \Bzero\,\frac{\sqrt{8\pi G}}{\sqrt{2}} = \Bzero\,\sqrt{4\pi G}
\end{equation}
These are identical: $\kappa/2 = \sqrt{4\pi G} = \Bzero/(\sqrt{2}\,M_P)$~\checkmark

\subsubsection{P\&R's Error}

P\&R use $g = \eta + h$ with $\lag = -(1/16\pi G)\,(\partial h)^2$. Their graviton kinetic term is non-canonical. The correct mixing element is $\sqrt{4\pi G}\,\Bzero$; they obtain $\sqrt{G}\,\Bzero$, missing a factor of $\sqrt{4\pi} \approx 3.54$.

The error ratio in the weak-field limit:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{P_\text{TIDAL}}{P_\text{PR}} = \frac{\sin^2(\sqrt{4\pi G}\,\Bzero D)}{\sin^2(\sqrt{G}\,\Bzero D)} \approx \frac{4\pi G\,\Bzero^2 D^2}{G\,\Bzero^2 D^2} = 4\pi
\end{equation}


\subsection{Graviton Effective Mass and $P_\text{max}$ Correction}
\label{sec:mass-correction}

\subsubsection{Physical Origin}

The $h_7$ equation contains a mass term $-\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa^2 \Bzero^2\, h_7$, acting as an effective mass squared $m_\text{eff}^2 = \kappa^2 \Bzero^2/2$ for the graviton, arising from the background EM stress-energy (confirmed by Hwang \& Noh~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton}, who derive the same coefficient $8\pi G\,\Bzero^2 = \kappa^2\Bzero^2/2$ in the potential formulation for transverse~$B$). The photon $a_2$ is massless.

\subsubsection{Effect on Energy-Based Conversion Measurement}

TIDAL measures conversion as an energy ratio $P(t) = E_\text{target}(t) / E_\text{source}(0)$. For initial condition $h_7 = A\cos(kz)$:
\begin{align}
  E_\text{source}(0) &\propto \frac{k^2 + \kappa^2\Bzero^2/2}{2\kappa^2}\, A^2 L \quad\text{(graviton with mass)} \\
  E_\text{target}(t_\text{peak}) &\propto \frac{k^2}{2\kappa^2}\, A^2 L \quad\text{(massless photon)}
\end{align}
Therefore:
\begin{equation}
  P_\text{max} = \frac{k^2}{k^2 + \kappa^2 \Bzero^2 / 2}
  \label{eq:Pmax}
\end{equation}
The full corrected formula:
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{P_\text{corrected}(\Bzero, t) = \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\kappa\,\Bzero\, t}{2}\right) \times \frac{k^2}{k^2 + \kappa^2\,\Bzero^2/2}}
  \label{eq:P-corrected}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Scope: $P_\text{max}$ vs.\ $P_\text{final}$}

The corrected formula~\cref{eq:P-corrected} applies to the \emph{peak envelope}
$P_\text{max}$ of the Rabi oscillation---the maximum conversion probability
reachable via energy transfer.  It does \textbf{not} apply to $P_\text{final}
= P(t_\text{end})$, the instantaneous conversion at the end of the simulation.

For $P_\text{final}$ at any specific time $D$, the \emph{bare} formula is exact:
\begin{equation}
  P_\text{final}(D) = \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\kappa\,\Bzero\, D}{2}\right)
  \label{eq:P-final-bare}
\end{equation}
This is because the beat frequency $\Delta\omega = \kappa\Bzero$ is independent
of the effective mass $m_\text{eff}^2 = \kappa^2\Bzero^2$ (the mass shifts both
eigenfrequencies equally, leaving their difference unchanged).

The $P_\text{max}$ correction arises from \emph{energy accounting}: the initial
graviton energy includes the mass contribution $\kappa^2\Bzero^2 A^2/(2\kappa^2)$
which is not transferable to the massless photon.  At peak conversion, the
photon captures only the kinetic + gradient fraction of the source energy.

\subsection{Detuned Generalisation}

The formula generalises to the detuned case (plasma effects, massive graviton):
\begin{equation}
  P = \left(\frac{\mu}{\omega_m}\right)^2 \sin^2(\omega_m\, D),
  \qquad \mu = \frac{\kappa\Bzero}{2}, \quad
  \omega_m = \sqrt{\Delta^2 + \mu^2}, \quad
  \Delta = \frac{m_\gamma^2 - m_g^2}{2\omega}
  \label{eq:detuned}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Weak-field limit:} $P \approx \kappa^2 \Bzero^2 D^2 / 4 = 4\pi G\, \Bzero^2 D^2$ (not $G\,\Bzero^2 D^2$ as P\&R claim).


\subsection{Literature Notes}
\label{sec:literature-notes}

\begin{description}
  \item[Gertsenshtein (1962)~\cite{gertsenshtein1962wave}] Original prediction. Weak-field limit: $P \approx (\kappa B L/2)^2$, consistent with $\sin^2 \approx x^2$ for small argument.
  \item[Raffelt \& Stodolsky (1988)] Established the $2\times 2$ mixing-matrix formalism by analogy with neutrino oscillations. Introduced MSW resonance for graviton-photon mixing.
  \item[Hwang \& Noh (2023)~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton}] Proper EM field definitions in curved spacetime; formulation-dependent graviton mass/instability term; critiques ad-hoc approaches including P\&R.
  \item[Lella et al.\ (2024)~\cite{lella2024constraining}] Full 4-component mixing (two GW polarisations $\times$ two photon polarisations). \textbf{Confirms TIDAL's formula:} $P = \sin^2(\sqrt{4\pi G}\,\Bzero D)$.
  \item[Domcke et al.\ (2025)~\cite{domcke2025scattering}] GW scattering on localised magnetic fields. Extension target for TIDAL.
\end{description}


\subsection{Implications}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Our formula is correct.} The TIDAL pipeline (xAct $\to$ VarD $\to$ ExportJSON normalisation) correctly derives the coupled equations.
  \item \textbf{P\&R is not a reliable validation target.} The 2023 and 2024 papers both contain the same normalisation error. Lella et al.~\cite{lella2024constraining} should be used as the primary cross-check.
  \item \textbf{The formula generalises cleanly} to the detuned case via \cref{eq:detuned}.
\end{enumerate}
