\section{Gertsenshtein Effect: Physics Background and Validation Targets}
\label{sec:gertsenshtein}

\subsection{Overview}
\label{subsec:gertsenshtein-overview}

The \textbf{Gertsenshtein effect}~(1962) is the resonant conversion of gravitational waves (gravitons) into electromagnetic waves (photons) --- and vice versa --- in an external magnetic field. The coupling arises from the standard Einstein--Maxwell system: the spacetime metric appears in the Maxwell action, so linearising around a background magnetic field generates cross-terms between the metric perturbation $h_{\mu\nu}$ and the EM perturbation $a_\mu$.

This effect is the simplest instance of \textbf{graviton--matter wave mixing} and serves as the foundation for extensions to torsion waves, axion--photon--graviton mixing, and Chern--Simons gravity.

\subsection{Physics}
\label{subsec:gertsenshtein-physics}

\subsubsection{The Einstein--Maxwell System}
\label{subsec:einstein-maxwell}

The full Lagrangian:
\begin{equation}
  \lag = \frac{1}{\kappa^2}\, R - \frac{1}{4}\, F_{\mu\nu}\, F^{\mu\nu}
  \label{eq:gertsenshtein-lagrangian}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa^2 = 16\pi G$, $R$ is the Ricci scalar, and $F_{\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu A_\nu - \partial_\nu A_\mu$ is the electromagnetic field strength tensor.

\subsubsection{Background Configuration}
\label{subsec:background-config}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Metric}: flat Minkowski $\etamunu = \mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$
  \item \textbf{EM field}: background 4-potential $\bg{A}_\mu$ generating a uniform magnetic field $\Bzero$
    \begin{itemize}
      \item For $\Bzero$ along $x$-axis: $\bg{A}_y = -\Bzero z$ (Coulomb gauge)
      \item Background field strength: $\bar{F}_{yz} = \partial_z \bg{A}_y = -\Bzero$, i.e., $B_x = \Bzero$
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Perturbation Expansion}
\label{subsec:perturbation-expansion}

Both fields are expanded around their backgrounds:
\begin{align}
  g_{\mu\nu} &= \eta_{\mu\nu} + \varepsilon\, h_{\mu\nu} \quad\text{(metric perturbation --- graviton)} \\
  A_\mu      &= \bg{A}_\mu + \varepsilon\, a_\mu \quad\text{(EM perturbation --- photon)}
\end{align}

The second-order action $\delta^2(\sqrtg\, \lag)$ contains:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{$h \times h$ terms}: graviton self-interaction (Fierz--Pauli)
  \item \textbf{$a \times a$ terms}: photon kinetic energy (Maxwell)
  \item \textbf{$h \times a$ terms}: graviton--photon coupling via $\bar{F}$ --- \textbf{the Gertsenshtein effect}
\end{itemize}

The coupling coefficients are proportional to the background field strength $\Bzero$.

\subsubsection{Gauge Fixing}
\label{subsec:gauge-fixing}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Metric}: Transverse-traceless (TT) gauge --- reduces to physical polarisation modes $h_+$, $h_\times$
  \item \textbf{EM}: Lorenz gauge $\partial_\mu a^\mu = 0$ --- eliminates unphysical degrees of freedom
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Plane-Wave Reduction}
\label{subsec:plane-wave-reduction}

For GW propagating along $z$-axis with $\Bzero$ along $x$-axis:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Set $\ddx = \ddy = 0$ (plane-wave ansatz)
  \item Reduces 3+1D tensor+vector system to effective 1+1D coupled equations
  \item Only $h_+$ polarisation couples to $a_y$ (the transverse EM mode parallel to $\Bzero$)
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Conversion Probability}
\label{subsec:conversion-probability}

\paragraph{Vacuum, uniform $\Bzero$, propagation distance $D = c \cdot t$.}
\label{par:vacuum-conversion}

The coupled equations from our Lagrangian $(1/\kappa^2)R - (1/4)F^2$ are (after TT + Lorenz gauge, plane-wave reduction, and dividing by kinetic coefficients):
\begin{align}
  \ddt^2 h_+ &= \ddz^2 h_+ - \tfrac{1}{2}\kappa^2 \Bzero^2\, h_+ - \Bzero \kappa^2\, \ddz a_y \label{eq:gert-eom-h} \\
  \ddt^2 a_y &= \ddz^2 a_y + \Bzero\, \ddz h_+ \label{eq:gert-eom-a}
\end{align}

The coupling is asymmetric: $\Bzero\kappa^2$ on $h$ (from dividing by $1/(2\kappa^2)$ kinetic prefactor) vs $\Bzero$ on $a$ (unit kinetic prefactor). The eigenmode analysis gives beat frequency $\Delta\omega = \Bzero\kappa$ (geometric mean of couplings), yielding:
\begin{equation}
  P(\text{graviton} \to \text{photon}) = \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\kappa\,\Bzero\, D}{2}\right)
  \label{eq:gert-P-vacuum}
\end{equation}

This is confirmed numerically (RMS error $< 0.015$ across a 40-point $\Bzero$ sweep at $N = 1024$, using the corrected amplitude formula --- see \cref{sec:gertsenshtein-formula}).

\paragraph{Note on P\&R error.}
\label{par:pr-error}

Palessandro \& Rothman~(2023, Eq.~26; 2024, arXiv:2405.01407) quote $P = \sin^2(\sqrt{G}\,\Bzero\,D)$, which is incorrect by a factor of $\sqrt{4\pi} = 2\sqrt{\pi} \approx 3.54$ in the argument. They fail to properly account for the $1/(16\pi G)$ kinetic prefactor when extracting the mixing frequency from their non-canonically-normalised system. Hwang \& Noh~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton} independently criticise P\&R's linearisation. Our formula is confirmed by Dandoy, Lella et al.~\cite{dandoy2024constraining}, who use canonical normalisation and obtain $\Delta_{g,\gamma} = \sqrt{4\pi G}\,\Bzero$, matching $\kappa\Bzero/2$ exactly. See \cref{sec:gertsenshtein-formula} for the full derivation and literature comparison.

\paragraph{Graviton effective mass from background EM field.}
\label{par:graviton-mass}

The graviton equation includes a mass-like term $-\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa^2\Bzero^2\, h_+$ from the interaction of the metric perturbation with the background EM stress-energy. This gives an effective graviton mass $m_g^2 = \kappa^2\Bzero^2/2$. The photon remains massless (in vacuum). This mass detuning limits the peak conversion for each $k$-mode:
\begin{equation}
  P_\mathrm{max}(k) = \frac{k^2}{k^2 + \kappa^2 \Bzero^2 / 2}
  \label{eq:gert-Pmax-k}
\end{equation}

For $k \gg \kappa\Bzero$, the detuning is negligible and $P_\mathrm{max} \to 1$ (full conversion). For low-$k$ modes ($k \sim \kappa\Bzero$), the detuning is significant. A Gaussian IC (broad $k$-spectrum, dominant low-$k$) will show $P_\mathrm{peak} < 1$ even in the absence of numerical error. Use a plane-wave IC with sufficiently high $k$ for clean validation against $P = \sin^2(\kappa\Bzero t/2)$.

Verified numerically: Gaussian IC ($\sigma = 1.5$, $L = 50$) gives $P_\mathrm{peak} = 0.975$ at $N = 512$; plane-wave IC ($k = 2$) gives $P_\mathrm{peak} = 0.999$ at $N = 1024$ --- both consistent with the mass correction factor.

\paragraph{With detuning (plasma frequency $\omega_p$, or effective photon mass $m_\gamma$).}
\label{par:detuned-conversion}

\begin{align}
  P &= f^2\, \sin^2(\omega_m\, D) \label{eq:gert-P-detuned} \\
  \intertext{where:}
  \Delta &= \frac{m_\gamma^2 - m_g^2}{2\omega} \quad\text{(mass-squared difference / $2\omega$)} \nonumber \\
  \mu &= \frac{\kappa\,\Bzero}{2} \quad\text{(coupling strength)} \nonumber \\
  \omega_m &= \sqrt{\Delta^2 + \mu^2} \quad\text{(mixing frequency)} \nonumber \\
  f &= \frac{\mu}{\omega_m} \quad\text{(mixing angle)} \nonumber
\end{align}

\paragraph{Weak-field limit ($\mu D \ll 1$).}
\label{par:weak-field}

\begin{equation}
  P \approx \frac{\kappa^2\, \Bzero^2\, D^2}{4}
  \label{eq:gert-P-weak}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Oscillation length.}
\label{par:osc-length}

\begin{equation}
  L_\mathrm{osc} = \frac{2\pi}{\kappa\,\Bzero}
  \label{eq:gert-Losc}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Torsion-Independence of the Gertsenshtein Effect}
\label{subsec:torsion-independence}

A key result of this project (issue~\#199, \#200) is that the standard
Gertsenshtein conversion channel is \emph{algebraically independent} of torsion
for the most general quadratic Poincar\'{e} Gauge Theory (PGT) Lagrangian with
minimal electromagnetic coupling:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = \frac{1}{\kappa^2}\widetilde{R}
    + \alpha_1 I_1 + \alpha_2 I_2 + \alpha_3 I_3
    + b_5 \widetilde{R}^2
    - \tfrac{1}{4} F_{ab} F^{ab}
  \label{eq:pgt-em-lagrangian}
\end{equation}
where $\widetilde{R}$ is the Riemann--Cartan Ricci scalar, $I_1, I_2, I_3$ are
the three independent torsion-squared invariants, and $b_5\widetilde{R}^2$
provides torsion kinetic terms.

\paragraph{Polarisation block-diagonal structure.}
After plane-wave reduction ($\partial_x = \partial_y = 0$, propagation along~$z$),
the 23-component field equations decompose into two completely decoupled
polarisation channels with \emph{zero cross-talk}:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}llll@{}}
\toprule
Channel & Fields & Torsion-dependent? & Stable? \\
\midrule
$h_\times \leftrightarrow a_x$ & $h_5 \leftrightarrow a_1$ & No & Yes \\
trace $\leftrightarrow a_y$ & $h_4/h_7/h_9 \leftrightarrow a_2 \leftrightarrow$ torsion & Yes & No (ghost) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The photon $a_1$ depends only on $\{a_1, h_5\}$ and the photon $a_2$ depends
only on $\{a_2, h_0, h_3, h_4, h_7, h_9\}$.  There is no coupling path between
the two channels; the structure is exact and algebraic.

\paragraph{Double inaccessibility of torsion coupling.}
The torsion-sensitive trace channel is inaccessible from physical initial conditions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Algebraic decoupling:} TT initial conditions (valid for any vacuum
    GW) excite only $h_5$.  There is no path from $h_5$ to the trace sector
    ($h_4, h_7, h_9$) in the plane-wave reduced theory.
  \item \textbf{Ghost instability:} The $b_5\widetilde{R}^2$ term introduces
    Ostrogradsky 4th-order time derivatives, creating ghost modes.  The trace
    channel exhibits exponential blowup ($\sim 10^{30}$ at $t{=}50$).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Coupling--instability tension.}
The $b_5\widetilde{R}^2$ term simultaneously enables propagating torsion modes
(without it, torsion is non-propagating in Einstein--Cartan) and introduces
Ostrogradsky ghosts.  The very term needed for torsion-mediated conversion also
destroys it.  This is a structural consequence of Ostrogradsky's theorem for
non-degenerate higher-derivative theories.

However, Blagojevi\'{c} \& Cvetkovi\'{c}~\cite{blagojevic2018ghost} show that
$\widetilde{R}^2$ is \emph{not} the only route to propagating torsion: the $0^-$
(pseudoscalar) tordion propagates ghost-free with just a Riemann-squared coupling
($r_2 R^{ABCD}R_{ABCD}$, with $r_2 < 0$, $t_2 > 0$), requiring no Ricci scalar
squared at all.  Of 1918 critical parameter cases they identify, 450 are
ghost-and-tachyon free, including cases with propagating $0^+$ and $0^-$ torsion
modes.  Nikiforova et~al.~\cite{nikiforova2009torsion} provide explicit ghost-free
conditions for the massive torsion spectrum around Minkowski and de~Sitter backgrounds.
Aoki~\cite{aoki2020ghostfree} shows that PGT can be made non-linearly ghost-free
via equivalence to ghost-free massive bigravity, though the $4D$ completion requires
non-polynomial curvature terms.

\paragraph{Implication.}
For minimal PGT coupling, torsion cannot amplify the Gertsenshtein effect for
\emph{any} vacuum gravitational wave.  Three paths to torsion-mediated conversion
have been identified in the literature:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Non-minimal torsion--EM coupling:} Lagrangian terms of the form
    $F^{\mu\nu}\widetilde{R}_{[\mu\nu]}$~\cite{bahamonde2025torsion} or
    $F^2 T^2$~\cite{itin2003maxwell,rubilar2003torsion} break the block-diagonal
    polarisation structure and create direct torsion--photon conversion channels.
    Itin classifies 17 independent $F^2 T^2$ coupling structures, two of which
    produce vacuum birefringence and an induced axion field from the torsion
    background.
  \item \textbf{Alternative ghost-free kinetic terms:} Replace $b_5\widetilde{R}^2$
    with Riemann-squared or Weyl-squared couplings that propagate torsion without
    Ostrogradsky instability~\cite{blagojevic2018ghost}.
  \item \textbf{Higher-order (cubic) PGT:} Cubic $R \cdot T \cdot T$ invariants
    eliminate ghost pathologies in the axial and vector torsion
    sectors~\cite{bahamonde2025cubic}.
\end{enumerate}

Theory files: \texttt{examples/torsion\_gertsenshtein/theory.toml} (combined
PGT+EM), \texttt{examples/gertsenshtein/theory\_ungauged.toml} (gauge-independence
baseline).  See also \texttt{docs/tex/torsion.tex} for the PGT framework.

\paragraph{Related literature.}
The torsion-independence result is consistent with several independent analyses:
Obukhov \& Trukhanova~\cite{obukhov2024electrodynamics} find that the spin-torsion--EM
coupling constant $\chi \propto \ell_\mathrm{Pl}/\ell_\rho$ with $\ell_\rho \gg
\ell_\mathrm{Pl}$, making minimal torsion--photon coupling negligibly small.
Shapiro~\cite{shapiro2002torsion} shows that propagating torsion faces unitarity and
renormalization obstacles, requiring torsion masses above all coupled fermion masses for
quantum consistency.
Barker~\cite{barker2024poincare} finds that ghost-free conditions in PGT particle
spectra are sector-specific with no universal closed-form constraint for general
$(\alpha_1, \alpha_2, \alpha_3, b_5)$.

\subsubsection{Metric Dependence of EM Fields (Hwang \& Noh)}
\label{subsec:hwang-noh}

Hwang \& Noh~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton} show that the covariant decomposition
$F_{ij} = \eta_{ijk}(B^k - h^{k\ell}B_\ell)$ involves the metric perturbation, whereas
ad-hoc approaches that write $F_{ij} = \eta_{ijk}\hat{B}^k$ without the metric give
incorrect Maxwell equations.  The incorrect equations happen to give correct results
\emph{only} for uniform constant~$B$, planar gravitational waves, and TT gauge; for
non-uniform or time-varying backgrounds, the two approaches diverge.

\paragraph{Two formulations, two graviton mass terms.}
Hwang \& Noh derive the graviton equation in both the \textbf{EM-field} and
\textbf{potential} formulations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{EM-field formulation:} $(\partial_0^2 - \partial_z^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}\kappa^2 B^2)\,h_+ = \text{source}$
    --- the $+\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa^2 B^2$ term (tachyonic for $\lambda > \lambda_B$)
    comes from including $h_{ij}$ in the stress-energy tensor $T_{ab}$.
  \item \textbf{Potential formulation:}
    $[\partial_0^2 - \partial_z^2 - \tfrac{1}{2}\kappa^2(B_\parallel^2 - B_\perp^2)]\,h_+ = \text{source}$
    --- the sign depends on the angle between $\vec{B}$ and the propagation direction.
    For $\vec{B}$ transverse to propagation ($B_\parallel = 0$), this becomes a
    \emph{stabilising} mass term $-\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa^2 B_\perp^2$.
\end{itemize}
The two formulations are physically equivalent; the sign difference arises because the
relation between EM fields and potentials itself involves the metric:
$b^i = \eta^{ijk}\partial_j A_k + h^i_j B^j$.

\paragraph{TIDAL's equations match the potential formulation.}
TIDAL derives equations from the Lagrangian $\lag = (1/\kappa^2)R - \tfrac{1}{4}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}$,
written in terms of the four-potential~$A_\mu$.  The xPert pipeline expands
$\sqrt{-g}\,\lag$ to second order, automatically capturing all metric-dependent
quantities ($\sqrt{-g}$, $g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}$ in index raising, Christoffel
connections).  No manual EM field identification is performed.  The resulting graviton
mass coefficient $-\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa^2\Bzero^2$ matches Hwang \& Noh's potential-formulation
result for transverse~$B$ exactly (their Eq.~\eqref{eq:gert-eom-h}).  For non-uniform
$B$-fields (e.g.\ the Gaussian scattering example), the Lagrangian approach remains
correct, whereas ad-hoc methods would give different (incorrect) equations.

\subsection{Backreaction and Regime Validity}
\label{subsec:backreaction}

\subsubsection{Separation of Scales: Flat-Background Validity}
\label{subsec:separation-of-scales}

When computing the Gertsenshtein effect, we linearize around flat Minkowski plus a
uniform background~$\Bzero$.  However, $\Bzero$ has nonzero stress-energy
$T_{\mu\nu} \sim \Bzero^2/2$, which sources curvature via Einstein's equations.
The true background should satisfy the full nonlinear Einstein--Maxwell system;
using flat Minkowski is valid only if the metric correction~$\delta g$ sourced
by~$\Bzero$ is negligible.

Hwang \& Noh~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton} provide the key argument (\S\,III.A,
eqs.~22--31).  They define the critical wavenumber
$k_B \equiv \sqrt{8\pi G}\,B_0/c^2$ and show that the backreaction parameter is:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{L^2}{\lambda_B^2} = \frac{2}{\pi}\,\frac{G\,B_0^2\,L^2}{c^4}
  \sim \frac{G M}{L c^2}\,\frac{B_0^2}{\varrho c^2}
  \label{eq:backreaction-param}
\end{equation}
where $L$ is the domain size, $M \sim \varrho L^3$ is the characteristic mass,
and the two factors are respectively the compactness and the magnetic energy
fraction.  Crucially, $L^2/\lambda_B^2 \sim P/(2\pi^2)$ where $P$ is the
conversion probability.  So \textbf{backreaction is negligible whenever
$P \ll 1$}, which is always the case for physical magnetic fields:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrl@{}}
\toprule
System & $B_0$ (G) & $L$ (cm) & $(L/\lambda_B)^2$ \\
\midrule
Lab magnet      & $10^5$    & $10^2$  & $5.2 \times 10^{-35}$ \\
Neutron star    & $10^{13}$ & $10^7$  & $5.2 \times 10^{-9}$ \\
Magnetar        & $10^{15}$ & $10^7$  & $5.2 \times 10^{-5}$ \\
Extreme magnetar& $10^{16}$ & $10^7$  & $5.2 \times 10^{-3}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Even for the most extreme astrophysical scenarios, the backreaction is at most
$\sim 10^{-3}$.  The local mixing-rate correction from backreaction is
$(\kappa B_0)^2 = 16\pi G B_0^2/c^4$, negligible for all physical fields.

\subsubsection{Physical Regime: $P \ll 1$ Always}
\label{subsec:physical-regime}

The conversion probability in the Gertsenshtein effect is always small in physical
systems.  The mixing length is~\cite{palessandro2023simple}:
\begin{equation}
  L_\text{mix} = \frac{2c^2}{\sqrt{G}\,B_0}
    \approx 2\,\text{Mpc}\,\left(\frac{1\,\text{G}}{B_0}\right)
  \label{eq:mixing-length-phys}
\end{equation}
For a magnetar ($B_0 \sim 10^{14}\,$G, field extent $D \sim 10\,$km):
$D/L_\text{mix} \sim 10^{-5}$ and $P \sim 10^{-10}$.  Full Rabi oscillations
($P \sim 1$) require $D \sim L_\text{mix}$, which never occurs for any
astrophysical or laboratory configuration.

\textbf{Implication for simulations:} TIDAL simulations with $\kappa = 1$,
$B_0 = 0.3$ operate in the Rabi regime ($P \sim 88\%$) for numerical convenience.
This is a valid mathematical solution of the linearized equations, but does not
correspond to any physical system.  The physically meaningful quantity is the
\emph{conversion coefficient} $C = P/B_0^2$, which is $B_0$-independent at
leading order and determines conversion for any physical $B_0$.

\subsubsection{Sweep Measurement Methodology}
\label{subsec:sweep-methodology}

For torsion parameter sweeps, the primary metric is the \textbf{conversion
coefficient}:
\begin{equation}
  C(\delta_1, \alpha, \omega_0) \equiv \frac{P}{B_0^2}
    \approx \frac{\kappa_\text{eff}^2\,t_\text{end}^2}{4}
  \label{eq:conversion-coeff}
\end{equation}
valid in the early-time quadratic regime ($\kappa_\text{eff}\,B_0\,t_\text{end} \ll 1$).
The \textbf{amplification factor} is:
\begin{equation}
  A = \frac{\kappa_\text{eff}}{\kappa}
    = \sqrt{\frac{C_\text{torsion}}{C_\text{EM}}}
    = \frac{B_\text{min}(\text{EM})}{B_\text{min}(\text{torsion})}
  \label{eq:amplification}
\end{equation}
The last equality shows that $A$ is identical to the B-field lowering factor from
the critical field analysis (\S\ref{sec:critical-field}): ``torsion reduces the
required $B$-field by factor~$A$ for the same conversion.''

In the linearized regime, $P = c_2\,B_0^2 + c_4\,B_0^4 + \cdots$, where $c_4$
corrections arise from $B_0$-dependent eigenfrequencies (graviton mass term
$-\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa^2 B_0^2$, torsion-induced detuning).  At $B_0 = 0.01$,
these corrections are $\mathcal{O}(10^{-4})$; the leading-order extraction of~$C$
is robust.

\paragraph{Frequency-dependent diagnostics.}
For propagating torsion with mass $\mu = \sqrt{\alpha/b_5}$, the conversion
coefficient $C(\omega_0)$ depends on the initial GW frequency relative to~$\mu$.
Scanning $k_0$ (and hence $\omega_0 = k_0$) maps the torsion mass detuning:
$C(\omega_0 \gg \mu)$ is enhanced (torsion responds fully),
$C(\omega_0 \ll \mu)$ reduces to $C_\text{EM}$ (torsion frozen out),
$C(\omega_0 \sim \mu)$ reveals the resonance structure.
This frequency scan is the key diagnostic for distinguishing propagating from
non-propagating torsion.

\subsubsection{Target 3: Detuned Conversion (Phase F1)}
\label{subsec:target3}

\textbf{Status: NOT IMPLEMENTED --- requires gauge-invariant mass mechanism}

Adding a Proca-type photon mass $-\omega_p^2/2 \cdot a_{-\mu}\, \eta^{\mu\nu}\, a_{-\nu}$ to the full 4D Lagrangian generates position-dependent coefficients $\Bzero^2\kappa^2\omega_p^2\, z^2$ on the graviton equations. These arise from the volume element expansion $(\varepsilon/2)\mathrm{Tr}(h) \times (\omega_p^2/2)|\bg{A}|^2$ where $|\bg{A}|^2 = \Bzero^2 z^2$ (since $\bg{A}_y = -\Bzero z$ in the plane-wave gauge). With periodic BCs, $z^2$ is discontinuous at the wrap point --- incorrect for a uniform plasma model.

\textbf{Root cause}: xPert correctly evaluates the full second-order action including the metric coupling to the background EM energy density. In the plasma context (which is a medium effect, not a fundamental Proca field), this coupling is unphysical. No Lagrangian reformulation within the current 4D gauge-field framework can avoid this: any gauge representation of a transverse $B$-field has $|\bg{A}|^2 \propto z^2$ in 1D. An effective 1+1D scalar theory ($h_\mathrm{gw}$, $a_\mathrm{em}$ scalars) yields constant coefficients but is no different from the existing \texttt{coupled\_scalars} example.

\textbf{What would be needed}: A gauge-invariant effective mass that couples only to the transverse photon polarisation without coupling to the background gauge potential through the volume element. Possible approaches: (a)~a Stueckelberg mass in unitary gauge with a carefully chosen gauge field, or (b)~a medium/refractive-index formulation at the level of the equations of motion (bypassing the Lagrangian for the plasma sector).

\textbf{Reference}: Raffelt \& Stodolsky~(1988, PRD 37:1237); Dandoy, Lella et al.~\cite{dandoy2024constraining}

\subsubsection{Target 4: Finite-Region Scattering (Phase F2)}
\label{subsec:target4}

\textbf{Status: IMPLEMENTED} --- see \texttt{examples/gertsenshtein/theory\_localized.toml}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Gaussian $\Bzero(z) = B_\mathrm{peak} \times \exp(-z^2/(2R^2))$ along $x$-axis. The gauge potential $\bg{A}_y = -B_\mathrm{peak} \times R \times \sqrt{\pi/2} \times \mathrm{Erf}(z/(\sqrt{2}\cdot R))$ satisfies $\partial_z(\bg{A}_y) = -\Bzero(z)$. The $\mathrm{Erf}$ function is supported by the TIDAL evaluator (\texttt{\_eval\_utils.py} \texttt{\_FUNCTION\_MAP}). Constant named \texttt{Bpeak} (not \texttt{B0\_peak}) --- in Wolfram, \texttt{X\_Y} parses as \texttt{Pattern[X, Blank[Y]]} (a pattern expression, not a symbol), silently corrupting the symbolic computation.

\paragraph{Analytical target (Boccaletti 1970, weak-field Gaussian).}

\begin{equation}
  P(\text{graviton} \to \text{photon}) = \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\kappa}{2} \int \Bzero(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right) = \sin^2\!\left(\kappa \cdot B_\mathrm{peak} \cdot R \cdot \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\right)
  \label{eq:gert-boccaletti}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Simulation parameters.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Domain: $[-100, 100]$, 1024 points, non-periodic (Neumann BCs) --- periodic BCs are incorrect since $\bg{A}_y$ is not periodic
  \item IC: Gaussian wavepacket ($h_7$) at $z = -50$, $\sigma = 5$, $k = 2.0$, $t_\mathrm{end} = 120$ (stops after one pass through $B$-field, before wavepacket hits right boundary at $t \approx 150$)
  \item Measurement: \texttt{--what conversion --source h\_7 --target a\_2} (NOT \texttt{--what energy} --- position-dependent coefficients cause known limitation)
  \item \textbf{Bounds/centre require \texttt{=} syntax}: \texttt{--bounds="-100:100"}, \texttt{--ic-center=-50.0} (shell interprets leading \texttt{-} as flag otherwise)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Confirmed numerical result} (defaults: $\kappa = 1$, $B_\mathrm{peak} = 0.1$, $R = 5 = \sigma$):

The Boccaletti formula $P = \sin^2(\kappa/2 \times \int \Bzero\,\mathrm{d}z)$ is \textbf{exact for massless vacuum conversion} for any $R/\sigma$ ratio. For massless graviton--photon conversion (no plasma), both modes travel at $c$ with identical dispersion ($k_h = k_\gamma$), so $\Delta k = 0$ exactly. The conversion amplitude at $q = \Delta k = 0$ is just the DC component $\int \Bzero\,\mathrm{d}z$ --- independent of $R/\sigma$ (Boccaletti et al.~1970; Domcke, Garcia-Cely \& Lee~\cite{domcke2025scattering}, Section~4.2).

The thin-lens / thick-lens distinction applies when $\Delta k \neq 0$ (plasma detuning, axion mass), creating a coherence length $L_\mathrm{coh} = 1/|\Delta k|$ that limits coherent accumulation. Without plasma, $L_\mathrm{coh} \to \infty$ and the formula holds for all $R$ (see \cref{sec:gertsenshtein-localized}).
