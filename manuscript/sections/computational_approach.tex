\section{Computational approach}%
\label{ComputationalApproach}

% Paragraph 1 — TIDAL pipeline (~80 w).
\paragraph*{TIDAL pipeline}
\TIDAL{} (Tensor Integration and Derivation for Any Lagrangian) automates
the linearised-PDE survey pipeline: a Lagrangian specification in TOML format
drives a \xAct{}/\Mathematica{} symbolic backend that derives the Euler--Lagrange
equations, exports them as a JSON theory file, and passes the result to
native Python PDE solvers backed by SUNDIALS, leapfrog, or spectral methods.
The automation is necessary for a survey across PGT theory space: each of the
[N] theory variants examined here requires a distinct set of coupled tensor PDEs
that would be error-prone to derive by hand.
Full pipeline architecture is documented in \cref{Architecture}.

\paragraph*{Relation to other automation tools}
\TIDAL{} complements \PSALTer{}~\cite{barker2024psalter,barker2025psalter2}
(particle-spectrum extraction from arbitrary tensor Lagrangians) and
\Hamilcar{}~\cite{barker2025hamilcar}
(Poisson-bracket and constraint-algebra computation in canonical gravity)
by automating the dynamical-evolution step — solving the linearised PDEs
that the spectrum and constraint analyses do not address.

% Paragraph 3 — Fourier modal solver (~100 w).
\paragraph*{Fourier modal solver}
\TIDAL{} auto-selects a Fourier modal backend for flat-metric, periodic-boundary
systems and delivers machine-precision eigendecompositions of the full
coupled-field evolution operator.
The Fourier decomposition eliminates spatial operators analytically,
reducing the PDE system to a dense matrix ODE per wavenumber mode that
is then solved by eigendecomposition or Krylov matrix-exponential methods.
This gives machine-precision conversion amplitudes and eliminates the spatial
discretisation error that limits finite-difference solvers.
Algorithmic detail and convergence behaviour are documented in
\cref{NumericalEvolution}.

% Paragraph 4 — Other backends (~60 w).
\paragraph*{Solver fallbacks}
For non-periodic boundaries or curved-space theories the \TIDAL{} solver
auto-selects from four fallback backends based on equation structure:
SUNDIALS IDA for DAE systems with algebraic constraints,
SUNDIALS CVODE for stiff-wave-like ODEs,
a native leapfrog integrator for Hamiltonian-conserving evolution,
and \texttt{scipy.integrate} for general-purpose ODE systems.
Details are in \cref{NumericalEvolution}.

\paragraph*{Tachyon-permissive inference}
Bayesian parameter estimation uses PolyChord nested sampling with a
\emph{tachyon-permissive} likelihood architecture: linear instabilities are
assigned a soft floor $\log\mathcal{L} = {-100} + \mathcal{N}(0,\,\sigma)$
rather than being rejected, providing a stability gradient that guides
sampler exploration across multi-modal posterior landscapes.
Coupling priors are compactified via $\arctan$-uniform
distributions, covering the full real line without hard boundaries.
Each simulation is tagged with a \texttt{run\_status} label
(\texttt{success}, \texttt{simulation\_diverged}, \texttt{metric\_nan},
etc.)\ enabling post-chain filtering and diagnostic analysis.
The Bayesian-inference architecture, soft-penalty mathematics, and
prior-compactification scheme are documented in \cref{InferenceArchitecture}.

\paragraph*{Validation and survey scale}
The pipeline is validated against the analytic Boccaletti conversion
formula~\cite{boccaletti1970conversion} to better than $[\text{B.2 \%}]$
across the full sweep of survey parameters; the validation hierarchy is
documented in \cref{Validation}.
The survey covers [N] PGT theory variants in parameter spaces ranging from
