\section{Perturbative reduction in higher-curvature Lagrangians}%
\label{PerturbativeReduction}

\TIDAL{} treats a correction $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ to a well-posed base
Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_0$ as a perturbative shift to the base-theory dynamics.
The effective-field-theory background, the %order-by-order
equations-of-motion reduction, and the Lagrangian perturbative substitution used
for Hamiltonian analysis
%  --- together with the conditions under which it fails ---
are described here.

\paragraph*{Higher-order terms as effective corrections}
For theories whose Lagrangian admits the split
$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_0 + \varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ with $\varepsilon$ a small
dimensionless coupling, the correction $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ is treated
throughout as a perturbative correction to the dynamics of $\mathcal{L}_0$,
not as an independent dynamical source.

Within effective field theory, terms of this form are the low-energy residues of an
unknown ultraviolet completion, encoding perturbative corrections to base-theory
observables and not independent dynamics in their own right~\cite{Simon:1990ic,
Simon:1990jn,Donoghue:1994dn,burgess2003quantum}.
Grosse-Knetter~\cite{Grosse-Knetter:1993tae} proves, within the canonical formalism for
higher-derivative theories~\cite{ostrogradsky1850memoires,Woodard:2015zca}, that
expressing the higher time derivatives in $\mathcal{L}_1$ by substituting the
base-theory equations of motion
% --- which solve for $\partial_t^2\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$
% as a function of fields and first derivatives --- 
is equivalent to a field redefinition
$\varphi^{\mathcal{I}} \to \varphi^{\mathcal{I}} + \varepsilon\,\delta\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$
in the action.
Such a redefinition is a canonical transformation: it preserves the Poisson bracket
relations between coordinates and momenta, so that Hamilton's equations and all physical
observables take identical form in the new variables.
The original higher-derivative Lagrangian and the perturbatively reduced one are therefore
physically equivalent within the perturbative regime, and the order-by-order expansion of
\cref{EOMHierarchy} reproduces the physical predictions of $\mathcal{L}$ to first order
in $\varepsilon$.
This equivalence requires that the base-theory equation of motion for each reduced field
take the form $\partial_t^2\varphi^{\mathcal{I}} = F^{\mathcal{I}}[\varphi,\partial_t\varphi,
\nabla\varphi]$, since this equation is the substitution rule itself; fields whose
base-theory equation is purely algebraic violate the requirement and the canonical
equivalence breaks down --- the failure mode addressed below.

The alternative --- treating $\mathcal{L}$ as an exact theory rather than a truncated
expansion --- encounters a structural obstruction.
Ostrogradsky's theorem~\cite{ostrogradsky1850memoires,Pais:1950za,Woodard:2015zca} states
that any non-degenerate Lagrangian whose Euler--Lagrange equations are of differential
order greater than two has a Hamiltonian unbounded below: the momentum conjugate to the
highest time derivative enters the Hamiltonian linearly so that the energy is unbounded
below for arbitrary sign of that momentum, and the corresponding mode is a ghost.%
\footnote{Apparent counterexamples in gravity --- the Gauss--Bonnet term in four
spacetime dimensions~\cite{Lovelock:1971yv} and the higher-derivative terms in the
Einstein--Hilbert Lagrangian~\cite{hobson2006general,wald1984general} --- are excluded:
the former is topological and contributes only a boundary term, and the latter reduce to
a total divergence after integration by parts, leaving the Euler--Lagrange equations of
order two in both cases.}
The theorem is non-perturbative; the ghost is present for any non-zero $\varepsilon$.
The ghost mode has characteristic frequency $\sim 1/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, exactly
the scale at which $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ becomes comparable to $\mathcal{L}_0$
and the perturbative expansion itself breaks down; the ghost is therefore an artefact of
extrapolating the truncated Lagrangian beyond its regime of validity, not a physical
degree of freedom.
We therefore treat $\mathcal{L}_0$ as the base theory and account for the effect
of $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ as an iterative correction.

\paragraph*{Perturbative reduction of the equations of motion}
We solve the equations of motion by the iterative order-reduction method of Parker
and Simon~\cite{parker1993reduced}, generalised to the multi-field linearised-PDE
regime of \TIDAL{}.

Using $q = \{\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}\}$ to denote the collection of perturbation fields
and writing the full equation of motion as $E[q;\,\varepsilon] = 0$~\cite{parker1993reduced,
jaen1986reduction},
the base operator is $E_{\mathrm{base}}[q] \equiv E[q;\,\varepsilon{=}0]$ and the correction operator
is $E_{\mathrm{corr}}[q] \equiv \partial_\varepsilon E[q;\,\varepsilon]\big|_{\varepsilon=0}$
from $\mathcal{L}_1$, which may carry higher time derivatives.
Expanding $q = q^{(0)} + \varepsilon\,q^{(1)} + \cdots$ and collecting by order:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^0) &:\quad
    E_{\mathrm{base}}\bigl[q^{(0)}\bigr] = 0, \notag \\[2pt]
  \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^1) &:\quad
    E_{\mathrm{base}}\bigl[q^{(1)}\bigr] = -E_{\mathrm{corr}}\bigl[q^{(0)}\bigr].
  \label{EOMHierarchy}
\end{align}
The essential property is that the left-hand side operator at every order is always the
ghost-free second-order base operator $E_{\mathrm{base}}$; the higher-derivative
contributions from $\mathcal{L}_1$ appear only as sources evaluated on the
previous-order solution.
We call the order-$\varepsilon^0$ solution \emph{Pass~0} and the
order-$\varepsilon^1$ correction \emph{Pass~1}.

Two alternatives were investigated and rejected: Lagrangian-level substitution of the
order-zero equations of motion directly into the higher-derivative
correction~\cite{jaen1986reduction} --- expressing each $\partial_t^n\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$
via repeated differentiation of the base-theory equations --- has no substitution target
when a field equation is purely algebraic at order zero; and
introducing each time-derivative order of the fields as a separate dynamical
variable~\cite{ostrogradsky1850memoires,Woodard:2015zca} ---
$Q_n^{\mathcal{I}} \equiv \partial_t^n\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$ for $n = 0, 1, \ldots, N-1$
with $N$ the order of the highest time derivative in $\mathcal{L}$ --- to reduce the
system to a first-order form promotes the ghost modes predicted by Ostrogradsky's
theorem to live degrees of freedom, producing unphysical exponential growth in long-time
simulation.

On flat periodic domains, the Pass~0 system at wavenumber $k$ reduces to the per-mode
ODE $\partial_t\hat{y}^{(0)} = M(k)\,\hat{y}^{(0)}$ (cf.~\cref{ModalPerMode}), where
$\hat{y}^{(0)}$ stacks the Fourier amplitudes of every field and velocity slot.
For the perturbative-reduction path, the eigendecomposition $M(k) = V D V^{-1}$ is
used rather than the Pad\'e scaling-and-squaring path of \cref{ModalSolver}, since the
per-eigenmode expansion is required explicitly for the Duhamel integral.
With $D = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\ldots)$ collecting the eigenvalues of $M(k)$
and $V$ the right-eigenvector matrix, the Pass~0 solution at initial data
$\hat{y}^{(0)}(0)$ is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}^{(0)}(t) = V\,e^{Dt}\,\alpha,
  \qquad
  \alpha = V^{-1}\hat{y}^{(0)}(0).
  \label{PassZeroSolution}
\end{equation}

Pass~1 satisfies the inhomogeneous system
$\partial_t\hat{y}^{(1)} = M(k)\,\hat{y}^{(1)} + C_{\mathrm{src}}\,\hat{y}^{(0)}(t)$
with $\hat{y}^{(1)}(0) = 0$, where $C_{\mathrm{src}}$ is the source coupling matrix
assembled from the $\varepsilon^1$ terms in the equations of motion.
Substituting $\hat{y}^{(1)} = V\hat{z}^{(1)}$ and the Pass~0 solution
$\hat{y}^{(0)}(t) = V e^{Dt}\alpha$ into this system and multiplying through by $V^{-1}$
diagonalises the homogeneous part ($V^{-1}M(k)V = D$) and rotates the source into the
eigenbasis ($V^{-1}C_{\mathrm{src}}V \equiv \beta$), giving
\begin{equation}
  \dot{\hat{z}}^{(1)}
  \;=\;
  D\,\hat{z}^{(1)}
  \;+\; \beta\,e^{Dt}\,\alpha,
  \qquad \hat{z}^{(1)}(0) = 0.
  \label{EigenODESystem}
\end{equation}
Component-wise this is a set of decoupled scalar inhomogeneous ODEs,
\begin{equation}
  \dot{\hat{z}}^{(1)}_i
  \;=\;
  \lambda_i\,\hat{z}^{(1)}_i
  \;+\; \sum_j\beta_{ij}\,\alpha_j\,e^{\lambda_j t},
  \qquad \hat{z}^{(1)}_i(0) = 0,
  \label{EigenODE}
\end{equation}
the homogeneous term carrying the eigenvalue $\lambda_i$ of mode $i$ and the source term
expressing the rotated coupling of every Pass~0 mode $j$ into the Pass~1 evolution of
mode $i$.
Multiplying through by $e^{-\lambda_i t}$ converts the left side to
$\tfrac{d}{dt}[e^{-\lambda_i t}\hat{z}^{(1)}_i]$; integrating both sides from $0$ to
$t$~\cite{hochbruck2010exponential,almohy2011action} then gives
\begin{equation}
  e^{-\lambda_i t}\hat{z}^{(1)}_i(t)
  \;=\;
  \sum_j \beta_{ij}\,\alpha_j
  \int_0^t e^{(\lambda_j - \lambda_i)\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,
  \label{DuhamelIntegral}
\end{equation}
in which the exponential integrand evaluates in closed form.
Restoring the factor $e^{\lambda_i t}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \hat{z}^{(1)}_i(t)
  \;=\;
  \sum_j \beta_{ij}\,\alpha_j\,G(\lambda_i,\lambda_j;\,t),
  \label{DuhamelSolution}
\end{equation}
where the Duhamel kernel $G$ is
\begin{equation}
  G(\lambda,\mu;\,t)
  \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{e^{\mu t} - e^{\lambda t}}{\mu - \lambda} & \mu \neq \lambda, \\[6pt]
    t\,e^{\lambda t} & \mu = \lambda,
  \end{cases}
  \label{DuhamelKernel}
\end{equation}
and the full Pass~1 state is recovered as $\hat{y}^{(1)}(t) = V\,\hat{z}^{(1)}(t)$.
Pass~1 is therefore computed entirely from the Pass~0 eigensystem; no additional
differential equation need be solved.
Near the degenerate limit $|(\mu-\lambda)t| \to 0$, direct evaluation of
\cref{DuhamelKernel} suffers catastrophic cancellation; setting $z = (\mu-\lambda)t$,
\TIDAL{} uses the Taylor expansion~\cite{almohy2011action}
\begin{equation}
  G\bigl|_{|z|<10^{-5}}
  \;=\;
  t\,e^{\lambda t}\sum_{j=0}^{12}\frac{z^{j}}{(j+1)!},
  \label{DuhamelTaylor}
\end{equation}
the threshold and term count following~\cite{almohy2011action}.

The iterative approximation holds while the cumulative phase error satisfies
$\varepsilon\,\omega\,t_{\mathrm{end}} \ll 1$~\cite{parker1993reduced}, monitored
at run time by \TIDAL{}'s validity diagnostic.

\subsection{Perturbative reduction of the Hamiltonian}
Energy measurement and Noether consistency require a Hamiltonian, and the standard
Legendre transform applies once the Lagrangian contains at most first-order time
derivatives.
Integration by parts reduces most higher-derivative products, but certain residues
are irreducible: a term such as
$(\partial_t^2 h_a)(\partial_t^2 h_b)$ leaves a second-time-derivative product
after any finite sequence of integration-by-parts moves.

To reduce the Lagrangian before the Legendre transform, \TIDAL{} implements
\emph{Lagrangian perturbative substitution} (LPS).
The order-$\varepsilon^0$ equations of motion are solved simultaneously for the
second time derivative of each field,
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t^2 q_i
  \;=\;
  F_i\!\bigl[q,\,\partial_t q,\,\nabla q\bigr]
  \;+\; \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon),
  \label{LPSSubstitution}
\end{equation}
where $F_i$ depends only on first-order time derivatives and spatial derivatives.
Where the correction $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ introduces terms with time derivatives
of order greater than two the rule \cref{LPSSubstitution} is extended by
differentiating with respect to $t$ as many times as needed, substituting
\cref{LPSSubstitution} for any $\partial_t^2 q_j$ that appears at each step.
The resulting substitution is applied to the order-$\varepsilon^1$ Lagrangian, and the
series is truncated at $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$; the reduced Lagrangian carries at
most first-order time derivatives and is admissible for the standard Legendre transform
of \cref{EulerLagrange}.
LPS is faithful exactly when the Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina regularity
condition holds~\cite{jaen1986reduction}: the order-zero equations must contain a
time-derivative term to solve for.
When the condition fails, the substitution silently drops a promoted degree of
freedom and the reduced theory loses physical content.
The limit of LPS is the subject of the paragraphs that follow.

\paragraph*{Constraint promotion and perturbative-reduction failure}
Constraint promotion arises when the small coupling $\varepsilon$
promotes an algebraic constraint of the base theory to a propagating degree of
freedom.
In the Einstein--Cartan limit $\varepsilon = 0$, certain metric-perturbation
components satisfy algebraic relations and carry no kinetic term.
Turning on $\varepsilon$ infinitesimally promotes these relations to dynamical
equations and introduces new propagating modes; the constraint structure of the
theory changes discontinuously across $\varepsilon = 0$.
An order-by-order expansion in $\varepsilon$ is blind to this promotion because
the new mode is absent at $\varepsilon = 0$ and cannot be reached by a power-series
expansion.
Constraint promotion therefore implies that no perturbative expansion in $\varepsilon$
connects the base theory to the small-$\varepsilon$ theory once a constraint has
been promoted.

\paragraph*{Failure mode I: Ostrogradsky}
The Ostrogradsky theorem~\cite{ostrogradsky1850memoires,Woodard:2015zca} is the
cleanest statement of the barrier at the Lagrangian level: a non-degenerate
higher-derivative kinetic structure produces a Hamiltonian unbounded below.
In the quadratic-PGT theories carrying the $\tilde{R}^2$ correction, the higher-curvature
term generates fourth-order time derivatives of the metric perturbation, and the
Ostrogradsky diagnosis applies directly.
The theorem is non-perturbative; it holds for any non-zero value of the coupling,
and it is the mechanism by which constraint promotion enters the Hamiltonian analysis
at the Lagrangian level.

\paragraph*{Failure mode II: order-by-order substitution}
The same order-by-order substitution strategy that drives the EOM reduction of
\cref{EOMHierarchy} was also investigated for Hamiltonian order-reduction.
Parker and Simon~\cite{parker1993reduced} identified the precise boundary of its
validity in semi-classical gravity: the procedure is faithful when the
higher-derivative correction perturbs a well-posed lower-order theory, and it
fails when the correction introduces degrees of freedom with no lower-order
analogue.
In the Hamiltonian setting, LPS cannot execute \cref{LPSSubstitution} for a
field whose order-zero equation is algebraic rather than differential, because
there is no $\partial_t^n\varphi$ term on the left-hand side to solve for.
The substitution step is therefore undefined whenever the base-theory equation is
algebraic, and LPS cannot bridge the discontinuity that the Ostrogradsky
diagnosis already identifies at the Lagrangian level.

\paragraph*{Failure mode III: the Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina obstruction}
Ja{\'e}n, Llosa and Molina~\cite{jaen1986reduction} formalised the failure for
the general higher-derivative Lagrangian.
They showed that a finite-order reduction by repeated use of the order-zero
equation of motion can in principle be constructed, provided the Lagrangian
satisfies a regularity condition; that condition fails precisely when an
algebraic constraint of the unperturbed theory is promoted to a dynamical
equation at first order in $\varepsilon$.
Their result fits the PGT structure exactly: the order-zero theory is the
Einstein--Cartan system with algebraic torsion constraints, and the
higher-curvature operators in the quadratic-PGT action promote some of those
constraints to propagating fields.
Grosse-Knetter's canonical-equivalence result~\cite{Grosse-Knetter:1993tae}
reaches the same conclusion from the Hamiltonian side: the field redefinition
that realises the perturbative substitution requires the base-theory equation
of motion to contain a $\partial_t^2\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$ term to solve for ---
the same prerequisite that the Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina condition formalises ---
and is therefore equally inapplicable to algebraically constrained fields.
The Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina obstruction gives the sharpest analytical
criterion, distinguishing theories where LPS is faithful from those where it
loses physical content.

\paragraph*{Structural analogues}
Constraint promotion belongs to a wider class of discontinuous
limits in gravity and gauge theory.
The van Dam--Veltman--Zakharov
discontinuity~\cite{vandam1970massive,zakharov1970linearized} shows that the
massless limit of a Fierz--Pauli graviton recovers only five of the six
general-relativistic polarisations: the sixth arises through a constraint
promoted at non-zero mass.
The Boulware--Deser non-decoupling~\cite{boulware1972gravitation} extends this
to the non-linear theory by identifying a ghost-like sixth mode once the
Fierz--Pauli tuning is broken.
The Maxwell--Proca boundary is the simplest instance: at finite vector-boson
mass the algebraic relation $\rcD{_\mu}\FieldA{^\mu} = 0$ holds as a
constraint, while at zero mass it becomes a gauge symmetry; no perturbative
expansion in the mass encodes the transition.
The quadratic-PGT case is the quadratic-curvature analogue of
this structural pattern.

\paragraph*{Software implementation and failure detection}
\TIDAL{} handles both sides of the perturbative-reduction framework
automatically.
For the equations of motion, the Wolfram derivation stage tags each operator
term with an \texttt{order\_in\_eps} integer; at simulation time,
\texttt{PerturbativeSolver} separates base-theory terms from correction terms
and runs Pass~0 followed by Pass~1 via the Duhamel kernel of
\cref{DuhamelKernel}, without theory-specific code.

For the Hamiltonian, \TIDAL{} attempts LPS symbolically at derivation time.
Failure is detected by checking whether the order-$\varepsilon^0$ momentum
kernel changes rank when $\varepsilon$ is set to a non-zero test value; a rank
jump signals constraint promotion and the software emits a diagnostic that
marks the theory as having no LPS-reduced Hamiltonian.
The three affected theories --- \texttt{graviton\_torsion},
\texttt{torsion\_gertsenshtein}, and \texttt{torsion\_gertsenshtein\_combined},
all carrying $\tilde{R}^2$ --- retain their pre-LPS Hamiltonians, and
their energy diagnostics are recorded separately rather than alongside
conversion results.

\paragraph*{Implications for the surveys}
The EOM and Hamiltonian reductions have different scopes, and the surveys of
\cref{Results} are structured accordingly.
The iterative EOM reduction of \cref{EOMHierarchy} succeeds for all surveyed
theories, including the $\tilde{R}^2$ quadratic-PGT theories: conversion
probabilities are computed from field trajectories evolved by Pass~0 and
Pass~1, and no Hamiltonian is required for that computation.
Constraint promotion applies only to the Hamiltonian side and only
for the three theories named above; their energy diagnostics are not presented
alongside the conversion amplitudes.
Theories without higher-curvature terms are unaffected and use the standard
linearised methodology of \cref{SymbolicComputation} throughout.
The Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina obstruction is therefore a precise statement of
which analyses the linearised methodology can perform for each theory class,
not a defect of the software; the non-perturbative treatment needed to go
further is outlined in \cref{Discussion}.
