\section{Perturbative reduction in higher-curvature Lagrangians}%
\label{PerturbativeReduction}

\TIDAL{} treats a correction $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ to a well-posed base
Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_0$ as a perturbative shift to the base-theory dynamics.
The effective-field-theory background, the equations-of-motion reduction, and the
Lagrangian reduction used to obtain a Hamiltonian are described here.

\paragraph*{Higher-order terms as effective corrections}
For theories whose Lagrangian admits the decomposition
$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_0 + \varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ with $\varepsilon$ a small
dimensionless coupling, $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ is treated throughout as a
perturbative correction within the regime $\varepsilon\ll1$.

Such higher-order operators are the low-energy residues of an unknown ultraviolet
completion in the effective-field-theory
interpretation~\cite{Simon:1990ic,Simon:1990jn,Donoghue:1994dn,burgess2003quantum}.
Grosse-Knetter~\cite{Grosse-Knetter:1993tae,ostrogradsky1850memoires,Woodard:2015zca}
proves that expressing the higher time derivatives in $\mathcal{L}_1$ by substituting
the base-theory equations of motion is equivalent to a canonical field redefinition
$\varphi^{\mathcal{I}} \to \varphi^{\mathcal{I}} + \varepsilon\,\delta\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$
that preserves the Poisson bracket structure.
The original and reduced Lagrangians are
therefore physically equivalent within the perturbative regime.
This equivalence requires the base-theory equation of motion for each field undergoing the reduction to
take the form $\partial_t^2\varphi^{\mathcal{I}} = F^{\mathcal{I}}[\varphi,\partial_t\varphi,
\{\nabla^n\varphi\}_{n\geq 1}]$~\cite{Grosse-Knetter:1993tae}. Fields whose base-theory
equation is purely algebraic (in the sense of \cref{SymbolicComputation}) violate the
requirement, and the perturbative-reduction framework then fails.

The alternative --- treating $\mathcal{L}$ as a fundamental theory ---
encounters Ostrogradsky's theorem.
Ostrogradsky's theorem~\cite{ostrogradsky1850memoires,Pais:1950za} states
that any non-degenerate Lagrangian whose EL equations are of differential
order greater than two has a ghost~\cite{Woodard:2015zca}: a dynamical degree of
freedom whose Hamiltonian is unbounded below.%
\footnote{Some Lagrangians appear to contain higher-derivative terms but do not
generate higher-order EL equations. The Gauss--Bonnet term in four spacetime
dimensions~\cite{Lovelock:1971yv} is topological and contributes only a boundary
term, and the higher-derivative terms in the Einstein--Hilbert
Lagrangian~\cite{wald1984general} reduce to a total
divergence~\cite{hobson2006general}; the EL equations remain of order two in both
cases. The non-degeneracy condition in the theorem also excludes Lagrangians whose
higher derivatives are an artefact of variable choice (e.g.\ writing
$\varphi = \partial_x \chi$ makes $\varphi$-derivatives appear higher than they
physically are).}
The theorem is non-perturbative; the ghost is present for any non-zero $\varepsilon$.
The ghost is an artefact of extrapolating the truncated Lagrangian beyond its regime of
validity, not a physical degree of freedom, and a perturbative treatment within
$\varepsilon \omega t \ll 1$~\cite{parker1993reduced} never
encounters it.
We therefore treat $\mathcal{L}_0$ as the base theory and $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$
perturbatively.

\subsection{Perturbative reduction of the equations of motion}
We solve the equations of motion by the iterative order-reduction method of Parker
and Simon~\cite{parker1993reduced}, applied here to multiple linearised perturbation
fields evolving under a quadratic Lagrangian.

Using $q = \{\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}\}$ to denote the collection of dynamical fields
that remain after the constraint-elimination step of \cref{ModalSchur} and writing
the full equation of motion as $E[q;\,\varepsilon] = 0$~\cite{parker1993reduced,
jaen1986reduction},
the base operator is $E_{\mathrm{base}}[q] \equiv E[q;\,\varepsilon{=}0]$ and the correction operator
is $E_{\mathrm{corr}}[q] \equiv \partial_\varepsilon E[q;\,\varepsilon]\big|_{\varepsilon=0}$
from $\mathcal{L}_1$, which may carry derivatives of \emph{any} order.
Expanding $q = q^{(0)} + \varepsilon\,q^{(1)} + \cdots$ and collecting by order:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^0) &:\quad
    E_{\mathrm{base}}\bigl[q^{(0)}\bigr] = 0, \notag \\[2pt]
  \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^1) &:\quad
    E_{\mathrm{base}}\bigl[q^{(1)}\bigr] = -E_{\mathrm{corr}}\bigl[q^{(0)}\bigr].
  \label{EOMHierarchy}
\end{align}
At every order the left-hand-side operator is the ghost-free second-order base operator
$E_{\mathrm{base}}$; the higher-derivative contributions from $\mathcal{L}_1$ appear
only as sources evaluated on the previous-order solution.
We call the order-$\varepsilon^0$ solution \emph{Pass~0} and the
order-$\varepsilon^1$ correction \emph{Pass~1}.

The Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina (JLM) substitution~\cite{jaen1986reduction}, which substitutes the
$\varepsilon^0$ EL equation directly into the higher-derivative correction in
the Lagrangian, has no substitution target when a field equation is purely algebraic at
$\varepsilon^0$, since no $\partial_t^n\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$ is available to solve for. The
Ostrogradsky auxiliary-field reduction~\cite{ostrogradsky1850memoires,Woodard:2015zca}
introduces each time-derivative order as a separate dynamical variable
$Q_n^{\mathcal{I}} \equiv \partial_t^n\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$ for $n = 0, 1, \ldots, N-1$,
with $N$ the order of the highest time derivative in $\mathcal{L}$, yielding a first-order
system. This treats the full $\varepsilon$-finite
theory as exact, in which case the Ostrogradsky ghost is part of the dynamics. Direct
numerical evolution therefore produces unbounded ghost growth, with no physical
interpretation in the EFT regime.

\paragraph*{Modal derivation and the Duhamel kernel}
On flat periodic domains, the Pass~0 system at wavenumber $k$ reduces to the per-mode
ODE $\partial_t\hat{y}^{(0)} = M(k)\,\hat{y}^{(0)}$ (cf.~\cref{ModalPerMode}), where
$\hat{y}^{(0)}$ stacks the Fourier amplitudes of every field and velocity slot.
For the perturbative-reduction path, the eigendecomposition $M(k) = V D V^{-1}$ is
used rather than the Pad\'e path of \cref{ModalSolver}, since the
per-eigenmode expansion is required for the Duhamel integral
\cref{DuhamelIntegral,DuhamelSolution,DuhamelKernel}.
With $D = \mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\ldots)$ collecting the eigenvalues of $M(k)$
and $V$ the right-eigenvector matrix, the initial data $\hat{y}^{(0)}(0)$ is projected
into the eigenbasis as $\hat{c} = V^{-1}\hat{y}^{(0)}(0)$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}^{(0)}(t) = V\,e^{Dt}\,\hat{c}.
  \label{PassZeroSolution}
\end{equation}

Pass~1 satisfies the inhomogeneous system
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\hat{y}^{(1)} = M(k)\,\hat{y}^{(1)} + C_{\mathrm{corr}}\,\hat{y}^{(0)}(t),
  \qquad \hat{y}^{(1)}(0) = 0,
  \label{PassOneSystem}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\mathrm{corr}}$ is the Fourier representation of the correction operator
$E_{\mathrm{corr}}$ that appears as the order-$\varepsilon^1$ source in
\cref{EOMHierarchy}.
Substituting $\hat{y}^{(1)} = V\hat{z}^{(1)}$ and the Pass~0 solution
$\hat{y}^{(0)}(t) = V e^{Dt}\hat{c}$ into this system and multiplying through by $V^{-1}$
diagonalises the homogeneous part ($V^{-1}M(k)V = D$) and rotates the source into the
eigenbasis ($V^{-1}C_{\mathrm{corr}}V \equiv S$), giving
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\hat{z}^{(1)}
  \;=\;
  D\,\hat{z}^{(1)}
  \;+\; S\,e^{Dt}\,\hat{c},
  \qquad \hat{z}^{(1)}(0) = 0.
  \label{EigenODESystem}
\end{equation}
Component-wise this is a set of decoupled scalar inhomogeneous ODEs,
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t\hat{z}^{(1)}_i
  \;=\;
  \lambda_i\,\hat{z}^{(1)}_i
  \;+\; \sum_j S_{ij}\,\hat{c}_j\,e^{\lambda_j t},
  \qquad \hat{z}^{(1)}_i(0) = 0.
  \label{EigenODE}
\end{equation}
Multiplying through by $e^{-\lambda_i t}$;
% converts the left side to
% $\tfrac{d}{dt}[e^{-\lambda_i t}\hat{z}^{(1)}_i]$;
integrating both sides from $0$ to
$t$~\cite{hochbruck2010exponential,almohy2011action} then gives
\begin{equation}
  e^{-\lambda_i t}\hat{z}^{(1)}_i(t)
  \;=\;
  \sum_j S_{ij}\,\hat{c}_j
  \int_0^t e^{(\lambda_j - \lambda_i)\tau}\,\mathrm{d}\tau,
  \label{DuhamelIntegral}
\end{equation}
in which the integral has a closed-form value.
Restoring the factor $e^{\lambda_i t}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \hat{z}^{(1)}_i(t)
  \;=\;
  \sum_j S_{ij}\,\hat{c}_j\,G(\lambda_i,\lambda_j;\,t),
  \label{DuhamelSolution}
\end{equation}
where the Duhamel kernel $G$ is~\cite{hochbruck2010exponential,almohy2011action}
\begin{equation}
  G(\lambda,\mu;\,t)
  \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{e^{\mu t} - e^{\lambda t}}{\mu - \lambda} & \mu \neq \lambda, \\[6pt]
    t\,e^{\lambda t} & \mu = \lambda,
  \end{cases}
  \label{DuhamelKernel}
\end{equation}
The eigenbasis-component form is what the solver computes, and the original-basis
state is recovered by rotating back:
$\hat{y}^{(1)}(t) = V\,\hat{z}^{(1)}(t)$. Pass~1 is therefore computed entirely from
the Pass~0 eigensystem $(V, D, \hat{y}^{(0)}(0))$; no additional time-stepping is
required.
% The $V$ and $V^{-1}$ factors implicit in $\hat{c}$ and $S$ do not
% cancel when the rotation is performed, because the kernel $G(\lambda_i,\lambda_j;\,t)$
% differs entry-by-entry in the eigenbasis.
Near the degenerate limit $|(\mu-\lambda)t| \to 0$, direct evaluation of
\cref{DuhamelKernel} suffers catastrophic cancellation. The Taylor expansion of
$G(\lambda,\mu;t)$ in $(\mu-\lambda)t$ around the degenerate limit gives
\begin{equation}
  G(\lambda,\mu;t)\bigl|_{|(\mu-\lambda)t|<10^{-5}}
  \;=\;
  t\,e^{\lambda t}\sum_{j=0}^{12}\frac{[(\mu-\lambda)t]^{j}}{(j+1)!},
  \label{DuhamelTaylor}
\end{equation}
the threshold and term count following~\cite{almohy2011action}.

\subsection{Perturbative reduction of the Hamiltonian}
Energy measurement requires a Hamiltonian, and the Legendre transform
of~\cref{LegendreTransform} applies once the reduced Lagrangian contains
at most first-order time derivatives.
Quadratic terms whose total time-derivative order exceeds two remain irreducible to a
Lagrangian with at most first-order time derivatives.% on every factor.

To reduce the Lagrangian, \TIDAL{} substitutes the
order-$\varepsilon^0$ equations of motion into the higher-derivative terms of
$\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$~\cite{jaen1986reduction,Cheyette1988,Grosse-Knetter:1993tae}.
The order-$\varepsilon^0$ equations are solved simultaneously for the second time
derivative of each field,
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t^2 q_i
  \;=\;
  F_i\!\bigl[q,\,\partial_t q,\,\{\nabla^n q\}_{n\geq 1}\bigr]
  \;+\; \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon),
  \label{LPSSubstitution}
\end{equation}
where $F_i$ depends on first-order time derivatives only, but on spatial derivatives of
any order.
Higher spatial derivatives in $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ do not affect the Legendre
transform; only higher \emph{time} derivatives require reduction.
Where the correction introduces terms with time derivatives of order greater than two,
the rule \cref{LPSSubstitution} is extended by differentiating with respect to $t$ as
many times as needed, substituting \cref{LPSSubstitution} for any $\partial_t^2 q_j$
that appears at each step.
The resulting substitution is applied to the order-$\varepsilon^1$ Lagrangian,
truncating at $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$; the reduced Lagrangian carries at
most first-order time derivatives and is admissible for the Legendre transform of~\cref{LegendreTransform}.
The substitution is faithful exactly when the JLM regularity
condition~\cite{jaen1986reduction} holds: the $\varepsilon^0$ equation of motion for
each field undergoing the reduction must admit a $\partial_t^2\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$
that can be isolated and
substituted; equivalently, the leading-order kinetic matrix must be invertible.
Algebraic equations of motion fail this condition, and the substitution then produces
a reduced Lagrangian that omits the promoted degree of freedom.

\paragraph*{Constraint promotion and perturbative-reduction failure}
The case where the JLM condition fails arises physically as constraint promotion. In
the quadratic-PGT theories where constraint promotion arises,
$\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1 = \varepsilon\,\tilde{R}^2$; the $\varepsilon = 0$
Einstein--Cartan limit has metric and torsion components satisfying algebraic
relations. Turning on $\varepsilon$ infinitesimally adds kinetic content that promotes
these relations to dynamical equations. The constraint structure of the theory
therefore changes discontinuously across $\varepsilon = 0$. The new mode is absent at
$\varepsilon = 0$ and cannot be reached by a power-series expansion in $\varepsilon$.

The Dirac--Bergmann Hamiltonian analysis of quadratic-torsion PGT carried
out in~\cite{barker2021hamiltonian} confirms that the propagating-mode count depends
discontinuously on the action parameters --- the same phenomenon we describe here;
the same physics is studied as strong coupling in PGT~\cite{barker2023higgs}, where
the constraint structure of the theory changes discontinuously as parameters are
varied.

Three independent reduction approaches reach the same conclusion. The
JLM substitution~\cite{jaen1986reduction} cannot execute its rule
without a $\partial_t^2\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$ to isolate. Grosse-Knetter's
canonical-equivalence result~\cite{Grosse-Knetter:1993tae} requires the same
substitution to define the field redefinition. Glavan~\cite{Glavan2018} compares the
JLM method, EFT field redefinitions, and the Cayuso--Lehner non-perturbative
scheme~\cite{CayusoLehner2017} and shows that all three break down at constraint
promotion: each requires the leading-order kinetic matrix (\cref{KineticMatrix}) to be
invertible.

\paragraph*{Structural analogues}
Constraint promotion belongs to a wider class of discontinuous limits in gravity and
gauge theory~\cite{deRham2014,hinterbichler2012theoretical}. The van Dam--Veltman--Zakharov
discontinuity~\cite{vandam1970massive,zakharov1970linearized} shows that the massless
limit of a Fierz--Pauli graviton recovers only five of the six general-relativistic
polarisations: the sixth arises through a constraint promoted at non-zero mass.
In the nonlinear completion of Fierz--Pauli massive gravity, a sixth mode emerges
that is ghost-like and does not decouple as $m \to 0$~\cite{boulware1972gravitation}.
At the Maxwell--Proca boundary, massive Proca propagates three polarisations while
massless Maxwell propagates only two; the third (longitudinal) mode becomes pure
gauge as $m \to 0$ and is removed by the Maxwell $U(1)$ gauge symmetry. The mode
count therefore changes discontinuously at $m = 0$, even though the family of
Lagrangians is smooth in $m$.

The St\"uckelberg mechanism~\cite{RueggRuizAltaba2003} smooths the Maxwell--Proca
constraint structure across the limit through an auxiliary field.
A non-perturbative Hamiltonian analysis of the pure $R^2$ analogue has been carried
out~\cite{barker2024r2}, but extending the perturbative-reduction framework of this
appendix to cover the constraint-promotion case in a theory-agnostic way remains for
our future work.

\paragraph*{Primary constraints and singular kinetic matrix}\label{DiracBergmann}
A second obstruction to obtaining a Hamiltonian arises when the linearised kinetic
matrix $\mathcal{K}$ of \cref{KineticMatrix} is singular from the start. Gauge
potentials and other fields that enter the action algebraically contribute to such a
singular $\mathcal{K}$, and the naive Legendre transform of \cref{LegendreTransform}
fails for two related reasons: the relation $\pi_i = \mathcal{K}_{ij} v_j$ cannot be
inverted to give the velocities as independent functions of the momenta, and the
constrained components no longer correspond to independent canonical pairs. The
correct treatment is the Dirac--Bergmann
procedure~\cite{Dirac:1950pj,henneaux1992quantization}; the
case of quadratic-torsion PGT is reported
in~\cite{barker2021hamiltonian}, with a software implementation
in~\cite{barker2023higgs}.
\TIDAL{} does not implement the Dirac--Bergmann reduction; where energy diagnostics
are reported in this work, the measurement is restricted to configurations in which
the Legendre transform of the relevant sector is well-defined, so the
singular-$\mathcal{K}$ obstruction does not enter. The conversion probability of
\cref{GertsenshteinFormula} is gauge-invariant by construction and is unaffected.

\paragraph*{Failure detection}
\TIDAL{} detects constraint promotion by checking whether the
order-$\varepsilon^0$ momentum kernel changes rank when $\varepsilon$ is set to a
non-zero test value; a rank jump signals constraint promotion.
%  and the software emits a
% diagnostic that marks the theory as having no reduced Hamiltonian.
The conversion-and-energy analysis of the affected theories (\ie those carrying
the $\tilde{R}^2$ correction) is deferred to future work.
