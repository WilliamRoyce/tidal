\section{Perturbative reduction in higher-curvature Lagrangians}%
\label{PerturbativeReduction}

The iterative algorithm by which \TIDAL{} evolves theories whose Lagrangian contains
a small correction $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ alongside a well-posed base
$\mathcal{L}_0$ is described here: its formulation as an order-by-order expansion in
$\varepsilon$; the closed-form Duhamel kernel that integrates the first-order correction
within the Fourier modal solver of \cref{ModalSolver}; and the constraint-promotion
barrier that prevents the same expansion from extending to the Legendre transform in
certain higher-curvature PGT theories.

\paragraph*{Higher-order terms as effective corrections}
For the theories studied here --- those whose Lagrangian admits the split
$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_0 + \varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ with $\varepsilon$ a small
dimensionless coupling --- the correction $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ is treated
throughout as a perturbative shift to the dynamics of the base Lagrangian $\mathcal{L}_0$,
not as a source of new independent degrees of freedom.
The split arises naturally in each case: $b_5\,\tilde{R}^{2}$ in quadratic Poincar\'e
gauge theory with $\varepsilon = b_5$; the Euler--Heisenberg
$(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})^{2}$ correction to Maxwell electrodynamics with
$\varepsilon = \rho$; and the Klein--Gordon mass-shift $-\varepsilon\,\phi^{2}$ used in
\cref{Validation} to calibrate the algorithm.

The effective-field-theory programme establishes this interpretation on physical
grounds: terms of this form are the low-energy residues of an unknown ultraviolet
completion, encoding perturbative corrections to base-theory observables and not
independent dynamics in their own right~\cite{Simon:1990ic,Simon:1990jn,Donoghue:1994dn,
burgess2003quantum}.
Grosse-Knetter~\cite{Grosse-Knetter:1993tae} shows, within the Ostrogradsky canonical
formalism, that field redefinitions using the equations of motion map the higher-order
Lagrangian to the reduced one through a canonical transformation; the two are exactly
equivalent through that transformation, not approximations to one another.

The alternative --- treating $\mathcal{L}$ as an exact theory rather than a truncated
expansion --- encounters a structural obstruction.
Ostrogradsky's theorem~\cite{ostrogradsky1850memoires,Woodard:2015zca} states that any
non-degenerate Lagrangian containing time derivatives of order higher than one has a
Hamiltonian unbounded below: at least one canonical momentum enters linearly, and the
corresponding mode is a ghost.
The theorem is non-perturbative; the ghost is present for any non-zero $\varepsilon$.
For the theories we study, the ghost mode has characteristic frequency
$\sim 1/\sqrt{\varepsilon}$, exactly the scale at which $\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$
becomes comparable to $\mathcal{L}_0$ and the perturbative expansion itself breaks
down; the ghost is therefore an artefact of extrapolating the truncated Lagrangian
beyond its regime of validity, not a physical degree of freedom.
We keep $\mathcal{L}_0$ as the dynamical backbone and compute the effect of
$\varepsilon\,\mathcal{L}_1$ iteratively as a correction to the base-theory solution.

\paragraph*{Perturbative reduction of the equations of motion}
We solve the equations of motion by the iterative order-reduction method of Parker
and Simon~\cite{parker1993reduced}, generalised to the multi-field linearised-PDE
regime of \TIDAL{}.
Direct substitution of the order-zero equations into the higher-derivative terms,
following Ja{\'e}n, Llosa and Molina~\cite{jaen1986reduction}, and the mechanical
Ostrogradsky path via auxiliary fields were both investigated and rejected: the former
has no substitution target when a field is algebraically constrained at order zero
(see the constraint-promotion barrier below), and the latter introduces ghost modes
as explicit degrees of freedom, producing unphysical growth in long-time simulation.

Writing the full equation of motion as $E[q;\,\varepsilon] = 0$, we define the base
operator $E_{\mathrm{base}}[q] \equiv E[q;\,\varepsilon{=}0]$ --- the second-order
ghost-free operator from $\mathcal{L}_0$ --- and the correction operator
$E_{\mathrm{corr}}[q] \equiv \partial_\varepsilon E[q;\,\varepsilon]\big|_{\varepsilon=0}$
from $\mathcal{L}_1$, which may carry higher time derivatives.
Expanding $q = q^{(0)} + \varepsilon\,q^{(1)} + \cdots$ and collecting by order:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^0) &:\quad
    E_{\mathrm{base}}\bigl[q^{(0)}\bigr] = 0, \notag \\[2pt]
  \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^1) &:\quad
    E_{\mathrm{base}}\bigl[q^{(1)}\bigr] = -E_{\mathrm{corr}}\bigl[q^{(0)}\bigr].
  \label{EOMHierarchy}
\end{align}
The essential property is that the left-hand side operator at every order is always the
ghost-free second-order base operator $E_{\mathrm{base}}$; the higher-derivative
contributions from $\mathcal{L}_1$ appear only as sources evaluated on the
previous-order solution.
We call the order-$\varepsilon^0$ solution \emph{Pass~0} and the
order-$\varepsilon^1$ correction \emph{Pass~1}; these names are adopted in
\cref{Validation} where the solver is calibrated against analytic benchmarks.

On flat periodic domains, the Pass~0 system at wavenumber $k$ reduces to the per-mode
ODE $\partial_t\hat{y}^{(0)} = M(k)\,\hat{y}^{(0)}$ (cf.~\cref{ModalPerMode}), where
$\hat{y}^{(0)}$ stacks the Fourier amplitudes of every field and velocity slot.
For the perturbative-reduction path, the eigendecomposition $M(k) = V D V^{-1}$ is
used rather than the Pad\'e scaling-and-squaring path of \cref{ModalSolver}, since the
per-eigenmode expansion is required explicitly for the Pass~1 Duhamel integral.
With $D$ diagonal with eigenvalues $\{\lambda_i\}$ and $V$ the right-eigenvector
matrix, the Pass~0 solution at initial data $\hat{y}^{(0)}(0)$ is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y}^{(0)}(t) = V\,e^{Dt}\,\alpha,
  \qquad
  \alpha = V^{-1}\hat{y}^{(0)}(0).
  \label{PassZeroSolution}
\end{equation}

Pass~1 satisfies the inhomogeneous system
$\partial_t\hat{y}^{(1)} = M(k)\,\hat{y}^{(1)} + C_{\mathrm{src}}\,\hat{y}^{(0)}(t)$
with $\hat{y}^{(1)}(0) = 0$, where $C_{\mathrm{src}}$ is the source coupling matrix
assembled from the $\varepsilon^1$ terms in the equations of motion.
Working in the eigenbasis with $\beta = V^{-1}C_{\mathrm{src}}V$, the component
$\hat{z}^{(1)}_i = [V^{-1}\hat{y}^{(1)}]_i$ is given by the variation-of-constants
integral~\cite{hochbruck2010exponential,almohy2011action}
$\hat{z}^{(1)}_i(t) = \sum_j \beta_{ij}\,\alpha_j\,G(\lambda_i,\lambda_j;t)$;
the Duhamel kernel $G$ is
\begin{equation}
  G(\lambda,\mu;t)
  \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{e^{\mu t} - e^{\lambda t}}{\mu - \lambda} & \mu \neq \lambda, \\[6pt]
    t\,e^{\lambda t} & \mu = \lambda,
  \end{cases}
  \label{DuhamelKernel}
\end{equation}
and the full Pass~1 state is recovered as $\hat{y}^{(1)}(t) = V\,\hat{z}^{(1)}(t)$.
Pass~1 is therefore computed entirely from the Pass~0 eigensystem; no additional
differential equation need be solved.
Near the degenerate limit $|(\mu-\lambda)t| \to 0$, direct evaluation of
\cref{DuhamelKernel} suffers catastrophic cancellation; setting $z = (\mu-\lambda)t$,
\TIDAL{} uses the Taylor expansion~\cite{almohy2011action}
\begin{equation}
  G\bigl|_{|z|<10^{-5}}
  \;=\;
  t\,e^{\lambda t}\sum_{j=0}^{12}\frac{z^{j}}{(j+1)!},
  \label{DuhamelTaylor}
\end{equation}
the threshold and term count from Al-Mohy \& Higham~\cite{almohy2011action}
Table~3.1; the twelve-term sum maintains relative error below $10^{-13}$ across
$|z| \in [10^{-15},1]$, validated against 50-digit \texttt{mpmath} reference values.

The iterative approximation holds while the cumulative phase error satisfies
$\varepsilon\,\omega\,t_{\mathrm{end}} \ll 1$~\cite{parker1993reduced}, monitored
at run time by \TIDAL{}'s validity diagnostic.
The Pass~0 and Pass~1 solver is calibrated in \cref{Validation} against analytic
solutions for the Klein--Gordon mass-shift correction, a higher-derivative biharmonic
perturbation, and the Euler--Heisenberg QED vacuum birefringence ratio.

\paragraph*{Perturbative reduction of the Hamiltonian}
Energy measurement and Noether consistency require a Hamiltonian, and the standard
Legendre transform applies once the Lagrangian contains at most first-order time
derivatives.
Integration by parts reduces most higher-derivative products, but certain residues
are irreducible: a term such as
$b_5(\partial_t^2 h_a)(\partial_t^2 h_b)$ leaves a second-time-derivative product
after any finite sequence of integration-by-parts moves.

To reduce the Lagrangian before the Legendre transform, \TIDAL{} implements
\emph{Lagrangian perturbative substitution} (LPS).
The order-$\varepsilon^0$ equations of motion are solved for the highest time
derivative of each field in terms of lower-order and spatial derivatives,
\begin{align}
  \partial_t^{(n)}\varphi
  \;=\;
  f_0\!\bigl[\varphi,\,\partial_t^{(<n)}\varphi,\,\partial_x^{(*)}\varphi\bigr]
  \;+\; \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon),
  \label{LPSSubstitution}
\end{align}
and substituted into the order-$\varepsilon^1$ Lagrangian before the series
is truncated at $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$.
The reduced Lagrangian carries at most first-order time derivatives and is
admissible for the standard Legendre transform of \cref{EulerLagrange}.
LPS is faithful exactly when the Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina regularity
condition holds~\cite{jaen1986reduction}: the order-zero equations must contain a
time-derivative term of order $n$ to solve for.
When the condition fails, the substitution silently drops a promoted degree of
freedom and the reduced theory loses physical content.
The limit of LPS is the subject of the paragraphs that follow.

\paragraph*{The constraint-promotion barrier}
The constraint-promotion barrier arises when the small coupling $\varepsilon$
promotes an algebraic constraint of the base theory to a propagating degree of
freedom.
In the Einstein--Cartan limit $\varepsilon = 0$, certain metric-perturbation
components satisfy algebraic relations and carry no kinetic term.
Turning on $\varepsilon$ infinitesimally promotes these relations to dynamical
equations and introduces new propagating modes; the constraint structure of the
theory changes discontinuously across $\varepsilon = 0$.
An order-by-order expansion in $\varepsilon$ is blind to this promotion because
the new mode is absent at $\varepsilon = 0$ and cannot be reached by a power-series
expansion.
The barrier is the statement that no perturbative expansion in $\varepsilon$
connects the base theory to the small-$\varepsilon$ theory once a constraint has
been promoted.

\paragraph*{Failure mode I: Ostrogradsky}
The Ostrogradsky theorem~\cite{ostrogradsky1850memoires,Woodard:2015zca} is the
cleanest statement of the barrier at the Lagrangian level: a non-degenerate
higher-derivative kinetic structure produces a Hamiltonian unbounded below.
In the quadratic-PGT theories that carry $b_5\,\tilde{R}^2$, the correction
term generates fourth-order time derivatives of the metric perturbation, and the
Ostrogradsky diagnosis applies directly.
The theorem is non-perturbative; it holds regardless of how small $b_5$ is, and
it is the mechanism by which constraint promotion enters the Hamiltonian analysis
at the Lagrangian level.

\paragraph*{Failure mode II: order-by-order substitution}
The same order-by-order substitution strategy that drives the EOM reduction of
\cref{EOMHierarchy} was also investigated for Hamiltonian order-reduction.
Parker and Simon~\cite{parker1993reduced} identified the precise boundary of its
validity in semi-classical gravity: the procedure is faithful when the
higher-derivative correction perturbs a well-posed lower-order theory, and it
fails when the correction introduces degrees of freedom with no lower-order
analogue.
In the Hamiltonian setting, LPS cannot execute \cref{LPSSubstitution} for a
field whose order-zero equation is algebraic rather than differential, because
there is no $\partial_t^n\varphi$ term on the left-hand side to solve for.
The substitution step is therefore undefined at the constraint-promotion
boundary, and LPS cannot bridge the discontinuity that the Ostrogradsky
diagnosis already identifies at the Lagrangian level.

\paragraph*{Failure mode III: the Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina obstruction}
Ja{\'e}n, Llosa and Molina~\cite{jaen1986reduction} formalised the failure for
the general higher-derivative Lagrangian.
They showed that a finite-order reduction by repeated use of the order-zero
equation of motion can in principle be constructed, provided the Lagrangian
satisfies a regularity condition; that condition fails precisely when an
algebraic constraint of the unperturbed theory is promoted to a dynamical
equation at first order in $\varepsilon$.
Their result fits the PGT structure exactly: the order-zero theory is the
Einstein--Cartan system with algebraic torsion constraints, and the
higher-curvature operators in the quadratic-PGT action promote some of those
constraints to propagating fields.
The Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina obstruction gives the sharpest analytical
criterion, distinguishing theories where LPS is faithful from those where it
loses physical content.

\paragraph*{Structural analogues}
The constraint-promotion barrier belongs to a wider class of discontinuous
limits in gravity and gauge theory.
The van Dam--Veltman--Zakharov
discontinuity~\cite{vandam1970massive,zakharov1970linearized} shows that the
massless limit of a Fierz--Pauli graviton recovers only five of the six
general-relativistic polarisations: the sixth arises through a constraint
promoted at non-zero mass.
The Boulware--Deser non-decoupling~\cite{boulware1972gravitation} extends this
to the non-linear theory by identifying a ghost-like sixth mode once the
Fierz--Pauli tuning is broken.
The Maxwell--Proca boundary is the simplest instance: at finite vector-boson
mass the algebraic relation $\rcD{_\mu}\FieldA{^\mu} = 0$ holds as a
constraint, while at zero mass it becomes a gauge symmetry; no perturbative
expansion in the mass encodes the transition.
The $b_5\,\tilde{R}^2$ PGT barrier is the quadratic-curvature analogue of
this structural pattern.

\paragraph*{Software implementation and failure detection}
\TIDAL{} handles both sides of the perturbative-reduction framework
automatically.
For the equations of motion, the Wolfram derivation stage tags each operator
term with an \texttt{order\_in\_eps} integer; at simulation time,
\texttt{PerturbativeSolver} separates base-theory terms from correction terms
and runs Pass~0 followed by Pass~1 via the Duhamel kernel of
\cref{DuhamelKernel}, without theory-specific code.

For the Hamiltonian, \TIDAL{} attempts LPS symbolically at derivation time.
Failure is detected by checking whether the order-$\varepsilon^0$ momentum
kernel changes rank when $\varepsilon$ is set to a non-zero test value; a rank
jump signals constraint promotion and the software emits a diagnostic that
marks the theory as having no LPS-reduced Hamiltonian.
The three affected theories --- \texttt{graviton\_torsion},
\texttt{torsion\_gertsenshtein}, and \texttt{torsion\_gertsenshtein\_combined},
all carrying $b_5\,\tilde{R}^2$ --- retain their pre-LPS Hamiltonians, and
their energy diagnostics are recorded separately rather than alongside
conversion results.

\paragraph*{Implications for the surveys}
The EOM and Hamiltonian reductions have different scopes, and the surveys of
\cref{Results} are structured accordingly.
The iterative EOM reduction of \cref{EOMHierarchy} succeeds for all surveyed
theories, including the $b_5\,\tilde{R}^2$ quadratic-PGT theories: conversion
probabilities are computed from field trajectories evolved by Pass~0 and
Pass~1, and no Hamiltonian is required for that computation.
The constraint-promotion barrier applies only to the Hamiltonian side and only
for the three theories named above; their energy diagnostics are not presented
alongside the conversion amplitudes.
Theories without higher-curvature terms are unaffected and use the standard
linearised methodology of \cref{SymbolicComputation} throughout.
The Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina obstruction is therefore a precise statement of
which analyses the linearised methodology can perform for each theory class,
not a defect of the software; the non-perturbative treatment needed to go
further is outlined in \cref{Discussion}.
