\section{Constraint promotion in higher-curvature PGT}%
\label{ConstraintBarrier}

For Lagrangians that contain higher-curvature terms multiplied by a
small coupling, the canonical-analysis programme used elsewhere in
this report meets a structural obstruction --- the
\emph{constraint-promotion barrier} --- that bears on which PGT
theories the pipeline of \cref{SymbolicComputation} can survey
reliably.
This appendix sets out the barrier in its three classical statements,
records the structural analogues familiar from other gravity and
field-theory limits, and describes the \emph{Lagrangian perturbative
substitution} algorithm by which the pipeline handles the cases it can
handle.

\paragraph*{The constraint-promotion barrier}
A quadratic action that becomes a constrained system in the limit
$\varepsilon \to 0$ --- the small parameter typically being a
coefficient of a higher-curvature operator such as $b\,\tilde{R}^{2}$
in PGT --- admits an algebraic relation between the field components
at $\varepsilon = 0$.
Turning $\varepsilon$ on infinitesimally promotes this algebraic
constraint to a dynamical equation, and a new degree of freedom enters
the spectrum.
Generically this degree of freedom carries the wrong sign in its
kinetic term: it is a ghost, and the small-parameter theory therefore
has more physical content than its $\varepsilon \to 0$ limit, with a
part of that content unphysical.
Naive perturbation theory in $\varepsilon$ is blind to the promotion
because the new mode appears only when $\varepsilon \neq 0$.
The barrier is the statement that no perturbative expansion can
connect the $\varepsilon = 0$ theory to the $\varepsilon \neq 0$
theory once a constraint has been promoted.

\paragraph*{Failure mode I: Ostrogradsky}
The Ostrogradsky
theorem~\cite{ostrogradsky1850memoires}
states that a non-degenerate Lagrangian containing time derivatives of
order higher than one possesses a Hamiltonian that is linear in at
least one of the canonical momenta.
Linearity in a momentum is fatal: the Hamiltonian is unbounded below,
and the corresponding mode is a ghost.
The theorem is non-perturbative; it applies whenever the
higher-derivative kinetic structure is non-degenerate, regardless of
the magnitude of its coefficient.
In our setting, a generic PGT Lagrangian containing terms quadratic in
curvature with non-zero coefficient fails the non-degeneracy condition
only in special parameter directions, and the generic case carries an
Ostrogradsky ghost.
The Ostrogradsky statement is the cleanest formulation of the barrier
because it diagnoses the pathology directly at the level of the
higher-derivative Lagrangian rather than at the level of any
perturbative expansion.

\paragraph*{Failure mode II: Parker--Simon reduction}
Parker and Simon~\cite{parker1993reduced} addressed a closely related
question for the late-time effective theory of semi-classical gravity:
when can one reduce a higher-derivative equation of motion to one of
standard second order by treating the higher-derivative terms as
small corrections?
Their procedure --- order-by-order substitution of the lower-order
equation into the higher-derivative terms --- is faithful when the
higher-derivative corrections genuinely encode a small perturbation
around a well-posed lower-order theory.
It fails when the higher-derivative terms introduce new degrees of
freedom that have no analogue at lower order: the substitution then
drops those modes silently, producing a reduced equation that no
longer reflects the full spectrum.
The pathology is the perturbative-limit version of the Ostrogradsky
diagnosis: solutions present in the small-$\varepsilon$ theory that
are absent in the $\varepsilon = 0$ theory cannot be recovered by a
power-series substitution.

\paragraph*{Failure mode III: the Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina obstruction}
Ja{\'e}n, Llosa and Molina~\cite{jaen1986reduction} formalised the
perturbative-limit failure for the general higher-derivative
Lagrangian.
They showed that a finite-order reduction can in principle be
constructed by repeated use of the order-zero equation of motion,
provided the original Lagrangian satisfies a regularity condition;
their condition fails precisely when an algebraic constraint of the
unperturbed theory is promoted to a dynamical equation at first order
in $\varepsilon$.
Their result is the closest fit to the PGT-with-small-coupling
structure we encounter: the order-zero theory is the Einstein--Cartan
system with algebraic torsion constraints, and the higher-curvature
operators in the quadratic-PGT action promote some of those
constraints to propagating fields.
The Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina obstruction tells us that a uniform
perturbative reduction does not work for every quadratic-PGT theory,
and gives a sharp criterion that distinguishes the theories where the
reduction is faithful from those where it loses content.

\paragraph*{Structural analogues}
The barrier sits within a wider class of perturbative-limit failures
in gravity and gauge theory.
In the massive-graviton limit, the van Dam--Veltman--Zakharov
discontinuity~\cite{vandam1970massive,zakharov1970linearized} shows
that the massless limit of a Fierz--Pauli graviton recovers only five
of the six polarisations of general relativity, with the missing one
appearing through a constraint that is promoted at non-zero mass.
The Boulware--Deser non-decoupling~\cite{boulware1972gravitation}
extends this diagnosis to the non-linear theory by identifying a
ghost-like sixth mode once the Fierz--Pauli tuning is broken.
The Maxwell--Proca boundary is the simplest instance: at finite
vector-boson mass the algebraic relation $\rcD{_\mu}\FieldA{^\mu} = 0$
holds, but at zero mass it becomes a gauge symmetry, and no
perturbative expansion encodes the transition.
The PGT barrier is the quadratic-curvature analogue of the same
structural pattern.

\paragraph*{Lagrangian perturbative substitution}
To handle higher-curvature PGT theories where the regularity condition
holds, the pipeline implements \emph{Lagrangian perturbative
substitution} (LPS).
The order-$\varepsilon^{0}$ equations of motion are extracted and
solved for the highest time derivative in terms of lower-order and
spatial derivatives,
\begin{align}
  \partial_{t}^{(n)}\varphi
  \;=\;
  f_{0}\!\bigl[\varphi,\,\partial_{t}^{(<n)}\varphi,\,\partial_{x}^{(*)}\varphi\bigr]
  \;+\; \mathcal{O}(\varepsilon),
  \label{LPSReduction}
\end{align}
and substituted back into the order-$\varepsilon^{1}$ Lagrangian
before a series truncation.
The reduced Lagrangian carries at most first-order time derivatives
and is therefore admissible for the standard Legendre transform of
\cref{EulerLagrange}.
The substitution is faithful exactly when the
Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina regularity condition holds.
For theories where the condition fails, the substitution silently
drops the promoted mode and the reduced theory loses physical
content; the pipeline detects the failure during derivation, by
checking whether the order-$\varepsilon^{0}$ momentum kernel changes
rank when $\varepsilon$ is turned on, and emits a diagnostic that
prevents the reduced theory from being used as a substitute for the
full one.

\paragraph*{Implications for the surveys}
The barrier delimits the scope of what the surveys of \cref{Results}
can claim.
Theories without higher-curvature terms, and theories where LPS
succeeds, are surveyed against the standard linearised pipeline of
\cref{SymbolicComputation}.
Theories where LPS fails are flagged at derivation time and excluded
from the channel-by-channel analysis, with the failure recorded
against the theory in the campaign log.
The Ja{\'e}n--Llosa--Molina obstruction is therefore not a defect of
the pipeline but a sharp statement of which corners of the PGT
theory space the linearised methodology can address; the remainder
requires the non-perturbative treatment outlined in \cref{Discussion}.
