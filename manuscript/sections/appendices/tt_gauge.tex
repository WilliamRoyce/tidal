\section{Transverse-traceless gauge in the presence of matter}%
\label{TTGaugeAdmissibility}

In this appendix we show why the standard derivation of the
transverse-traceless~(TT) gauge for the linearised metric perturbation
$\FieldH{_\mu_\nu}$ fails in the presence of matter,
justifying the gauge-free evolution adopted in \cref{Linearisation}.
We follow Hobson, Efstathiou \&
Lasenby~\cite[Ch.~17--18]{hobson2006general}, with the same result in
Maggiore~\cite[\S1.2]{maggiore2007gravitational} and Misner, Thorne
\& Wheeler~\cite[Box~35.1]{misner1973gravitation}.

\paragraph*{Lorenz gauge}
We write
\begin{align}
  g_{\mu\nu} &= \eta_{\mu\nu} + \FieldH{_\mu_\nu},
  \qquad |\FieldH{_\mu_\nu}| \ll 1 ,
  \label{TTGWeakField}
\end{align}
an infinitesimal
coordinate transformation $x^{\mu}\to x^{\mu}+\xi^{\mu}(x)$ acts on
the perturbation as
\begin{align}
  h'_{\mu\nu} &= h_{\mu\nu}
    - \partial_{\mu}\xi_{\nu} - \partial_{\nu}\xi_{\mu} ,
  \label{TTGGaugeTransform}
\end{align}
\cite[eqs.~(17.1),~(17.4)]{hobson2006general}. With the trace-reversed
perturbation
$\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\FieldH{_\mu_\nu}-\tfrac{1}{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}h$,
the gauge transformation
\cref{TTGGaugeTransform} shifts
$\partial^{\mu}\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}\to\partial^{\mu}\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}-\Box^{2}\xi_{\nu}$;
choosing $\xi^{\mu}$ to satisfy
$\Box^{2}\xi^{\mu}=\partial_{\rho}\bar{h}^{\mu\rho}$ imposes the
Lorenz condition $\partial^{\mu}\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}=0$ \cite[eq. (17.14)]{hobson2006general} while reducing the
linearised Einstein field
equation~\cite[eq.~(17.11)]{hobson2006general} to
\begin{align}
  \Box^{2}\bar{h}^{\mu\nu} &= -2\kappa_{\mathrm{E}}\,T^{\mu\nu} .
  \label{TTGLorenzWaveEq}
\end{align}

\paragraph*{Failure in the presence of matter}
In vacuum, the standard derivation completes by a further residual
gauge transformation with $\Box^{2}\xi^{\mu}=0$ used to set four
linear combinations of $\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}$ to zero, yielding the TT
conditions $\bar{h}_{\mathrm{TT}}^{\,0i}=\bar{h}_{\mathrm{TT}}=0$ and
their Lorenz-induced
consequences~\cite[\S18.3,~eqs.~(18.8)--(18.9)]{hobson2006general}.
This step uses $\Box^{2}\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}=0$; the residual
transformations can only annul four linear combinations of
$\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}$ when it satisfies the source-free equation. In
the presence of matter \cref{TTGLorenzWaveEq} reads
$\Box^{2}\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}=-2\kappa_{\mathrm{E}}T_{\mu\nu}\neq 0$,
the combinations the residual transformations would annul in vacuum
are fixed by the source, and the reduction cannot be completed.

For the Gertsenshtein configuration of \cref{Linearisation} the
matter is the static external field $\bar{\bm{B}}=\Bzero\hat{x}$,
with
\begin{align}
  T_{00} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\Bzero^{2} , &
  T_{0i} &= 0 , &
  T_{ij} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\Bzero^{2}\delta_{ij}
           - \bar{B}_{i}\bar{B}_{j} , &
  T^{\mu}{}_{\mu} &= 0 .
  \label{TTGMaxwellStress}
\end{align}
The non-zero energy density $T_{00}=\tfrac{1}{2}\Bzero^{2}$ forces a
non-zero scalar perturbation of the metric, obstructing $h_{00}=0$.
Beyond the static background, the perturbed Maxwell stress-energy
itself depends on $\FieldH{_\mu_\nu}$, with
$\delta T_{ij}\supset \tfrac{1}{2}\bar{B}^{2}h_{ij}$; the TT-gauge
treatment of the Gertsenshtein channel is correct only for a planar
wave on a uniform constant
background~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton}.

\paragraph*{Independent failure in $\MAGFt{}^{2}$ theories}
Two facts about pure Einstein gravity carry the reduction above: the
linearised field equation \cref{TTGLorenzWaveEq} is second order in
derivatives, and the residual gauge generated by
$\Box^{2}\xi^{\mu}=0$ has the same kernel as the kinetic operator
governing $\bar{h}_{\mu\nu}$. Both fail in theories with a
curvature-squared kinetic term --- $R^{2}$ in Riemannian gravity, or
$\MAGFt{}^{2}$ in the Poincar\'{e} theories of
\cref{PGTEnumeration}. The linearised equation gains a
higher-derivative piece propagating an additional scalar mode (the
scalar graviton of $f(R)$ gravity, or in PGT a propagating
trace mode that mixes with $h^{\sigma}{}_{\sigma}$), which the
residual gauge $\Box^{2}\xi^{\mu}=0$ cannot reach. Imposing
$h^{i}{}_{i}=0$ as a gauge condition therefore eliminates a physical
degree of freedom rather than a coordinate redundancy.

An issue raised by
Ejlli~\cite[footnote on p.~2]{ejlli2020graviton} is whether imposing
the gauge conditions in the action and on the equations of motion give
the same graviton--photon dynamics.
