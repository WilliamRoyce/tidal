\section{Perturbative-regime bound in the torsionful background}
\label{PerturbativeRegime}

\paragraph*{Background-perturbativity in Einstein--Maxwell}

The standard Gertsenshtein
analysis~\cite{gertsenshtein1962wave,boccaletti1970conversion,raffelt1988mixing},
takes the magnetic field $\Bzero$ as
externally prescribed rather than self-consistently sourcing the
background metric.
Hwang \& Noh~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton} examine the validity of
this assumption: the EM stress-energy modifies the
graviton through a tachyonic mass-like term
$-(\kappa^2/2)\Bzero^2$.
There is a critical scale $\lambda_B = 2\pi/k_B$, with
$k_B = \sqrt{8\pi G}\,\Bzero/c^2$, above which the dimensionless
backreaction parameter $D^2/\lambda_B^2$ ceases to be small ---
the assumption that the magnetic field's own
gravitational field is negligible breaks down.
Hwang \& Noh show~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton} that the backreaction parameter is proportional to
$\Pconv$:
\begin{align}
  \frac{D^2}{\lambda_B^2}
  \;\sim\; \Pconv,
  \label{HwangNohBound}
\end{align}
Since $\Pconv$ is itself $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^2)$ in the small-$\Bzero$ limit
(\cref{GertsenshteinFormula}), the bound is vanishing alongside the conversion
probability. Equivalently, $\kappa\Bzero D\ll 1$, i.e. $\Pconv\ll 1$.\footnote{The Hwang \& Noh peak-amplitude estimate
is $P\sim 8\pi^2\,(D/\lambda_B)^2$.
This differs by a factor of four from the small-angle expansion of
\cref{GertsenshteinFormula},
$\Pconv\approx\kappa^2\Bzero^2 D^2/4$:
Hwang \& Noh estimate the energy ratio by scaling the peak amplitude,
treating the graviton as
building linearly with propagation distance,
$h\sim\kappa\Bzero D$, ignoring the back-coupling that
returns energy to the photon channel.
The exact conversion probability \cref{GertsenshteinFormula} has
$h\propto\sin(\kappa\Bzero D/2)$;
in the small-$\kappa\Bzero D$ limit this is half the
estimate, and squaring gives the factor of
four.}


\paragraph*{Self-consistency at vanishing background field}

PGT coupled to electromagnetism adds a torsion perturbation
$\delta\MAGT{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ alongside the graviton $h_{\mu\nu}$
and photon perturbation $\delta\FieldA{_\mu}$ (\cref{Linearisation}).
The Taylor expansion \cref{TaylorPconv} is centred on $\Bzero=0$,
where the configuration $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}$,
$\bar{\FieldA{_\mu}}=0$, $\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$ trivially solves the full
nonlinear field equations.
The leading coefficient $c_2$ is therefore extracted at the unperturbed
background, free of the metric-backreaction shift discussed above;
the harder $\Bzero\neq 0$ check is given below.

\paragraph*{Analytic dependence on the background field}

The kinetic matrix $\mathcal{K}$ of \cref{KineticMatrix}
acquires an explicit $\Bzero$-dependence
$\mathcal{K}(\partial_t,\partial_z;\Bzero)$ that is polynomial in
$\Bzero$.
At $\Bzero=0$ the off-diagonal graviton--photon couplings vanish and
the channels are decoupled, so $\Pconv(\Bzero=0)=0$.
For small $\Bzero$ the off-diagonal couplings are a perturbation on
this decoupled theory; standard perturbation theory expresses the
conversion amplitude $\mathcal{M}(\Bzero)$ as a sum over diagrams
with vertex insertions of $\bar{\FieldF{}}\propto\Bzero$, each diagram
contributing a finite polynomial in $\Bzero$.
This series has a non-zero radius of convergence around $\Bzero=0$
because the unperturbed propagators are non-singular,
and $\Pconv = |\mathcal{M}|^2$ therefore admits a convergent Taylor
expansion in $\Bzero$ in a neighbourhood of zero.%
\footnote{The argument does not invoke a diagonalisation of
$\mathcal{K}$ and so tolerates the eigenvalue coincidences that occur
in the surveyed parameter space.}
The setup is invariant under rotation by $\pi$ about the propagation
axis $\hat{z}$: this sends $\Bzero\to-\Bzero$ and the transverse
photon $\delta\FieldA{_{x,y}}\to-\delta\FieldA{_{x,y}}$ while leaving
the transverse--traceless graviton polarisations
$h_+=\tfrac{1}{2}(h_{xx}-h_{yy})$ and $h_\times=h_{xy}$ invariant.
The conversion amplitude picks up a single sign from the photon flip,
so $\mathcal{M}(-\Bzero)=-\mathcal{M}(\Bzero)$:
$\mathcal{M}$ contains only odd powers of $\Bzero$, and
$\Pconv = |\mathcal{M}|^2$ contains only even powers:
\begin{align}
  \Pconv \;=\; c_2\Bzero^2 + c_4\Bzero^4 + \cdots ,
  \label{TaylorPconv}
\end{align}
with $c_2$ finite by analyticity.
This coefficient is identified with the survey coefficient $\Czero$
defined below in \cref{SurveyCoefficient}.

\paragraph*{Vanishing of the background torsion}

On the uniform-field flat background,
$\partial_\mu\bar{\FieldF{}}_{\nu\rho}=0$.
The Cartan equation --- the field equation for torsion, obtained by
varying the PGT Lagrangian of \cref{PGTBackground} with respect to $\MAGT{}$
--- collects contributions from every Lagrangian term that depends on
$\MAGT{}$, either directly or through the Riemann--Cartan curvature
$\MAGFt{}$, which inherits a torsion-dependence through the contortion
$K\propto\MAGT{}$ from the Palatini split (\cref{PalatiniDecomposition});
substituting into \cref{Curvature} gives $\MAGFt{}$ a $\rcD{}K$ piece
and a $K{\cdot}K$ piece.
At the background $(\eta_{\mu\nu},\,\bar{\FieldF{}},\,\bar{\MAGT{}}=0)$,
every term type in the Lagrangian vanishes:
the pure-torsion terms ($\MAGT{}^2$, $(\rcD{}\MAGT{})^2$) are at least
quadratic in $\MAGT{}$ and their variation is at least linear in
$\MAGT{}$, vanishing at $\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$;
the pure-curvature terms ($\MAGFt{}^2$) vanish as a product because
$\MAGFt{}|_{\eta,\,\bar{\MAGT{}}=0}=0$ identically;
the non-minimal coupling $\Del{1}\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$
has two torsion-dependent pieces through $K$ --- the $\rcD{}K$ piece is
handled by integration by parts, transferring the covariant derivative
onto $\bar{\FieldF{}}$ and using $\rcD{_\rho}\bar{\FieldF{}}^{\mu\nu}=0$
for the uniform background; the $K{\cdot}K$ piece vanishes at
$\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$ directly because its variation is proportional to
$K|_{\bar{\MAGT{}}=0}=0$.
Thus $\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$ solves the Cartan equation on this background
exactly for the parity-even quadratic theory class surveyed.
This conclusion relies on $\partial_\mu\bar{\FieldF{}}=0$:
for non-uniform fields or curved backgrounds
$\rcD{}\bar{\FieldF{}}\neq 0$ and $\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$ need not
hold~\cite{bahamonde2025torsion}.

\paragraph*{Torsion-perturbation bound}

The preceding paragraph established $\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$ at the background.
To bound the torsion perturbation $\delta\MAGT{}$, expand the same
Cartan equation to first order in the perturbations
$\{h_{\mu\nu},\delta\FieldA{_\mu},\delta\MAGT{}\}$.
The $\rcD{}K$ piece of $\MAGFt{}$ contributes a term that cancels at
first order by the same integration by parts as in the preceding
paragraph: $\rcD{_\rho}\bar{\FieldF{}}^{\mu\nu}=0$.
The non-vanishing source comes from one order higher in the perturbation
expansion.
The Riemann--Cartan connection is the sum of the Levi-Civita piece
(depending on the metric perturbation $h$) and the contortion
(depending on $\delta\MAGT{}$); their product contributes a cross-term
of the form $(\partial h){\cdot}K[\delta\MAGT{}]$ to $\MAGFt{}$ at
second perturbative order.
Varying $\Del{1}\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\bar{\FieldF{}}^{\mu\nu}$ with
respect to $\delta\MAGT{}$ selects this cross-term, producing a source
$\propto\Del{1}\bar{\FieldF{}}\,\partial h$.
In the algebraic-torsion form,
$\delta\MAGT{}$ has no kinetic operator and the linearised
Cartan equation is purely algebraic,
$2\Alp{}\,\delta\MAGT{} = -\text{source}$;
inverting this algebraic equation directly gives
$|\delta\MAGT{}|\sim(|\Del{1}|/\Alp{})\,\Bzero A$
for a graviton of dimensionless amplitude $A$ (\cref{Linearisation}).
This is a self-consistency check on the linearised treatment of the
torsion sector: the Cartan equation receives cubic corrections of
order $(\delta\MAGT{})^2$ from the $\MAGT{}^3$ self-interactions,
which are negligible compared to the linear mass term
$2\Alp{}\,\delta\MAGT{}$ only when $|\delta\MAGT{}|/\Alp{}\ll 1$, hence
\begin{align}
  |\delta\MAGT{}|\ll\Alp{}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  |\Del{1}|\,\Bzero\,A \;\ll\; \Alp{}^{\,2} .
  \label{PerturbativeBound}
\end{align}
The same dimensional argument applies when the Barker kinetic term
is active and $\delta\MAGT{}$ propagates: the algebraic inversion is
then replaced by a Green's function inversion, but the magnitude of
the response and the scaling of the bound are unchanged with $\Alp{}$
generalised to the dominant torsion mass scale.
The extension to the rest of the surveyed parameter space is in the
following paragraph.

\paragraph*{Generality beyond the algebraic-torsion theory}

The preceding properties hold for any ghost-safe polynomial PGT
Lagrangian coupled to electromagnetism~\cite{blagojevic2018hamiltonian}.
The bound \cref{PerturbativeBound} fixes specific values
$\Del{1}$ and $\Alp{}$ within the algebraic-torsion sub-class.
Across the surveyed parameter space, the same IBP-and-inversion
argument applies with two extensions.
First, \cref{PerturbativeBound} with $\Del{1}\to g_{\mathrm{NM}}$ and
$\Alp{}\to m_T^2$ gives
$|\delta\MAGT{}|\sim(g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)\,\Bzero A$;
the cubic self-interaction then contributes a correction of order
$(\delta\MAGT{})^2 \sim (g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)^2\,\Bzero^2$ to the
linearised Cartan equation, placing the nonlinear torsion correction
to $\Pconv$ at
$\mathcal{O}\!\left(\Bzero^4(g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)^2\right)$
and the correction to $C=\Pconv/\Bzero^2$ at
$\mathcal{O}\!\left(\Bzero^2(g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)^2\right)$.
Second, when the Barker kinetic term is active and $\delta\MAGT{}$
propagates, the linearised Cartan equation takes the form
$[\mathcal{D}(\partial)+\Alp{}]\,\delta\MAGT{} = -\text{source}$,
where $\mathcal{D}(\partial)$ is the second-order kinetic operator
from the Barker term;
the algebraic inversion of \cref{PerturbativeBound} is then replaced
by a Green's function inversion
$\delta\MAGT{} = \int G\,(\text{source})$,
$G = [\mathcal{D}(\partial)+\Alp{}]^{-1}$, with the
propagating-torsion mode structure given
in~\cite{blagojevic2018hamiltonian}.
The magnitude of $\delta\MAGT{}$ is set by the dominant mass-squared
scale of $G$, and the same
$\mathcal{O}\!\left(\Bzero^2(g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)^2\right)$ scaling
controls the correction with $m_T$ the realised torsion mass scale
at each survey point.
For theories without non-minimal coupling the torsion sector
decouples at $\Bzero=0$ and contributes nothing.

\paragraph*{The survey coefficient}

The parameter survey of \cref{SectorSurvey} evaluates the limiting
survey coefficient
\begin{align}
  \Czero \;\equiv\; \lim_{\Bzero\to 0}\,\frac{\Pconv}{\Bzero^2}
  \;=\; c_2 ,
  \label{SurveyCoefficient}
\end{align}
the leading Taylor coefficient of \cref{TaylorPconv}.

Since $\Pconv$ vanishes at $\Bzero=0$ and contains only even powers of
$\Bzero$ (\cref{TaylorPconv}),
$c_2$ is the lowest non-zero Taylor coefficient of $\Pconv$.
The survey coefficient $\Czero=c_2$ is therefore the leading term in
$C=\Pconv/\Bzero^2$ --- not by definition, but as a consequence of the
vertex-vanishing and analyticity arguments above.
In the Einstein--Maxwell limit $\Czero$ reduces to $(\kappa D/2)^2$
from \cref{GertsenshteinFormula}; in the torsionful theory $\Czero=c_2$
is the total graviton-photon conversion coefficient at leading order in
$\Bzero$, taking a value determined by the full kinetic matrix of
\cref{KineticMatrix} and differing from $\Czero^{\mathrm{EM}}$ when
torsion couplings are active.
The amplification factor
$\Aamp\equiv\sqrt{\Czero^{\mathrm{torsion}}/\Czero^{\mathrm{EM}}}$
of \cref{SectorSurvey} measures this deviation and is
$\Bzero$-independent to $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^2)$ by the same limit argument.

The torsion signal --- the deviation of $\Czero$ from its
Einstein--Maxwell value $(\kappa D/2)^2$ --- is contained in $c_2$
itself: each surveyed theory realises its own $c_2$, set by the full
torsionful kinetic matrix at $\Bzero=0$, and the survey reads off
this value via $\Czero=\lim_{\Bzero\to 0}\Pconv/\Bzero^2$.
Two further mechanisms also contribute to $\Pconv$ but not to $c_2$:
the metric-backreaction detuning $\Delta\propto\Bzero^2$ enters
$\Pconv$ as $(\Delta/g)^2\propto\Bzero^2$ on top of the leading
$\Bzero^2$ amplitude;
the torsion-sector nonlinearity contributes at
$\mathcal{O}((\delta\MAGT{})^2)\propto\Bzero^2$ relative to the linear
$\delta\MAGT{}$ response of \cref{PerturbativeBound}.
Both place their contribution at $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^4)$ in $\Pconv$
and $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^2)$ in $C$, strictly subleading to $c_2$, and
the exact-limit definition extracts $\Czero$ free of either: any term
proportional to $\Bzero^k$ for $k\geq 2$ drops out of
$\lim_{\Bzero\to 0}\Pconv/\Bzero^2$ and appears instead among
$c_4,c_6,\ldots$.
These are the corrections we want to neglect --- they are not the
linear-response torsion signal, and the discrete integer-power
structure of the Taylor expansion ensures the separation is exact.

The metric backreaction enters via the tachyonic-mass term
$-(\kappa^2/2)\Bzero^2 h$,
which detunes the graviton dispersion by $\Delta\propto\Bzero^2$.
The mixing of two modes coupled at strength $g\propto\Bzero$ with
detuning $\Delta\propto\Bzero^2$ yields the two-mode oscillation
formula~\cite{raffelt1988mixing}
\begin{align*}
  \Pconv \;=\; \frac{g^2}{g^2+\Delta^2}
  \,\sin^2\!\!\left(\!\sqrt{g^2+\Delta^2}\,D\right) ;
\end{align*}
substituting $\Delta/g\propto\Bzero$ gives a correction to $C$
at $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^2)$, consistent with the analyticity bound.
The torsion nonlinearity contributes at
$\mathcal{O}\!\left(\Bzero^2(g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)^2\right)$ per
\cref{PerturbativeBound}, again consistent.

The limiting coefficient $\Czero^{\mathrm{EM}}$ matches the
potential-formulation derivation of~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton}:
their conversion coefficient $\Delta_{g\gamma}=\kappa\Bzero/2$
is exactly linear in $\Bzero$, so $\Czero^{\mathrm{EM}}$ has no
$\Bzero$-independent backreaction shift.
