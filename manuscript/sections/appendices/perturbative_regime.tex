\section{Perturbative-regime bound in the torsionful background}
\label{PerturbativeRegime}

\paragraph*{Background-perturbativity in Einstein--Maxwell}

The standard Gertsenshtein
analysis~\cite{gertsenshtein1962wave,boccaletti1970conversion,raffelt1988mixing},
takes the magnetic field $\Bzero$ as
externally prescribed rather than self-consistently sourcing the
background metric.
Hwang \& Noh~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton} examine the
self-consistency of this linear-on-flat-background analysis:
including the metric perturbation in the EM stress-energy
produces a tachyonic mass-like term $-(\kappa^2/2)\Bzero^2$ for
the graviton, but the regime in which this
term would drive an exponential instability coincides with the
regime in which the gravity of the background magnetic field can
no longer be ignored.
The linear analysis therefore cannot self-consistently establish
the instability, and the framework remains internally consistent
within its regime of validity.
There is a critical scale $\lambda_B = 2\pi/k_B$, with
$k_B = \sqrt{8\pi G}\,\Bzero/c^2$, above which the dimensionless
backreaction parameter $D^2/\lambda_B^2$ ceases to be small ---
the assumption that the magnetic field's own
gravitational field is negligible breaks down.
Hwang \& Noh show~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton} that the backreaction parameter is proportional to
the conversion probability $\Pconv$ of \cref{GertsenshteinFormula}:
\begin{align}
  \frac{D^2}{\lambda_B^2}
  \;\sim\; \Pconv.
  \label{HwangNohBound}
\end{align}
Since $D^2/\lambda_B^2 \propto \Bzero^2 \to 0$, the bound holds
trivially in the small-$\Bzero$ limit;
equivalently, $\kappa\Bzero D\ll 1$, \ie $\Pconv\ll 1$.\footnote{The Hwang \& Noh peak-amplitude estimate
is $P\sim 8\pi^2\,(D/\lambda_B)^2$.
This differs by a factor of four from the small-angle expansion of
\cref{GertsenshteinFormula},
$\Pconv\approx\kappa^2\Bzero^2 D^2/4$:
Hwang \& Noh estimate the energy ratio by scaling the peak amplitude,
treating the graviton as
building linearly with propagation distance,
$h\sim\kappa\Bzero D$, ignoring the back-coupling that
returns energy to the photon channel.
The exact conversion probability \cref{GertsenshteinFormula} has
$h\propto\sin(\kappa\Bzero D/2)$;
in the small-$\kappa\Bzero D$ limit this is half the
estimate, and squaring gives the factor of
four.}

% equations of motion derived from the PGT--Maxwell theories
% enumerated in \cref{PGTEnumeration} on a flat Minkowski background
% with externally prescribed $\Bzero$, retaining the tachyonic
% mass-like term automatically.


\paragraph*{Self-consistency at vanishing background field}

PGT coupled to electromagnetism adds a torsion perturbation
$\delta\MAGT{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ alongside the graviton $h_{\mu\nu}$
and photon perturbation $\delta\FieldA{_\mu}$ (\cref{Linearisation}).
The Taylor expansion \cref{TaylorPconv} is centred on $\Bzero=0$,
where the configuration $g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}$,
$\bar{\FieldA{_\mu}}=0$,%
\footnote{$\bar{\FieldF{}}=\diff\bar{\FieldA{}}=0$ fixes
$\bar{\FieldA{}}$ only up to a gauge transformation;
$\bar{\FieldA{}}=0$ is a valid gauge on the flat, simply-connected
background of \cref{Linearisation}.}
$\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$ trivially solves the full nonlinear field equations.
The Taylor coefficient $c_2 = \lim_{\Bzero\to 0}\Pconv/\Bzero^2$ is read
off in the limit $\Bzero\to 0$, where the Hwang--Noh tachyonic
mass-like dispersion shift $-(\kappa^2/2)\Bzero^2 h$
--- itself contributing to $\Pconv$ only at $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^4)$ and
--- vanishes.
We now examine whether the torsion sector remains within
the linearised regime at finite $\Bzero$.

\paragraph*{Analytic dependence on the background field}

At $\Bzero=0$ the system is rotationally invariant about the
propagation axis $\hat{z}$, so the linearised theory conserves the
angular momentum projected on $\hat{z}$ (helicity).
% , and any coupling
% between two perturbations requires that they carry the same helicity.
The transverse graviton is
spin-2~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,barker2024poincare,cillis1996graviton}
and the transverse photon is spin-1, so at $\Bzero=0$ they live
in disjoint sectors and $\Pconv(\Bzero=0)=0$.
Switching on $\Bzero$ introduces a background tensor
$\bar{\FieldF{}}\propto\Bzero\hat{x}$ (\cref{BackgroundPotential}) that breaks the rotational symmetry
and lifts the helicity-conservation selection rule
forbidding graviton-photon transitions.
The kinetic matrix $\mathcal{K}$ of \cref{KineticMatrix} acquires an
explicit $\Bzero$-dependence
$\mathcal{K}(\partial_t,\partial_z;\Bzero)$ that is polynomial in
$\Bzero$, and the conversion amplitude
$\mathcal{M}(\Bzero)$ defined via $\Pconv=|\mathcal{M}|^2$ depends
analytically on $\mathcal{K}$ wherever the unperturbed kinetic
operator is non-singular;
$\Pconv$ is therefore analytic in $\Bzero$ in a neighbourhood of
zero.
A rotation of the lab frame by $\pi$ about $\hat{z}$ is a coordinate
relabelling that sends $\bm{B}\to-\bm{B}$: the rotated frame describes
the same physical system with the parameter $\Bzero$ replaced by
$-\Bzero$.
Since $\Pconv$ is a coordinate-invariant observable, this gives
$\Pconv(-\Bzero)=\Pconv(\Bzero)$, so $\Pconv$ is even in $\Bzero$.
Combined with $\Pconv(\Bzero=0)=0$ and analyticity, $\Pconv$ starts at
$\Bzero^2$ with only even powers:
\begin{align}
  \Pconv \;=\; c_2\Bzero^2 + c_4\Bzero^4 + \cdots ,
  \label{TaylorPconv}
\end{align}
with $c_2$ finite by analyticity.
This coefficient is identified with the survey coefficient $\Czero$
defined below in \cref{SurveyCoefficient}.

\paragraph*{Vanishing of the background torsion}

On the uniform-field flat background,
$\partial_\mu\bar{\FieldF{}}_{\nu\rho}=0$.
The Cartan equation~\cite{blagojevic2013gauge,hehlobukhov2007cartan}
--- the field equation for torsion, obtained by varying the PGT
Lagrangian of \cref{PGTBackground} \wrt $\MAGT{}$
--- collects contributions from every Lagrangian term that depends on
$\MAGT{}$, either directly or through the Riemann--Cartan curvature
\begin{align}
  \MAGFt{} \;=\; \rR{} \,+\, \rcD{}K \,+\, K{\cdot}K ,
  \label{RiemannCartanDecomp}
\end{align}
where $\rR{}$ is the Riemannian curvature of the Levi-Civita connection.
At the background $(\eta_{\mu\nu},\,\bar{\FieldF{}},\,\bar{\MAGT{}}=0)$,
every term type in the Lagrangian vanishes.
% The pure-torsion terms are at least
% quadratic in $\MAGT{}$, so vanish at $\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$.
% The pure-curvature terms ($\MAGFt{}^2$) vanish as a product because
% $\MAGFt{}|_{\eta,\,\bar{\MAGT{}}=0}=0$ identically.
The non-minimal coupling $\Del{1}\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$
has two torsion-dependent pieces through $K$ \cref{RiemannCartanDecomp}.
The $\rcD{}K$ term of \cref{RiemannCartanDecomp} is handled by integration by parts: the covariant
derivative is transferred onto $\bar{\FieldF{}}$, and
$\rcD{_\rho}\bar{\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}}=0$ on the externally-prescribed
uniform background (\cref{Linearisation}).
% The $K{\cdot}K$ piece vanishes at $\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$ directly because
% its variation is proportional to $K|_{\bar{\MAGT{}}=0}=0$.
Thus $\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$ solves the Cartan equation on this background
exactly for the parity-even quadratic theory class.%
\footnote{This conclusion relies on $\partial_\mu\bar{\FieldF{}}=0$:
for non-uniform fields or curved backgrounds
$\rcD{}\bar{\FieldF{}}\neq 0$ and $\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$ need not
hold~\cite{bahamonde2025torsion}.}

\paragraph*{Torsion-perturbation bound}

With $\bar{\MAGT{}}=0$ at the background, expand the Cartan equation
to first order in the perturbations
$\{h_{\mu\nu},\delta\FieldA{_\mu},\delta\MAGT{}\}$ to bound the
torsion perturbation $\delta\MAGT{}$.
The $\rcD{}K$ term of \cref{RiemannCartanDecomp} contracted with $\bar{\FieldF{}}$
cancels at first order by the same integration by parts as in the
preceding paragraph: $\rcD{_\rho}\bar{\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}}=0$.
The dominant source therefore comes from varying
$\Del{1}\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\bar{\FieldF{}}^{\mu\nu}$ \wrt
$\delta\MAGT{}$ using the Palatini split
(\cref{PalatiniDecomposition}), which produces a source
$\mathcal{S}\propto\Del{1}\bar{\FieldF{}}\,\partial h$.%
\footnote{The same $\rcD{}K$ piece contracted with $\delta\FieldF{}$
instead of $\bar{\FieldF{}}$ gives an additional first-order source
$\propto\Del{1}\,\partial\delta\FieldA{}$ that is $\Bzero$-independent;
in the conversion regime $|\delta\FieldA{}|\sim\Bzero|h|$
(\cref{GertsenshteinFormula}) this is the same order as the dominant
source $\propto\Del{1}\bar{\FieldF{}}\,\partial h$, so
\cref{PerturbativeBound} is unchanged.}
In the algebraic-torsion form,
% $\delta\MAGT{}$ has no kinetic operator and the linearised
% Cartan equation is purely algebraic,
$2\Alp{}\,\delta\MAGT{} = -\mathcal{S}$;
% inverting this algebraic equation directly gives
giving $|\delta\MAGT{}|\sim(|\Del{1}|/\Alp{})\,\Bzero A$
for a graviton of dimensionless amplitude $A$.
The linearised Cartan equation drops $(\delta\MAGT{})^2$ corrections
from the $K{\cdot}K$ term of \cref{RiemannCartanDecomp}: these become
comparable to the linear $2\Alp{}\,\delta\MAGT{}$ term when
$|\delta\MAGT{}|/\Alp{}\sim 1$, so the linearised treatment is
self-consistent only when $|\delta\MAGT{}|/\Alp{}\ll 1$.
Substituting the linearised solution above into this requirement gives
\begin{align}
  |\delta\MAGT{}|\ll\Alp{}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  |\Del{1}|\,\Bzero\,A \;\ll\; \Alp{}^{\,2} .
  \label{PerturbativeBound}
\end{align}
The same dimensional argument applies when the torsion-derivative
kinetic term
$(\rcD{}\MAGT{})^2$~(\cref{PGTEnumeration})
is active and
$\delta\MAGT{}$ propagates: the algebraic inversion is
then replaced by a Green's function inversion, but the magnitude of
the response and the scaling of the bound are unchanged with $\Alp{}$
generalised to the dominant torsion mass scale.

\paragraph*{Generality beyond the algebraic-torsion theory}

The preceding properties were established for the parity-even
quadratic theory class with the $\Del{1}\MAGFt{}\FieldF{}$
non-minimal coupling; the same argument extends to the broader
ghost-safe polynomial PGT class spanned by the
survey~\cite{blagojevic2018hamiltonian} with the two modifications
detailed below.
The bound \cref{PerturbativeBound} fixes specific values
$\Del{1}$ and $\Alp{}$ within the algebraic-torsion sub-class.
Across the surveyed parameter space, the same IBP-and-inversion
argument applies with two extensions.
First, \cref{PerturbativeBound} with $\Del{1}\to g_{\mathrm{NM}}$ and
$\Alp{}\to m_T^2$ gives
$|\delta\MAGT{}|\sim(g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)\,\Bzero A$;
the $K{\cdot}K$ term of \cref{RiemannCartanDecomp} then contributes a
correction of order
$(\delta\MAGT{})^2 \sim (g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)^2\,\Bzero^2$ to the
linearised Cartan equation, placing the nonlinear torsion correction
to $\Pconv$ at
$\mathcal{O}\!\left(\Bzero^4(g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)^2\right)$
and the correction to $C=\Pconv/\Bzero^2$ at
$\mathcal{O}\!\left(\Bzero^2(g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)^2\right)$.
Second, when the torsion-derivative kinetic term is active and
$\delta\MAGT{}$ propagates, the linearised Cartan equation takes
the form $[\mathcal{D}(\partial)+\Alp{}]\,\delta\MAGT{} = -\mathcal{S}$,
where $\mathcal{D}(\partial)$ is the second-order kinetic operator;
the algebraic inversion of \cref{PerturbativeBound} is then replaced
by a Green's function inversion
$\delta\MAGT{} = \int G\,\mathcal{S}$,
$G = [\mathcal{D}(\partial)+\Alp{}]^{-1}$.
The magnitude of $\delta\MAGT{}$ is set by the dominant mass-squared
scale of $G$, and the same
$\mathcal{O}\!\left(\Bzero^2(g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)^2\right)$ scaling
controls the correction with $m_T$ the realised torsion mass scale
at each survey point.
For theories without non-minimal coupling the torsion sector
decouples at $\Bzero=0$ and contributes nothing.

\paragraph*{The survey coefficient}

The parameter survey of \cref{SectorSurvey} evaluates the limiting
survey coefficient
\begin{align}
  \Czero \;\equiv\; \lim_{\Bzero\to 0}\,\frac{\Pconv}{\Bzero^2}
  \;=\; c_2 ,
  \label{SurveyCoefficient}
\end{align}
the leading Taylor coefficient of \cref{TaylorPconv}.

Since $\Pconv$ vanishes at $\Bzero=0$ and contains only even powers of
$\Bzero$ (\cref{TaylorPconv}),
$c_2$ is the lowest non-zero Taylor coefficient of $\Pconv$.
The survey coefficient $\Czero=c_2$ is therefore the leading term in
$C=\Pconv/\Bzero^2$.
In the Einstein--Maxwell limit $\Czero$ reduces to $(\kappa D/2)^2$
from \cref{GertsenshteinFormula}; in the torsionful theory $\Czero=c_2$
is the total graviton-photon conversion coefficient at leading order in
$\Bzero$, taking a value determined by the full kinetic matrix of
\cref{KineticMatrix} and differing from $\Czero^{\mathrm{EM}}$ when
torsion couplings are active.
The amplification factor
\begin{align}
  \Aamp \;\equiv\; \sqrt{\Czero^{\mathrm{torsion}}/\Czero^{\mathrm{EM}}},
  \label{AmplificationFactor}
\end{align}
reported in \cref{SectorSurvey}, measures this deviation and is
$\Bzero$-independent to $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^2)$ by the same limit
argument.

The torsion signal --- the deviation of $\Czero$ from its
Einstein--Maxwell value $(\kappa D/2)^2$ --- is contained in $c_2$
itself: each surveyed theory realises its own $c_2$, set by the full
torsionful kinetic matrix at $\Bzero=0$, and the survey reads off
this value via \cref{SurveyCoefficient}.
Two further mechanisms also contribute to $\Pconv$ but not to $c_2$:
the metric-backreaction detuning $\Delta\propto\Bzero^2$ enters
$\Pconv$ as $(\Delta/g)^2\propto\Bzero^2$ on top of the leading
$\Bzero^2$ amplitude, where $g\propto\Bzero$ is the
graviton-photon coupling strength of the two-mode mixing formula
below;
the torsion-sector nonlinearity contributes at
$\mathcal{O}((\delta\MAGT{})^2)\propto\Bzero^2$ relative to the linear
$\delta\MAGT{}$ response of \cref{PerturbativeBound}.
Both place their contribution at $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^4)$ in $\Pconv$
and $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^2)$ in $C$, strictly subleading to $c_2$;
the exact-limit definition extracts $\Czero$ free of either.
The discrete integer-power structure of the Taylor expansion
separates $c_2$ from the higher Taylor coefficients exactly:
the survey coefficient $\Czero$ isolates the linear-response
torsion signal from the metric-backreaction and torsion-nonlinearity
corrections.

The metric backreaction enters via the tachyonic-mass term
$-(\kappa^2/2)\Bzero^2 h$, giving the graviton effective mass
$m_h^2 = (\kappa^2/2)\Bzero^2$ and detuning the dispersion against
the massless photon partner. The relativistic two-mode reduction
$\Delta = (\omega_h - \omega_a)/2 \simeq m_h^2/(4\omega)$ then yields
\begin{align*}
  g \;=\; \frac{\kappa\Bzero}{2},
  \qquad
  \Delta \;=\; \frac{\kappa^2 \Bzero^2}{8\omega} ,
\end{align*}
with $\omega \simeq k$ the carrier frequency of the photon mode.
The mixing of two modes coupled at strength $g\propto\Bzero$ with
detuning $\Delta\propto\Bzero^2$ yields the two-mode oscillation
formula~\cite{raffelt1988mixing}
\begin{align*}
  \Pconv \;=\; \frac{g^2}{g^2+\Delta^2}
  \,\sin^2\!\!\left(\!\sqrt{g^2+\Delta^2}\,D\right) ;
\end{align*}
substituting $\Delta/g = \kappa\Bzero/(4\omega) \propto\Bzero$ gives
a correction to $C$ at $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^2)$, consistent with the
analyticity bound.
The torsion nonlinearity contributes at
$\mathcal{O}\!\left(\Bzero^2(g_{\mathrm{NM}}/m_T^2)^2\right)$ from
squaring the linearised solution of \cref{PerturbativeBound},
again consistent.

The limiting coefficient $\Czero^{\mathrm{EM}}$ matches the
potential-formulation derivation of~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton},
in which the graviton-photon coupling is exactly linear in $\Bzero$:
$\Czero^{\mathrm{EM}}$ therefore has no $\Bzero$-independent
backreaction shift.
