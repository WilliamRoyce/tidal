\section{Poincar\'{e} gauge theory: field content and conventions}
\label{PGTBackground}

PGT is constructed by gauging the Poincar\'{e}
group: the ten rigid symmetries of a matter Lagrangian are promoted
to local ones, and the invariance is restored by two
compensating connection fields, the spin connection $\tensor{A}{^{ij}_\mu}$
and the tetrad $\tensor{b}{^i_\mu}$.

\paragraph*{Gauging the Poincar\'{e} group}
\label{FieldContent}
The starting point is a matter Lagrangian
$\lag_{\mathrm M}(\phi,\partial_k\phi)$ for a field carrying
energy-momentum and (in general) spin,
invariant under \emph{rigid} Poincar\'{e}
transformations~\cite{kibble1961lorentz,utiyama1956invariant,sciama1962physical,blagojevic2003lectures,lasenby2016scale}.
Promoting the ten constant Poincar\'{e} parameters to spacetime-dependent
functions breaks invariance through derivative terms acting on the
local parameters. A finite local
Lorentz rotation $\Omega^{ij}(x)$ acts on a matter field $\phi$ carrying a Lorentz representation
as~\cite{lasenby2016scale}
\begin{align}
  \phi &\mapsto \exp\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\Omega^{ij}(x)\,\Sigma_{ij}\Bigr)\phi,
  \label{LorentzMatterTransform}
\end{align}
with $\Sigma_{ij} \equiv \Sigma_{[ij]}$ the
Lorentz-generator matrix in $\phi$'s representation.
The Lorentz-rotation generators
are compensated by a \emph{spin connection} $\tensor{A}{^{ij}_\mu}$ that
enters the covariant
derivative~\cite[eq.~(1.7)]{blagojevic2003lectures}
\begin{align}
  \nabla_\mu\phi &= \bigl(\partial_\mu
    + \tfrac{1}{2}\tensor{A}{^{ij}_\mu}\,\Sigma_{ij}\bigr)\phi.
  \label{LorentzCovariantDeriv}
\end{align}
A finite local translation $\xi^\mu(x)$ shifts the argument of
$\phi$~\cite{lasenby2016scale},
\begin{align}
  \phi(x) &\mapsto \phi\bigl(x - \xi(x)\bigr).
  \label{TranslationMatterTransform}
\end{align}
The translation generators are compensated by a \emph{tetrad}
$\tensor{b}{^i_\mu}$ together with its inverse
$\tensor{h}{_i^\mu}$~\cite{hehl1976spin,blagojevic2002gravitation,blagojevic2003lectures,hehlobukhov2007cartan,blagojevicHehl2013,hehl1995metric,barker2024poincare}.
This pair provides the basis-conversion rule between
Lorentz-frame and coordinate indices,
\begin{subequations}
\begin{gather}
  V^\mu = \tensor{h}{_i^\mu}\,V^i,
  \qquad
  V^i = \tensor{b}{^i_\mu}\,V^\mu, \\
  \tensor{b}{^i_\mu}\,\tensor{h}{_i^\nu} = \delta^\nu_\mu,
  \qquad
  \tensor{b}{^i_\mu}\,\tensor{h}{_j^\mu} = \delta^i_j,
  \label{TetradIndexConversion}
\end{gather}
\end{subequations}
and relates the curved-space metric to the Minkowski metric of the
local Lorentz frame via the tetrad-bilinear
relation~\cite{blagojevic2003lectures},
\begin{align}
  \g{_{\mu\nu}} &= \eta_{ij}\,\tensor{b}{^i_\mu}\,\tensor{b}{^j_\nu}.
  \label{TetradMetric}
\end{align}
A manifold with metric admits two frame types at each tangent
space (\cref{TetradFrames}): the coordinate basis $\partial_\mu$
(fixed by the chart $x^\mu$) and an \emph{orthonormal} Lorentz frame
$\mathbf{e}_i$ (chosen at each point so that
$\g{_{\mu\nu}}\mathbf{e}^\mu_i\,\mathbf{e}^\nu_j = \eta_{ij}$).

The covariant derivative on a tensor of mixed type acts with the
spin connection on each Latin index and with an analogous
\emph{affine} connection $\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ (recall~\cref{PalatiniDecomposition})
on each Greek index with $+$ on each upper index and $-$ on each lower index.
For a tensor $\tensor{\Phi}{^{i\,\mu}_{j\,\nu}}$ with mixed Latin and
Greek indices,
\begin{multline}
  \nabla_\rho \tensor{\Phi}{^{i\,\mu}_{j\,\nu}}
    = \partial_\rho \tensor{\Phi}{^{i\,\mu}_{j\,\nu}}
       + \tensor{A}{^i_{k\rho}}\,\tensor{\Phi}{^{k\,\mu}_{j\,\nu}}
       - \tensor{A}{^k_{j\rho}}\,\tensor{\Phi}{^{i\,\mu}_{k\,\nu}} \\
       + \MAGA{^\mu_{\rho\sigma}}\,\tensor{\Phi}{^{i\,\sigma}_{j\,\nu}}
       - \MAGA{^\sigma_{\rho\nu}}\,\tensor{\Phi}{^{i\,\mu}_{j\,\sigma}}.
  \label{MixedCovariantDerivative}
\end{multline}

In any gauge theory the field strength can be read off the
commutator of two covariant derivatives acting on a matter
field $\phi$~\cite{blagojevic2002gravitation,lasenby2016scale}: were the connection
trivial the commutator would vanish, so the commutator is linear
in the field strength. For
electrodynamics this gives the familiar relation
$[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]\phi = i e\,\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\phi$ on a
matter field. In PGT there is one term for each generator
type~\cite[eq.~(1.11)]{blagojevic2003lectures},
\begin{align}
  [\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]\phi
    &= \tfrac{1}{2}\,\MAGF{^{ij}_{\mu\nu}}\,\Sigma_{ij}\phi
       - \MAGT{^i_{\mu\nu}}\,\nabla_i\phi.
  \label{PGTCommutator}
\end{align}
The first term acts through the Lorentz spin matrix and identifies
$\MAGF{^{ij}_{\mu\nu}}$, the \emph{Riemann--Cartan curvature}, as the
Lorentz-rotation field strength;
the second term acts on the Lorentz-frame derivative of $\phi$
and identifies $\MAGT{^i_{\mu\nu}}$, the \emph{torsion}, as the translation field
strength. Their explicit
forms~\cite[eq.~(1.11)]{blagojevic2003lectures} are
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
  \MAGF{^{ij}_{\mu\nu}}
    &= 2\,\partial_{[\mu} \tensor{A}{^{ij}_{\nu]}}
       + 2\,\tensor{A}{^i_{s[\mu}}\,\tensor{A}{^{sj}_{\nu]}},
  \label{CurvatureDef} \\
  \MAGT{^i_{\mu\nu}}
    &= 2\,\nabla_{[\mu} \tensor{b}{^i_{\nu]}}
     = 2\,\partial_{[\mu}\tensor{b}{^i_{\nu]}}
       + 2\,\tensor{A}{^i_{j[\mu}}\,\tensor{b}{^j_{\nu]}}.
  \label{TorsionDef}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

The first measures the
rotation of a vector parallel-transported around an infinitesimal
closed loop; the second measures the non-closure of infinitesimal
parallelograms formed from parallel-transported vectors. Both
interpretations are illustrated in
\cref{DiffGeometryObstructions}.

The geometric picture is heuristic. As Barker \etal
emphasise~\cite{barker2024poincare,barker2025metricaffine}, every
metric-affine model can be recast as a Riemannian theory
coupled to a rank-three tensor matter field.\footnote{In
metric-compatible PGT this field is the contortion of~\cref{ContortionSplit}.}
The matter-field framing manifests
the standard quantum-field-theoretic pathologies typical of
high-rank matter: ghosts and tachyons in the linearised
spectrum~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,lin2019pgt},
and strong-coupling instabilities at non-linear
order~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear}.
On a generic linear connection the metric and the connection are
independent, so the covariant derivative of the metric does not
vanish; this defines the \emph{non-metricity}
tensor~\cite[eq.~(2.5)]{blagojevic2003lectures}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{Q}_{\lambda\mu\nu} \equiv -\rcD{_\lambda}\g{_{\mu\nu}},
  \label{NonMetricityDef}
\end{equation}
which measures the failure of parallel transport to preserve inner
products (\cref{DiffGeometryObstructions}). The
most general space carrying all three is the
metric-affine space $(L_4, \g{})$
(\cref{MetricAffineClasses})~\cite{hehl1995metric}. Demanding that
the connection preserves the metric under parallel transport,
equivalently that $\rcD{_\mu}\g{_{\nu\lambda}} = 0$, is the
\emph{metric-compatibility}
condition~\cite[eq.~(2.5)]{blagojevic2003lectures}. PGT adopts
metric compatibility as a defining
requirement~\cite{hehl1976spin,blagojevic2003lectures}.

\paragraph*{From field strengths to a gauge-invariant action}
With $\MAGF{}$ and $\MAGT{}$ as the dynamical gauge fields, the
gravitational action is built from them by the same principle as
any other gauge theory: the free Lagrangian must be a scalar
under local Lorentz and coordinate
transformations, built
from the field strengths and their covariant derivatives, and
integrated against an invariant volume element on
spacetime~\cite[eq.~(1.13)]{blagojevic2003lectures,lin2019pgt}.
These scalars are formed by full index contractions with
$\g{_{\mu\nu}}$ and $\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$,\footnote{The
$\varepsilon$-contractions give parity-odd invariants which are
often neglected in PGT applications; their treatment in the
linearised spectrum is the subject of recent
work~\cite{karananas2014parity,barker2025psalter2}.} exactly as
in other gauge field theories: the QED kinetic
action $-\tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$ is the
unique Lorentz scalar quadratic in the field strength up to a
total derivative,\footnote{The parity-odd
$\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldFtilde{^{\mu\nu}}$ being topological.} and the
PGT analogue is the set of all independent contractions
of $\MAGF{}$ and $\MAGT{}$ with one
another~\cite{barker2024poincare,sezgin1980ghostfree,hehl1995metric}.
The invariants are catalogued in
\cref{PGTEnumeration}.

\paragraph*{Spacetime classification and the Palatini formulation}
\label{SpacetimeClassification}
Different combinations of vanishing curvature, torsion, and
non-metricity define the standard metric-affine spacetime
hierarchy.~\Cref{MetricAffineClasses} shows the
classification: the most general space is metric-affine
$(L_4, \g{})$, and setting subsets of
$\MAGF{}, \MAGT{}, \mathcal{Q}$ to zero recovers the named
special cases. PGT inhabits the Riemann--Cartan space $U_4$
defined by $\mathcal{Q} = 0$.
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/metric_affine_classes.pdf}
  \caption{Classification of metric-affine spacetime geometries by
    which of the three fields (curvature $\MAGF{}$,
    torsion $\MAGT{}$, and non-metricity $\mathcal{Q}$) are
    non-zero. The inner region is the most general metric-affine
    space $(L_4, \g{})$. General relativity inhabits the
    Riemannian space $V_4$ ($\MAGT{} = \mathcal{Q} = 0$);
    Poincar\'{e} gauge theory inhabits the Riemann--Cartan space
    $U_4$ ($\mathcal{Q} = 0$); the flat-connection (teleparallel)
    case $W_4$ has $\MAGF{} = 0$;
    setting all three to zero recovers Minkowski
    $\mathbb{M}^4$. Adapted from
    figure~3 of~\cite{bahamonde2021teleparallel}.}
  \label{MetricAffineClasses}
\end{figure}

The two formulations, gauge-theoretic
$(\tensor{b}{^i_\mu}, \tensor{A}{^{ij}_\mu})$ and metric-affine
$(\g{_{\mu\nu}}, \MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu})$, are related by the
\emph{tetrad postulate}~\cite[eq.~(2.9)]{blagojevic2003lectures}.
Any tangent vector $u$ can be expanded in either basis,
$u = u^i\,\mathbf{e}_i = u^\mu\,\partial_\mu$;
parallel-transport must give the same result whether the
spin connection $\tensor{A}{^{ij}_\mu}$ acts on the Lorentz-frame
components $u^i$ or the affine connection $\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$
acts on the coordinate-basis components $u^\mu$. Equivalently,
the covariant derivative of the tetrad vanishes,
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\mu \tensor{b}{^i_\nu}
    = \partial_\mu \tensor{b}{^i_\nu}
    + \tensor{A}{^i_{j\mu}}\,\tensor{b}{^j_\nu}
    - \MAGA{^\rho_{\nu\mu}}\,\tensor{b}{^i_\rho}
    = 0,
  \label{TetradPostulate}
\end{equation}
imposing simultaneous covariance under both Latin (Lorentz) and
Greek (coordinate) indices.~\Cref{TetradPostulate} solves
for the affine connection in terms of the gauge fields,
\begin{align}
  \MAGA{^\rho_{\nu\mu}}
    &= h_i{}^\rho\bigl(\partial_\mu b^i{}_\nu
       + A^i{}_{j\mu}\,\tensor{b}{^j_\nu}\bigr),
  \label{AffineFromGauge}
\end{align}
fixing a one-to-one map between $\tensor{A}{^{ij}_\mu}$ and
$\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ once a tetrad is chosen, so both
formulations encode the \emph{same} Riemann--Cartan geometry.
Imposing metricity forces the
decomposition~\cite[eq.~(2.6)]{blagojevic2003lectures}
\begin{align}
  \MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu} &= \rCon{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}
    + K^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu},
  \label{ContortionSplit}
\end{align}
which is the same content as~\cref{PalatiniDecomposition}.

\Cref{Theory} and~\cref{SymbolicComputation} use the metric-affine
notation $(\g{_{\mu\nu}}, \MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu})$
throughout~\cite{palatini1919deduzione,ferraris1982variational,barker2024palatini}.
\Cref{PGTRiemannCartanTranslation} summarises the
dictionary between this and the
$(\tensor{b}{^i_\mu}, \tensor{A}{^{ij}_\mu})$ notation used above.

\paragraph*{Irreducible torsion decomposition}
\label{IrreducibleTorsionFull}
The torsion tensor $\MAGT{^\alpha_{\beta\mu}}$, antisymmetric in
its lower two indices, splits into three irreducible
representations of the local Lorentz
group~\cite{hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric,shapiro2002torsion,obukhov2024electrodynamics}.
The trace vector and axial pseudovector
\begin{equation}
  \T[2]{_\mu} \equiv \MAGT{^\lambda_\lambda_\mu}\!,
  \qand
  \T[3]{^\mu} \equiv \varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\mu}\,
    \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\gamma}}\!,
  \label{TraceAxialVector}
\end{equation}
respectively, each collects four components, and the traceless tensor part
$\T[1]{^\alpha_{\beta\mu}}$ collects the remaining,
transforming as a single irreducible tensor
representation of the Lorentz group~\cite{hehl1976spin}.\footnote{Shapiro's
letter labels $T_\mu, S^\mu, q_{\alpha\beta\mu}$~\cite{shapiro2002torsion}
for the same three components appear in the older literature.}
The torsion tensor can be expressed in terms of these three irreducible parts as
\begin{align}
  \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}
    = \tfrac{1}{3}\bigl(\T[2]{_\beta}\,\g{_{\alpha\mu}}
                       - \T[2]{_\mu}\,\g{_{\alpha\beta}}\bigr)
    - \tfrac{1}{6}\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\,\T[3]{^\nu}
    + \T[1]{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}.
  \label{IrreducibleTorsion}
\end{align}

The torsion-squared sector of the action admits two equivalent
parameterisations. In the irreducible basis it reads $a\,(\T[1]{})^2 +
b\,(\T[3]{})^2 + c\,(\T[2]{})^2$, with $(\T[1]{})^2 \equiv
\T[1]{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}\T[1]{^{\alpha\beta\mu}}$ and the other
squared norms defined analogously. Equivalently, in the
\emph{direct basis} of scalar contractions (following the convention
of~\cite{lin2019pgt}; see
also~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,shapiro2002torsion})
\begin{align}
  I_1 \equiv \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}\MAGT{^{\alpha\beta\mu}},
  \quad
  I_2 \equiv \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}\MAGT{^{\beta\alpha\mu}},
  \quad
  I_3 \equiv \MAGT{^\alpha_\alpha_\mu}\MAGT{^\beta_\beta^\mu},
  \label{TorsionDirectBasis}
\end{align}
it reads $\beta_1 I_1 + \beta_2 I_2 + \beta_3 I_3$. Substituting
\cref{IrreducibleTorsion} into the direct-basis
contractions gives the linear map
\begin{align}
  \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{pmatrix}
  &= \frac{1}{6} \begin{pmatrix}
       6 & 6 & 0 \\
       -1 & 1 & 0 \\
       4 & 2 & 6
     \end{pmatrix}
     \begin{pmatrix} \beta_1 \\ \beta_2 \\ \beta_3 \end{pmatrix},
  \label{DirectIrreducibleTransform}
\end{align}
which is invertible.
