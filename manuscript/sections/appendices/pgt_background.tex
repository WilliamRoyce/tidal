\section{Poincar\'{e} gauge theory: field content and conventions}
\label{PGTBackground}

PGT is constructed by gauging the Poincar\'{e}
group: the ten rigid symmetries of a matter Lagrangian are promoted
to local ones, and the invariance is restored by two
compensating connection fields, the spin connection $A^{ij}{}_\mu$
and the tetrad $b^i{}_\mu$.

\paragraph*{Gauging the Poincar\'{e} group}
\label{FieldContent}
The starting point is a matter Lagrangian
$\mathcal{L}_M(\phi,\partial_k\phi)$ for a field carrying
energy-momentum and (in general) spin-angular-momentum,
invariant under \emph{rigid} (constant-parameter) Poincar\'{e}
transformations~\cite{kibble1961lorentz,utiyama1956invariant,sciama1962physical,blagojevic2003lectures,lasenby2016scale}.
Promoting the ten Poincar\'{e} parameters to spacetime-dependent
functions breaks invariance through derivative terms acting on the
local parameters. A finite local
Lorentz rotation $\Omega^{ij}(x)$ acts on a matter field $\phi$ carrying a Lorentz representation
as~\cite{lasenby2016scale}
\begin{align}
  \phi &\mapsto \exp\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}\Omega^{ij}(x)\,\Sigma_{ij}\Bigr)\phi,
  \label{LorentzMatterTransform}
\end{align}
with $\Sigma_{ij} = \Sigma_{[ij]}$ the antisymmetric
Lorentz-generator.
The Lorentz-rotation generators
are compensated by a \emph{spin connection} $A^{ij}{}_\mu$ that
enters the covariant
derivative~\cite[eq.~(1.7)]{blagojevic2003lectures}
\begin{align}
  \nabla_\mu\phi &= \bigl(\partial_\mu
    + \tfrac{1}{2}A^{ij}{}_\mu\,\Sigma_{ij}\bigr)\phi.
  \label{LorentzCovariantDeriv}
\end{align}
The translation generators are compensated by a \emph{tetrad}
$b^i{}_\mu$ together with its inverse
$h_i{}^\mu$~\cite{hehl1976spin,blagojevic2002gravitation,blagojevic2003lectures,hehlobukhov2007cartan,blagojevicHehl2013,hehl1995metric,barker2024poincare}.
The pair provides the explicit basis-conversion rule between
Lorentz-frame and coordinate indices,
\begin{subequations}
\begin{gather}
  V^\mu = h_i{}^\mu\,V^i,
  \qquad
  V^i = b^i{}_\mu\,V^\mu, \\
  b^i{}_\mu\,h_i{}^\nu = \delta^\nu_\mu,
  \qquad
  b^i{}_\mu\,h_j{}^\mu = \delta^i_j,
  \label{TetradIndexConversion}
\end{gather}
\end{subequations}
and relates the curved-space metric to the Minkowski metric of the
local Lorentz frame via the tetrad-bilinear
relation~\cite{blagojevic2003lectures},
\begin{align}
  \g{_{\mu\nu}} &= \eta_{ij}\,b^i{}_\mu\,b^j{}_\nu.
  \label{TetradMetric}
\end{align}
A manifold with metric admits two frame types at each tangent
space (\cref{TetradFrames}): the coordinate basis $\partial_\mu$
(fixed by the chart $x^\mu$, generally non-orthonormal in a
curved space) and an \emph{orthonormal} Lorentz frame
$\mathbf{e}_i$ (chosen at each point so that
$\g{_{\mu\nu}}\mathbf{e}^\mu_i\,\mathbf{e}^\nu_j = \eta_{ij}$).
The tetrad $b^i{}_\mu$ is the linear map between the two.

Unlike the spin connection, the tetrad does \emph{not} appear as
a connection term inside the covariant derivative on a matter
field \cref{LorentzCovariantDeriv}; it instead converts the
resulting spacetime-index covariant derivative to Lorentz-frame-index
form, $\nabla_k\phi = h_k{}^\mu\,\nabla_\mu\phi$~\cite[eq.~(1.7b)]{blagojevic2003lectures}.
The tetrad enters the gauge dynamics through the metric relation
\cref{TetradMetric} and the torsion field strength \cref{TorsionDef}
below.

The covariant derivative on a tensor of mixed type acts with the
spin connection on each Latin index and with an analogous
\emph{affine} connection $\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ on each Greek
index --- the independent Riemann--Cartan connection of the
Palatini formulation, introduced in \cref{PGTframework} ---
with $+$ on each upper index and $-$ on each lower index.
For a tensor $\Phi^{i\,\mu}{}_{j\,\nu}$ with mixed Latin and
Greek indices,
\begin{multline}
  \nabla_\rho \Phi^{i\,\mu}{}_{j\,\nu}
    = \partial_\rho \Phi^{i\,\mu}{}_{j\,\nu}
       + A^i{}_{k\rho}\,\Phi^{k\,\mu}{}_{j\,\nu}
       - A^k{}_{j\rho}\,\Phi^{i\,\mu}{}_{k\,\nu} \\
       + \MAGA{^\mu_{\rho\sigma}}\,\Phi^{i\,\sigma}{}_{j\,\nu}
       - \MAGA{^\sigma_{\rho\nu}}\,\Phi^{i\,\mu}{}_{j\,\sigma}.
  \label{MixedCovariantDerivative}
\end{multline}
\Cref{MixedCovariantDerivative} is the per-index form used in the
symbolic implementation of torsion and
curvature~\cite[eq.~(1.7a)]{blagojevic2003lectures}
(\cref{SymbolicComputation}).

In any gauge theory the field strength can be read off the
commutator of two covariant derivatives acting on a matter
field~\cite{blagojevic2002gravitation,lasenby2016scale}: were the connection
trivial the commutator would vanish, so the commutator is linear
in the field strength. For
electrodynamics this gives the familiar relation
$[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]\phi = i e\,\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}\phi$ on a
matter field. In PGT the analogous
calculation produces two terms, one for each generator
type~\cite[eq.~(1.11)]{blagojevic2003lectures},
\begin{align}
  [\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]\phi
    &= \tfrac{1}{2}\,\MAGFt{^{ij}_{\mu\nu}}\,\Sigma_{ij}\phi
       - \MAGT{^i_{\mu\nu}}\,\nabla_i\phi.
  \label{PGTCommutator}
\end{align}
The first term acts through the Lorentz spin matrix and identifies
$\MAGFt{^{ij}_{\mu\nu}}$ as the Lorentz-rotation field strength;
the second term acts on the Lorentz-frame derivative of $\phi$
and identifies $\MAGT{^i_{\mu\nu}}$ as the translation field
strength. Their explicit
forms~\cite[eq.~(1.11)]{blagojevic2003lectures} are
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
  \MAGFt{^{ij}_{\mu\nu}}
    &= 2\,\partial_{[\mu}A^{ij}{}_{\nu]}
       + 2\,A^i{}_{s[\mu}A^{sj}{}_{\nu]},
  \label{CurvatureDef} \\
  \MAGT{^i_{\mu\nu}}
    &= 2\,\nabla_{[\mu} b^i{}_{\nu]}
     = 2\,\partial_{[\mu}b^i{}_{\nu]}
       + 2\,A^i{}_{j[\mu}\,b^j{}_{\nu]}.
  \label{TorsionDef}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}

The first measures the
rotation of a vector parallel-transported around an infinitesimal
closed loop; the second measures the non-closure of infinitesimal
parallelograms formed from parallel-transported vectors. Both
interpretations\footnote{The geometric framing
is heuristic. As Barker, Marzo and Santoni
emphasise~\cite{barker2024poincare,barker2025metricaffine}, the
affine connection in the most general metric-affine
setting~\cite{hehl1995metric} decomposes as
$\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu} = \rCon{^\sigma_\mu_\nu} + \mathcal{D}^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}$,
with the distortion $\mathcal{D}^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}$ a rank-three
tensor field that subsumes torsion and non-metricity (\cref{NonMetricityDef})
together; in the metric-compatible PGT (and \cref{PalatiniDecomposition}), the
distortion reduces to the contortion $K^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}$ of
\cref{ContortionSplit}, and the rank-three matter content is
purely torsion-driven. Every metric-affine model can therefore be
recast as a Riemannian theory of gravity coupled to a rank-three
tensor matter field. Unlike the geometric picture of general
relativity --- which maps cleanly onto a healthy spin-2
graviton~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,lin2019pgt} --- the
metric-affine geometric picture obscures the rank-three
matter content, which inherits the standard
quantum-field-theoretic pathologies typical of high-rank fields:
ghosts and tachyons in the linearised
spectrum~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,lin2019pgt},
and strong-coupling instabilities at non-linear
order~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear}. The physical content is in this
sense insensitive to whether one adopts the geometric or the
matter-field framing, but the matter-field framing makes the
pathologies manifest.} are illustrated in
\cref{DiffGeometryObstructions}.
On a generic linear connection the metric and the connection are
independent, so the covariant derivative of the metric does not
vanish; this defines the \emph{non-metricity}
tensor~\cite[eq.~(2.5)]{blagojevic2003lectures}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{Q}_{\lambda\mu\nu} \equiv -\rcD{_\lambda}\g{_{\mu\nu}},
  \label{NonMetricityDef}
\end{equation}
which measures the failure of parallel transport to preserve inner
products (\cref{DiffGeometryObstructions}). The
most general space carrying all three is the
metric-affine space $(L_4, \g{})$
(\cref{MetricAffineClasses})~\cite{hehl1995metric}. Demanding that
the connection preserves the metric under parallel transport,
equivalently that $\rcD{_\mu}\g{_{\nu\lambda}} = 0$, is the
\emph{metric-compatibility}
condition~\cite[eq.~(2.5)]{blagojevic2003lectures}. PGT adopts
metric compatibility as a defining
requirement~\cite{hehl1976spin,blagojevic2003lectures}, with
$\MAGFt{}$ and $\MAGT{}$ as the gauge-theoretic field strengths.

\paragraph*{From field strengths to a gauge-invariant action}
With $\MAGFt{}$ and $\MAGT{}$ as the dynamical gauge fields, the
gravitational action is built from them by the same principle as
any other gauge theory: the free Lagrangian must be a scalar
under local Lorentz and general-coordinate
transformations, built
from the field strengths and their covariant derivatives, and
integrated against an invariant volume element on
spacetime~\cite[eq.~(1.13)]{blagojevic2003lectures,lin2019pgt}.
These scalars are formed by full index contractions with
$\g{_{\mu\nu}}$ and $\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$,\footnote{The
$\varepsilon$-contractions give parity-odd invariants which are
often neglected in PGT applications; their treatment in the
linearised spectrum is the subject of recent
work~\cite{karananas2014parity,barker2025psalter2}.} exactly as
in other gauge field theories: the QED kinetic
action $-\tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$ is the
unique Lorentz scalar quadratic in the field strength up to a
total derivative,\footnote{The parity-odd
$\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\tensor{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}}{^{\mu\nu}}$ being topological.} and the
PGT analogue is the set of all independent contractions
of $\MAGFt{}$ and $\MAGT{}$ with one
another~\cite{barker2024poincare,sezgin1980ghostfree,hehl1995metric}.
The invariants are catalogued in
\cref{PGTEnumeration}.

\paragraph*{Spacetime classification and the Palatini formulation}
\label{SpacetimeClassification}
Different combinations of vanishing curvature, torsion, and
non-metricity define the standard metric-affine spacetime
hierarchy. \Cref{MetricAffineClasses} shows the
classification: the most general space is metric-affine
$(L_4, \g{})$, and setting subsets of
$\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{T}, \mathcal{Q}$ to zero recovers the named
special cases. PGT inhabits the Riemann--Cartan space $U_4$
defined by $\mathcal{Q} = 0$.

    non-zero. The outer region is the most general metric-affine
    space $(L_4, \g{})$. General relativity inhabits the
    Riemannian space $V_4$ ($\mathcal{T} = \mathcal{Q} = 0$);
    Poincar\'{e} gauge theory inhabits the Riemann--Cartan space
    $U_4$ ($\mathcal{Q} = 0$); the flat-connection (teleparallel)
    case $W_4$ has $\mathcal{R} = 0$, with the metric-teleparallel
    ($\mathcal{R} = \mathcal{Q} = 0$, the classical Weitzenb\"{o}ck

    ($\mathcal{R} = \mathcal{T} = 0$) sub-cases obtained by
    additionally setting $\mathcal{Q} = 0$ or $\mathcal{T} = 0$;

The bridge between the gauge-theoretic formulation (with fields
$b^i{}_\mu$ and $A^{ij}{}_\mu$) and the metric-affine formulation
(with fields $\g{_{\mu\nu}}$ and $\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$) is the
\emph{tetrad postulate}~\cite[eq.~(2.9)]{blagojevic2003lectures}.
Any tangent
vector $u$ can be expanded in either basis,
$u = u^i\,\mathbf{e}_i = u^\mu\,\partial_\mu$;
parallel-transporting $u$ must give the same result whether the
spin connection $A^{ij}{}_\mu$ acts on the Lorentz-frame
components $u^i$ or the affine connection $\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$
acts on the coordinate-basis components $u^\mu$. Equivalently,
the full covariant derivative of the tetrad vanishes,
\begin{equation}
  \nabla_\mu b^i{}_\nu
    = \partial_\mu b^i{}_\nu
    + A^i{}_{j\mu}\,b^j{}_\nu
    - \MAGA{^\rho_{\nu\mu}}\,b^i{}_\rho
    = 0,
  \label{TetradPostulate}
\end{equation}
imposing simultaneous covariance under both Latin (Lorentz) and
Greek (coordinate) indices. \Cref{TetradPostulate} solves
algebraically for the affine connection in terms of the
gauge-theoretic fields,
\begin{align}
  \MAGA{^\rho_{\nu\mu}}
    &= h_i{}^\rho\bigl(\partial_\mu b^i{}_\nu
       + A^i{}_{j\mu}\,b^j{}_\nu\bigr),
  \label{AffineFromGauge}
\end{align}
fixing a one-to-one map between $A^{ij}{}_\mu$ and
$\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ once a tetrad is chosen, so the two
formulations encode the \emph{same} Riemann--Cartan geometry.
Imposing metricity then forces
the
decomposition~\cite[eq.~(2.6)]{blagojevic2003lectures}
\begin{align}
  \MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu} &= \rCon{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}
    + K^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu},
  \label{ContortionSplit}
\end{align}
which is the same content as \cref{PalatiniDecomposition}.

\Cref{Theory} and \cref{SymbolicComputation} use the metric-affine
notation $(\g{_{\mu\nu}}, \MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu})$
throughout~\cite{palatini1919deduzione,ferraris1982variational,barker2024palatini}.
\Cref{PGTRiemannCartanTranslation} summarises the object-by-object
dictionary between this notation and the gauge-theoretic notation
$(b^i{}_\mu, A^{ij}{}_\mu)$ used above, after
Blagojevi\'{c}~\cite[Table~1]{blagojevic2003lectures}.

\paragraph*{Irreducible torsion decomposition}
\label{IrreducibleTorsionFull}
The torsion tensor $\MAGT{^\alpha_{\beta\mu}}$ has 24 independent
components --- antisymmetric in its lower two indices and
otherwise unconstrained --- which split into three irreducible
representations of the local Lorentz
group~\cite{hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric,shapiro2002torsion,obukhov2024electrodynamics}.
The trace vector and axial pseudovector
\begin{equation}
  \T[2]{_\mu} \equiv \MAGT{^\lambda_\lambda_\mu}\!,
  \quad \text{and} \quad
  \T[3]{^\mu} \equiv \varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\mu}\,
    \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\gamma}}\!,
  \label{TraceAxialVector}
\end{equation}
respectively, each collects another components, and the totally traceless tensor part
$\T[1]{^\alpha_{\beta\mu}}$ collects the remaining sixteen
components, transforming as a single irreducible tensor
representation of the Lorentz group~\cite{hehl1976spin}, and
satisfying the orthogonality conditions
$\T[1]{^\lambda_\lambda_\mu} = 0$ and
$\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\T[1]{_{\alpha\beta\gamma}} = 0$.\footnote{Shapiro's
letter labels $T_\mu, S^\mu, q_{\alpha\beta\mu}$~\cite{shapiro2002torsion}
for the same three components appear in the older literature.}
The reconstruction formula \cref{IrreducibleTorsion}
expresses the torsion in terms of its irreducible parts.

The torsion-squared sector of the action admits two equivalent
parameterisations. In the irreducible basis it reads $a\,(\T[1]{})^2 +
b\,(\T[3]{})^2 + c\,(\T[2]{})^2$, with $(\T[1]{})^2 \equiv
\T[1]{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}\T[1]{^{\alpha\beta\mu}}$ and the other
squared norms defined analogously; in the direct basis of scalar
contractions of \cref{TorsionDirectBasis} it reads $\beta_1 I_1 +
\beta_2 I_2 + \beta_3 I_3$. Substituting
\cref{IrreducibleTorsion} into the direct-basis
contractions and using the orthogonality of the three irreducible
parts gives the linear
map~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,hehl1995metric,lin2019pgt}
\begin{align}
  \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{pmatrix}
  &= \frac{1}{6} \begin{pmatrix}
       6 & 6 & 0 \\
       -1 & 1 & 0 \\
       4 & 2 & 6
     \end{pmatrix}
     \begin{pmatrix} \beta_1 \\ \beta_2 \\ \beta_3 \end{pmatrix},
  \label{DirectIrreducibleTransform}
\end{align}
which is invertible.
