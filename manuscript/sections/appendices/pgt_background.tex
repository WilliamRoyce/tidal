\section{Poincar\'{e} gauge theory: field content and conventions}
\label{PGTBackground}

PGT is constructed by gauging the Poincar\'{e}
group: the ten rigid symmetries of a matter Lagrangian are promoted
to local ones, and the resulting non-invariance is cancelled by two
compensating connection fields, the spin connection $A^{ij}{}_\mu$
and the tetrad $b^i{}_\mu$.

\paragraph*{Gauging the Poincar\'{e} group}
\label{FieldContent}
The starting point is a matter Lagrangian
$\mathcal{L}_M(\phi,\partial_k\phi)$ that is invariant under
\emph{rigid} (constant-parameter) Poincar\'{e}
transformations~\cite{kibble1961lorentz,utiyama1956invariant,sciama1962physical,blagojevic2003lectures}.
Promoting the ten Poincar\'{e} parameters to spacetime-dependent
functions breaks invariance through derivative terms acting on the
local parameters; these are removed by introducing one
compensating connection per generator type. The Lorentz-rotation generators
are compensated by a \emph{spin connection} $A^{ij}{}_\mu$ that
enters the covariant
derivative~\cite[eq.~(1.7)]{blagojevic2003lectures}
\begin{align}
  \nabla_\mu\phi &= \bigl(\partial_\mu
    + \tfrac{1}{2}A^{ij}{}_\mu\,\Sigma_{ij}\bigr)\phi
  \label{LorentzCovariantDeriv}
\end{align}
acting on a matter field $\phi$ in the representation with spin
matrix $\Sigma_{ij}$. The translation generators are compensated
by a \emph{tetrad} $b^i{}_\mu$ together with its inverse
$h_i{}^\mu$, which together provide the explicit basis-conversion
rule between Lorentz-frame and coordinate
indices~\cite{hehl1976spin,blagojevic2002gravitation,blagojevic2003lectures,hehlobukhov2007cartan,blagojevicHehl2013,hehl1995metric,barker2024poincare},
\begin{subequations}
\begin{gather}
  V^\mu = h_i{}^\mu\,V^i,
  \qquad
  V^i = b^i{}_\mu\,V^\mu, \\
  b^i{}_\mu\,h_i{}^\nu = \delta^\nu_\mu,
  \qquad
  b^i{}_\mu\,h_j{}^\mu = \delta^i_j,
  \label{TetradIndexConversion}
\end{gather}
\end{subequations}
and which relate the curved-space metric to the Minkowski metric
of the local Lorentz frame via the tetrad-bilinear relation
\begin{align}
  \g{_{\mu\nu}} &= \eta_{ij}\,b^i{}_\mu\,b^j{}_\nu.
  \label{TetradMetric}
\end{align}
A manifold with metric admits two frame types at each tangent space:
the coordinate basis $\partial_\mu$ (fixed by the chart $x^\mu$,
generally non-orthonormal in a curved space) and an orthonormal
Lorentz frame $\mathbf{e}_i$ (chosen at each point so that
$\g{_{\mu\nu}}\mathbf{e}^\mu_i\,\mathbf{e}^\nu_j = \eta_{ij}$).
The tetrad $b^i{}_\mu$ is the linear map between the two;
\cref{TetradFrames} illustrates this at a single point $P$.
\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{figures/tetrad.pdf}
  \caption{At each point $P$ of a differentiable manifold, the

    $\mathbf{e}_i|_P$ (chosen at $P$ independently of the
    coordinates) span the same tangent space. The tetrad
    $b^i{}_\mu$ and its inverse $h_i{}^\mu$ relate the components

  \label{TetradFrames}

Unlike the spin connection, the tetrad does \emph{not} appear as
a connection term inside the covariant derivative on a matter
field; it instead converts the resulting spacetime-index
covariant derivative to Lorentz-frame-index form,
$\nabla_k\phi = h_k{}^\mu\,\nabla_\mu\phi$~\cite[eq.~(1.7b)]{blagojevic2003lectures}.
The tetrad instead enters as a gauge field through the metric
relation \cref{TetradMetric} and the torsion field strength
\cref{TorsionDef}.

The covariant derivative on a tensor of mixed type acts with the
spin connection on each Latin index and with the affine connection
$\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ (the independent Riemann--Cartan
connection of the Palatini formulation, introduced in
\cref{PGTframework}) on each Greek index, with $+$ on each upper
index and $-$ on each lower index.
For a tensor $\Phi^{i\,\mu}{}_{j\,\nu}$ with mixed Latin and
Greek indices,
\begin{align}
  \nabla_\rho \Phi^{i\,\mu}{}_{j\,\nu}
    &= \partial_\rho \Phi^{i\,\mu}{}_{j\,\nu}
       + A^i{}_{k\rho}\,\Phi^{k\,\mu}{}_{j\,\nu}
       - A^k{}_{j\rho}\,\Phi^{i\,\mu}{}_{k\,\nu} \nonumber \\
    &\quad
       + \MAGA{^\mu_{\rho\sigma}}\,\Phi^{i\,\sigma}{}_{j\,\nu}
       - \MAGA{^\sigma_{\rho\nu}}\,\Phi^{i\,\mu}{}_{j\,\sigma}.
  \label{MixedCovariantDerivative}
\end{align}
The matter-field rule \cref{LorentzCovariantDeriv} is the same
construction evaluated on the spinor representation of the
Lorentz group: the spin matrix $\Sigma_{ij}$ in
\cref{LorentzCovariantDeriv} is replaced here by the explicit
index-action of the spin connection on each Lorentz-tensor
index~\cite[eq.~(1.7a)]{blagojevic2003lectures}.
\Cref{MixedCovariantDerivative} underlies the symbolic
decomposition of covariant derivatives implemented in
\cref{SymbolicComputation}.

In any gauge theory the field strength can be read off the
commutator of two covariant derivatives acting on a matter field:
were the connection trivial the commutator would vanish, so the
commutator is linear in the field strength. For
electrodynamics this gives the familiar relation
$[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]\phi = i e\,\mathcal{F}_{\mu\nu}\phi$ on a charged
matter field. In PGT the analogous
calculation produces two terms, one for each generator
type~\cite[eq.~(1.11)]{blagojevic2003lectures},
\begin{align}
  [\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]\phi
    &= \tfrac{1}{2}\,\MAGFt{^{ij}_{\mu\nu}}\,\Sigma_{ij}\phi
       - \MAGT{^i_{\mu\nu}}\,\nabla_i\phi.
  \label{PGTCommutator}
\end{align}
The first term acts through the Lorentz spin matrix and identifies
$\MAGFt{^{ij}_{\mu\nu}}$ as the Lorentz-rotation field strength;
the second term acts on the Lorentz-frame derivative of $\phi$
and identifies $\MAGT{^i_{\mu\nu}}$ as the translation field
strength. Their explicit
forms~\cite[eq.~(1.11)]{blagojevic2003lectures} are
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
  \MAGFt{^{ij}_{\mu\nu}}
    &= 2\,\partial_{[\mu}A^{ij}{}_{\nu]}
       + 2\,A^i{}_{s[\mu}A^{sj}{}_{\nu]},
  \label{CurvatureDef} \\
  \MAGT{^i_{\mu\nu}}
    &= 2\,\nabla_{[\mu} b^i{}_{\nu]}
     = 2\,\partial_{[\mu}b^i{}_{\nu]}
       + 2\,A^i{}_{j[\mu}\,b^j{}_{\nu]}.
  \label{TorsionDef}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
The schematic torsion form $2\nabla_{[\mu} b^i{}_{\nu]}$ unfolds
to its explicit form via \cref{MixedCovariantDerivative} applied
to $b^i{}_\nu$: the spin connection acts on the Lorentz index,
while the antisymmetrisation on $[\mu\nu]$ kills the Christoffel
correction on the Greek index.

The first measures the
rotation of a vector parallel-transported around an infinitesimal
closed loop; the second measures the non-closure of infinitesimal
parallelograms formed from parallel-transported vectors. Both
interpretations are illustrated in
\cref{DiffGeometryObstructions}.\footnote{The geometric framing
is heuristic. As Barker, Marzo and Santoni
emphasise~\cite{barker2024poincare,barker2025metricaffine}, the
affine connection in the most general metric-affine
setting~\cite{hehl1995metric} decomposes as
$\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu} = \rCon{^\sigma_\mu_\nu} + \mathcal{D}^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}$,
with the distortion $\mathcal{D}^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}$ a rank-three
tensor field that subsumes torsion and non-metricity content
together; in the metric-compatible case adopted throughout
PGT (and \cref{PalatiniDecomposition}), the
distortion reduces to the contortion $K^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}$ of
\cref{ContortionSplit}, and the rank-three matter content is
purely torsion-driven. Every metric-affine model can therefore be
recast as a Riemannian theory of gravity coupled to a rank-three
tensor matter field. Unlike the geometric picture of general
relativity --- which maps cleanly onto a healthy spin-2
graviton~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,lin2019pgt} --- the
metric-affine geometric picture obscures the rank-three
matter content, which inherits the standard
quantum-field-theoretic pathologies typical of high-rank fields:
ghosts and tachyons in the linearised
spectrum~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,lin2019pgt},
and strong-coupling instabilities at non-linear
order~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear}. The physical content is in this
sense insensitive to whether one adopts the geometric or the
matter-field framing, but the matter-field framing makes the
pathologies manifest.}
On a generic linear connection the metric and the connection are
independent, so the covariant derivative of the metric does not
vanish. The resulting tensor is the \emph{non-metricity}
$\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda\mu\nu} \equiv -\rcD{_\lambda}\g{_{\mu\nu}}$~\cite[eq.~(2.5)]{blagojevic2003lectures},
which measures the failure of parallel transport to preserve inner
products (\cref{DiffGeometryObstructions}). The
most general space carrying all three is the
metric-affine space $L_4$ (\cref{MetricAffineClasses})~\cite{hehl1995metric};
demanding that the connection preserves the metric under parallel
transport, equivalently that
$\rcD{_\mu}\g{_{\nu\lambda}} = 0$, is the
\emph{metric-compatibility}
condition~\cite[eq.~(2.5)]{blagojevic2003lectures}, and it sets
$\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda\mu\nu} = 0$ identically. PGT adopts metric compatibility as a defining
requirement~\cite{hehl1976spin,blagojevic2003lectures}, so only
curvature and torsion remain as independent field strengths.

\paragraph*{From field strengths to a gauge-invariant action}
With $\MAGFt{}$ and $\MAGT{}$ as the dynamical gauge fields, the
gravitational action is built from them by the same principle as
any other gauge theory: the free Lagrangian must be a scalar
under local Lorentz and general-coordinate
transformations, built
from the field strengths and their covariant derivatives, and
integrated against an invariant volume element on
spacetime~\cite[eq.~(1.13)]{blagojevic2003lectures,lin2019pgt}.
These scalars are formed by full index
contractions with the metric $\g{_{\mu\nu}}$, the flat-frame
metric $\eta_{ij}$, the tetrad pair, and the totally
antisymmetric tensor $\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$, exactly as
in other gauge field theories: the QED kinetic
action $-\tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$ is the
unique Lorentz scalar quadratic in the field strength, and the
PGT analogue is the set of all independent quadratic contractions
of $\MAGFt{}$ and $\MAGT{}$ with one
another~\cite{barker2024poincare,sezgin1980ghostfree,hehl1995metric}.
The independent invariants per sector are catalogued in
\cref{PGTEnumeration}.

The construction above used the gauge-theoretic field pair
$(b^i{}_\mu, A^{ij}{}_\mu)$, while \cref{Theory} and the
symbolic-computation pipeline (\cref{SymbolicComputation}) both
work in the affine-connection language with the metric and
connection pair $(\g{_{\mu\nu}}, \MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu})$. The
two parameterisations describe the same geometric data, related
object-by-object as in \cref{PGTRiemannCartanTranslation}, after
Blagojevi\'{c}~\cite[Table~1]{blagojevic2003lectures}.

\paragraph*{Spacetime classification and the Palatini formulation}
\label{SpacetimeClassification}
Different combinations of vanishing curvature, torsion, and
non-metricity define the standard hierarchy of geometric arenas
for theories of gravity. \Cref{MetricAffineClasses} shows the
classification: the outermost set is metric-affine
$(L_4, \g{})$, with the three obstructions
$\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{T}, \mathcal{Q}$ generating distinguished
sub-arenas by switching off subsets. PGT
inhabits the Riemann--Cartan space $U_4$ defined by
$\mathcal{Q} = 0$.
\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{figures/metric_affine_classes.pdf}
  \caption{Classification of metric-affine spacetime geometries by
    which of the three obstructions --- curvature $\mathcal{R}$,
    torsion $\mathcal{T}$, and non-metricity $\mathcal{Q}$ --- are
    non-zero. The outer region is the most general metric-affine
    space $(L_4, \g{})$. General relativity inhabits the
    Riemannian sub-arena $V_4$ ($\mathcal{T} = \mathcal{Q} = 0$);
    Poincar\'{e} gauge theory inhabits the Riemann--Cartan space
    $U_4$ ($\mathcal{Q} = 0$); the dual case is Weitzenb\"{o}ck
    teleparallel $W_4$ ($\mathcal{R} = \mathcal{Q} = 0$); setting
    all three obstructions to zero recovers Minkowski
    $\mathbb{M}^4$. Adapted from
    figure~3 of~\cite{bahamonde2021teleparallel}.}
  \label{MetricAffineClasses}
\end{figure}

The tetrad-and-spin-connection presentation just developed is
equivalent to the Palatini-style formulation adopted in
\cref{PGTframework}~\cite{palatini1919deduzione,ferraris1982variational,barker2024palatini},
in which the metric $\g{_{\mu\nu}}$ and the affine connection
$\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ are the independent variational fields.
The bridge between the two formulations is the \emph{tetrad
postulate}~\cite[eq.~(2.9)]{blagojevic2003lectures}. Intuitively,
it enforces that parallel transport is a single geometric
operation independent of whether we describe a tangent vector in
the Lorentz frame or in the coordinate basis: the spin connection
$A^{ij}{}_\mu$ acting on the Lorentz-frame components $u^i$ and
the affine connection $\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ acting on the
coordinate-basis components $u^\mu$ must produce the same
parallel-transported vector when applied to any $u$.
Equivalently, the full covariant derivative of the tetrad
vanishes,
\begin{align}
  \partial_\mu b^i{}_\nu
    + A^i{}_{j\mu}\,b^j{}_\nu
    - \MAGA{^\rho_{\nu\mu}}\,b^i{}_\rho
    &= 0,
  \label{TetradPostulate}
\end{align}
imposing simultaneous covariance under both Latin (Lorentz) and
Greek (coordinate) indices. \Cref{TetradPostulate} fixes a
one-to-one map between $A^{ij}{}_\mu$ and $\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$
once a tetrad is chosen, so the two formulations describe the
same geometric data. Imposing metricity ($\mathcal{Q} = 0$) then
forces the
decomposition~\cite[eq.~(2.6)]{blagojevic2003lectures}
\begin{align}
  \MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu} &= \rCon{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}
    + K^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu},
  \label{ContortionSplit}
\end{align}
which is the same content as \cref{PalatiniDecomposition} written
in spin-connection language $A^{ij}{}_\mu = \Delta^{ij}{}_\mu(b)
+ K^{ij}{}_\mu$~\cite[eq.~(2.12)]{blagojevic2003lectures}. The
Riemannian part of the spin connection is given explicitly in
terms of the tetrad by
\begin{align}
  \Delta_{ij\mu}(b)
    &= \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(c_{ijm} - c_{mij} + c_{jmi}\bigr)\,e^m{}_\mu,
  \label{RiemannianSpinConnection}
\end{align}
where $c^i{}_{\mu\nu} \equiv \partial_\mu e^i{}_\nu
- \partial_\nu e^i{}_\mu$ is the object of anholonomity --- the
spin-connection counterpart of the Christoffel symbols
$\rCon{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$.

\paragraph*{Irreducible torsion decomposition}
\label{IrreducibleTorsionFull}
The torsion tensor $\MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}$ has 24 independent
components --- antisymmetric in its last two indices and
otherwise unconstrained --- which split into three irreducible
representations of the local Lorentz
group~\cite{hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric,shapiro2002torsion,obukhov2024electrodynamics}.
The trace vector
\begin{subequations}
\begin{align}
  \T[2]{_\mu} &\equiv \MAGT{^\lambda_\lambda_\mu}
  \label{TraceVector}
\end{align}
collects four components; the axial pseudovector
\begin{align}
  \T[3]{^\mu} &\equiv \varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\mu}\,
    \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\gamma}}
  \label{AxialVector}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
collects another four, and the totally traceless tensor part
$\T[1]{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}$ collects the remaining sixteen
components, transforming as a single irreducible tensor
representation of the Lorentz group~\cite{hehl1976spin}, and
satisfying the orthogonality conditions
$\T[1]{^\lambda_\lambda_\mu} = 0$ and
$\varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\T[1]{_{\alpha\beta\gamma}} = 0$.
Shapiro's letter labels $T_\mu, S^\mu, q_{\alpha\beta\mu}$~\cite{shapiro2002torsion}
for the same three components appear in the older literature.
The reconstruction formula \cref{IrreducibleTorsion}
expresses the torsion in terms of its irreducible parts.

The torsion-squared sector of the action admits two equivalent
parameterisations. In the irreducible basis it reads $a\,(\T[1]{})^2 +
b\,(\T[3]{})^2 + c\,(\T[2]{})^2$, with $(\T[1]{})^2 \equiv
\T[1]{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}\T[1]{^{\alpha\beta\mu}}$ and the other
squared norms defined analogously; in the direct basis of scalar
contractions of \cref{TorsionDirectBasis} it reads $\beta_1 I_1 +
\beta_2 I_2 + \beta_3 I_3$. Substituting
\cref{IrreducibleTorsion} into the direct-basis
contractions and using the orthogonality of the three irreducible
parts gives the linear
map~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,hehl1995metric,lin2019pgt}
\begin{align}
  \begin{pmatrix} a \\ b \\ c \end{pmatrix}
  &= \begin{pmatrix}
       1 & 1 & 0 \\
       -\tfrac{1}{6} & \tfrac{1}{6} & 0 \\
       \tfrac{2}{3} & \tfrac{1}{3} & 1
     \end{pmatrix}
     \begin{pmatrix} \beta_1 \\ \beta_2 \\ \beta_3 \end{pmatrix},
  \label{DirectIrreducibleTransform}
\end{align}
which is invertible.
