\section{Bayesian inference architecture}%
\label{InferenceArchitecture}

This appendix describes the nested-sampling layer used to survey the
coupling space of each PGT+EM theory studied in this work.  The
amplification $A(\theta)$ returned by the PDE solver at coupling vector
$\theta$ depends on the initial-condition amplitude and integration time
chosen for the simulation, so the absolute values it reports are not
the object of interest.  The survey instead asks structural questions:
which couplings carry a statistically significant correlation with $A$,
and whether the response is concentrated on a narrow parameter region
or spread across the whole theory.  Nested
sampling~\cite{Skilling:2004pqw,Ashton:2022grj} delivers two
diagnostics that quantify this structure: the prior-averaged
amplification $\mathcal{Z} = \langle A\rangle_\pi$, which the algorithm
computes as the Bayesian evidence (\cref{EvidenceIntegral}); and the
Kullback--Leibler divergence $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$
(\cref{KLDivergence}) between the score-reweighted posterior and the
prior, which measures how concentrated the posterior is and so reads
out whether amplification (or its absence) is generic in the theory or
specific to a fine-tuned coupling region.  Both anchor the survey
results of \cref{Results}.

\paragraph*{Amplification score}
The PDE solver returns at each coupling vector $\theta$ a deterministic
conversion probability $\Pmax(\theta)$, the peak photon amplitude
reached during one simulation in the Einstein--Maxwell--torsion
background of the Gertsenshtein kernel \cref{GertsenshteinFormula}.
We work with its amplification over the Einstein--Maxwell baseline,
\begin{equation}
  A(\theta) \;=\; \frac{\Pmax(\theta)}{\sin^2(\kappa\Bzero t/2)},
  \label{AmplificationScore}
\end{equation}
the per-coupling-vector score that drives the survey.\footnote{Distinct
from the asymptotic survey-coefficient ratio $\Aamp$ of
\cref{AmplificationFactor}, which is the $\Bzero\!\to\!0$ limit of the
same comparison; the two agree to $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^2)$ in the
validation regime of \cref{Validation}.}  The nested-sampling
likelihood is
\begin{equation}
  \log L(\theta) \;=\; \pm\log A(\theta),
  \label{NestedScore}
\end{equation}
with the sign chosen to direct the survey toward amplifying ($+$) or
suppressing ($-$) regions of coupling space; a Gaussian variant
targeting a specified value of $\Pmax$ is also available, although the
sign-selecting form is what we use throughout.

\paragraph*{Evidence integral}
Nested sampling targets the Bayesian evidence
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{Z} \;=\; \int L(\theta)\,\pi(\theta)\,\diff\theta,
  \label{EvidenceIntegral}
\end{equation}
the prior-weighted integral of the score over the coupling space.  The
physical content $\mathcal{Z}$ acquires under \cref{NestedScore} is
developed in \cref{EvidencePrediction}.  In a high-dimensional coupling
space the volume where $L$ is appreciably non-zero shrinks to a small
corner of the prior, and a flat Monte-Carlo estimate of
\cref{EvidenceIntegral} wastes most of its samples on uninformative
regions.  Skilling~\cite{Skilling:2004pqw} reformulates the integral
as a one-dimensional integral over prior volume and samples along
likelihood contours rather than across the parameter space, producing
posterior-weighted samples as a by-product of the evidence estimate.

\paragraph*{PolyChord}
We use PolyChord~\cite{Handley:2015fda,Handley:2015vkr} as the
nested sampler throughout this work.  Its replacement step generates
each new sample by slice sampling along $\mathcal{O}(d)$ random
directions of the unit hypercube, giving a per-replacement cost that
scales as $\mathcal{O}(d^2)$ in the dimensionality $d$ of the coupling
vector.  The four-to-eight-dimensional coupling spaces we survey sit
comfortably within this regime.  The shape of the prior $\pi(\theta)$
is carried into the algorithm by a prior-transform map from the unit
cube to coupling space, the construction of which we describe next.

\paragraph*{Priors}
Each scalar coupling is sampled from one of two families.  Log-uniform
priors are used for positive-definite kinetic and mass-like
coefficients that span several decades, giving each decade of magnitude
equal prior weight.  Arctan-uniform priors are used for dimensionless
couplings whose sign is not fixed a priori; they map the full real line
onto a bounded sampling space with a density peaked at zero.  Bounds
for each campaign's priors are listed in \cref{Results}.  Since $\mathcal{Z}$
weights $A$ by the prior~\cref{EvidenceIntegral}, the choice of prior
shape sets what $\mathcal{Z}$ means.  A prior concentrated on
amplifying couplings inflates it; a prior that ranges widely across
non-amplifying background dilutes it.  Joint priors over multiple
couplings are usually a product of the per-coupling priors above; the
exception, used when magnitude and direction in a coupling subspace
should be decoupled, is the cubed-sphere construction described next.

\paragraph*{Cubed-sphere joint prior}
A coupling vector $\boldsymbol c \in \mathbb{R}^N$ can be decomposed as
$\boldsymbol c = r\,\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}$ with $r>0$ drawn from a
log-uniform prior and $\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}$ drawn uniformly on the
unit sphere $S^{N-1}$.  Direct parametrisation of $S^{N-1}$ by
latitude--longitude angles suffers polar crowding, so we sample
$\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}$ instead via a gnomonic cubed-sphere atlas:
the $N$-cube is wrapped around the origin and each face is radially
projected onto $S^{N-1}$, yielding a quasi-rectilinear angular grid
without polar singularities~(\cref{CubedSphereAtlas}).  Each face
partitions further into sub-tiles whose constrained-prior chains run
independently and are pooled into the joint $\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}$
distribution at the end.\footnote{The atlas used here is adapted from
W.~E.~V.~Barker's unreleased extension of the \textsc{psalter}
package~\cite{barker2024psalter,barker2025psalter2}, shared with this
project for implementation guidance.  The underlying gnomonic
projection is the standard cubed-sphere construction of Ronchi,
Iacono and Paolucci (J.~Comp.~Phys.~124, 93--114, 1996).}
Decoupling magnitude from direction lets a single decade-spanning
radial axis coexist with a compact angular grid; the prior weight
along $r$ and along $\hat{\boldsymbol\theta}$ is set independently and
on physical grounds.

\paragraph*{Evidence as a prior expectation}
With $L(\theta) = A(\theta)$, the evidence integral
\cref{EvidenceIntegral} reduces to a prior expectation,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{Z} \;=\; \int A(\theta)\,\pi(\theta)\,\diff\theta
  \;=\; \langle A\rangle_\pi,
  \label{EvidencePrediction}
\end{equation}
the average amplification of the Gertsenshtein conversion over the
coupling prior.  A theory with $\log\mathcal{Z}\approx 0$ is
General-Relativistic on average across the prior.  A theory with
$\log\mathcal{Z}\gg 0$ has typical coupling vectors that amplify; one
with $\log\mathcal{Z}\ll 0$ has typical coupling vectors that suppress.
$\mathcal{Z}$ here is not a Bayes factor in the conventional sense.
We are not comparing two models against the same observed dataset, so
the Jeffreys-scale interpretation of $\log B_{12}$ does not apply.
The prior dependence is intrinsic to the measurement: the prior fixes
which coupling values the theory considers physically reasonable, and
$\mathcal{Z}$ asks whether amplification occurs within that range.  The
evidence does not by itself say whether the amplification is generic
across the prior or concentrated on a specific coupling region; that
distinction is the role of the divergence introduced next.

\paragraph*{Posterior contraction}
The nested-sampling chain returns samples from the score-weighted
posterior
\begin{equation}
  p(\theta\mid A) \;\propto\; A(\theta)\,\pi(\theta),
  \label{ScorePosterior}
\end{equation}
the reweighting of the prior by the amplification score.  Its
information content relative to the prior is the Kullback--Leibler
divergence
\begin{equation}
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p(\theta\mid A)\,\|\,\pi(\theta)\big)
  \;=\; \int p(\theta\mid A)\,
  \log\!\frac{p(\theta\mid A)}{\pi(\theta)}\,\diff\theta,
  \label{KLDivergence}
\end{equation}
measured in nats.  When $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ is small the score-weighted
posterior is indistinguishable from the prior and the result is
generic to the theory across the surveyed range; when $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$
is large the posterior has contracted onto a specific coupling region
and the result is parameter-specific.  Taken with $\mathcal{Z}$, these
two diagnostics read the survey jointly: $\mathcal{Z}$ records the
magnitude of the prior-averaged response, and $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ records
whether that response is diffuse or concentrated.  We additionally
extract the per-coupling marginal $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ from the chain
output with anesthetic~\cite{Handley:2019mfs}, identifying which
individual couplings carry the signal.  Campaign values for
$\mathcal{Z}$, $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$, and the marginal $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$
are reported in \cref{Results}.

\paragraph*{Validity gates}
The amplification score \cref{AmplificationScore} is meaningful only
inside the linearised regime in which the PDE solver operates, set by
the two conditions $\Pmax \ll 1$ and $\kappa\Bzero t \ll 1$ that keep
the Gertsenshtein baseline in its small-angle limit.  Outside this
regime, $A$ acquires a spurious $\Bzero$ dependence and the linearised
conversion measurement breaks down~(\cref{PerturbativeRegime}).  Two
physics gates encode this in the inference layer: a tachyonic-instability
gate that excludes coupling vectors with positive-real-part
eigenvalues, since these signal a propagating-mode pathology upstream
of the inference question; and a perturbativity flag at $\Pmax > 0.5$
marking samples that may lie outside the validity window of
\cref{HwangNohBound}, derived in App~G from the Hwang--Noh
backreaction analysis~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton}.
