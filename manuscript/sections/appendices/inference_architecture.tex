\section{Bayesian inference architecture}%
\label{InferenceArchitecture}

The inference layer producing the survey results of
\cref{Results} explores the high-dimensional PGT+EM coupling space
catalogued in \cref{PGTEnumeration}, where many equivalent
parametrisations appear in the
literature~\cite{barker2024poincare,lin2019pgt,karananas2014parity,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,bahamonde2025cubic},
and each coefficient is an arbitrary, parametrisation-dependent
grouping of the underlying interactions rather than a single
fundamental quantity.  We use PolyChord~\cite{Handley:2015fda,Handley:2015vkr} for the nested
sampling~\cite{Skilling:2006gxv,Feroz:2008xx,Brewer:2010,Brewer:2016scw,Speagle:2019ivv},
with anesthetic~\cite{Handley:2019mfs} for the posterior visualisation.
%  as corner plots and as per-tile panel
% maps over the atlas of \cref{CubedSphereAtlas}. 
We obtain the prior-averaged amplification $\mathcal{Z}$
(\cref{EvidenceIntegral}) and the Kullback--Leibler divergence
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}$~\cite{Kullback:1951zyt,Handley:2019mfs}, which
measures how tightly the amplification is concentrated onto
particular couplings. We map where in coupling
space the Gertsenshtein conversion is most strongly amplified, and
which combinations of invariants drive it. For further detail on
Bayesian inference and nested sampling, see~\cite{MacKay:2003,Hobson:2010,Trotta:2017wnx,Sivia:2006,Ashton:2022grj}.

\paragraph*{Amplification score}
The PDE solver~(\cref{NumericalEvolution}) evolves the system
for an integration time $t_\mathrm{end}$ and returns
the exit conversion probability $P(\theta, t_\mathrm{end})$ at each
coupling vector $\theta$ (the parametrisation of
\cref{PGTEnumeration}).  The score function for the nested sampler
is the amplification of this exit value over its Einstein--Maxwell
baseline~\cref{GertsenshteinFormula},
\begin{equation}
  A(\theta) \;=\; \frac{P(\theta, t_\mathrm{end})}{\sin^2(\kappa\Bzero t_\mathrm{end}/2)},
  \label{AmplificationScore}
\end{equation}
with the baseline shown for the uniform-magnetic-background setup
of \cref{Results}; for a localised-$\Bzero$ configuration we
use the path-integrated form
\cref{BoccalettiPathIntegrated}.\footnote{$A(\theta)$ and the asymptotic
survey-coefficient ratio $\Aamp$ of \cref{AmplificationFactor} are
different objects: $A(\theta)$ is the actual finite-time exit ratio
at the simulation's $t_\mathrm{end}$, while $\Aamp$ is the
leading-order Taylor coefficient extracted in the small-$\kappa\Bzero
t$ limit.  The two agree to leading order whenever the perturbative regime
$\kappa\Bzero t \ll 1$ holds --- as in the uniform-background,
perturbative-$\Pmax$ regime of this work --- and
diverge whenever the wave has time to accumulate non-leading-order
behaviour, as can happen once the localised-$\Bzero$ extensions
introduce a finite interaction-region traversal time.}  The
nested-sampling likelihood is
\begin{equation}
  \log L(\theta) \;=\; \pm\log A(\theta),
  \label{NestedScore}
\end{equation}
with the sign chosen to direct the survey toward amplifying ($+$)
or suppressing ($-$) regions of coupling space.

\paragraph*{Theory-agnostic acceptance of exponentially-growing modes}
The modal-solver evolution that produces $P(\theta, t_\mathrm{end})$
does not pre-filter exponentially-growing modes from the linearised
system: whatever modes the input Lagrangian propagates are integrated
through and contribute to the exit conversion probability. Earlier
iterations of the pipeline truncated such modes on the assumption
that exponential growth always indicates an unphysical pathology, but
this choice is not theory-agnostic --- a numerical pipeline cannot in
general decide whether exponential linearised growth is a genuine
amplification mechanism, a non-ghost tachyonic instability of an
unbounded background ansatz, or a wrong-sign-kinetic ghost. The
distinction depends on the specific term subset and parameter values
in the input theory and is the subject of an extensive
literature~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,lin2019pgt,Yo:1999ex,Yo:2001sy}.
We therefore report what the input Lagrangian produces and defer
the discrimination of physical amplification from instability
artefacts to (i) the finite-extent geometry of \cref{Discussion},
which saturates any genuine instability while preserving channel
mixing, and (ii) the full Hamiltonian particle-spectrum analysis
provided by the PSALTer
tool~\cite{barker2024psalter,barker2025psalter2}, integration with
which we identify as future work.

\paragraph*{Cubed-sphere joint prior}
We decompose a coupling vector $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^N$ as
$\theta = r\,\hat n$, sampling magnitude and direction separately.  The magnitude $r>0$ sets how far the theory
sits from the Einstein--Maxwell base theory: it is the overall
strength of the couplings beyond it, treated as perturbative
corrections in the effective-field-theory
regime~(\cref{PerturbativeReduction}).  The unit vector
$\hat n \in S^{N-1}$ gives the direction in the parametrisation of
\cref{PGTEnumeration}.  The survey question becomes which direction
$\hat n$ at fixed $r$ most strongly amplifies the Gertsenshtein
conversion.  We sample $\hat n$
uniformly on
$S^{N-1}$ via a gnomonic cubed-sphere atlas: the $N$-cube wrapped
around the origin is radially projected onto $S^{N-1}$, yielding a
quasi-rectilinear angular grid without polar
singularities~(\cref{CubedSphereAtlas}).  Each face is subdivided
into sub-tile cells, each sampled by its own independent
constrained-prior chain.  The subdivision fans the survey out into
independent parallel runs, and because each chain explores only its
own cell, a narrow amplifying direction within that cell is
resolved rather than under-sampled across a much larger prior.  The
cells of a face are then combined into that face's panel in the
atlas, and all cells together form the joint $\hat n$ distribution.
In the limit of fine sub-tile resolution
each tile becomes locally rectilinear and the chart approaches a
flat Cartesian one on the sphere, so the construction interpolates
smoothly between a coarse global parametrisation of $S^{N-1}$ and
the local-flat limit familiar from Cartesian coupling-space
sampling.\footnote{The atlas used here is adapted from
W.~E.~V.~Barker's unreleased extension of the \textsc{psalter}
package~\cite{barker2024psalter,barker2025psalter2}, shared with this
project for implementation guidance.  The underlying gnomonic
projection is the standard cubed-sphere construction
of~\cite{ronchi1996cubedsphere}.}

\paragraph*{Bayesian evidence}
With $L(\theta) = A(\theta)$ from \cref{NestedScore}, the Bayesian
evidence targeted by the nested sampler is a prior expectation over
the amplification score,
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{Z} \;=\; \int A(\theta)\,\pi(\theta)\,\diff\theta
  \;=\; \langle A\rangle_\pi,
  \label{EvidenceIntegral}
\end{equation}
the average amplification of the Gertsenshtein conversion over the
coupling prior.  A theory with $\log\mathcal{Z}\approx 0$ offers no
amplification on average across the prior; a theory with
$\log\mathcal{Z}\gg 0$ has typical coupling vectors that amplify the
conversion above its Einstein--Maxwell baseline; one with
$\log\mathcal{Z}\ll 0$ has $\langle A\rangle_\pi \ll 1$.  The
magnitude of $\log\mathcal{Z}$ is sensitive to the choice of prior
support, whereas its sign, and the ordering of $\log\mathcal{Z}$
across theories, are not; these are what the survey draws on,
together with $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$, introduced next.

\paragraph*{Posterior contraction}
Nested sampling produces samples from the score-reweighted posterior
\begin{equation}
  p(\theta\mid A) \;\propto\; A(\theta)\,\pi(\theta),
  \label{ScorePosterior}
\end{equation}
the reweighting of the prior by the amplification score.  Its
information content relative to the prior is the Kullback--Leibler
divergence $D_{\mathrm{KL}}\big(p(\theta\mid A)\,\|\,\pi(\theta)\big)$:
\begin{equation}
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}
  \;=\; \int p(\theta\mid A)\,
  \log\!\frac{p(\theta\mid A)}{\pi(\theta)}\,\diff\theta = \Big\langle\log \frac{p}{\pi}\Big\rangle_{p},
  \label{KLDivergence}
\end{equation}
measured in nats.  Small $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ means the posterior is
indistinguishable from the prior and the response is generic to the
theory; large $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ means the
posterior has contracted onto a specific region and the
response is parameter-specific.  When $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ is large at
small $\mathcal{Z}$ the theory is generically non-amplifying over the
surveyed prior while a small region of coupling space \emph{is}
amplifying. The per-coupling
marginal $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ extracted with
anesthetic~\cite{Handley:2019mfs} then picks out which invariants
the chain has contracted along, telling us not just \emph{that} a
region of coupling space carries the response but \emph{which
directions in operator space} it spans.  Values of
$\mathcal{Z}$, $D_{\mathrm{KL}}$, and the marginal
$D_{\mathrm{KL}}$ for each surveyed theory are reported in
\cref{Results}.

\paragraph*{Information gain and per-coupling $D_\mathrm{KL}$}%
\label{KLMethods}
The per-coupling marginal $D_\mathrm{KL}$ extracted with
anesthetic~\cite{Handley:2019mfs} resolves the joint contraction of
\cref{KLDivergence} onto each coupling $\theta_i$. Writing $P_\pm$
for the posteriors of the two sign choices in \cref{NestedScore}
and $P_\pm(\theta_i)$ for their marginals,
\begin{align}
  D_{\mathrm{KL},\pm}(\theta_i) &\;\equiv\;
    D_\mathrm{KL}\!\big(P_\pm(\theta_i)\,\|\,\pi(\theta_i)\big),
    \notag\\
  D_\mathrm{KL}(P_+\|P_-)(\theta_i) &\;\equiv\;
    D_\mathrm{KL}\!\big(P_+(\theta_i)\,\|\,P_-(\theta_i)\big).
  \label{PerCouplingKL}
\end{align}
The two marginals measure the per-coupling compression from the
prior to the amplification and suppression posteriors; the cross
divergence measures how the two posteriors differ in $\theta_i$,
isolating the couplings that distinguish amplification from
suppression independently of the prior baseline. The per-theory
tables that follow rank the top-$k$ couplings by these quantities,
identifying the carrier operators in each surveyed sector.
