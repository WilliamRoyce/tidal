\section{Coupling-space exploration with nested sampling}%
\label{InferenceArchitecture}

This appendix documents how we explore the PGT+EM coupling space
catalogued in~\cref{PGTEnumeration} for regions of strong
Gertsenshtein conversion. Many equivalent parametrisations of that
space appear in the
literature~\cite{barker2024poincare,lin2019pgt,karananas2014parity,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,bahamonde2025cubic};
each coefficient is an arbitrary, parametrisation-dependent
combination of the underlying interactions rather than a fundamental
quantity. We sample with nested sampling~\cite{Skilling:2006gxv,Feroz:2008xx,Brewer:2010,Brewer:2016scw,Speagle:2019ivv}
as implemented in PolyChord~\cite{Handley:2015fda,Handley:2015vkr},
applied here as a coupling-space exploration tool driven by the
synthetic score $A(\theta)$ of~\cref{AmplificationScore} rather
than by a data-derived likelihood. Nested sampling on a synthetic
or composite score for parameter-space search is established in
both the statistical-mechanics
literature~\cite{Partay:2021materials,Martiniani:2014sens,Burkoff:2012protein},
where it maps potential-energy surfaces, and in
beyond-the-Standard-Model phenomenology and cosmological parameter
estimation~\cite{GAMBIT:2017zdo,Giani:2023gfq}, where it scans
constraint-satisfaction regions over high-dimensional coupling
spaces; the field survey of~\cite{Ashton:2022grj} discusses both.
PolyChord delivers samples from the score-reweighted distribution
$p(\theta\mid A) \propto A(\theta)\,\pi(\theta)$, visualised with
anesthetic~\cite{Handley:2019mfs} as corner-plot 2D marginals over
each operator pair; from those same samples anesthetic computes the
per-coupling marginal information gain $\D$
\cite{Kullback:1951zyt,Handley:2019mfs} ranking the operator axes
by how narrowly the amplifying region projects onto each individual
coupling axis.
The corner-plot projections and per-coupling $\D$ rankings are the
two descriptors the results section reports. For further detail on
nested sampling generally, see~\cite{MacKay:2003,Hobson:2010,Trotta:2017wnx,Sivia:2006}.

\paragraph*{Amplification score}
The PDE solver~(\cref{NumericalEvolution}) evolves the system
for an integration time $t_\mathrm{end}$ and returns
the exit conversion probability $P(\theta, t_\mathrm{end})$ at each
coupling vector $\theta$ (the parametrisation of
\cref{PGTEnumeration}).  The score function for the nested sampler
is the amplification of this exit value over its Einstein--Maxwell
baseline~\cref{GertsenshteinFormula},
\begin{equation}
  A(\theta) \;=\; \frac{P(\theta, t_\mathrm{end})}{\sin^2(\kappa\Bzero t_\mathrm{end}/2)},
  \label{AmplificationScore}
\end{equation}
with the baseline shown for the uniform-magnetic-background setup
of~\cref{Results}; for a localised-$\Bzero$ configuration we
use the path-integrated form
\cref{PathIntegratedFormula}.\footnote{$A(\theta)$ and the asymptotic
survey-coefficient ratio $\Aamp$ of~\cref{AmplificationFactor} are
different objects: $A(\theta)$ is the actual finite-time exit ratio
at the simulation's $t_\mathrm{end}$, while $\Aamp$ is the
leading-order Taylor coefficient extracted in the small-$\kappa\Bzero
t$ limit.  The two agree to leading order whenever the perturbative regime
$\kappa\Bzero t \ll 1$ holds --- as in the uniform-background,
perturbative-$\Pmax$ regime of this work --- and
diverge whenever the wave has time to accumulate non-leading-order
behaviour, as can happen once the localised-$\Bzero$ extensions
introduce a finite interaction-region traversal time.}  The
nested-sampling likelihood is
\begin{equation}
  \log L(\theta) \;=\; \pm\log A(\theta),
  \label{NestedScore}
\end{equation}
with the sign chosen to direct the survey toward amplifying ($+$)
or suppressing ($-$) regions of coupling space.

\paragraph*{Theory-agnostic acceptance of exponentially-growing modes}
The modal-solver evolution that produces $P(\theta, t_\mathrm{end})$
does not pre-filter exponentially-growing modes from the linearised
system: whatever modes the input Lagrangian propagates are integrated
through and contribute to the exit conversion probability. Earlier
iterations of the implementation truncated such modes on the assumption
that exponential growth always indicates an unphysical pathology, but
this choice is not theory-agnostic --- a numerical framework cannot in
general decide whether exponential linearised growth is a genuine
amplification mechanism, a non-ghost tachyonic instability of an
unbounded background ansatz, or a wrong-sign-kinetic ghost. The
distinction depends on the specific term subset and parameter values
in the input theory and is the subject of an extensive
literature~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,lin2019pgt,Yo:1999ex,Yo:2001sy}.
We therefore report what the input Lagrangian produces and defer
the discrimination of physical amplification from instability
artefacts to (i) the finite-extent geometry of~\cref{Discussion},
which saturates any genuine instability while preserving channel
mixing, and (ii) the full Lagrangian-level particle-spectrum analysis
provided by the \PSALTer
tool~\cite{barker2024psalter,barker2025psalter2}, integration with
which we identify as future work.

\paragraph*{Prior-averaged amplification}
With $L(\theta) = A(\theta)$ from~\cref{NestedScore}, PolyChord
estimates the prior-averaged amplification
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{Z} \;=\; \int A(\theta)\,\pi(\theta)\,\diff\theta
  \;=\; \langle A\rangle_\pi,
  \label{EvidenceIntegral}
\end{equation}
the average amplification of the Gertsenshtein conversion over the
coupling prior. In standard Bayesian usage $\log\mathcal{Z}$ is the
marginal likelihood that drives model comparison. For the present
synthetic score it is neither cross-truncation comparable (each
truncation has its own prior support and parametrisation) nor
stable on the exponentially-growing-mode sectors
of~\cref{ParityOddGeomIncomp,XiKineticClosure} (where $A(\theta)$
depends on $t_\mathrm{end}$). We therefore do not quote
$\log\mathcal{Z}$ or its directional difference
$\log B = \log\mathcal{Z}_+ - \log\mathcal{Z}_-$ in the results
prose; the corner-plot projections and per-coupling
information-gain rankings of~\cref{Results} are what the survey
reports.

\paragraph*{Total information gain $\D$}
The Kullback--Leibler divergence of the score-reweighted
distribution $p(\theta\mid A)\propto A(\theta)\,\pi(\theta)$ from
the prior,
\begin{equation}
  D_{\mathrm{KL}}
  \;=\; \int p(\theta\mid A)\,
  \log\!\frac{p(\theta\mid A)}{\pi(\theta)}\,\diff\theta = \Big\langle\log \frac{p}{\pi}\Big\rangle_{p},
  \label{KLDivergence}
\end{equation}
in nats, summarises how sharply the score-reweighted distribution
has contracted from the prior. Small $\D$ means the
score-reweighted distribution is close to the prior --- the
response is generic over coupling space; large $\D$ means the
response concentrates on a specific subset of the prior support.
Its absolute value depends on the prior support of each truncation
and on the simulation horizon $t_\mathrm{end}$ when the score grows
multi-modally and exponentially, so $\D$ is not a cross-truncation
comparator. The operator-resolved per-coupling marginal $\D$
of the next paragraph is the descriptor the results prose leans on.

\paragraph*{Per-coupling information gain}
The per-coupling marginal $\D$ extracted with
anesthetic~\cite{Handley:2019mfs} resolves the joint contraction of
\cref{KLDivergence} onto each coupling $\theta_i$. Writing $P_\pm$
for the posteriors of the two sign choices in~\cref{NestedScore}
and $P_\pm(\theta_i)$ for their marginals,
\begin{align}
  D_{\mathrm{KL},\pm}(\theta_i) &\;\equiv\;
    \D\!\big(P_\pm(\theta_i)\,\|\,\pi(\theta_i)\big),
    \notag\\
  \D(P_+\|P_-)(\theta_i) &\;\equiv\;
    \D\!\big(P_+(\theta_i)\,\|\,P_-(\theta_i)\big).
  \label{PerCouplingKL}
\end{align}
The two marginals measure the per-coupling compression from the
prior to the amplification and suppression posteriors; the cross
divergence measures how the two posteriors differ in $\theta_i$,
isolating the couplings that distinguish amplification from
suppression independently of the prior baseline.

\paragraph*{Cubed-sphere joint prior}
We decompose a coupling vector $\theta \in \mathbb{R}^N$ as
$\theta = r\,\hat n$, with magnitude $r > 0$ and unit vector
$\hat n \in S^{N-1}$ giving the direction in the parametrisation
of \cref{PGTEnumeration}. We sample $\hat n$ uniformly on $S^{N-1}$
via a gnomonic cubed-sphere atlas: the $N$-cube wrapped around the
origin is radially projected onto $S^{N-1}$, yielding a
quasi-rectilinear angular grid without polar singularities
(\cref{CubedSphereAtlas}). Each face is subdivided into sub-tile
cells sampled by independent constrained-prior chains in parallel,
with the cells of a face combining into that face's panel of the
atlas. In the limit of fine sub-tile resolution each tile becomes
locally rectilinear and the chart approaches a flat Cartesian one
on the sphere, so the construction interpolates smoothly between a
coarse global parametrisation of $S^{N-1}$ and the local-flat
limit familiar from Cartesian coupling-space
sampling.\footnote{The atlas used here is adapted from
W.~E.~V.~Barker's unreleased extension of the \PSALTer
package~\cite{barker2024psalter,barker2025psalter2}. The underlying gnomonic
projection is the standard cubed-sphere construction
of~\cite{ronchi1996cubedsphere}.}

\paragraph*{Direction at fixed magnitude}
The amplification landscape is the dependence of $A(\theta)$ on
the direction $\hat n \in S^{N-1}$ at fixed magnitude $r$, and the
decomposition $\theta = r\,\hat n$ exposes it directly: a
per-coupling product prior --- uniform on each $\theta_i$
independently --- mixes direction and magnitude in its $\theta_i$
marginals, so any narrow amplifying ridge along a direction
misaligned with the coordinate axes is buried in the
higher-dimensional joint. Holding $r$ small restricts the survey
to the perturbative regime $|\theta| \ll 1$ within which the
linearised PDE underlying $P(\theta, t_\mathrm{end})$ is valid
(\cref{PerturbativeReduction}); the magnitude dependence --- at
what overall coupling strength the amplification turns on --- we
defer to a separate study. We take the prior on $\hat n$ to be
uniform on $S^{N-1}$ and sample it via the gnomonic cubed-sphere
chart of \cref{CubedSphereAtlas}: spherical polar coordinates
carry pole singularities, and uniform Cartesian sampling on
$\mathbb{R}^N$ over-samples large-$|\theta|$ regions.
\cref{AtlasMethodologyYMPGT} shows the per-axis posteriors for the
YM--PGT sector of \cref{NonMinimalLagrangian} ($N = 8$ BSM
couplings) with the positive- ($\uparrow$) and negative-
($\downarrow$) sign faces of each axis overlaid in one panel ---
positive in magenta, negative in yellow, following the convention
of the results-section corner plots. Each panel is the
lower-triangle 2D kernel density of the face-local cube
coordinates $\theta_1, \ldots, \theta_{N-1} \in [-1, 1]^{N-1}$ with
the diagonal carrying the 1D marginal of each $\theta_i$; $\theta_i$
refers to a different physical coupling on each panel because the
dominant axis pinned at $\pm 1$ by the gnomonic projection cycles
through the eight couplings.
