\section{Symbolic computation of the equations of motion}%
\label{SymbolicComputation}

From a Lagrangian, a background ansatz, and a parameter set, the
symbolic computation produces a JSON specification of the linearised
field equations and the Hamiltonian density.
The high-level architecture is shown in \cref{ArchitectureDiagram},
and the equation outputs for the surveyed theories are collected in
\cref{SymbolicOutputs}.
Every partial differential equation evolved by the numerical solver of
\cref{NumericalEvolution} is the output of the symbolic stage; for
higher-curvature theories, an additional perturbative-reduction stage
described in \cref{PerturbativeReduction} precedes the variation.

\paragraph*{xAct environment}
The symbolic stage runs in \Mathematica{}~\cite{mathematica} on the
\xAct{}~\cite{martingarcia2007xact} suite, with
\xPert{}~\cite{brizuela2009xpert} providing the perturbation-theory
layer and \xTras{}~\cite{nutma2014xtras} the Einstein--Cartan
operators used throughout the PGT theories of \cref{PGTEnumeration}.
The manifold, metric, Levi-Civita and Riemann--Cartan covariant
derivatives, and the torsion tensor are declared once at session start;
the Christoffel symbols, the inverse metric, and the Levi-Civita
pseudotensor $\sqrt{-g}\,\varepsilon$ are computed once and cached
against the declared metric.

\paragraph*{Lagrangian input from TOML}
The theory is specified by a single TOML file, which isolates
everything theory-dependent from the derivation so the same code can
serve any quadratic Lagrangian without per-theory editing.
The TOML declares the spacetime (dimension, signature, coordinate
names); the dynamical fields, with their tensor type and index
symmetries; any algebraically-derived tensors such as the
electromagnetic field strength $\FieldF{_\mu_\nu}$; the background
fields; the parameter symbol set; the Lagrangian expression in
\xAct{} syntax; and optional blocks for gauge fixing, linearisation,
and perturbative reduction.
A representative theory file is shown in \cref{TOMLExample}.
The symbolic stage is the map
\begin{align*}
  \textsc{TOML} \bigl[\lag,\,\{\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}_n\}_{n=1}^{N},
                     \,\{p_a\}_{a=1}^{P}\bigr]
  \longmapsto
  \textsc{JSON} \bigl[\{\textsf{EOM}^{\mathcal{I}}_n\}_{n=1}^{N},
                     \,\ham\bigr],
\end{align*}
with $n$ labelling the $N$ dynamical fields, $a$ labelling the $P$
scalar parameters, and $\mathcal{I}$ standing for each field's
suppressed tensor indices (a convention used throughout this
appendix); the internal stages occupy the rest of this appendix.

\paragraph*{Linearisation around backgrounds}
The software implements the background-and-perturbation decomposition
of \cref{Linearisation} by expanding the action to second order in the
perturbations using \xPert{}~\cite{brizuela2009xpert}, with the metric
and any declared matter fields perturbed simultaneously.

Each dynamical field is declared with its own
\texttt{DefTensorPerturbation} call (the metric carries one through
\texttt{SetupMetricPerturbation}), and the perturbation expansion is a
single operation applying \texttt{Perturbation} of
order two to $\sqrt{-g}\,\lag$, producing every bilinear product of
first-order perturbations.
Each matter field $\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$ declared in the
matter-perturbations block of \cref{TOMLExample} yields
\begin{align}
  g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + \varepsilon\,h_{\mu\nu},
  \qquad
  \varphi^{\mathcal{I}} = \bar{\varphi}^{\mathcal{I}}
                          + \varepsilon\,\delta\varphi^{\mathcal{I}},
  \label{MultiFieldPerturbation}
\end{align}
\cref{MultiFieldPerturbation} is the symbolic-stage reading of
\cref{PerturbationAnsatz}.
The background-fields have their component values fixed by
\texttt{xCoba}'s \texttt{ComponentValue} declarations, \eg
the uniform magnetic background
$\bar{A}_y = -\Bzero\,z$ of \cref{BackgroundPotential}.%
\footnote{Antisymmetric derived tensors whose
definition references a perturbation field --- most prominently
$\FieldF{_\mu_\nu} = \rcD{_\mu}\FieldA{_\nu} - \rcD{_\nu}\FieldA{_\mu}$
when $\FieldA{}$ is itself a matter perturbation --- must remain
abstract through the perturbation expansion and be substituted only
afterwards. Without this deferral, the canonicalisation step that
follows collapses the antisymmetric kinetic structure to zero before
perturbation indices are assigned, and the photon equations emerge as
non-dynamical constraints. The software detects deferral candidates
automatically from the TOML symmetry annotation and threads them
through the expansion as abstract tensors with their own
\texttt{DefTensorPerturbation} registration.}

The resulting $\lag^{(2)}$, returned by \texttt{ExpandPerturbation},
contains two kinds of terms: products of first-order perturbations
(the \textsf{LI}[1] symbols of \xPert{}), which constitute the kinetic
and coupling structure of the linearised theory, and linear couplings
to isolated second-order perturbation tensors (the \textsf{LI}[2]
symbols, parametrising the $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{2})$ corrections
to the fields themselves) which are dropped via the
substitution rule $\textsf{LI}[2] \mapsto 0$.
% The first kind we keep verbatim; the second we drop via the
% substitution rule $\textsf{LI}[2] \mapsto 0$, since the linearised
% equations of motion are obtained by variation with respect to
% first-order fields and are unaffected by the \textsf{LI}[2] content.
The retained
\begin{equation*}
  \lag^{(2)}
  =
  \underset{\textsf{LI}[2]\,\mapsto\,0}{\textsf{ExpandPerturbation}} \Bigl[
    \textsf{Perturbation} \bigl[\sqrt{-g}\,\lag,\,2 \bigr]
  \Bigr]
  % \label{QuadraticExpansion}
\end{equation*}
is the linearised action of the multi-field system.

The remaining abstract covariant derivatives of background tensors are
resolved against the \texttt{ComponentValue} declarations during the
chart conversion.
On the flat Minkowski background of \cref{Linearisation} the
Christoffel symbols vanish and the background carries no torsion, so
the covariant derivative reduces to the partial; substituting the
uniform background \cref{BackgroundPotential} then gives
$\PD{_z}\bar{A}_y = -\Bzero$.
A localised background gives a coordinate-dependent expression that
enters the JSON specification as a position-dependent coefficient.

\paragraph*{Euler--Lagrange derivation}
The field equation for every dynamical perturbation is obtained by
variational differentiation of the quadratic action $\lag^{(2)}$
above: for fields $\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$
of arbitrary tensor type, the equation of motion is
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \lag^{(2)}}{\partial \varphi^{\mathcal{I}}}
  \;-\; \rcD{_\mu} \frac{\partial \lag^{(2)}}{\partial(\rcD{_\mu}\varphi^{\mathcal{I}})}
  \;+\; \rcD{_\mu}\rcD{_\nu} \frac{\partial \lag^{(2)}}{\partial(\rcD{_\mu}\rcD{_\nu}\varphi^{\mathcal{I}})}
  \;-\; \cdots \;=\; 0,
  \label{EulerLagrange}
\end{equation}
where the series terminates at the highest derivative order present in
$\lag^{(2)}$.
The differentiation is performed by \xAct{}'s
\texttt{VarD}~\cite{martingarcia2007xact}, which handles the chain rule
and integration-by-parts for arbitrary tensor structure and arbitrary
derivative chains, and returns the equation in abstract-index form
before any component reduction.

The equations are kept in second-order velocity form, with explicit
velocity slots $v^{\mathcal{I}} = \PD{_t}\varphi^{\mathcal{I}}$
alongside the fields; rationale and consequences for solver dispatch
are discussed in \cref{NumericalEvolution}.
Higher-time-derivative Lagrangians arising from small-parameter
expansions are handled by a perturbative substitution before the
variation, discussed with its scope and limitations in
\cref{PerturbativeReduction}.

\paragraph*{Legendre transform and Hamiltonian density}
The Hamiltonian density is the Legendre transform of $\lag^{(2)}$
with respect to the velocity slots $v^{\mathcal{I}}_n$,
\begin{equation}
  \ham^{(2)}
  \;\equiv\;
  \sum_n \pi^{\mathcal{I}}_n\,v^{\mathcal{I}}_n \;-\; \lag^{(2)},
  \label{LegendreTransform}
\end{equation}
where $\pi^{\mathcal{I}}_n \equiv \partial\lag^{(2)}/\partial v^{\mathcal{I}}_n$
are the canonical momenta.
The circumstances under which the transform is admissible, and the
perturbative-reduction procedure that makes it available for
higher-curvature theories, are the subject of \cref{ConstraintBarrier}.

\paragraph*{Component decomposition}
The abstract-index equations returned by \texttt{VarD} are reduced to
scalar component equations on the declared chart before numerical
evaluation.
Four operations are performed in sequence: the metric is separated
from any contracted indices; the expression is converted to chart
components by \texttt{ToBasis}, which projects an abstract-index
tensor expression onto the chart's coordinate basis,
\begin{equation*}
  \textsf{ToBasis} \bigl[T_{\mu_1\cdots\mu_n}\bigr]
  \;=\; T_{i_1\cdots i_n}\,
        \mathrm{d}x^{i_1} \otimes \cdots \otimes \mathrm{d}x^{i_n},
  % \label{ToBasisProjection}
\end{equation*}
with Roman indices labelling chart components; repeated indices are
summed by \texttt{TraceBasisDummy}; and the abstract covariant
derivative is converted to partial derivatives plus the connection
correction,
\begin{equation*}
  \rcD{_\mu}\varphi_{\nu}
  \;=\;
  \PD{_\mu}\varphi_{\nu} \;-\; \MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}\,\varphi_{\sigma},
  % \label{CovariantDerivativeExpansion}
\end{equation*}
where $\MAGA{}$ is the full Cartan connection of
\cref{PalatiniDecomposition}, with one such term per coordinate slot
for tensors of higher rank; the general mixed-type form is
\cref{MixedCovariantDerivative}.

\paragraph*{Gauge fixing}%
\label{GaugeFixing}
Gauges are fixed only where a physically motivated choice
is available for the field content.
When a gauge is to be fixed, \TIDAL{} implements two structurally distinct
mechanisms: some gauges are most naturally imposed before the
Euler--Lagrange variation, others after.

The first mechanism adds a gauge-fixing term to the Lagrangian before
variation~\cite{faddeev1967feynman}.
For a vector field $\FieldA{_\mu}$, the Lorenz condition
$\rcD{_\mu}\FieldA{^\mu} = 0$ is imposed by
\begin{align}
  \lag \;\longrightarrow\;
  \lag \;-\; \frac{1}{2\xi}\bigl(\rcD{_\mu}\FieldA{^\mu}\bigr)^{2},
  \label{LorenzGauge}
\end{align}
with $\xi$ the gauge-fixing weight, defaulting to $\xi=1$
($\xi\to 0$ recovers the hard Lorenz condition); the modified Lagrangian then enters the variational
procedure in the usual way.
The second mechanism imposes a constraint on the field components
after decomposition, as in the transverse-traceless prescription that
sets $h_{0\mu} = 0$ and $h^\mu{}_\mu = 0$ on the metric-perturbation
components --- a constraint expressible only at the component level
because it refers to component indices, not abstract ones.

Six named presets cover the gauges in routine use: Lorenz for vector
fields, de Donder~\cite{wald1984general} for the metric perturbation,
Coulomb for the spatial part of a vector field, temporal
($A_0 = 0$), axial ($A_3 = 0$), and transverse-traceless.
Arbitrary user-specified gauges are declared by writing the gauge
expression directly in the gauge block of \cref{TOMLExample}, which is
treated as either a Lagrangian term or a component constraint
according to a mode-selection field in the gauge block.

\paragraph*{JSON export}
The symbolic stage terminates by writing a JSON specification, the
sole interface to the numerical side of \TIDAL{}; fixing this interface
cleanly is what allows parameter sweeps and grid changes without
re-derivation.
The specification has five top-level entries: a spacetime block, the
field list with each field's time-derivative order recorded
explicitly, the equations carried as linear combinations of named
differential operators with parameter-dependent coefficients, the
canonical block holding the Hamiltonian density in the same operator
format, and a metadata block recording the source Lagrangian, the
gauge, the linearisation flag, the parameter values, and a derivation
hash.
\cref{JSONExample} shows a representative excerpt for the
Einstein--Maxwell (EM) theory of \cref{TOMLExample}.
Each term carries both its numerical coefficient and its symbolic
expression.%, so a runtime parameter override never regenerates the
% JSON; the equation specification is the centre arrow of
% \cref{ArchitectureDiagram}.
