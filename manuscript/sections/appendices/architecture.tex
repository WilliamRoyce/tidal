\section{\TIDAL{} software architecture}%
\label{Architecture}

\paragraph*{Overview}
In this appendix we describe \emph{Tensor Integration and Derivation
for Any Lagrangian} (\TIDAL{}), the computer-algebra and numerical
software with which we obtained the results of \cref{Results}.
\TIDAL{} comprises two parts: a symbolic computation that derives the
linearised field equations from an arbitrary tensor Lagrangian on a
flat or curved background, and a numerical integration in time.
The architecture is shown in~\cref{ArchitectureDiagram}.

\paragraph*{Symbolic computation}
The symbolic computation, written in \Mathematica{}~\cite{mathematica}
on the \xAct{}~\cite{martingarcia2007xact} suite, expands the
Lagrangian to quadratic order around the declared background using
\xPert{}~\cite{brizuela2009xpert}, applies the Euler--Lagrange (EL)
variation, and decomposes the resulting tensor equations into their
components with the aid of \xTras{}~\cite{nutma2014xtras}.
A complete description of the symbolic operations is
given in \cref{SymbolicComputation}.

\paragraph*{Equation specification}
The equation specification, shown in \cref{ArchitectureDiagram}, is
the interface between the symbolic and numerical parts of
\TIDAL{}.
It records each field's equation of motion as a sum of differential
operators with parameter-dependent coefficients, the Hamiltonian
density in the same form, and the time-derivative order of each
field's equation, used by the solver dispatch of
\cref{NumericalEvolution}.
Decoupling the two parts in this way allows the same derivation to
support arbitrary grids, boundary conditions, initial conditions,
and parameter choices without re-derivation, which is what makes
parameter surveys of the kind reported in \cref{Results} fast
and feasible.

\paragraph*{Numerical integration}
\TIDAL{} dispatches automatically between several solver backends
from the algebraic structure of the equations; the backends and the
dispatch logic are described in \cref{NumericalEvolution}.
The Fourier modal solver (\cref{ModalSolver}) was used throughout
\cref{Results}.

\paragraph*{Observables}
The numerical integration produces field snapshots at each saved
timestep, from which physics observables are extracted.
For the surveys of \cref{Results}, the observable is the
graviton–photon conversion probability $\Pconv(t)$, compared between
theories in \cref{SectorSurvey}.
