\section{\TIDAL{} software architecture}%
\label{Architecture}

\paragraph*{Overview}
In this appendix we describe \emph{Tensor Integration and Derivation
for Any Lagrangian} (\TIDAL{}), the computer-algebra and numerical
software with which we obtained the results of \cref{Results}.
\TIDAL{} comprises two parts: a symbolic computation that derives the
linearised field equations from a Lagrangian, and a numerical
integration that evolves them in time.
All equations of motion are derived automatically from the input
Lagrangian; nothing else is specified by hand.
The architecture is shown in~\cref{ArchitectureDiagram}.

\paragraph*{Symbolic computation}
The symbolic computation, written in \Mathematica{}~\cite{mathematica}
on the \xAct{}~\cite{martingarcia2007xact} suite, expands the
Lagrangian to quadratic order around the declared background using
\xPert{}~\cite{brizuela2009xpert}, applies the Euler–Lagrange
variation, and decomposes the resulting tensor equations into scalar
components with the aid of \xTras{}~\cite{nutma2014xtras}.
Gauge fixing is carried out either by adding a gauge-fixing term to
the Lagrangian or by imposing the gauge condition as a constraint
after the variation; the specific gauge is declared in the theory
specification.
The complete description of the symbolic operations is given in
\cref{SymbolicComputation}; the higher-derivative terms in the
quadratic PGT action and the obstructions to their perturbative
reduction are discussed in \cref{PerturbativeReduction}.

\paragraph*{Equation specification}
The equation specification is the sole interface between the symbolic
and numerical parts of \TIDAL{}.
It records each field's equation of motion as a sum of differential
operators with parameter-dependent coefficients, the Hamiltonian
density in the same form, and a declaration of each field's time
order — wave-like, first-order, or algebraically constrained.
Decoupling the two parts in this way allows the same derivation to
support arbitrary grid resolutions, initial conditions, and parameter
choices without re-derivation, which is what makes parameter surveys
of the kind reported in \cref{Results} feasible.

\paragraph*{Numerical integration}
The numerical integration evolves the equations in time.
\TIDAL{} provides five solver backends and dispatches between them
automatically from the algebraic structure of the equations; both the
backends and the dispatch logic are described in
\cref{NumericalEvolution}.
For all surveys reported in \cref{Results}, the Fourier modal solver
was used.

\paragraph*{Observables}
The numerical integration produces field snapshots at each saved
timestep, from which physics observables are extracted.
For the surveys of \cref{Results}, the observable is the
graviton–photon conversion probability $\Pconv(t)$; the run-maximum
$\Pmax$ provides the quantity compared between theories in the
sector-by-sector study of \cref{SectorSurvey}.
