\section{Calibration against analytic limits and reference models}%
\label{Validation}

\paragraph*{Reproduction of the Boccaletti kernel on a uniform background}
The Gertsenshtein conversion kernel of \cref{GertsenshteinFormula}
provides the closed-form analytic baseline against which we calibrate
\TIDAL{} on a uniform background magnetic field. We sweep $\Bzero$
over the perturbative regime $\Bzero \in [0.005, 0.20]$ at four values
of the propagation distance $D = c\,t_\mathrm{end} \in \{25, 50, 75, 100\}$
on a periodic grid of $N=1024$ points with fourth-order finite-difference
operators, and overlay three analytic predictions on the same axis: the
bare formula $\Pconv = \sin^2(\kappa\Bzero t/2)$; the energy-cap form
$\Pmax = \sin^2(\kappa\Bzero t/2)\cdot k^2/(k^2 + \kappa^2\Bzero^2/2)$
that accounts for the graviton effective-mass energy the massless
photon cannot absorb; and the Raffelt--Stodolsky two-mode formula
with frequency detuning~\cite{raffelt1988mixing}
\begin{align}
  \Pconv = \frac{g^2}{g^2 + \Delta^2}
  \sin^2\!\left(\sqrt{g^2+\Delta^2}\,D\right),
  \quad g = \tfrac{\kappa\Bzero}{2},
  \quad \Delta = -\tfrac{\kappa^2 \Bzero^2}{4\omega},
  \label{TwoModeRabi}
\end{align}
in which both the amplitude prefactor and the oscillation frequency
receive corrections from the effective mass; the regime of validity
follows \cref{PerturbativityHierarchy}. The smoke-resolution run at
$N=128$ over a four-cell slice already gives a maximum $P_\mathrm{final}$
residual of $1.4 \times 10^{-3}$ against the bare formula and a
Raffelt--Stodolsky residual on $P_\mathrm{max}$ as small as
$5\times 10^{-6}$ at the regime point $\Bzero=0.05$. \Cref{BoccalettiUniform}
displays the calibration overlay, the residual band against all three
analytic forms, and the multi-resolution convergence at the regime
point. The reproduction calibrates the uniform-background sector;
localised-profile reproduction is calibrated separately in
\cref{BoccalettiLocalised}.

\paragraph*{Path-integrated Boccaletti reproduction on a Gaussian-localised profile}
For a finite magnetised region of profile $B_x(z)$ the Gertsenshtein
formula generalises to the path-integrated form
\begin{align}
  \Pconv = \sin^2\!\left(
    \tfrac{\kappa}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\!B_x(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right),
  \label{BoccalettiPathIntegrated}
\end{align}
and for the Gaussian profile
$B_x(z) = B_\mathrm{peak}\exp(-z^2/(2R^2))$ the integral is
$\int B\,\mathrm{d}z = B_\mathrm{peak}\,R\sqrt{2\pi}$, giving
$\Pconv = \sin^2(\kappa B_\mathrm{peak} R \sqrt{\pi/2})$. We
calibrate against this closed-form on a $(B_\mathrm{peak}, R)$ grid
using the derived \texttt{gertsenshtein\_localized.json} specification
and a Gaussian wave-packet initial condition on $h_+$ at $z=-50$
traversing the magnetised region at $z=0$. Neumann boundary conditions
are essential: the gauge potential $\bar{A}_y \propto \mathrm{erf}(z/\sqrt{2}R)$
is non-periodic, and periodic boundaries introduce spectral leakage
that triggers spurious exponential growth. Smoke-mode residual is
$1.2\times 10^{-3}$ in $\Pconv$ at $B_\mathrm{peak}=0.1, R=5$ (a 0.35\%
relative deviation), in line with the documented sub-percent agreement
of the localised path. \Cref{BoccalettiLocalised} displays the
$(B_\mathrm{peak}, R)$ residual heatmap and a line cut at fixed $R$.
This calibrates the path-integrated kernel; finite-frequency dispersion
corrections are calibrated in \cref{ConvergenceCalibration}.

\paragraph*{Forced-oscillator calibration of the closed-form Duhamel kernel}
The perturbative reduction's Pass 0 + Pass 1 path solves an
inhomogeneous linear ODE via the closed-form Duhamel kernel
$\varphi_1(\lambda_i, \lambda_j; t)$ in the eigenbasis of the base
operator~\cite{almohy2011action}; we calibrate the kernel against the
analytical perturbative expansion of a driven Klein--Gordon oscillator
with an order-$\varepsilon$ mass-shift correction,
$\partial_t^2\phi = \partial_x^2\phi - m^2\phi - \varepsilon\phi$.
Pass 0 evolves the $\varepsilon=0$ base; Pass 1 integrates the
correction against the Pass 0 trajectory; the combined
$\phi^{(0)} + \phi^{(1)}$ matches the full-$\varepsilon$ modal solution
to $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$ by construction. Across the smoke
sweep $\varepsilon \in \{0.1, 0.05, 0.01\}$ at $k=1$, the log--log
slope of $|\phi^{\mathrm{sim}} - \phi^{\mathrm{full}}|$ versus
$\varepsilon$ fits to $1.99$ against the expected $2.0$, and the
improvement ratio over Pass 0 alone grows from $7.4$ at $\varepsilon=0.1$
to $81$ at $\varepsilon=0.01$. The calibration is non-circular: the
closed-form Duhamel kernel is a generic ODE-theory construct, derived
independently of \TIDAL{}'s implementation, and the small parameter
$\varepsilon$ appears in the correction source, not in the tuning of
the test target. \Cref{ForcedOscillator} displays the error scaling
and the improvement ratio.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/figD_forced_oscillator}
  \caption{Forced-oscillator calibration of the Pass 0 + Pass 1
    Duhamel kernel. (a) Pass 0 error and combined Pass 0 + Pass 1
    error versus $\varepsilon$ on log--log axes, with the
    $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)$ and $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$
    guide lines. The fitted combined slope is close to $2.0$.
    (b) Improvement ratio: combined-path accuracy gain over Pass 0

\end{figure}

\paragraph*{Higher-derivative perturbative calibration on a Parker--Simon-style correction}
The closed-form Duhamel kernel calibrates against a second perturbative
structure --- a fourth-order spatial correction
$\partial_t^2 \phi = \partial_x^2 \phi - m^2 \phi - \varepsilon\,\partial_x^4 \phi$
that is the canonical higher-derivative form encountered in the
perturbative reductions of \cite{parker1993reduced}. The base is the
same Klein--Gordon wave; the correction exercises a different
Fourier multiplier ($k^4$) from the mass shift ($1$), and therefore a
different code path through the correction-source assembly of the
solver. Smoke-mode behaviour at $k=2$ shows the Pass 0 + Pass 1
truncation error decreasing rapidly with $\varepsilon$, well below the
$\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$ worst-case bound demanded by the
perturbative theorem; the improvement ratio over Pass 0 alone reaches
$\sim 10^4$ at $\varepsilon=0.01$. \Cref{ParkerSimon} shows the error
scaling. The literal 1+0D Parker--Simon FLRW reduction at
equations~3.39--3.44 of~\cite{parker1993reduced} is documented as a
planned regression test and is the cosmological counterpart of this
higher-derivative calibration; the present test probes the same Pass 1
machinery on the flat-background field-theoretic analogue.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/figD_parker_simon}

    Klein--Gordon wave; the combined-path error falls faster than the
    worst-case $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$ bound at small

\paragraph*{Convention-anchored agreement with the Euler--Heisenberg QED birefringence}
The QED Euler--Heisenberg effective action predicts a vacuum
birefringence with refractive-index shifts
$n_\perp - 1 = (8\alpha^2/45)\,(B/B_c)^2$ and
$n_\parallel - 1 = (14\alpha^2/45)\,(B/B_c)^2$~\cite{adler1971photon},
giving the canonical ratio
$(n_\parallel - 1)/(n_\perp - 1) = 7/4$~\cite{dunne2004heisenberg}.
\TIDAL{}'s symbolic stage produces both absolute coefficients
analytically at the convention mapping $\sigma/\rho = 7/16$, in full
accordance with~\cite{adler1971photon}; the agreement is at the level
of individual coefficients, not just the ratio, and constitutes a
genuine analytic match. The Hamiltonian-path test
\texttt{test\_order1\_h\_ratio\_matches\_adler} provides an additional
non-circular cross-check via an independent measurement code path:
$H_1/H_0 = -3(n_\perp - 1)_\mathrm{Adler}$ recovered from the symbolic
Hamiltonian, with no involvement of the equation-of-motion solver.
The bare ratio test
$(n_\parallel-1)/(n_\perp-1) \to 7/4$ at small $\varepsilon$ is
reported but flagged as weakly tautological: the leading-order \TIDAL{}
formula is $4\sigma/\rho$ by construction, and $4\cdot(7/16)=7/4$ is
arithmetic forced by the convention mapping.

\paragraph*{Cross-formulation equivalence: fundamental-vector and TorsionCDT}
\label{FVCDTEquivalence}
For complex PGT theories where no closed-form solution or weak-field
analytic limit is available --- the bulk of the \cref{SectorSurvey}
campaigns --- the only available consistency check is to derive the
same continuum action through two mathematically distinct field
formulations and verify identical observables to the IEEE round-off
limit. The dark-photon-plasma sector affords this comparison directly.
The \emph{fundamental-vector} representation
$\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{FV} \supset
   -\tfrac14 \xi (\partial T)^2
   - \tfrac12 m_T^2\,t\!\cdot\! t
   + \delta_m\,F\!\cdot\!(\partial T)$
carries 10 dynamical fields with a clean kinetic structure
($\operatorname{cond}(V)\sim 10^{8}$--$10^{10}$); the
\emph{TorsionCDT} representation
$\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{CDT} \supset
   -\alpha_3\,I_3
   - \tfrac14 \xi (\partial T_{[\mu\nu]})^2
   + \delta_m\,F\!\cdot\!(\partial T_{[\mu\nu]})$
carries 18 fields including redundant constrained directions
($\operatorname{cond}(V)\sim 10^{15}$--$10^{17}$). The continuum
equivalence map (post-2026-04-24 convention, GitHub issue \#318) is
$m_T^2 = +2\alpha_3$. At the canonical campaign point
$(m_A^2 = 0.955, \delta_m = 0.01, \xi = 0.274, \alpha_3 = +0.123)$
the two formulations agree on $\Pmax$ to a relative difference
$\Delta\Pmax / \Pmax = 1.4\times 10^{-15}$ --- bit-exact at the
IEEE-754 round-off floor of $2.22\times 10^{-16}$; the agreement is
preserved at the same level across five distinct parameter points
spanning the surveyed sector.

The cross-formulation comparison has been load-bearing: it surfaced
four numerical-methods bugs that would have been silently invisible
to single-formulation testing. (i) Off-grid plane-wave initial
conditions leak amplitude onto every discrete Fourier mode (issue
\#305); on PGT theories with tachyonic eigenvalues at some $k$-modes
this produces spurious exponential growth that disagrees between
formulations until the initial-condition wavevector is snapped to
the nearest discrete mode. (ii) The TorsionCDT kinetic matrix is
rank-deficient and gives $\operatorname{cond}(V)$ above the Higham
\S2.3 threshold for eigendecomposition~\cite{higham2008functions}
(issue \#320); the eigendecomposition path loses precision while
fundamental-vector remains accurate, and bit-exact agreement is
restored only by switching to the scaling-and-squaring matrix
exponential~\cite{higham2009scaling} for the base evolution and the
augmented matrix exponential of~\cite{almohy2011action} for the
Pass 1 correction. (iii) The QZ generalised-eigenvalue solver
produced spurious finite eigenvalues on TorsionCDT's null-space-rich
kinetic matrix (issue \#264); the cure was an SVD null-space
projection before the QZ. (iv) An inference-posterior cross-check
between formulations exposed a 0.36\% bias in a candidate template
cache, which was reverted before merging. \Cref{FVCDT} displays the
side-by-side $\Pmax$ comparison and the per-point relative-difference
bars. The equivalence is a cross-formulation consistency check, not
an independent reproduction of an analytic limit, but it is the only
available non-trivial check on the linearisation,
constraint-elimination, and numerical-evolution paths for the complex
PGT theories surveyed in \cref{SectorSurvey}.

\paragraph*{Multi-method agreement: backend, measurement, and reduction self-consistency}
\TIDAL{} dispatches to one of several solver backends according to the
equation specification (see \cref{SolverDispatchFlowchart}); we
calibrate the dispatcher's backend-selection consistency by running
representative theories under every applicable backend and checking
pairwise relative agreement in $\Pmax$. Eight backends --- modal,
\CVODE{}, \IDA{}, leapfrog Yoshida-2, leapfrog Yoshida-4, \SciPy{}
DOP853, \SciPy{} Radau, and \SciPy{} BDF~\cite{hindmarsh2005sundials} ---
are exercised on the Einstein--Maxwell baseline of
\cref{GertsenshteinFormula} and on a gradient-coupled scalar pair
that is the analogue of the Raffelt--Stodolsky two-channel mixing
model~\cite{raffelt1988mixing}. The same calibration also runs a
measurement-path cross-check: $H(t)$ derived from the symbolic
Hamiltonian (Parseval gradient energy on periodic domains) is
compared to the EOM-based energy diagnostic, two independent code
paths for the same physical quantity. The smoke run on four backends
shows a median pairwise relative difference of $5\times 10^{-3}$
across all pairs; a single backend pair on the gradient-coupled
theory at smoke resolution shows the expected outlier (the explicit
leapfrog and implicit \IDA{} drift toward their default tolerances)
which the HPC-resolution sweep removes. \Cref{MultiMethod} displays
the backend-pair heatmap on each theory together with the pairwise
rel\_diff distribution. These are internal-consistency checks across
independent computational paths; only the calibrations against
external analytic predictions in the earlier paragraphs constitute
independent reproductions.

\paragraph*{Spatial and temporal convergence}
\label{ConvergenceCalibration}
The spatial-operator catalogue is calibrated against analytic
derivatives in \cref{FDConvergence}, where the Fornberg
finite-difference orders 2, 4 and 6 recover their predicted
algebraic rates and the pseudo-spectral path falls to the round-off
floor by $N\approx 64$~\cite{fornberg1988generation,burns2020dedalus}.
We extend the spatial calibration to a physical example by running
the Proca-mass equation at $\Bzero=0$ for a $k$-sweep at three
values of $m^2 \in \{0.1, 1.0, 10.0\}$; for each $(k, m^2)$ the
angular frequency is recovered by spatial-FFT mode identification
followed by time-FFT with a Hann window and quadratic peak
refinement. The recovered dispersion matches
$\omega(k) = \sqrt{k^2 + m^2}$ across the swept $k$-range, with the
median rel error at smoke resolution at $3.4\times 10^{-3}$. The
Rabi-frequency grid convergence on the Einstein--Maxwell baseline
exhibits the predicted $(k\Delta x)^2$ scaling of the FD-4 stencil
error~\cite{fornberg1988generation}, decreasing as $N$ increases.
Time-integration convergence is calibrated by sweeping the
integrator step $\Delta t$ across seven schemes: the leapfrog
Yoshida-2 fits a log--log slope of $2.07$ (predicted
$2.0$~\cite{yoshida1990symplectic,hairer2006geometric}); the
Yoshida-4 saturates at the spatial-discretisation floor at the
coarser smoke ladder; the implicit \CVODE{}, \IDA{}, and \SciPy{}
DOP853/Radau/BDF schemes each saturate at their rtol setting
independently of $\Delta t$, as expected.
\Cref{ConvergenceFigure} displays the three convergence diagnostics.
These calibrate discretisation and integrator order against analytic
targets; they do not probe modelling errors upstream of the
discretised system.

\paragraph*{Energy-conservation audit across the example library}
Energy conservation provides a structural diagnostic that engages
every layer of the workflow at once: a constraint solver that returns
inconsistent initial conditions, a symbolic stage that loses
cross-coupling terms in field decomposition, or a time integrator
that drifts past its predicted order, all surface as relative energy
drift. We audit the canonical example library --- Einstein--Maxwell
Gertsenshtein, the gradient-coupled scalar pair, the propagating-PGT
and nonminimal torsion variants of \cref{SectorSurvey}, the
dark-photon-plasma sector in both FV and CDT formulations, the
Proca-mass case, and several flat- and curved-background tests ---
and record the maximum relative energy drift $|dE/E|_\mathrm{max}$
per example. At smoke resolution the gertsenshtein and
coupled-scalars examples both achieve drift of $\sim 10^{-15}$ at
machine precision; the larger-system audit follows the same pattern,
with two expected outliers --- de-Sitter Klein--Gordon (physical
Hubble friction $\sim 30\%$) and \texttt{massive\_3form}
(Hamiltonian skipped at rank-3 Lagrangian decomposition) ---
documented separately. The volume-element fix of GitHub issue
\#308 dropped the curvilinear spherical and polar drifts from
43--66\% to $\sim 10^{-9}$, and the Parseval gradient-energy fix of
issue \#312 brought the Euler--Heisenberg test to its
$\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$-compliant floor.
\Cref{ConservationAudit} displays the per-example drift bar chart
with the default $10^{-3}$ conservation threshold drawn for reference.
Per-example precision is set by the backend the dispatcher selects;
the audit is a structural regression diagnostic, not a per-example
precision statement.

\paragraph*{Torsion-limit recovery as a kinematic identity}
At $\xi \to 0$ the propagating-PGT (T3) theory of
\cref{PropagatingKineticOutput} reduces to the Einstein--Maxwell
baseline bit-exactly in the $h_\times \leftrightarrow a_x$ channel,
by the kinematic identity reported in \cref{ChannelStructure}. The
agreement is bit-equal across all surveyed $\xi$ rather than
approached as a limit; this is the kinematic statement of the
channel-structure observation, and not an independent calibration.
