\paragraph*{Boccaletti kernel on a uniform background}
The Gertsenshtein conversion kernel of \cref{GertsenshteinFormula}
is one of the closed-form analytic predictions we test \TIDAL{}
against. We scan $\Bzero$ over the perturbative regime and \cref{BoccalettiUniform}
overlays the simulation against two analytic predictions:
\cref{GertsenshteinFormula} and the
Raffelt--Stodolsky formula with graviton-effective-mass
detuning~\cite{raffelt1988mixing},
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    \Pconv &= \frac{g^2}{g^2 + \Delta^2}\,
      \sin^2\!\left(\sqrt{g^2+\Delta^2}\,D\right), \\
    g &= \frac{\kappa\Bzero}{2},
    \qquad \Delta = -\frac{\kappa^2 \Bzero^2}{8\omega},
  \end{split}
  \label{TwoModeRabi}
\end{equation}
where $\omega \simeq k$ is the carrier angular frequency of the photon
mode. The graviton acquires an effective mass
$m_h^2 = \kappa^2\Bzero^2/2$ from the $\Bzero^2$ metric
backreaction~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton}, detuning its dispersion
against the massless photon by $\Delta = m_h^2/(4\omega)$.
The two predictions coincide while
$\kappa\Bzero t/2 \ll \pi/2$; they diverge at larger $\Bzero$ along
the analytic $|\sin^2(\kappa\Bzero t/2) - P_\mathrm{RS}|$ curve
overlaid in \cref{BoccalettiUniform}(a).
\TIDAL{} evolves the full system without the approximations
that produce \cref{TwoModeRabi};
the residual $|P_\mathrm{TIDAL} - P_\mathrm{RS}|$ then measures the
size of the higher-order physics discarded,
which \cref{PerturbativityHierarchy} places at $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^4)$.
\Cref{BoccalettiUniform}(a) shows this residual rising from
the modal-solver IEEE double-precision floor at small $\Bzero$ into
the range $10^{-8}$--$10^{-6}$ as $\Bzero$ grows, of the same order
as the $(\Delta/g)^4$ prediction at those values, and panel (b)
shows the modal/CVODE plateau at the same level.

\paragraph*{Boccaletti kernel on a Gaussian profile}
For a finite magnetised region $B_x(z)$ the Gertsenshtein formula
\cref{GertsenshteinFormula} generalises to the path-integrated
form~\cite{raffelt1988mixing}
\begin{equation}
  \Pconv = \sin^2\!\left(
    \frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\!B_x(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right),
  \label{BoccalettiPathIntegrated}
\end{equation}
taking the Gaussian profile
$B_x(z) = B_\mathrm{peak}\exp(-z^2/(2R^2))$. We
reproduce this closed-form by scanning the
$(B_\mathrm{peak}, R)$ plane using the derived localised
Gertsenshtein theory. \Cref{BoccalettiLocalised} shows the
scan collapsing onto the single curve
$P = \sin^2(s)$ in the dimensionless variable
$s = \kappa B_\mathrm{peak}\,R\sqrt{\pi/2}$ within the perturbative
regime $s < \pi/2$, reproducing \cref{BoccalettiPathIntegrated}.
The scan extends past the first quarter-period ($s > \pi/2$), where
the $P_\mathrm{conv} \ll 1$ row of \cref{PerturbativityHierarchy}
fails and the bare path-integrated formula no longer applies;
points past this quarter-period deviate from the curve as the
bare-formula coherent approximation breaks down.

\paragraph*{Klein--Gordon perturbative calibration}
The perturbative-reduction machinery of \cref{PerturbativeReduction}
evolves the system as a base (order-$\varepsilon^{0}$) solution
$\phi^{(0)}$ plus a correction (order-$\varepsilon^{1}$)
$\phi^{(1)}$. \Cref{ForcedOscillator,ParkerSimon} show the resulting
truncation-error scaling. We test the $\phi^{(0)} + \phi^{(1)}$
approximation against the exact full-$\varepsilon$ modal solution of
a Klein--Gordon wave with a small mass-shift correction,
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t^2\phi - \partial_x^2\phi + m^2\phi
  \;=\; -\varepsilon\,\phi,
  \label{KGMassShift}
\end{equation}
The order-$\varepsilon^{0}$ solution evolves the $\varepsilon=0$
base; the order-$\varepsilon^{1}$ correction is integrated against
the base trajectory; the
$|\phi^{(0)} + \phi^{(1)} - \phi^\mathrm{exact}|$ truncation error
is $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$ by construction.
\Cref{ForcedOscillator} shows the slope of
$|\phi^{(0)} + \phi^{(1)} - \phi^\mathrm{exact}|$ versus $\varepsilon$
recovers the expected algebraic order of error scaling.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/figD_forced_oscillator}
  \caption{Perturbed-Klein--Gordon calibration of the Duhamel kernel
    that combines the order-$\varepsilon^{0}$ base evolution
    $\phi^{(0)}$ with the order-$\varepsilon^{1}$ correction
    $\phi^{(1)}$. The base-solution error
    $|\phi^{(0)} - \phi^\mathrm{exact}|$ and the corrected error
    $|\phi^{(0)} + \phi^{(1)} - \phi^\mathrm{exact}|$ versus
    $\varepsilon$ for the mass-shift correction \cref{KGMassShift},
    sampled at eight logarithmically spaced points covering
    $\varepsilon \in [10^{-3}, 0.2]$ at $k=1$; fitted slopes and
    $R^2$ values are reported in the legend. The corrected error
    scales as $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$, an order of magnitude
    per half-decade in $\varepsilon$.}
  \label{ForcedOscillator}
\end{figure}

\paragraph*{Higher-derivative perturbative calibration}
A second perturbative calibration replaces the mass shift of
\cref{KGMassShift} with a fourth-order spatial correction,
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t^2 \phi - \partial_x^2 \phi + m^2 \phi
  \;=\; -\varepsilon\,\partial_x^4 \phi,
  \label{KGBiharmonic}
\end{equation}
the biharmonic $\partial_x^4 \phi$ correction characteristic of
Parker--Simon~\cite{parker1993reduced}.
At $k=2$ the order-$\varepsilon^{0,1}$
truncation error falls faster than the $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$
worst-case scaling of the reduced-order
construction~\cite{parker1993reduced}; the improvement ratio over
the base solution reaches $\sim 10^4$ at $\varepsilon=0.01$ in
\cref{ParkerSimon}.

  \caption{Higher-derivative perturbative calibration. Base-solution
    error $|\phi^{(0)} - \phi^\mathrm{exact}|$ and corrected error
    $|\phi^{(0)} + \phi^{(1)} - \phi^\mathrm{exact}|$ versus
    $\varepsilon$ for the biharmonic correction \cref{KGBiharmonic}
    on the Klein--Gordon base of \cref{KGMassShift}. The corrected

    $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$ bound at small $\varepsilon$.

\paragraph*{Euler--Heisenberg birefringence ratio}
The QED Euler--Heisenberg effective action in the weak-field
limit~\cite{heisenberg1936consequences,schwinger1951gauge,dunne2004heisenberg,adler1971photon}
is, to leading order in the field strength,
\begin{equation}
  \lag_{\mathrm{EH}} \;=\;
    -\tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
    + \tfrac{1}{8}\,\rho\,
      \bigl(\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}\bigr)^{2}
    + \tfrac{1}{8}\,\sigma\,
      \bigl(\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldFtilde{^{\mu\nu}}\bigr)^{2},
  \label{LagEH}
\end{equation}
with $\rho = 4\alpha^{2}/(45\,m_{e}^{4}\,\pi^{2})$ and
$\sigma = 7\alpha^{2}/(90\,m_{e}^{4}\,\pi^{2})$ the standard QED
weak-field coefficients. \TIDAL{} correctly projects the
$(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})^{2}$ correction onto the perpendicular
photon polarisation and the $(F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu})^{2}$
correction onto the parallel polarisation; the resulting
refractive-index shifts
$n_\perp - 1 = (8\alpha^2/45)(B/B_c)^2$ and
$n_\parallel - 1 = (14\alpha^2/45)(B/B_c)^2$, where $B_c = m_e^2/e$ is the
Schwinger critical field~\cite{schwinger1951gauge}, reproduce the
Adler birefringence ratio
$(n_\parallel - 1)/(n_\perp - 1) = 4\sigma/\rho =
7/4$~\cite{adler1971photon} exactly.

\paragraph*{Cross-formulation equivalence: Proca versus vector-torsion}%
\label{ProcaVTEquivalence}
For complex PGT theories where no closed-form solution is available,
the only available consistency check is to compare the same
continuum action derived via two mathematically distinct field
formulations. The dark-photon-plasma sector of \cref{SectorSurvey}
affords this directly: its \emph{Proca}
representation~\cite{caputo2021darkphoton,pospelov2008secluded},
\begin{equation}
  \lag_{\mathrm{Proca}} \;\supset\;
   -\tfrac14 \xi\,(\partial T)^2
   - \tfrac12 m_T^2\,t\!\cdot\! t
   + \delta_m\,\FieldF{}\!\cdot\!(\partial T),
  \label{LagFV}
\end{equation}
carrying $10$ dynamical fields with kinetic-matrix condition number
$\operatorname{cond}(V)$~\cite{higham2008functions}~$\sim 10^{8\text{--}10}$.

The
\emph{vector-torsion} representation expresses the same theory using
the trace of the spacetime torsion tensor evolved with the
Maxwell-like field-strength tensor
$\FieldFT{_{\mu\nu}} \equiv 2\,\partial_{[\mu} T_{\nu]}$ of the
propagating vector-torsion construction of~\cite{barker2024poincare},
with the torsion trace $T_\mu \equiv T^\nu{}_{\nu\mu}$ promoted to a
propagating vector field,
%TODO replace prose description with a direct cref to the propagating-vector-torsion sector definition once that lands in results.tex.
kinetic prefactor $\xi$,
gauge-coupling shift $\alpha_3$, and mass mixing $\delta_m$ (the
Lagrangian appears with the dark-photon-plasma sector in
\cref{SectorSurvey}%
%TODO replace with explicit equation cref once the vector-torsion Lagrangian lands in results.tex
), carrying $18$ fields including redundant constrained
directions ($\operatorname{cond}(V) \sim 10^{15\text{--}17}$). The
continuum equivalence is recovered under the map
$m_T^2 = +2\alpha_3$. The two formulations agree on $\Pmax$ at
the IEEE double-precision round-off floor.

\paragraph*{Spatial and temporal convergence}\label{ConvergenceCalibration}
The spatial-operator catalogue is tested against analytic
derivatives in \cref{FDConvergence}, where the Fornberg
FD orders recover their predicted
algebraic rates and the pseudo-spectral path falls to the round-off
floor~\cite{fornberg1988generation,burns2020dedalus}.
We extend the spatial calibration to a physical example by checking
\TIDAL{}'s reproduction of the free Proca dispersion relation
$\omega(k) = \sqrt{k^2 + m^2}$ at zero background for three values
of $m^2 \in \{0.1, 1.0, 10.0\}$.
% The Rabi-frequency extraction
% itself is FFT-bin-width bounded at
% $|\Omega_\mathrm{eff}/\Omega_\mathrm{theory} - 1| \approx 7\times 10^{-3}$
% across the surveyed $N$, well below the spatial FD-4 error of
% \cref{FDConvergence} and not the limiting diagnostic at this
% resolution.
Time-integration convergence is reported in
\cref{ConvergenceFigure} for the symplectic backends, where
Yoshida-2 recovers its formal slope to four-figure precision
($\hat{\alpha} = 2.00$, $R^2 = 1.000$). Yoshida-4 instead exhibits
reproducible non-power-law scatter spanning two orders of magnitude
at fixed $\Delta t$; the same pattern reappears in
\cref{BoccalettiUniform}(b) under grid refinement at fixed
$\Delta t$.
% The scatter is bit-identical across reruns with $2$
% versus $20$ snapshots and across $P_\mathrm{final}$ versus
% $P_\mathrm{max}$ extraction, ruling out snapshot-timing and
% measurement-extraction artefacts; modal, \CVODE, and Yoshida-2 are
% smooth on the same data.
We attribute the residual Yoshida-4
pathology to a still-uncharacterised interaction between the
Yoshida composition and either the coupled two-field structure or
the two well-separated frequency scales
($\kappa\Bzero/2 = 0.025$, $\omega_k \approx 2.0$) of the
EM baseline.

\paragraph*{Energy-conservation audit}
Energy conservation, when available, provides a diagnostic that engages
every layer of the workflow.
% at once: a constraint solver that
% returns inconsistent initial conditions, a symbolic stage that
% loses cross-coupling terms in field decomposition, or a time
% integrator that drifts past its predicted order, all reveal
% themselves as relative energy drift.
We audit the maximum relative
energy drift $|\Delta E/E|_\mathrm{max}$ across the theories of physical
relevance to the \cref{Results} campaigns.
% the EM
% Gertsenshtein baseline of \cref{GertsenshteinFormula}, a
% gradient-coupled scalar pair (an analogue of \cref{TwoModeRabi}),
% the propagating-PGT and nonminimal-torsion variants of
% \cref{SectorSurvey}, the dark-photon-plasma sector,
% a Proca-mass photon, and a linearised gravitational wave.
Successful examples
cluster at $\sim 10^{-15}$, near $\varepsilon_\mathrm{mach}$ of the
modal solver. The precision floor of each row in
\cref{ConservationAudit} is set by the backend the dispatcher
selects, which differs across theories.
