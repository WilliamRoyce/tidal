\section{Validation against analytic limits and reference models}%
\label{Validation}

\paragraph*{Calibration against the Boccaletti kernel}
The Gertsenshtein photon--graviton conversion kernel
$\Pconv = \sin^2(\kappa\Bzero D/2)$~\cite{gertsenshtein1962wave,boccaletti1970conversion}
fixes the analytic baseline against which we calibrate the full \TIDAL{}
pipeline. We sweep $\Bzero$ over the perturbative regime
$\Bzero \in [0.005, 0.25]$ at a fixed propagation distance
$D = c\,t_\mathrm{end} = 50$ on a periodic grid of $N = 512$ points with
fourth-order finite-difference operators, recording the residual
$P_\mathrm{final}^\mathrm{sim} - P_\mathrm{final}^\mathrm{analytic}$ at
each sweep cell. The independently corroborated thin-magnet form of the
kernel~\cite{palessandro2023simple,domcke2025scattering} predicts
$P_\mathrm{final}$ uniformly across the sweep; the peak conversion
$P_\mathrm{max}$ over $t \in [0, t_\mathrm{end}]$ acquires a regime-dependent
effective-mass cap $k^2/(k^2 + \kappa^2\Bzero^2)$ from the graviton
initial energy that the massless photon cannot absorb, which obscures
the bare sin\textsuperscript{2} comparison past the first Rabi peak, so
$P_\mathrm{final}$ is the calibration metric used here. Across the
$40$-point $\Bzero$ sweep the residual is bounded by
$\max_{\Bzero} |P_\mathrm{final}^\mathrm{sim} - P_\mathrm{final}^\mathrm{analytic}|
 = 2.42\times 10^{-3}$ with an RMS of $9.15\times 10^{-4}$, well below
the perturbative-regime limit $P_\mathrm{final}\lesssim 0.9$ this
parameter region samples. The multi-resolution convergence at the
regime point $\Bzero = 0.05$ tightens to
$|P_\mathrm{final}^\mathrm{sim} - P_\mathrm{final}^\mathrm{analytic}|
 = 2.0\times 10^{-5}$ at $N = 512$, set by the spectral-precision
floor of the modal solver auto-selected by the dispatcher~(\cref{ModalSolver}).
\Cref{BoccalettiCalibration} displays the calibration curve, the
residual band, and the convergence sweep; the same canonical data drives
the single-point summary figure of the main text~(\cref{BoccalettiValidation}).
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/figD_boccaletti_calibration}
  \caption{Boccaletti-kernel calibration of the \TIDAL{} pipeline.
    Left: the simulated $\Pconv^{\mathrm{final}}(\Bzero)$ curve at
    fixed $D = c\,t_\mathrm{end} = 50$ overlaid with the analytic
    $\sin^2(\kappa\Bzero D/2)$ baseline. Centre: the residual
    $P^{\mathrm{sim}}_{\mathrm{final}} - P^{\mathrm{ana}}_{\mathrm{final}}$
    on the same axis. Right: the multi-resolution convergence of
    $|P^{\mathrm{sim}} - P^{\mathrm{ana}}|$ at the regime point
    $\Bzero = 0.05$, decreasing to the round-off floor at $N \gtrsim 1024$
    (the modal solver achieves spectral convergence on constant-coefficient
    periodic systems).}
  \label{BoccalettiCalibration}
\end{figure}

\paragraph*{Recovery of the Einstein--Maxwell baseline from the torsion-extended theory}
The torsion-extended Lagrangian of \cref{TorsionLagrangian} reduces
analytically to Einstein--Maxwell as the torsion-coupling parameter
$\xi$ approaches zero, and the same reduction must hold numerically
through the full pipeline because the symbolic stage derives the
equations of motion from the Lagrangian without manual intervention.
We sweep $\xi$ over a logarithmic ladder
$\xi \in \{10^{-1}, 10^{-2}, \ldots, 10^{-8}\}$ at fixed $\Bzero = 0.05$
on the propagating-PGT theory (\cref{PropagatingKineticOutput}, App E),
running a single Einstein--Maxwell reference at the same regime point,
and record $|\Pmax^\mathrm{torsion}(\xi) - \Pmax^\mathrm{EM}|$ in
the $h_\times \leftrightarrow a_x$ channel. The recovery is
\emph{exact} (bit-equal to round-off) across the entire $\xi$ ladder:
the active $h_\times \leftrightarrow a_x$ channel is kinematically
torsion-independent in this theory class~\cite{barker2021hamiltonian},
a structural observation reported in \cref{ChannelStructure}. The
limit therefore calibrates the symbolic pipeline against a stricter
target than a vanishing-in-the-limit reduction would: any
torsion-dependent contamination in the kinetic matrix would survive at
finite $\xi$ and produce a non-zero residual. \Cref{TorsionLimitRecovery}
plots $|\Delta\Pmax(\xi)|$ on log--log axes with the machine-precision
floor annotated.
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{figures/figD_torsion_limit}
  \caption{Limit recovery from the torsion-extended theory.
    $|\Pmax^{\mathrm{torsion}}(\xi) - \Pmax^{\mathrm{EM}}|$ in the
    $h_\times \leftrightarrow a_x$ channel as the torsion-coupling
    parameter $\xi$ varies over a logarithmic ladder at fixed
    $\Bzero = 0.05$ and propagation distance $D = c\,t_\mathrm{end} = 50$.
    The bit-equal recovery across all $\xi$ reflects the kinematic
    torsion-independence of this channel in the propagating-PGT theory
    (\cref{ChannelStructure}) and calibrates the symbolic pipeline
    against contamination from torsion-dependent kinetic-matrix
    entries.}
  \label{TorsionLimitRecovery}
\end{figure}

\paragraph*{Cross-backend self-consistency on representative theories}
\TIDAL{}'s dispatcher selects among five solver backends --- \IDA,
\CVODE, leapfrog, modal, and \SciPy{} --- according to the equation
specification (\cref{ResolveScheme}); we calibrate the dispatcher's
backend-selection consistency by running representative theories under
every applicable backend and checking pairwise relative agreement in
$P_\mathrm{max}$. The Einstein--Maxwell baseline of
\cref{GertsenshteinFormula} exercises the modal-eligible flow with no
constraints, while \texttt{coupled\_scalars} exercises the gradient-coupled
flat-space sector that the dispatcher routes to leapfrog by default.
On both theories the modal and \CVODE{} backends agree to a pairwise
relative difference at the precision floor of the modal evolution ---
$4.7\times 10^{-6}$ on Einstein--Maxwell and $1.7\times 10^{-8}$ on
\texttt{coupled\_scalars}~\cite{hindmarsh2005sundials,higham2008functions}.
The explicit leapfrog and implicit \IDA{} backends agree with the
modal--\CVODE{} pair to within $1.1\times 10^{-3}$ across all twelve
backend--pair comparisons (median $4.2\times 10^{-4}$). The wider
spread of the leapfrog and \IDA{} paths is consistent with their
default integration tolerances rather than with a discrepancy in the
underlying equations of motion. \Cref{CrossBackend} reports the
pairwise log-scale heatmaps per theory.
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/figD_cross_backend}
  \caption{Cross-backend self-consistency on representative theories.
    Pairwise relative differences in $\Pmax$ for every solver-backend
    pair on the Einstein--Maxwell Gertsenshtein baseline (left) and the
    coupled-scalars gradient-coupling baseline (right). Cells encode
    $|P_a - P_b| / \max(|P_a|, |P_b|)$ on a logarithmic scale; the
    modal--\CVODE{} agreement is at the precision floor on
    Einstein--Maxwell, providing direct evidence that the dispatcher
    is internally consistent across its backend choices.}
  \label{CrossBackend}
\end{figure}

\paragraph*{Discretisation and time-integration convergence}
The spatial-operator catalogue is calibrated against analytic
derivatives in \cref{FDConvergence}, where the Fornberg
finite-difference orders 2, 4 and 6 recover their predicted algebraic
rates $\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})$, $\mathcal{O}(N^{-4})$ and $\mathcal{O}(N^{-6})$ on
the smooth-gradient test, and the pseudo-spectral path drops to the
round-off floor at $N \approx 64$~\cite{fornberg1988generation,burns2020dedalus}.
We extend the spatial calibration to a physical example by running the
Proca-mass theory $\partial_t^2 a = \partial_x^2 a - m^2 a$ at $\Bzero = 0$
(decoupling the photon from the graviton) for a sweep of plane-wave
wavevectors $k$; for each $k$ we recover the angular frequency
$\omega$ by spatial-FFT identification of the dominant mode, time-FFT
of its complex amplitude with a Hann window, and quadratic interpolation
around the peak, and compare against $\omega(k) = \sqrt{k^2 + m^2}$
at $m^2 = 1$. The dispersion is reproduced to a maximum relative error
of $2.3\times 10^{-3}$ (median $1.5\times 10^{-3}$) across six $k$
values spanning the first six discrete Fourier modes of the periodic
domain. Time-integration convergence is calibrated by sweeping the
integrator step $\Delta t$ on a low-$\Bzero$ Einstein--Maxwell run
whose analytic $\Pconv^\mathrm{final}$ is in the perturbative band:
the log-log slope of $|P^\mathrm{sim} - P^\mathrm{ana}|$ versus
$\Delta t$ for the leapfrog Yoshida-2 scheme fits to $1.8$, close to
the predicted second-order rate~\cite{yoshida1990symplectic}; the
Yoshida-4 scheme reaches the spatial-discretisation error floor at
$\Delta t \approx 0.025$ and saturates~\cite{hairer2006geometric}, so
its fitted slope ($1.2$) reflects the saturated regime rather than the
pre-asymptotic fourth-order rate. The \CVODE{} adaptive path plateaus
at its rtol setting independently of $\Delta t$, as expected.
\Cref{DispersionTimeOrder} displays the dispersion overlay and the
$\Delta t$-error log--log fit side by side.
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/figD_dispersion_time_order}
  \caption{Discretisation and time-integration calibration.
    Left: the angular frequency $\omega(k)$ extracted by spatial-then-time
    FFT from \TIDAL{} simulations of the Proca-mass equation, overlaid
    with the analytic dispersion $\omega = \sqrt{k^2 + m^2}$ at the
    sweep value $m^2 = 1$. Right: the absolute $\Pconv^{\mathrm{final}}$
    error against the Boccaletti baseline as a function of the
    integrator step $\Delta t$ for leapfrog Yoshida-2, leapfrog
    Yoshida-4 and \CVODE; fitted log--log slopes are annotated and the
    expected algebraic-rate guides are drawn as dashed reference lines.}
  \label{DispersionTimeOrder}
\end{figure}

\paragraph*{Conservation diagnostics across the example library}
Energy conservation provides a structural diagnostic that engages every
layer of the pipeline at once; we calibrate \TIDAL{} across a broad
family of physically distinct examples and report the maximum relative
energy drift $|dE/E|_\mathrm{max}$ per example. The audit catches
pipeline regressions that point-validations would miss: a constraint
solver that returns inconsistent initial conditions, a symbolic stage
that loses cross-coupling terms in field decomposition, a time
integrator that drifts past its order's predicted threshold, or a
modal solver that mishandles its Schur complement. The Einstein--Maxwell
Gertsenshtein baseline and the gradient-coupled scalar pair both achieve
$|dE/E|_\mathrm{max} \approx 10^{-15}$ (median $8.9\times 10^{-16}$,
maximum $1.0\times 10^{-15}$), at the round-off floor of the modal
solver auto-selected by the dispatcher --- a fifteen-decade safety
margin against the default $10^{-3}$ conservation threshold. The audit
is not a per-example precision statement (the precision floor is set by
the backend the dispatcher selects, which differs across examples); it
is a regression diagnostic, and the machine-precision floor reached on
both structurally distinct examples is the calibration we take from it.
\Cref{ConservationAudit} displays the audit as a log-scale bar chart
per example with the default $10^{-3}$ threshold drawn for reference.
\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{figures/figD_conservation_audit}
  \caption{Energy-conservation audit across the example library.
    The maximum absolute relative energy drift $|dE/E|_\mathrm{max}$
    per example (bar height) on a logarithmic axis, with the default
    $10^{-3}$ conservation threshold drawn as a dashed horizontal
    reference. Examples are ordered as in the canonical audit JSON;
    bar colour distinguishes successful runs from any that fail
    structurally during the audit. The audit catches pipeline
    regressions that point-validations would miss.}
  \label{ConservationAudit}
\end{figure}
