\paragraph*{Boccaletti kernel on a uniform background}
The Gertsenshtein conversion kernel of \cref{GertsenshteinFormula}
is one of the closed-form analytic predictions we test \TIDAL{}
against. We sweep $\Bzero$ over the perturbative regime and overlay
the simulation against two analytic predictions in
\cref{BoccalettiUniform}: \cref{GertsenshteinFormula} and the
Raffelt--Stodolsky two-mode formula with graviton-effective-mass
detuning~\cite{raffelt1988mixing},
\begin{equation}
  \begin{split}
    \Pconv &= \frac{g^2}{g^2 + \Delta^2}\,
      \sin^2\!\left(\sqrt{g^2+\Delta^2}\,D\right), \\
    g &= \frac{\kappa\Bzero}{2},
    \qquad \Delta = -\frac{\kappa^2 \Bzero^2}{8\omega},
  \end{split}
  \label{TwoModeRabi}
\end{equation}
where $\omega \simeq k$ is the carrier angular frequency of the photon
mode. The graviton acquires an effective mass
$m_h^2 = \kappa^2\Bzero^2/2$ from the $\Bzero^2$ metric backreaction
(\cref{PerturbativityHierarchy}), detuning its dispersion against
the massless photon by $\Delta = m_h^2/(4\omega)$. Both the amplitude
prefactor and the oscillation frequency receive corrections from
this detuning; the bare Boccaletti formula
$\Pconv = \sin^2(\kappa\Bzero D/2)$ is recovered in the
$\Delta\to 0$ limit. The two predictions coincide while
$\kappa\Bzero t/2 \ll \pi/2$; the swept $\Bzero$ range extends to
values where the effective-mass detuning $\Delta$ of \cref{TwoModeRabi}
becomes significant and the two predictions diverge
(\cref{BoccalettiUniform}(a)).
The two-mode formula \cref{TwoModeRabi} truncates the full
EM PDE in two ways: it expands the graviton dispersion to leading
order in $m_h^2/k^2$ (\cref{PerturbativityHierarchy}) and discards
fast-oscillating cross-terms in the field amplitudes. Both
truncations contribute to $\Pconv$ at $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^4)$
relative to the two-mode prediction. \TIDAL{} evolves the full PDE
without either approximation, so the residual
$|P_\mathrm{TIDAL} - P_\mathrm{RS}|$ measures the size of the
truncated higher-order physics. At small $\Bzero$ the
panel-(a) residual sits at the modal-evolution IEEE-754 round-off
floor; as $\Bzero$ grows it rises into the range
$10^{-8}$--$10^{-6}$, of the same order as the $(\Delta/g)^4$
prediction at those $\Bzero$ values. The panel-(b) plateau at
$\sim 4\times 10^{-8}$ is of the same order as
$(\Delta/g)^4 = 6.3\times 10^{-8}$ at the regime point
$\Bzero = 0.1275$. \TIDAL{} therefore captures the
$\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^4)$ truncation error of the two-mode reduction
at the expected order of magnitude. Yoshida-2 converges
monotonically toward the $(\Delta/g)^4$ plateau under grid
refinement; Yoshida-4 shows the same non-monotonic residual
scatter under grid refinement that is documented for $\Delta t$
refinement in \cref{ConvergenceFigure}. The swept range stays within the
perturbative regime where both predictions remain valid; the
localised-profile sweep of \cref{BoccalettiLocalised} extends
past the first quarter-period ($s > \pi/2$), where the
$P_\mathrm{conv} \ll 1$ row of \cref{PerturbativityHierarchy} fails
and the bare path-integrated formula no longer applies.

\paragraph*{Boccaletti kernel on a Gaussian profile}
For a finite magnetised region of profile $B_x(z)$ the Gertsenshtein
formula generalises to the path-integrated form~\cite{raffelt1988mixing}
\begin{equation}
  \Pconv = \sin^2\!\left(
    \frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\!B_x(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\right),
  \label{BoccalettiPathIntegrated}
\end{equation}
and for the Gaussian profile
$B_x(z) = B_\mathrm{peak}\exp(-z^2/(2R^2))$ the integral gives
$\Pconv = \sin^2(\kappa B_\mathrm{peak}\,R\sqrt{\pi/2})$. We
reproduce this closed-form by sweeping the
$(B_\mathrm{peak}, R)$ plane using the derived localised
Gertsenshtein theory and a Gaussian wave-packet initial condition on
$h_+$ traversing the magnetised region. \Cref{BoccalettiLocalised}
shows every cell of the $(B_\mathrm{peak}, R)$ sweep collapsing
onto the single curve $P = \sin^2(s)$ in the dimensionless variable
$s = \kappa B_\mathrm{peak}\,R\sqrt{\pi/2}$, reproducing the
path-integrated kernel; finite-frequency dispersion corrections are
calibrated in \cref{ConvergenceFigure}.

\paragraph*{Klein--Gordon perturbative calibration}
The perturbative-reduction machinery of \cref{PerturbativeReduction}
(Pass 0/Pass 1 Duhamel kernel, \cref{DuhamelKernel}) evolves the
system as a base (order-$\varepsilon^{0}$) solution plus a correction
(order-$\varepsilon^{1}$), denoted \emph{Pass 0} and \emph{Pass 1} in
the figure legends of \cref{ForcedOscillator,ParkerSimon}. We test the kernel
against the analytical perturbative expansion of a Klein--Gordon
wave with a small mass-shift correction,
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t^2\phi - \partial_x^2\phi + m^2\phi
  \;=\; -\varepsilon\,\phi,
  \label{KGMassShift}
\end{equation}
whose full-$\varepsilon$ modal solution is closed-form. The
order-$\varepsilon^{0}$ solution evolves the $\varepsilon=0$ base;
the order-$\varepsilon^{1}$ correction is integrated against the
base trajectory; the combined approximation
$\phi^{(0)} + \phi^{(1)}$ matches the full solution to
$\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$ by construction. \Cref{ForcedOscillator} shows the slope of
$|\phi^{(0)} + \phi^{(1)} - \phi^\mathrm{full}|$ versus $\varepsilon$
recovers the expected algebraic order of error scaling.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/figD_forced_oscillator}
  \caption{Perturbed-Klein--Gordon calibration of the Pass 0 + Pass 1
    Duhamel kernel. Pass 0 error and combined Pass 0 + Pass 1 error
    versus $\varepsilon$ for the mass-shift correction
    \cref{KGMassShift}, sampled at eight logarithmically spaced
    points covering $\varepsilon \in [10^{-3}, 0.2]$ at $k=1$; fitted
    slopes and $R^2$ values are reported in the legend. The
    Pass 0 + Pass 1 error scales as $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$, an
    order of magnitude per half-decade in $\varepsilon$.}
  \label{ForcedOscillator}
\end{figure}

\paragraph*{Higher-derivative perturbative calibration}
A second perturbative calibration replaces the mass shift of
\cref{KGMassShift} with a fourth-order spatial correction,
\begin{equation}
  \partial_t^2 \phi - \partial_x^2 \phi + m^2 \phi
  \;=\; -\varepsilon\,\partial_x^4 \phi,
  \label{KGBiharmonic}
\end{equation}
the canonical higher-derivative form encountered in the perturbative
reductions of Parker and Simon~\cite{parker1993reduced}. The correction exercises a
different Fourier multiplier ($k^4$) from the mass shift ($1$), and
therefore a different path through the correction-source assembly of
\cref{LPSSubstitution}. At $k=2$ the order-$\varepsilon^{0,1}$
truncation error falls faster than the $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$
worst-case scaling of the reduced-order
construction~\cite{parker1993reduced}; the improvement ratio over
the base solution reaches $\sim 10^4$ at $\varepsilon=0.01$ in
\cref{ParkerSimon}.

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/figD_parker_simon}
  \caption{Higher-derivative perturbative calibration. Pass 0 and
    Pass 0 + Pass 1 error versus $\varepsilon$ for the biharmonic
    correction \cref{KGBiharmonic} on the Klein--Gordon base of
    \cref{KGMassShift}. The combined-path error falls faster than
    the worst-case $\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^2)$ bound at small

\paragraph*{Convention-anchored agreement with Euler--Heisenberg QED birefringence}
The QED Euler--Heisenberg effective action in the weak-field
limit~\cite{heisenberg1936consequences,schwinger1951gauge,dunne2004heisenberg,adler1971photon}
is, to leading order in the field strength,
\begin{equation}
  \lag_{\mathrm{EH}} \;=\;
    -\tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
    + \tfrac{1}{8}\,\rho\,
      \bigl(\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}\bigr)^{2}
    + \tfrac{1}{8}\,\sigma\,
      \bigl(\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldFtilde{^{\mu\nu}}\bigr)^{2},
  \label{LagEH}
\end{equation}
with $\rho = 4\alpha^{2}/(45\,m_{e}^{4}\,\pi^{2})$ and
$\sigma = 7\alpha^{2}/(90\,m_{e}^{4}\,\pi^{2})$ the standard QED
weak-field coefficients. \TIDAL{}'s symbolic stage routes the
$(F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu})^{2}$ correction onto the perpendicular
photon polarisation and the $(F_{\mu\nu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu})^{2}$
correction onto the parallel polarisation; the resulting
refractive-index shifts
$n_\perp - 1 = (8\alpha^2/45)(B/B_c)^2$ and
$n_\parallel - 1 = (14\alpha^2/45)(B/B_c)^2$ reproduce the Adler
birefringence ratio
$(n_\parallel - 1)/(n_\perp - 1) = 4\sigma/\rho =
7/4$~\cite{adler1971photon} exactly.

\paragraph*{Cross-formulation equivalence: Proca versus vector-torsion}%
\label{ProcaVTEquivalence}
For complex PGT theories where no closed-form solution and no
weak-field analytic limit is available --- the bulk of the
\cref{SectorSurvey} campaigns --- the only available consistency
check is to compare the same continuum action derived via two
mathematically distinct field formulations. The
dark-photon-plasma sector affords this comparison directly. The
\emph{Proca} representation expresses the torsion-coupling as a
massive vector field $t_\mu$ with a canonical Proca kinetic
term~\cite{caputo2021darkphoton,pospelov2008secluded},
\begin{equation}
  \lag_{\mathrm{Proca}} \;\supset\;
   -\tfrac14 \xi\,(\partial T)^2
   - \tfrac12 m_T^2\,t\!\cdot\! t
   + \delta_m\,\FieldF{}\!\cdot\!(\partial T),
  \label{LagFV}
\end{equation}
carrying $10$ dynamical fields with kinetic-matrix condition number
$\operatorname{cond}(V)$~\cite{higham2008functions}~$\sim 10^{8\text{--}10}$.

The
\emph{vector-torsion} representation expresses the same theory using
the trace of the spacetime torsion tensor evolved with the
Maxwell-like field-strength tensor
$\FieldF{^{(T)}_{\mu\nu}} \equiv 2\,\partial_{[\mu} T_{\nu]}$ of the
propagating vector-torsion construction of~\cite{barker2024poincare},
with the torsion trace $T_\mu \equiv T^\nu{}_{\nu\mu}$ promoted to a
propagating vector field,
%TODO replace prose description with a direct cref to the propagating-vector-torsion sector definition once that lands in results.tex.
kinetic prefactor $\xi$,
gauge-coupling shift $\alpha_3$, and mass mixing $\delta_m$ (the
Lagrangian appears with the dark-photon-plasma sector in
\cref{SectorSurvey}%
%TODO replace with explicit equation cref once the vector-torsion Lagrangian lands in results.tex
), carrying $18$ fields including redundant constrained
directions ($\operatorname{cond}(V) \sim 10^{15\text{--}17}$). The
continuum equivalence is recovered under the map
$m_T^2 = +2\alpha_3$, derived by squaring the Proca mass term and
identifying it with the trace contribution to $I_3$. The two
formulations agree on $\Pmax$ to a maximum relative difference of
$1.1\times 10^{-14}$ across five parameter points spanning the
surveyed sector --- within a few times the IEEE-754 round-off floor
of the modal solver.

\paragraph*{Multi-method agreement across backends and measurement paths}
We audit the \TIDAL{} dispatcher (see \cref{NumericalEvolution})
by running each applicable backend on the

two-channel mixing model~\cite{raffelt1988mixing}, and checking

\paragraph*{Spatial and temporal convergence}\label{ConvergenceCalibration}
The spatial-operator catalogue is tested against analytic
derivatives in \cref{FDConvergence}, where the Fornberg
FD orders recover their predicted
algebraic rates and the pseudo-spectral path falls to the round-off
floor by $N\approx 64$~\cite{fornberg1988generation,burns2020dedalus}.
We extend the spatial calibration to a physical example by running
the Proca-mass equation at $\Bzero=0$ for a $k$-sweep at three
values of $m^2 \in \{0.1, 1.0, 10.0\}$; for each $(k, m^2)$ the
angular frequency is recovered by spatial-FFT identification of the
dominant mode, time-FFT of its complex amplitude with a Hann window,
and quadratic peak refinement, then compared to
$\omega(k) = \sqrt{k^2 + m^2}$. The Rabi-frequency extraction
itself is FFT-bin-width bounded at
$|\Omega_\mathrm{eff}/\Omega_\mathrm{theory} - 1| \approx 7\times 10^{-3}$
across the surveyed $N$, well below the spatial FD-4 error of
\cref{FDConvergence} and not the limiting diagnostic at this
resolution. Time-integration convergence is reported in
\cref{ConvergenceFigure} for the symplectic backends, where
Yoshida-2 recovers its formal slope to four-figure precision
($\hat{\alpha} = 2.00$, $R^2 = 1.000$). Yoshida-4 instead exhibits
reproducible non-power-law scatter spanning two orders of magnitude
at fixed $\Delta t$; the same pattern reappears in
\cref{BoccalettiUniform}(b) under grid refinement at fixed
$\Delta t$. The scatter is bit-identical across reruns with $2$
versus $20$ snapshots and across $P_\mathrm{final}$ versus
$P_\mathrm{max}$ extraction, ruling out snapshot-timing and
measurement-extraction artefacts; modal, \CVODE, and Yoshida-2 are
smooth on the same data. We attribute the residual Yoshida-4
pathology to a still-uncharacterised interaction between the
Yoshida composition and either the coupled two-field structure or
the two well-separated frequency scales
($\kappa\Bzero/2 = 0.025$, $\omega_k \approx 2.0$) of the
EM baseline.

\paragraph*{Energy-conservation audit}
Energy conservation provides a structural diagnostic that engages
every layer of the workflow.
% at once: a constraint solver that
% returns inconsistent initial conditions, a symbolic stage that
% loses cross-coupling terms in field decomposition, or a time
% integrator that drifts past its predicted order, all reveal
% themselves as relative energy drift.
We audit the maximum relative
energy drift $|\Delta E/E|_\mathrm{max}$ across the theories of physical
relevance to the \cref{Results} campaigns.
% the EM
% Gertsenshtein baseline of \cref{GertsenshteinFormula}, a
% gradient-coupled scalar pair (an analogue of \cref{TwoModeRabi}),
% the propagating-PGT and nonminimal-torsion variants of
% \cref{SectorSurvey}, the dark-photon-plasma sector,
% a Proca-mass photon, and a linearised gravitational wave.
Successful examples
cluster at $\sim 10^{-15}$, near the IEEE-754 round-off floor of
the modal solver. The precision floor of each row in
\cref{ConservationAudit} is set by the backend the dispatcher
selects, which differs across theories.
