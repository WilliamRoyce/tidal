\section{General quadratic PGT Lagrangian coupled to electromagnetism: sector-by-sector enumeration}
\label{PGTEnumeration}

Our scope is the set of PGT Lagrangians coupled to
electromagnetism whose dynamics are tractable at quadratic order
in the field perturbations around a static magnetic background.
The literature on Poincar\'{e} gauge
theory~\cite{kibble1961lorentz,hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric,blagojevic2002gravitation,blagojevic2003lectures,sezgin1980ghostfree,hayashi1979new,lin2019pgt,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,karananas2014parity,aoki2020nonlinear,barker2024poincare,barker2021hamiltonian,bahamonde2025cubic}
and on torsion--electromagnetism
couplings~\cite{hehl2000electromagnetic,itin2004maxwell,rubilar2003torsion,shapiro2002torsion,obukhov2024electrodynamics,bahamonde2025torsion}
motivates which combinations are studied as named theories in
\cref{SectorSurvey}, but the present catalogue covers the full
operator content within the cutoff below --- including sectors
that have been excluded for simplicity in the prior PGT
literature, such as the parity-odd quadratic invariants
explicitly omitted by~\cite{barker2024poincare} and treated only
in part by~\cite{karananas2014parity,blagojevic2018hamiltonian}.

We adopt an arbitrary cutoff at cubic field-strength count ---
operators with at most three factors drawn from
$\{\mathcal{R}, T, F\}$. The standard PGT cutoff is quadratic in
field
strengths~\cite{blagojevic2018hamiltonian,shapiro2002torsion},
but the cubic completions of \cref{CubicCompletions} contribute
at quadratic order in the perturbation through one or two
background-field insertions, so they are retained alongside the
quadratic terms. The cutoff is a convention rather than a
derivable boundary: ghost-safety alone admits operators of
arbitrarily high power in $T$~\cite{shapiro2002torsion}. An
effective-field-theory classification ordering operators by
canonical mass dimension would in principle remove this
arbitrariness, but assigning canonical dimensions requires first
identifying the propagating modes --- and the propagating modes
depend on which kinetic terms are present~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear},
which is exactly the choice the enumeration is meant to expose;
we therefore do not pursue an EFT ordering here.

Within this scope, the Lagrangian is built from
scalar contractions of
$\{\MAGFt{^\sigma_{\mu\nu\rho}},\,\MAGT{^\sigma_{\mu\nu}},\,\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}},\,\PD{_\mu},\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\}$,
arranged into the seven sector blocks of \cref{QuadraticAction}.
We generate the basis of contractions at each operator class
using the systematic-contraction enumeration
of~\cite{nutma2014xtras}, applying the within-pair antisymmetries
of the Riemann--Cartan curvature,
$\MAGFt{_{(\mu\nu)\rho\sigma}} = \MAGFt{_{\mu\nu(\rho\sigma)}} = 0$,
the antisymmetries $\MAGT{^\sigma_{[\mu\nu]}}$ and
$\FieldF{_{[\mu\nu]}}$, and the total antisymmetry of
$\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$, together with integration by
parts, to project out manifestly equivalent operators. We do not attempt the residual Bianchi-identity and
dimension-dependent-identity\footnote{Dimension-dependent
identities~\cite{edgarhoglund2002ddi} are algebraic relations
among tensors that hold only
in a fixed spacetime dimension --- here, $D=4$. The canonical
example is that antisymmetrisation over five or more indices
vanishes identically, so any tensor expression containing a
totally antisymmetric subblock on five or more indices reduces
to combinations of expressions with smaller antisymmetric
blocks. Their application reduces redundancies in the operator
basis beyond what Bianchi-identity reductions alone achieve.}
reductions of these ans\"atze in the present work; the
parity-odd, derivative, and cubic sectors are correspondingly
flagged in \cref{MasterParameterCount} with $\dagger$ as upper
bounds, and a complete reduction is left for future work.

The post-Riemannian field parameterisation of \cref{PGTBackground}
exchanges the affine connection for the metric and the torsion
tensor, allowing the torsion to be decomposed into its
irreducible Lorentz components; we use the irreducible labels of
\cref{IrreducibleTorsion} only as named handles for the counts
below, with the explicit $3\times 3$ basis transformation
between scalar and irreducible torsion-squared invariants given
at \cref{DirectIrreducibleTransform} of \cref{PGTBackground}.

The full inventory is collected in \cref{MasterParameterCount}, with
the literature equivalents of each sector noted alongside; the
remainder of this appendix discusses each block in turn.

\paragraph*{Linear sector}
\label{LinearSector}
The linear sector contains three independent
invariants~\cite{kibble1961lorentz,holst1995barbero} and reads
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_\text{lin}
  = -\frac{1}{\kappa^2}\,\MAGFt{}
  -\frac{2\Lambda}{\kappa^2}
  +\frac{1}{\kappa^2\,\gamma_\text{BI}}\,
   \varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\MAGFt{_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}},
  \label{LinearAction}
\end{align}
where $\gamma_\text{BI}$ is the Barbero--Immirzi
parameter~\cite{barbero1995real,immirzi1997real}.
The Holst term is a topological density in the torsion-free
limit, proportional there to the Pontryagin density modulo
Bianchi identities~\cite{hehlobukhov2007cartan,shapiro2002torsion},
but becomes dynamical with torsion present
through the Nieh--Yan identity~\cite{niehyan1982}; its variation
is therefore exchangeable with one parity-odd
$\varepsilon\!\cdot\!\mathcal{T}^2$ coefficient introduced below.
We retain $\gamma_\text{BI}$ and $d_{14}\dots d_{17}$ as
independent symbols in the catalogue and do not use the
Nieh--Yan trade to reduce the parameter count, since the natural
parametrisation in each sector is more transparent than the
reduced one.

\paragraph*{Parity-even quadratic sector}
\label{ParityEvenQuadratic}
The parity-even quadratic sector contains ten independent free
parameters together with the canonically-normalised Maxwell
term: six $\mathcal{R}^2$ contractions~\cite{lin2019pgt,barker2021hamiltonian,barker2024poincare},
three torsion-squared invariants in the direct basis
$\{I_1,I_2,I_3\}$ of \cref{TorsionDirectBasis}~\cite{shapiro2002torsion,hayashi1979new},
and the parity-even curvature--electromagnetic contraction
$\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$~\cite{bahamonde2025torsion},
alongside the Maxwell term $-\tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$
which carries no free coefficient. Together these read
\begin{widetext}
\begin{multline}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}^2}
  +\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}^2}
  +\mathcal{L}_{F^2}
  +\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R} F}
  =
  \Alp{1}\,\MAGFt{}^2
  +\MAGFt{_{\mu\nu}}\bigl(\Alp{2}\MAGFt{^{\mu\nu}}+\Alp{3}\MAGFt{^{\nu\mu}}\bigr)
  +\Alp{4}\,\MAGFt{_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}}\MAGFt{^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}}
  +\Alp{5}\,\MAGFt{_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}}\MAGFt{^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}}
  +\Alp{6}\,\MAGFt{_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}}\MAGFt{^{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}} \\
  +\Bet{1}\,I_1
  +\Bet{2}\,I_2
  +\Bet{3}\,I_3
  -\tfrac{1}{4}\,\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\,\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
  +\Del{1}\,\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\,\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}},
  \label{ParityEvenQuadraticAction}
\end{multline}
\end{widetext}
The labels $\Alp{n}$, $\Bet{n}$, $\Del{1}$ follow the convention
of~\cite{barker2024poincare}; \cref{MasterParameterCount} records
the alternative parametrisation of~\cite{lin2019pgt}.

All six $\mathcal{R}^2$ contractions are independent when torsion
is present: the Riemann--Cartan curvature lacks the pair-exchange
symmetry of the torsion-free Riemann tensor (i.e.,
$\MAGFt{_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}} \neq \MAGFt{_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}}$ in
general), since the antisymmetric Ricci $\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}$
does not vanish in the presence of
torsion~\cite{shapiro2002torsion,hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric,barker2024poincare},
and additional independent contractions enter beyond the
torsion-free Riemann-square count. The count reduces to two ---
a Riemann-square and the Gauss--Bonnet topological
combination~\cite{lin2019pgt} --- in the torsion-free limit. The
parity-even $\mathcal{R} F$ contraction $\Del{1}\,\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$
likewise vanishes in the torsion-free limit, since it requires a
non-zero antisymmetric Ricci to contract against the Maxwell
field strength; the parity-odd partners of this cross-coupling
are catalogued separately below.

Ghost- and tachyon-freedom of this combined parameter family is
analysed
in~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,aoki2020nonlinear,barker2021hamiltonian},
with the no-tachyon conditions of these analyses constraining
the parameter region accessible to the present work. Direct
integration of the present enumeration with
\PSALTer{}~\cite{barker2024psalter} for automated extraction of
the no-tachyon spectrum is left for future work. The
torsion-squared sub-Lagrangian is equivalently presented in the
irreducible basis $\{q^2,\,S^2,\,T^2\}$ via the linear map
collected at \cref{TorsionSquaredCouplingMap} below.

\paragraph*{Parity-odd quadratic sector}
\label{ParityOddQuadratic}
The parity-odd quadratic sector adds twenty-one
$\varepsilon$-weighted invariants, of which twenty are dynamical.
Thirteen are Pontryagin-type contractions~\cite{karananas2014parity,barker2025psalter2}
of the form $\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\MAGFt{}\MAGFt{}$
(with parameters $d_1\dots d_{13}$ in
\cref{MasterParameterCount}); these collapse to a single
topological density in the torsion-free
limit~\cite{shapiro2002torsion,karananas2014parity} but
generically propagate when torsion is present. Four are
Nieh--Yan-type contractions~\cite{niehyan1982} of the form
$\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\MAGT{}\MAGT{}$
($d_{14}\dots d_{17}$); the family contains the Nieh--Yan form
whose total-derivative identity dynamically links it to the
Holst term of \cref{LinearAction}. One is the electromagnetic
$\theta$-term~\cite{wilczek1987axion}
$d_{18}\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{_{\rho\sigma}} = 4\,d_{18}\,\PD{_\mu}\bigl(\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\FieldA{_\nu}\PD{_\rho}\FieldA{_\sigma}\bigr)$,
a total derivative on flat space and therefore non-dynamical. The remaining three
are parity-odd curvature--electromagnetic cross-terms
$\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\MAGFt{}\FieldF{}$
($d_{19},d_{20},d_{21}$), opening through the same antisymmetric
Ricci that activates the parity-even cross-coupling. The
propagator-level analysis of these
sectors~\cite{karananas2014parity,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,barker2025psalter2}
identifies the parity-violating mass spectra associated with each
combination, together with the additional parameter constraints
required to maintain ghost- and tachyon-freedom alongside those
of the parity-even sector.

\paragraph*{Ghost-free derivative extensions}
\label{DerivativeExtensions}
A covariant derivative adds one index, enabling cross-sectors
that vanish without it. In the first-order formalism
$\PD{_\mu}\MAGT{} \sim \PD{_\mu}\MAGA{} \sim \MAGFt{}$, so
operators built from $\{\MAGFt{},\,\MAGT{},\,\FieldF{},\,\PD{}\MAGT{}\}$\footnote{We
use the symbol $\partial$ for the derivative acting on $T$
throughout this appendix. In Riemann--Cartan geometry the natural
derivative on $T$ is the covariant $\nabla$; the two differ by
torsion-coupled lower-rank operators that are already enumerated
in the $\mathcal{T}^2$ family of \cref{ParityEvenQuadratic}, and
an explicit verification that the basis is closed under this
redefinition is left for future work.}
remain Yang--Mills-type and produce at most second-order
equations of motion; sectors involving $\PD{}\MAGFt{}$ or
$\PD{}\FieldF{}$ generate fourth-order equations and are excluded
as ghost-prone~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear,nikiforova2009torsion}.
Three parity-even safe sectors then follow:
$\PD{}\MAGT{}\!\times\!\FieldF{}$, generating three independent
invariants $\zeta_{1,2,3}$ in the parity-even sector of Shapiro's
non-minimal classification~\cite{shapiro2002torsion} (the
explicit linear map between the present basis and Shapiro's
$\theta_n$ is left for future work);
$\MAGFt{}\!\times\!\PD{}\MAGT{}$, of which Barker's $\chi$
kinetic mixing is a specific contraction~\cite{barker2024poincare};
and $(\PD{}\MAGT{})^2$, containing Barker's $\xi$ Maxwell-type
kinetic term~\cite{barker2024poincare,barker2021hamiltonian},
which is the unique contraction that propagates the trace
torsion vector. The two latter sectors carry up to ten and
sixteen invariants respectively before Bianchi reduction. Their
$\varepsilon$-twins furnish the corresponding parity-odd
derivative sectors $(\tilde\zeta,\tilde\chi,\tilde\xi)$. The ghost-free
conditions on these combinations follow from the standard
linearised propagator analysis applied to the post-Riemannian
expansion~\cite{lin2019pgt,blagojevic2018hamiltonian}.

\paragraph*{Cubic completions}
\label{CubicCompletions}
Cubic operators contribute at quadratic order in the perturbation
when one or two field-strength factors are absorbed into a
non-zero classical background, since each background insertion
removes one power of the dynamical fields. With a uniform
magnetic background $\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}$, four parity-even
cubic sectors enter the linearised analysis, each with the
schematic structure of a quadratic operator dressed by one or
two background insertions:
$\mathcal{R}\!\times\!F^2$ ($\lambda_{1\dots 4}$, the Gertsenshtein
channel into curvature--photon mixing~\cite{itin2004maxwell,bahamonde2025torsion}),
$\mathcal{T}^2\!\times\!F$ ($\kappa_{1\dots 3}$, generating
torsion-mass corrections proportional to $\bar B$),
$\mathcal{R}^2\!\times\!F$ ($\rho_{1\dots 8}$, modifying the
quadratic graviton self-interaction in the magnetic background),
and $\PD{}\mathcal{T}\!\times\!F^2$ ($\mu_{1\dots 5}$, a
torsion--photon conversion channel proportional to $\bar B$).
The parity-odd partners $(\tilde\lambda,\tilde\kappa,\tilde\mu)$
enumerated in \cref{MasterParameterCount} arise by
$\varepsilon$-contraction of the same parity-even contractions.
The cubic operators are independent coupling constants of the
full theory~\cite{bahamonde2025cubic}.

\paragraph*{Chern--Simons torsion--EM couplings}
\label{ChernSimonsCouplings}
Beyond the field-strength couplings catalogued above, the
electromagnetic gauge potential $\FieldA{_\mu}$ admits three
irreducible parity-odd contractions with the torsion
tensor~\cite{hehl2000electromagnetic,itin2004maxwell,rubilar2003torsion,obukhov2024electrodynamics}:
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_\text{CS}
  &= \chi^\text{CS}_1\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\T[2]{_\mu}\,\FieldA{_\nu}\,\FieldF{_{\rho\sigma}} \nonumber \\
  &\quad +\chi^\text{CS}_2\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\T[3]{_\mu}\,\FieldA{_\nu}\,\FieldF{_{\rho\sigma}} \nonumber \\
  &\quad +\chi^\text{CS}_3\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\T[1]{^\lambda_{\mu\alpha}}\,g^\alpha{}_\nu\,\FieldA{_\lambda}\,\FieldF{_{\rho\sigma}},
  \label{ChernSimonsAction}
\end{align}
contracting the photon with the trace, axial, and tensor
irreducible parts of the torsion respectively. The first two
families are Chern--Simons-like in the conventional sense ---
their $\MAGT{}\,\FieldA{}\,\FieldF{}$ structure mirrors the
gauge-field Chern--Simons three-form --- and the axial coupling
$\chi^\text{CS}_2$ sources the magnetic-helicity density
$\FieldA{_i}\FieldF{^{0i}}\!\sim\!\bm{A}\!\cdot\!\bm{B}$,
producing the parity-odd birefringence and dichroism signatures
analysed in~\cite{rubilar2003torsion,obukhov2024electrodynamics}.
The third coupling, contracting the Maxwell pair with the
tensor torsion, vanishes whenever the irreducible tensor part
$\T[1]{}$ vanishes; it is the natural channel through which a
non-trivial spin-two torsion sector enters the photon system.

\paragraph*{Direct--irreducible basis translation}
\label{DirectIrreducibleTranslation}
Two equivalent presentations of the parity-even torsion-squared
sub-Lagrangian appear in the literature: the direct basis
$\{I_1,I_2,I_3\}$ of \cref{TorsionDirectBasis}, in which each
invariant is a single index contraction of two torsion tensors,
and the irreducible basis $\{q^2,S^2,T^2\}$ built from the
squared norms of the irreducible parts $\T[1]{}$, $\T[3]{}$, and
$\T[2]{}$ of \cref{IrreducibleTorsion}. The two are related by an
invertible linear map; for the Lagrangian coefficients,
\begin{gather}
  \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}^2}
  = a\,q^2 + b\,S^2 + c\,T^2,
  \label{TorsionSquaredCouplingMap} \\
  a = \Bet{1}+\Bet{2},
  \quad
  b = -\tfrac{1}{6}\bigl(\Bet{1}-\Bet{2}\bigr),
  \quad
  c = \Bet{3}+\tfrac{1}{3}\bigl(2\Bet{1}+\Bet{2}\bigr), \nonumber
\end{gather}
following the conventions of~\cite{shapiro2002torsion}; the
explicit $3\times 3$ matrix between $\{I_1,I_2,I_3\}$ and
$\{q^2,S^2,T^2\}$ that induces this coefficient map, together
with its inverse, is collected at
\cref{DirectIrreducibleTransform} of \cref{PGTBackground}.
Coupling-constant orderings and basis labels differ across the
literature~\cite{hayashi1979new,sezgin1980ghostfree,lin2019pgt,blagojevic2018hamiltonian};
\cref{TorsionSquaredCouplingMap} fixes the convention used
throughout the present work, with $\Alp{n}$ multiplying
curvature-squared invariants and $\Bet{n}$ multiplying
torsion-squared invariants.
