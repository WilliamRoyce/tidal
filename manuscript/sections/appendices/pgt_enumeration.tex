\section{General quadratic PGT Lagrangian coupled to electromagnetism: sector-by-sector enumeration}
\label{PGTEnumeration}

We enumerate the independent invariants of the general PGT
Lagrangian coupled to electromagnetism around a static magnetic
background.
The literature on PGT~\cite{kibble1961lorentz,hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric,blagojevic2002gravitation,blagojevic2003lectures,sezgin1980ghostfree,hayashi1979new,lin2019pgt,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,karananas2014parity,aoki2020nonlinear,barker2024poincare,barker2021hamiltonian,bahamonde2025cubic}
and on torsion--electromagnetism
couplings~\cite{hehl2000electromagnetic,itin2004maxwell,rubilar2003torsion,shapiro2002torsion,obukhov2024electrodynamics,bahamonde2025torsion}
motivates which combinations are studied as named theories in
\cref{SectorSurvey}; the present catalogue covers the full
operator content within the cutoff below, including sectors
omitted by~\cite{barker2024poincare} and treated only partly
by~\cite{karananas2014parity,blagojevic2018hamiltonian}.

We adopt an arbitrary cutoff at cubic field-strength count, with
operators carrying at most three factors drawn from
$\{\mathcal{R}, \mathcal{T}, \mathcal{F}\}$. The standard PGT cutoff is quadratic in
field
strengths~\cite{blagojevic2018hamiltonian,shapiro2002torsion},
but cubic completions admitted below contribute at quadratic
order in the perturbation through one or two
background-field insertions, so they are retained alongside the
quadratic terms. No physical principle fixes this cutoff: ghost-safety alone
admits operators of arbitrarily high power in $\mathcal{T}$. An
effective-field-theory (EFT) classification ordering operators by
canonical mass dimension would remove this arbitrariness, but
the canonical dimensions depend on the propagating modes, which
in turn depend on which kinetic terms are
present~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear}; we therefore do not pursue an
EFT ordering here.

The Lagrangian is built from
scalar contractions of
$\{\MAGFt{^\sigma_{\mu\nu\rho}},\,\MAGT{^\sigma_{\mu\nu}},\,\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}},\,\rcD{_\mu},\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\}$,
arranged into the seven sector blocks of \cref{QuadraticAction}.
We generate the basis of contractions at each operator class
using the systematic-contraction enumeration
of~\cite{nutma2014xtras}, applying the pair antisymmetries $\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]\rho\sigma}}$
and $\MAGFt{_{\mu\nu[\rho\sigma]}}$ that survive in the
Riemann--Cartan setting,
the antisymmetries $\MAGT{^\sigma_{[\mu\nu]}}$ and
$\FieldF{_{[\mu\nu]}}$, and the total antisymmetry of
$\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$, together with integration by
parts, to remove operators equivalent up to a covariant total divergence. We do not attempt the residual Bianchi-identity and
dimension-dependent-identity\footnote{Dimension-dependent
identities~\cite{edgarhoglund2002ddi} are algebraic relations
among tensors that hold only
in a fixed spacetime dimension --- here, $D=4$. The canonical
example is that antisymmetrisation over five or more indices
vanishes identically, so any tensor expression containing a
totally antisymmetric subblock on five or more indices reduces
to combinations of expressions with smaller antisymmetric
blocks. Their application reduces redundancies in the operator
basis beyond what Bianchi-identity reductions alone achieve.}
reductions of these ans\"atze in the present work; the
parity-odd, derivative, and cubic sectors are correspondingly
flagged in \cref{MasterParameterCount} with $\dagger$ as upper
bounds, and a complete reduction is left for future work.

The irreducible torsion parts $\T[1]{},\T[2]{},\T[3]{}$ used in
the Chern--Simons couplings below follow the conventions of
\cref{PGTBackground}.

The inventory is collected in \cref{MasterParameterCount}.

\paragraph*{Linear sector}
\label{LinearSector}
The linear sector contains three independent
invariants~\cite{kibble1961lorentz,holst1995barbero} and reads
\begin{align}
  \mathcal{L}_\text{lin}
  = -\frac{1}{\kappa^2}\,\MAGFt{}
  -\frac{2\Lambda}{\kappa^2}
  +\frac{1}{\kappa^2\,\gamma_\text{BI}}\,
   \varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\MAGFt{_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}},
  \label{LinearAction}
\end{align}
where $\gamma_\text{BI}$ is the Barbero--Immirzi
parameter~\cite{barbero1995real,immirzi1997real}.
The Holst term is a topological density in the torsion-free
limit, proportional there to the Pontryagin density modulo
Bianchi identities~\cite{hehlobukhov2007cartan,shapiro2002torsion},
but becomes dynamical with torsion present
through the Nieh--Yan identity~\cite{niehyan1982}; its variation
is exchangeable with one parity-odd
$\varepsilon\!\cdot\!\mathcal{T}^2$ coefficient introduced below.
We retain $\gamma_\text{BI}$ and $d_{14}\dots d_{17}$ as
independent symbols rather than using the Nieh--Yan trade to
reduce the parameter count by one.

\paragraph*{Parity-even quadratic sector}
\label{ParityEvenQuadratic}
The parity-even quadratic sector contains ten independent free
parameters together with the canonically-normalised Maxwell
term: six $\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^2$ contractions~\cite{lin2019pgt,barker2021hamiltonian,barker2024poincare},
three torsion-squared invariants in the direct basis
$\{I_1,I_2,I_3\}$ of \cref{TorsionDirectBasis}~\cite{shapiro2002torsion,hayashi1979new},
and the parity-even curvature--electromagnetic contraction
$\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$~\cite{bahamonde2025torsion},
alongside the Maxwell term $-\tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$
which carries no free coefficient. Together these read
\begin{widetext}
\begin{multline}
  \mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^2}
  +\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}^2}
  +\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{F}^2}
  +\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{\mathcal{R}} \mathcal{F}}
  =
  \Alp{1}\,\MAGFt{}^2
  +\MAGFt{_{\mu\nu}}\bigl(\Alp{2}\MAGFt{^{\mu\nu}}+\Alp{3}\MAGFt{^{\nu\mu}}\bigr)
  +\Alp{4}\,\MAGFt{_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}}\MAGFt{^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}}
  +\Alp{5}\,\MAGFt{_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}}\MAGFt{^{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}}
  +\Alp{6}\,\MAGFt{_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}}\MAGFt{^{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}} \\
  +\Bet{1}\,I_1
  +\Bet{2}\,I_2
  +\Bet{3}\,I_3
  -\tfrac{1}{4}\,\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\,\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
  +\Del{1}\,\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\,\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}},
  \label{ParityEvenQuadraticAction}
\end{multline}
\end{widetext}
The labels $\Alp{n}$, $\Bet{n}$, $\Del{1}$ follow the convention
of~\cite{barker2024poincare}; \cref{MasterParameterCount} records
the alternative parametrisation of~\cite{lin2019pgt}.

All six $\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^2$ contractions are independent when torsion
is present: the Riemann--Cartan curvature lacks the pair-exchange
symmetry of the torsion-free Riemann tensor (i.e.,
$\MAGFt{_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}} \neq \MAGFt{_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}}$ in
general), since the antisymmetric Ricci $\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}$
does not vanish in the presence of
torsion~\cite{shapiro2002torsion,hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric,barker2024poincare},
and additional independent contractions enter beyond the
torsion-free Riemann-square count. In the torsion-free limit the
count reduces to two (a Riemann-square and the Gauss--Bonnet
topological combination~\cite{lin2019pgt}), and the parity-even
$\tilde{\mathcal{R}} \mathcal{F}$ contraction
$\Del{1}\,\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$ likewise
vanishes since it requires a non-zero antisymmetric Ricci to
contract against the Maxwell field strength; the parity-odd
partners of this cross-coupling are catalogued separately below.

Ghost- and tachyon-freedom of this combined parameter family is
analysed
in~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,aoki2020nonlinear,barker2021hamiltonian},
Integration with
\PSALTer{}~\cite{barker2024psalter} for automated no-tachyon
extraction remains for future work.

\paragraph*{Parity-odd quadratic sector}
\label{ParityOddQuadratic}
The parity-odd quadratic sector adds twenty-one
$\varepsilon$-weighted invariants, of which twenty are dynamical.
Thirteen are parity-odd curvature-square contractions
(Pontryagin-type~\cite{karananas2014parity,barker2025psalter2})
of the form $\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\MAGFt{}\MAGFt{}$,
generalising the Pontryagin topological density of
pseudo-Riemannian geometry to the torsionful setting (with
parameters $d_1\dots d_{13}$ in \cref{MasterParameterCount});
these collapse to a single topological density in the
torsion-free limit~\cite{shapiro2002torsion,karananas2014parity}
but generically propagate when torsion is present. Four are
parity-odd torsion-square contractions
(Nieh--Yan-type~\cite{niehyan1982}) of the form
$\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\MAGT{}\MAGT{}$
($d_{14}\dots d_{17}$); the family contains the Nieh--Yan form
$\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}T^a{}_{\mu\nu}T_{a\rho\sigma}$, a
Riemann--Cartan topological density whose total-derivative
identity dynamically links it to the Holst term of
\cref{LinearAction}. One is the electromagnetic
$\theta$-term~\cite{wilczek1987axion}
$d_{18}\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{_{\rho\sigma}} = 4\,d_{18}\,\PD{_\mu}\bigl(\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\FieldA{_\nu}\PD{_\rho}\FieldA{_\sigma}\bigr)$,
a topological U(1) Chern density that is a total covariant divergence on any spacetime background, and therefore non-dynamical. The remaining three
are parity-odd curvature--electromagnetic cross-terms
$\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\MAGFt{}\FieldF{}$
($d_{19},d_{20},d_{21}$), again requiring a non-zero
antisymmetric Ricci to contract against the photon as in the
parity-even cross-coupling. The propagator-level analysis of
these
sectors~\cite{karananas2014parity,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,barker2025psalter2}
identifies the parity-violating mass spectra and gives the
additional parameter constraints needed to avoid ghosts and
tachyons beyond those of the parity-even sector.

\paragraph*{Ghost-free derivative extensions}
\label{DerivativeExtensions}
A covariant derivative adds one index, opening three further
parity-even sectors beyond the algebraic enumeration above. In
the first-order
formalism $\rcD{_\mu}\MAGT{} \sim \MAGFt{}$, so
operators built from $\{\MAGFt{},\,\MAGT{},\,\FieldF{},\,\rcD{}\MAGT{}\}$
remain Yang--Mills-type and produce at most second-order
equations of motion; sectors involving $\PD{}\MAGFt{}$ or
$\PD{}\FieldF{}$ generate fourth-order equations and are excluded
as ghost-prone~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear,nikiforova2009torsion}.
Three parity-even safe sectors then follow:
$\rcD{}\MAGT{}\!\times\!\FieldF{}$, generating three independent
invariants $\zeta_{1,2,3}$ in the parity-even sector of Shapiro's
non-minimal classification~\cite{shapiro2002torsion} (the
explicit linear map to Shapiro's $\theta_n$ remains to be
derived);
$\MAGFt{}\!\times\!\rcD{}\MAGT{}$, of which Barker's $\chi$
kinetic mixing is a specific contraction~\cite{barker2024poincare};
and $(\rcD{}\MAGT{})^2$, containing Barker's $\xi$ Maxwell-type
kinetic term~\cite{barker2024poincare,barker2021hamiltonian},
which is the unique contraction that propagates the trace
torsion vector. Their
$\varepsilon$-twins furnish the corresponding parity-odd
derivative sectors $(\tilde\zeta,\tilde\chi,\tilde\xi)$. The ghost-free
conditions on these combinations follow from the standard
linearised propagator analysis applied to the post-Riemannian
expansion~\cite{lin2019pgt,blagojevic2018hamiltonian}.

\paragraph*{Cubic completions}
\label{CubicCompletions}
A cubic operator contributes at quadratic order in the
perturbation when one or two of its field-strength factors are
evaluated on a non-zero classical background, since each
background insertion converts one dynamical factor into a fixed
coefficient. With a uniform magnetic background
$\bar{F}_{\mu\nu}$, four parity-even cubic sectors enter the
linearised analysis, each formed by inserting the background
into one or two factors of a quadratic operator:
$\mathcal{R}\!\times\!\mathcal{F}^2$ ($\lambda_{1\dots 4}$, the Gertsenshtein
channel into curvature--photon mixing~\cite{itin2004maxwell,bahamonde2025torsion}),
$\mathcal{T}^2\!\times\!\mathcal{F}$ ($\kappa_{1\dots 3}$, generating
torsion-mass corrections proportional to $\Bzero$),
$\mathcal{R}^2\!\times\!\mathcal{F}$ ($\rho_{1\dots 8}$, modifying the
quadratic graviton self-interaction in the magnetic background),
and $\rcD{}\mathcal{T}\!\times\!\mathcal{F}^2$ ($\mu_{1\dots 5}$, a
torsion--photon conversion channel proportional to $\Bzero$).
The parity-odd partners $(\tilde\lambda,\tilde\kappa,\tilde\mu)$
enumerated in \cref{MasterParameterCount} arise by
$\varepsilon$-contraction of the same parity-even contractions.
The cubic operators are independent coupling constants of the
full theory~\cite{bahamonde2025cubic}.

\paragraph*{Chern--Simons torsion--EM couplings}
\label{ChernSimonsCouplings}
Beyond the field-strength couplings catalogued above, the
electromagnetic gauge potential $\FieldA{_\mu}$ admits three
irreducible parity-odd contractions with the torsion
tensor~\cite{hehl2000electromagnetic,itin2004maxwell,rubilar2003torsion,obukhov2024electrodynamics}:
\begin{widetext}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_\text{CS}
  = \chi^\text{CS}_1\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\T[2]{_\mu}\,\FieldA{_\nu}\,\FieldF{_{\rho\sigma}}
  +\chi^\text{CS}_2\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\T[3]{_\mu}\,\FieldA{_\nu}\,\FieldF{_{\rho\sigma}}
  +\chi^\text{CS}_3\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\T[1]{^\lambda_{\mu\alpha}}\,g^\alpha{}_\nu\,\FieldA{_\lambda}\,\FieldF{_{\rho\sigma}},
  \label{ChernSimonsAction}
\end{equation}
\end{widetext}
contracting the photon with the trace, axial, and tensor
irreducible parts of the torsion respectively. The first two
families share the $\MAGT{}\,\FieldA{}\,\FieldF{}$ structure of
the gauge-field Chern--Simons three-form, and the axial coupling
$\chi^\text{CS}_2$ sources the magnetic-helicity density
$\FieldA{_i}\FieldF{^{0i}}\!\sim\!\bm{A}\!\cdot\!\bm{B}$,
producing the parity-odd birefringence and dichroism signatures
analysed in~\cite{rubilar2003torsion,obukhov2024electrodynamics}.
The third coupling is the unique Chern--Simons interaction
selecting the spin-two torsion sector, contracting the Maxwell
pair with the tensor component $\T[1]{}$.
