\section{Results}%
\label{Results}

% Opening: BHL Pattern A (recall the action) + B (state the object of
% study).  No TOC-style subsection enumeration.  Anchors the appendix
% infrastructure (App I, G, J) and introduces both amplification
% quantities (\Aamp from App G, A(θ) from App J) used throughout §4.
We explore the Poincar\'{e} gauge action coupling space for regions
of strong Gertsenshtein conversion above the Einstein--Maxwell
baseline. The truncations we cover draw their operators from the
inventory catalogued in~\cref{PGTEnumeration}; the perturbative-regime
conditions under which the linearised treatment remains
self-consistent are set out in~\cref{PerturbativeRegime}; the
coupling-space exploration apparatus is documented
in~\cref{InferenceArchitecture}. On the uniform plane-wave
$\Bzero$ setup of this work, the amplification score
$A(\theta)$ of~\cref{AmplificationScore} grows exponentially in the
simulation horizon $t_\mathrm{end}$ for any sector with
positive-real-part linearised eigenvalues, so the numerical value
of the score at a given coupling vector carries no physical content
on its own. The survey reports instead the \emph{shape} of the
amplification distribution over coupling space: which operators
carry the amplification, where the amplifying regions sit in
operator space, and how multimodal they are. The small-$\Bzero$
asymptotic ratio $\Aamp$ of~\cref{AmplificationFactor} applies to
the plane-wave uniform-$\Bzero$ setup.

\subsection{Validation against the Einstein--Maxwell baseline}%
\label{ValidationResult}
% Three named paragraphs: setup, Boccaletti reproduction, survey
% observables.  The survey-observables paragraph carries the App G + App J
% amplification-metric cross-refs that anchor the per-theory verdicts in
% §4.2–§4.4.

\paragraph*{Validation setup}
We validate the implementation on the Gertsenshtein conversion
formula (\cref{GertsenshteinFormula}) in Einstein--Maxwell theory.
A uniform background field $\Bzero$ fills a periodic domain of length
$L=100$; the linearised system is initialised as a plane wave on a
$N=512$ grid and evolved to $t_\text{end}=50$, with photon carrier
wavenumber $k\approx2.011$ ($2\pi n/L$ mode, $n=32$),
sampling $\Bzero$ at 24 uniformly spaced values in $[0.005,0.25]$.

\paragraph*{Reproduction of the Gertsenshtein formula}
\TIDAL{} recovers the Gertsenshtein formula~\cref{GertsenshteinFormula}
in the small-$\Bzero$ limit to the double-precision arithmetic limit
of the modal evolution
($|P_{\TIDAL} - P_\text{bare}| \lesssim 10^{-8}$,
see~\cref{BoccalettiValidation}); the full two-mode comparison
against the detuning formula of~\cite{raffelt1988mixing} is
in~\cref{BoccalettiUniform}.
The path-integrated kernel~\cref{PathIntegratedFormula} is recovered in
\cref{BoccalettiLocalised}.

\subsection{Surveyed sectors}%
\label{SectorSurvey}%

\paragraph*{Landscape descriptors}
For each truncation we report the shape of the amplification
distribution over its coupling space, through two outputs of the
nested sampler. The corner-plot 2D marginals
(\cref{CornerEMRC,CornerT6Min,CornerT5D20,CornerT5D21,CornerT5D22,CornerYMUnionPair,CornerChiClosure,CornerChiClosurePair,CornerT9,CarrierT9Restricted,CornerT1,CornerEHGert})
project the amplifying region of coupling space onto each operator
pair, showing the shape, multimodality, and ridges of the
distribution directly. The per-coupling marginal information
gain~$\D$
(\cref{KLTableEMRCParityPair,KLTableYMUnified,KLTableChiClosureComparison,KLTableDarkPhoton})
ranks operators by how narrowly the amplifying region projects onto
each individual coupling axis; the cross
divergence~$\D(P_+\|P_-)$ of~\cref{PerCouplingKL} isolates the
operators that distinguish amplification from suppression. Posterior
samples are drawn by PolyChord~\cite{Handley:2015fda,Handley:2015vkr}
on the synthetic score~$\pm\log A(\theta)$ of~\cref{NestedScore} and
visualised with anesthetic~\cite{Handley:2019mfs}.

\paragraph*{The $\MAGF{^2}$-PGT sector}
We open with the $\MAGF{^2}$-PGT sector, the minimal-coupling GR extension
wherein torsion propagates as a dynamical field. The Lagrangian contains the three
torsion-squared invariants $I_{1\!-\!3}$
\cite{shapiro2002torsion,hayashi1979new} together with the
$\Alp{1}\MAGF{^2}$ curvature-squared term,
\begin{align*}
  \lag_{R^2}
    = -\tfrac{1}{\kappa^2}\,\MAGF{}
       + \sum_{i=1}^{3} \Bet{i} I_i
    - \tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
       + \Alp{1}\,\MAGF{^2} \, ,
  % \label{R2PGTLagrangian}
\end{align*}
giving a four-dimensional prior over
$\{\Bet{1\!-\!3}, \Alp{1}\}$. The $\Alp{1}$-weighted terms vanish
from the linearised equations of motion that source the
Gertsenshtein conversion,
\begin{align*}
  \partial_t^2 A_x &= B_0 \, \partial_z h_{xy} + \partial_z^2 A_x, \\
  -\tfrac{1}{\kappa^2} \partial_t^2 h_{xy} &= \tfrac{1}{2} B_0^2 \, h_{xy}
    + B_0 \, \partial_z A_x - \tfrac{1}{\kappa^2} \partial_z^2 h_{xy}.
\end{align*}
so the Ricci-Cartan scalar-squared term $\Alp{1}\MAGF{^2}$ has no
effect on the Gertsenshtein conversion at linear order.
\Cref{SymbolicMechanism} shows the full symbolic output.
The $\Alp{1}\MAGF{^2}$ term promotes the metric-perturbation
sector to a fourth-order system, placing this theory outside the
scope of the numerical framework
(\cref{PerturbativeReduction,SymbolicMechanism}).
% ; no published
% constraint-promotion Hamiltonian recipe exists for the
% $\Alp{1}\MAGF{}^{2}$ case~\cite{blagojevic2018hamiltonian}.
Torsion-fields do appear coupled
to the trace sector of the metric perturbation, however, fourth-order time
derivatives there (\cref{SymbolicMechanism}) introduce Ostrogradsky
ghost modes~\cite{Woodard:2015zca,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,lin2019pgt}.
We conclude that the minimal-PGT family does not stably
couple torsion to the Gertsenshtein effect at linear order.

\paragraph*{Riemann--Cartan electromagnetic coupling}
To break this decoupling we introduce the first
non-minimal vertex: the direct coupling
$\Del{1}\MAGF{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$ between the
antisymmetric Riemann--Cartan Ricci tensor and the electromagnetic
field strength, introduced by~\cite{bahamonde2025torsion}.
This theory sources the linearised conversion amplitude
directly at $\O(\Bzero)$ \emph{without} propagating torsion.
The lagrangian is
\begin{align}
  \lag_\text{EM--RC}
    = -\tfrac{1}{\kappa^2}\,\MAGF{}
      + \Bet{i} I_{i}
      - \tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
      + \Del{1}\,\MAGF{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}} \, ,
   \label{RicciEMLagrangian}
\end{align}
giving a four-dimensional prior over
$\{\Bet{1\!-\!3}, \Del{1}\}$. The amplifying and suppressing
landscapes both carry their dominant structure along the $\Del{1}$
axis, but in different ways (\cref{CornerEMRC}). The suppressing
marginal is sharply peaked at two symmetric $\Del{1}$ values
straddling the origin, while the amplifying marginal extends out
broadly to large $|\Del{1}|$ in both directions --- unlike most of
the surveyed couplings, whose amplifying posteriors concentrate
near the origin. The cross divergence $\D(P_+\|P_-) = 20.8$~nats
places $\Del{1}$ above the other three couplings by an order of
magnitude (\cref{KLTableEMRCParityPair}); the same vertex
$\Del{1}\MAGF{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$ sources amplification
and suppression at different $\Del{1}$ values.

\paragraph*{Parity-odd minimal coupling}
The EM--RC vertex broke the minimal decoupling with a single
parity-even non-minimal operator. Its parity-odd companion is the
$\varepsilon$-contracted twin operator of the EM--RC vertex itself,
\begin{equation*}
  d_{21}\,\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,
    \MAGF{_{\mu\nu}}\,\FieldF{_{\rho\sigma}},
\end{equation*}
drawn from the three
$\varepsilon\!\cdot\!\MAGF{}\!\cdot\!\FieldF{}$ contractions
enumerated in
\cref{ParityOddQuadratic}~\cite{karananas2014parity,blagojevic2018hamiltonian}.
The four-dimensional posterior over $\{\Bet{1\!-\!3}, d_{21}\}$
(\cref{CornerT6Min}): the suppressing $d_{21}$ marginal is again
sharply peaked at two symmetric values straddling the origin; the
amplifying marginal shows a broader pair of peaks at the same
locations, then tails back to zero --- unlike the parity-even
amplification, which extends to large $|\Del{1}|$.

The parity-even and parity-odd vertices redistribute the
per-coupling $\D$ weight in markedly different ways. In the
parity-even sector the separation concentrates almost entirely on
$\Del{1}$ ($\D(P_+\|P_-) = 20.8$~nats;~\cref{KLTableEMRCParityPair}).
In the parity-odd twin the vertex contribution drops to
$\D(P_+\|P_-) = 8.8$~nats and the two torsion-mass directions
$\Bet{1}$ and $\Bet{2}$ become comparable
(\cref{KLTableEMRCParityPair}).

\paragraph*{The Yang--Mills--PGT}
\label{YangMillsPGT}
The $\Del{1}$ vertex of \cref{RicciEMLagrangian} is one of several
non-minimal operators introduced in PGT. We survey three further
extensions of standing interest. The
Bahamonde~\cite{bahamonde2025torsion} extension adds the torsion
kinetic operator $\xi(\nabla\MAGT{})^2$, making torsion dynamical.
The Barker~\cite{barker2024poincare} extension adds the
derivative-mixing operator $\chi\MAGF{_{[\mu\nu]}}\mathcal{F}^{(T)\,\mu\nu}$.
The Shapiro~\cite{shapiro2002torsion} extension adds the three
$\zeta_{1,2,3}\,\rcD{}\MAGT{}{\cdot}\FieldF{}$ contractions. We
study each on top of the EM--RC base, then their union,
\begin{multline}
  \lag_\text{YM--PGT}
    = \lag_\text{EM--RC}
      - \tfrac{1}{4}\xi\,
              \mathcal{F}^{(T)}_{\mu\nu}\,
              \mathcal{F}^{(T)\,\mu\nu}
    + \chi\,\MAGF{_{[\mu\nu]}}\,
              \mathcal{F}^{(T)\,\mu\nu} \\
    + \zeta_1\,\rcD{_\mu}\MAGT{^{\nu}_{\nu\rho}}\,
              \FieldF{^{\mu\rho}}
    + \zeta_2\,\rcD{_\mu}\MAGT{^{\mu}_{\nu\rho}}\,
              \FieldF{^{\nu\rho}} \\
    + \zeta_3\,\rcD{_\mu}\MAGT{_{\nu}^{\mu}_{\rho}}\,
              \FieldF{^{\nu\rho}} \, ,
  \label{NonMinimalLagrangian}
\end{multline}
% The $\chi$ and $\zeta_{1,2,3}$ derivative-mixing operators above (and the
% extended $\chi_{2\!-\!10}$ deferred to the next subsection) are particular
% contractions drawn from the complete basis of
% $\rcD{}\MAGT{}{\times}\FieldF{}$ invariants enumerated in
%~\cref{DerivativeExtensions}.
Each of the four truncations concentrates its per-coupling $\D$
onto a different operator (\cref{KLTableYMUnified}): the torsion
kinetic operator $\xi(\nabla\MAGT{})^2$ in Bahamonde and Barker, the
torsion mass $\Bet{1}$ in Shapiro, and the derivative-mixing
operator $\chi$ in the union. No single operator dominates the
amplification across the YM--PGT family; which vertex concentrates
the posterior depends on which other vertices are switched on.
Corner-plot projections for each truncation are
in~\cref{CornerT5D20} (Bahamonde),~\cref{CornerT5D21} (Barker),
and~\cref{CornerT5D22} (Shapiro). The union's amplifying landscape
overlaid with its $\xi=0$ non-propagating-torsion control is
in~\cref{CornerYMUnionPair}.

The amplifying regions above produce exponential mode growth: an
eigenvalue $\lambda$ of $M(k)$ with $\Re\{\lambda\} > 0$ grows its
eigenvector exponentially in time (see~\cref{ModalCoupledEntry}).

in~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,lin2019pgt,Yo:1999ex,Yo:2001sy};

in~\cite{karananas2014parity}. The current frontier method for

is deferred to future work.

with the torsion kinetic coefficient $\xi$ zeroed, leaving the
torsion sector algebraic and non-propagating (NP). The Lagrangian
\cref{NonMinimalLagrangian} then introduces no new kinetic operator,
torsion is determined algebraically, and the no-ghost classification
is trivially satisfied. The NP-control posterior overlaid with the
propagating-torsion result of~\cref{CornerYMUnionPair} matches it
within the bootstrap uncertainty of the per-coupling marginal $\D$
in every operator direction (\cref{KLTableYMUnified}, NP-control
column). Amplification therefore does not require the torsion
kinetic sector to be active.

\paragraph*{Euler--Heisenberg effective action}
The Einstein--Maxwell baseline receives QED effective-field-theory
corrections at the $\FieldF{^4}$ level~\cite{adler1971photon,dunne2004heisenberg}, which can themselves
source further photon--graviton interactions in a
magnetic background. We include three EFT couplings,
\begin{multline}
  \lag_\text{EH}
    = -\tfrac{1}{\kappa^2}\,\MAGF{}
       - \tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
    + \lambda_\text{top}\,\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldFtilde{^{\mu\nu}} \\
    + \rho\,(\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}})^{2}
    + \sigma\,(\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldFtilde{^{\mu\nu}})^{2} \, ,
  \label{EHGertLagrangian}
\end{multline}
of which the $\FieldF{}\!\cdot\!\FieldFtilde{}$ topological term is a total covariant
derivative absent from the equations of motion
(\cref{SymbolicEulerHeisenberg}), leaving a two-dimensional prior
over $(\rho, \sigma)$. The $\FieldF{^4}$ corrections scale with the
background field to a higher power than the leading PGT vertices
and are subdominant in this regime. The 2D corner
(\cref{CornerEHGert}) is a tight, symmetric null: amplification and
suppression marginals on both $\rho$ and $\sigma$ are statistically
indistinguishable from the prior at the surveyed
$\Bzero=10^{-2}$. The one-loop EH
coefficients~\cite{adler1971photon,dunne2004heisenberg} are
loop-suppressed below the prior scales sampled here; the QED
contribution to the Gertsenshtein signal lies below the survey's
