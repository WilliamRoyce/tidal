\subsection{Validation against the Einstein--Maxwell baseline}%
\label{ValidationResult}
% Three named paragraphs: setup, Boccaletti reproduction, survey
% observables.  The survey-observables paragraph carries the App G + App J
% amplification-metric cross-refs that anchor the per-theory verdicts in
% §4.2–§4.4.

\paragraph*{Validation setup}
We validate the pipeline on the Boccaletti conversion
formula (\cref{GertsenshteinFormula}) in Einstein--Maxwell theory.
A uniform background field $\Bzero$ fills a periodic domain of length
$L=100$; the linearised system is initialised as a plane wave on a
$N=512$ grid and evolved to $t_\text{end}=50$, with photon carrier
wavenumber $k\approx2.011$ ($2\pi n/L$ mode, $n=32$),
sampling $\Bzero$ at 24 uniformly spaced values in $[0.005,0.25]$.

\paragraph*{Reproduction of the Boccaletti kernel}
\TIDAL{} recovers the Boccaletti kernel \cref{GertsenshteinFormula}
in the small-$\Bzero$ limit to the modal-evolution
round-off floor of $\sim 10^{-8}$. As $\Bzero$ grows
the simulation departs from the bare formula by $\mathcal{O}(\Bzero^2)$
due to the graviton effective-mass detuning; against the two-mode
prediction of~\cite{raffelt1988mixing} that includes this detuning,
the residual remains $\lesssim 10^{-8}$ across the grid
(\cref{BoccalettiValidation,BoccalettiUniform}).
The path-integrated kernel \cref{BoccalettiPathIntegrated} is recovered in
\cref{BoccalettiLocalised}.

\subsubsection{The $\MAGFt{}^2$-PGT sector}
We open with the $\MAGFt{}^2$-PGT sector, the minimal coupling GR extension
that promotes torsion to a dynamical field. The Lagrangian retains the three
parity-even torsion-squared invariants $I_{1,2,3}$ of
\cite{shapiro2002torsion,hayashi1979new} together with the
$\Alp{1}\MAGFt{}^{2}$ curvature-squared term,
\begin{align}
  \lag_{R^2}
    = -\frac{1}{\kappa^2}\,\MAGFt{}
       + \sum_{i=1}^{3} \Bet{i} I_i
    - \tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\alpha\beta}}\FieldF{^{\alpha\beta}}
       + \Alp{1}\,\MAGFt{}^{2} \, ,
  \label{R2PGTLagrangian}
\end{align}
giving a four-dimensional prior over
$\{\Bet{1\!-\!3}, \Alp{1}\}$. The Gertsenshtein channel is unaffected
by the $\Alp{1}$ coupling at linear order: the $\Alp{1}$-weighted
terms are absent from the channel equations
(\cref{ChannelEqAx,ChannelEqHxy}) that mediate the standard
$h_{xy} \leftrightarrow a_x$ conversion,
as the equations of motion in \cref{PropagatingKineticOutput} confirm
directly:
\begin{align}
  \partial_t^2 A_x &= B_0 \partial_z h_{xy} + \partial_z^2 A_x,
    \label{ChannelEqAx} \\
  -\kappa^{-2} \partial_t^2 h_{xy} &= \tfrac{B_0^2}{2} h_{xy}
    + B_0 \partial_z A_x - \kappa^{-2} \partial_z^2 h_{xy}.
    \label{ChannelEqHxy}
\end{align}
The $\Alp{1}\MAGFt{}^{2}$ term promotes the metric-perturbation
sector to a fourth-order system, placing this theory outside the
scope of the numerical pipeline
(\cref{PerturbativeReduction,SymbolicMechanism}).
% ; no published
% constraint-promotion Hamiltonian recipe exists for the
% $\Alp{1}\MAGFt{}^{2}$ case~\cite{blagojevic2018hamiltonian}.
Torsion components appear coupled
only into the trace sector of the metric perturbation, not into the
channels that
mediate the standard Gertsenshtein conversion. However, the fourth-order time
derivatives in that sector introduce Ostrogradsky
ghost modes~\cite{Woodard:2015zca,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,lin2019pgt}.
We conclude that the minimal-PGT family does not stably
couple torsion to the Gertsenshtein channel at linear order.

\subsubsection{Electromagnetic coupling to the Riemann--Cartan curvature}
To break the decoupling identified above we turn to the first
non-minimal vertex: the direct coupling
$\Del{1}\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$ between the
antisymmetric Riemann--Cartan Ricci tensor and the electromagnetic
field strength, introduced by~\cite{bahamonde2025torsion} as a
non-minimal linear coupling and not
previously investigated in this context.
This theory sources the linearised conversion amplitude
directly at order $\Bzero$ \emph{without} propagating torsion.
The lagrangian is
\begin{align}
  \lag_\text{EM--RC}
    = -\tfrac{1}{\kappa^2}\,\MAGFt{}
      + \Bet{i} I_{i}
      - \tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
      + \Del{1}\,\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}} \, ,
  \label{RicciEMLagrangian}
\end{align}
giving a four-dimensional prior over
$\{\Bet{1\!-\!3}, \Del{1}\}$. Nested sampling gives
\begin{equation*}
  \log\mathcal{Z}_+ = +13.37 \pm 0.06
  \quad \text{and} \quad
  \log\mathcal{Z}_- = +11.40 \pm 0.10,
\end{equation*}
thus both directions show informative evidence, so the vertex supports both
significant amplification and suppression of the conversion at different parameter values.
The Bayes factor $\log B = +1.98$ records that the prior-weighted integral is
marginally amplification-dominated. The $\Del{1}$-direction
carries $D_\mathrm{KL}(P_+\|P_-) = 20.8$~nats of
distinction between the two directions, more than an order of
magnitude above any other coupling; the
$\Del{1}\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$ operator is what drives
the bidirectional behaviour.

\subsubsection{The Yang--Mills--PGT}
Many authors have introduced non-minimal operators into PGT with different physical
motivations, and the $\Del{1}$ vertex above is just one of several that could break
the minimal decoupling. To probe what each family contributes to the Gertsenshtein
conversion we consider four nested sub-theories that each turn on one
further family, isolating their effect.

The progression is
\textit{Bahamonde base} (adding the torsion kinetic operator
$\xi(\nabla\mathcal{T})^2$ to the $\Del{1}$
vertex of~\cite{bahamonde2025torsion}; this is the first sub-theory in
which torsion propagates as a dynamical field);
\textit{Barker extension} (adding the
derivative-mixing operator $\chi\MAGFt{_{[\alpha\beta]}}\mathcal{F}^{(T)\,\alpha\beta}$
of~\cite{barker2024poincare}, whose authors identify it as the
completion required to expose the Yang--Mills-like kinetic structure
of vector torsion missing from earlier PGT formulations);
\textit{Shapiro extension} (adding the three
$\zeta_{1,2,3}\,\rcD{_\alpha}\mathcal{T}{\cdot}\FieldF{}$
contractions of~\cite{shapiro2002torsion});
% , motivated as the
% non-minimal terms induced by renormalisation of matter on
% torsion--curved spacetime);
and the \textit{full non-minimal} union of
all three families. The combined Lagrangian is
\begin{widetext}
\begin{align}
  \lag_\text{YM--PGT}
    = \lag_\text{EM--RC}
      - \tfrac{1}{4}\xi\,
              \mathcal{F}^{(T)}_{\alpha\beta}\,
              \mathcal{F}^{(T)\,\alpha\beta}
    + \chi\,\MAGFt{_{[\alpha\beta]}}\,
              \mathcal{F}^{(T)\,\alpha\beta}
    + \zeta_1\,\rcD{_\alpha}\mathcal{T}^{\beta}{}_{\beta\gamma}\,
              \FieldF{^{\alpha\gamma}}
    + \zeta_2\,\rcD{_\alpha}\mathcal{T}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma}\,
              \FieldF{^{\beta\gamma}}
    + \zeta_3\,\rcD{_\alpha}\mathcal{T}_{\beta}{}^{\alpha}{}_{\gamma}\,
              \FieldF{^{\beta\gamma}} \, ,
  \label{NonMinimalLagrangian}
\end{align}
\end{widetext}
enumerated against the full
$\rcD{}\mathcal{T}{\times}\FieldF{}$ class in
\cref{DerivativeExtensions}. Across the four sub-theories, nested sampling gives amplification log-evidences
($\log\mathcal{Z}_+$) climbing from $+7.68$ at the Bahamonde base to
$+10.03$ at the full non-minimal union, while the suppression
log-evidences vary between $+2.5$ and $+6.2$: both directions remain
informatively populated as in the $\Del{1}$ sector, with the
amplification direction increasingly dominant as the coupling space
is extended. The Bayes factors $\log B = +1.84, +2.80, +2.68, +7.37$
correspondingly grow as each new operator family adds a further
amplification corner. The carrier directions tracked by
$D_\mathrm{KL}(P_+\|P_-)$ in \cref{KLPerTheory} migrate across the
progression: the Bahamonde and Barker chains are dominated by the
$\xi(\nabla\mathcal{T})^2$ kinetic direction ($\approx 5$ nats), the
Shapiro chain by the torsion-squared coefficient $\Bet{1}$, and the
full non-minimal sector by the Barker $\chi$ vertex
($D_\mathrm{KL}(P_+\|P_-) = 3.9$ nats). Only the single Barker $\chi$
appears in the derivative class probed at this nine-parameter level;
the full $\chi_{1\!-\!10}$ closure of the same class is the
subject of the complete parity-even sector below.

\subsubsection{Non-propagating-torsion control}
The amplifying modes in the chains above are exponential in the
linearised plane-wave evolution: at every amplifying parameter point
the conversion grows because some mode in the linearised system has
positive $\mathrm{Re}\,\lambda$. Whether such growth signals a
genuine new amplification mechanism, a non-ghost tachyonic
instability of the unbounded plane-wave ansatz, or a wrong-sign
kinetic-mode ghost that would render the theory non-unitary is not
in general decidable from the chains alone. The set of torsion modes
that propagate, and whether any of them carries the wrong sign,
depends on the term subset and the
parameter values~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,lin2019pgt,Yo:1999ex,Yo:2001sy};
robust resolution requires the full Hamiltonian particle-spectrum
analysis carried out by the \PSALTer
tool~\cite{barker2024psalter,barker2025psalter2}, integration with
which we defer to future work.

To bound the ghost branch from below \emph{without} \PSALTer we run a
control test: the same Yang--Mills--PGT non-minimal sector with the
torsion kinetic coefficient $\xi$ pinned to zero. The Lagrangian then
introduces no new kinetic operator for any field beyond the
Einstein--Maxwell baseline; torsion is determined algebraically by
the Cartan equation. The no-ghost classification is trivially
satisfied --- there is no kinetic operator whose sign needs checking.
Informed by the $\Del{1}$ sector result above (where switching
on a non-propagating vertex produced suppression-dominated structure
on narrower prior support) we expected the control to collapse the
amplification corner. Instead nested sampling gives
\begin{equation*}
  \log\mathcal{Z}_+ = +10.98 \pm 0.15
  \quad \text{and} \quad
  \log\mathcal{Z}_- = +2.67 \pm 0.10,
\end{equation*}
($\log B = +8.31$), essentially
reproducing the propagating-torsion result. Repeating the test at
the seventeen-parameter $\chi$-closure sub-space
$\{\Bet{1\!-\!3}, \Del{1}, \zeta_{1\!-\!3}, \chi_{1\!-\!10}\}$ with
$\xi = 0$ returns
\begin{equation*}
  \log\mathcal{Z}_+ = +9.93 \pm 0.08 \, , \quad
  \log\mathcal{Z}_- = -3.28 \pm 0.07 \, ,

The pipeline accepts these exponentially-growing modes through the
modal-solver evolution by design (\cref{InferenceArchitecture}):
prejudging which mode behaviours are unphysical for an input theory
is not the role of a theory-agnostic survey. The remaining
interpretive question is whether the growth represents a physical
amplification --- which a finite-extent geometry would saturate while
preserving channel mixing --- or a non-ghost tachyonic-instability
artefact of the unbounded plane-wave ansatz. Several precedents in
the literature show that linearised exponential / tachyonic modes
need not be fatal pathologies. In the Standard Model the Higgs field
appears tachyonic about the unbroken vacuum but is perfectly unitary
about its symmetry-broken ground
state~\cite{legner2025torc}; analogously, in PGT the spin-connection
modes can be massive in the ultraviolet and integrate out at the
infrared to recover general relativity as the low-energy effective
theory, so linearised tachyons can be a signature of the wrong
vacuum rather than of a fatal pathology. Higher-derivative gravity
theories that exhibit Ostrogradsky ghosts at the linearised level
can be non-linearly ghost-free when appropriate
torsion or higher-curvature
terms are present~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear}. Recent metric-affine
catalogues show that ghost-tachyon tunings constructed at the
linearised level break down nonlinearly, and that the only
unitarity-protected branches are those where a gauge symmetry rather
than a parameter choice removes the ghost~\cite{barker2025irfound1,barker2025irfound2};
this is precisely the branch that the non-propagating-torsion control
sits in. These precedents do not prove that the linearised
exponential growth we observe is physical; they establish that it is
not automatically fatal, and that resolution belongs to either the
finite-extent geometry of \cref{Discussion} or to a future
\PSALTer-based particle-spectrum analysis.

\subsubsection{Dark-photon plasma }
The dark-photon-plasma analogue probes a distinct survey axis from
the PGT extensions above: the trace-vector torsion $\T[2]{_\mu}$ is
promoted to a Proca field with kinetic mixing to the photon and a
plasma-mass coupling, recovering the standard dark-photon
phenomenology of~\cite{caputo2021darkphoton} at $\Bzero \to 0$. The
bare kinetic-mixing model alone produced no conversion signal: the
modal eigendecomposition of the linearised system has a degeneracy
between the photon mode and the Proca-trace mode that closes the
mixing channel at leading order, so the inference returns the
Einstein--Maxwell baseline regardless of the kinetic-mixing strength.
We therefore added the Proca mass $m_A^2$ to lift the degeneracy and
open the mixing channel, with detuning coupling $\delta_m$
controlling the deviation of the photon mode from the
Proca-trace mode,
\begin{widetext}
\begin{align}
  \lag_\text{dp}
    = -\tfrac{1}{\kappa^2}\,\MAGFt{}
      + \Bet{3} I_3
      - \tfrac{1}{4}\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
      - \tfrac{1}{2}m_A^2\,\T[2]{_\mu}\T[2]{^\mu}
      + \xi\,\T[2]{_\mu}\PD{_\nu}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
      + \delta_m\,\T[2]{_\mu}\T[2]{^\mu}\,
              \FieldF{_{\alpha\beta}}\FieldF{^{\alpha\beta}} \, ,
  \label{DarkPhotonPlasmaLagrangian}
\end{align}
\end{widetext}
giving a four-dimensional prior over
$\{m_A^2, \xi, \delta_m, \Bet{3}\}$. Nested sampling gives

  \log\mathcal{Z}_- = +2.92 \pm 0.08
\end{align}

amplification log-evidence dominates the suppression log-evidence,
but per-parameter $D_\mathrm{KL}$ in \cref{KLPerTheory} identifies
the plasma-mass coupling $\delta_m$ as the dominant carrier
($D_\mathrm{KL}(P_+\|P_-) = 3.4$~nats); the kinetic mixing $\xi$
adds little structure beyond it. The result is therefore essentially
a Proca-mass-induced photon-mode-shift effect rather than a
torsion-electromagnetism coupling phenomenon, and is largely
