\section{Poincar\'{e} gauge theory and the Gertsenshtein kernel}%
\label{Theory}

\paragraph*{Conventions}
We work in the metric signature $(-,+,+,+)$ with natural units
$\hbar = c = 1$. Greek indices $\mu,\nu,\dots$ denote spacetime
coordinate components and Latin indices $i,j,\dots$ denote Lorentz-frame
components, following the modern BHL
convention~\cite{barker2024poincare}. The reduced Planck mass is $\MPl$ with
$\MPl^2 = (8\pi G)^{-1}$. Throughout we use the gravitational coupling
$\kappa$ defined by $\kappa^2 = 16\pi G$.\footnote{%
    Two distinct objects appear under the symbol $\kappa$ in the
    recent Gertsenshtein literature: the linearised
    graviton--photon vertex coupling adopted here, with
    $\kappa^2 = 16\pi G$ and $[\kappa] = [\mathrm{Mass}]^{-1}$, and
    the Einstein constant which we denote
    $\kappa_{(\mathrm{E})}\equiv 8\pi G$,
    $[\kappa_{(\mathrm{E})}] = [\mathrm{Mass}]^{-2}$, used as the
    prefactor of $\MAGFt{}$
    in~\cite{barker2024poincare,barker2023confinement,barker2024psalter}.
    A non-canonical kinetic normalisation in the derivations
    of~\cite{palessandro2023simple,palessandro2024gertsenshtein}
    propagates this ambiguity into a factor-of-$\sqrt{4\pi}$ error
    in the conversion-formula argument, while the convention
    adopted in~\cite{lella2024constraining} agrees with ours.
    The two normalisations are related by
    $\kappa^2 = 2\kappa_{(\mathrm{E})}$.
} This is the linearised graviton--photon vertex coupling, and the
action prefactor $\kappa^{-2}\,\MAGFt{}$ corresponds to a canonical
graviton kinetic term under the field redefinition
$\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu} = h_{\mu\nu}/\kappa$ used
in~\cite{lella2024constraining}. The totally antisymmetric
Levi--Civita tensor $\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$ is normalised to
$\varepsilon^{0123} = +1$.

\subsection{Field content and quadratic action}%
\label{PGTframework}
% Pedagogical sequence per genre_conventions_theory.md §(d) Steps (a)–(e).
% Target ~350 w.

% Step (a): Riemannian baseline (notation, not pedagogy).
\paragraph*{Riemannian baseline notation}
We follow~\cite{barker2024poincare} in distinguishing the Riemannian
Levi--Civita connection
\begin{align}
  \rCon{^\sigma_{\mu\nu}}
    = \tfrac{1}{2}\g{^{\sigma\rho}}\bigl(
        \PD{_\mu}\g{_{\nu\rho}} + \PD{_\nu}\g{_{\mu\rho}}
        - \PD{_\rho}\g{_{\mu\nu}}\bigr),
  \label{LeviCivita}
\end{align}
decorated with a ring, from the independent Riemann--Cartan
connection $\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ introduced below; the
corresponding covariant derivatives are denoted $\rD{_\mu}$ and
$\rcD{_\mu}$.

% Step (b): Promotion of connection to independent field. Palatini
% formulation declared explicitly.
\paragraph*{Independent connection in the Palatini formulation}
The Palatini formulation~\cite{palatini1919deduzione,ferraris1982variational,barker2024palatini}
treats the metric and the affine connection as independent
variational fields. Poincar\'{e} gauge theory of
Kibble~\cite{kibble1961lorentz},
Utiyama~\cite{utiyama1956invariant}, and
Sciama~\cite{sciama1962physical}, developed
by~\cite{trautman1972einstein,hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric},
promotes the metric and the affine connection
$\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ to independent dynamical fields in this
sense, with
torsion and curvature appearing as the field strengths of spacetime
translations and rotations respectively
(see~\cite{blagojevic2002gravitation,blagojevic2003lectures,blagojevicHehl2013,hehlobukhov2007cartan}
for textbook and lecture-note treatments,
\cite{barker2024poincare,barker2024psalter,barker2023confinement}
for recent applications, and~\cite{lin2019pgt,blagojevic2018hamiltonian}
for the ghost-free and no-tachyon classifications of the linearised
propagator, against which our empirically-constructed survey
priors can be cross-checked%
% TODO: complete the verification of survey priors against the
% Lin / Blagojevic ghost-free classifications before final
% submission. Per docs/tex/pgt_stability_priors.tex, partial
% verification has been done for the early-stage models; the
% later-stage models are pending.
). The full field-variable formalism,
including the vierbein, the spin connection, and the irreducible
torsion components used in the kinetic-matrix analysis below, is
collected in \cref{PGTBackground}.

% Step (c): Paired curvature and torsion field-strength definitions.
\paragraph*{Field strengths: curvature and torsion}
The translational and rotational gauge field strengths take the
standard form~\cite{kibble1961lorentz,hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric,blagojevic2003lectures,hehlobukhov2007cartan,barker2024poincare},
\begin{align}
  \MAGFt{^\sigma_\mu_\nu_\rho}
    &\equiv 2\,\PD{_{[\mu}}\MAGA{^\sigma_{\nu]}_\rho}
       + 2\,\MAGA{^\sigma_{[\mu|\lambda|}}\MAGA{^\lambda_{\nu]}_\rho},
  \label{Curvature} \\
  \MAGT{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}
    &\equiv 2\,\MAGA{^\sigma_{[\mu\nu]}}.
  \label{Torsion}
\end{align}
Curvature reduces to its Riemannian limit when the antisymmetric part
of the connection vanishes; torsion is the obstruction to that limit.

% Step (d): General quadratic action with named couplings.
\paragraph*{General action}
The most general PGT+EM action constructed from the gauge field
strengths $\MAGFt{^\sigma_{\mu\nu\rho}}$, $\MAGT{^\sigma_{\mu\nu}}$,
and $\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}$ decomposes schematically into
sector blocks~\cite{lin2019pgt,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,blagojevic2003lectures,barker2024poincare,sezgin1980ghostfree,aoki2020nonlinear,bahamonde2025cubic},
\begin{multline}
  S = \int \diff^4 x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Bigl[
        \mathcal{L}_\text{lin}
        + \mathcal{L}_{\tilde{R}^2}
        + \mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}^2}
        + \mathcal{L}_{F^2}
        + \mathcal{L}_{\tilde{R}F} \\[-0.5em]
        + \mathcal{L}_\text{odd}
        + \mathcal{L}_\partial
        + \mathcal{L}_\text{cubic}
        + \mathcal{L}_\text{CS}
      \Bigr],
  \label{QuadraticAction}
\end{multline}
where $\mathcal{L}_\text{lin}$ collects the linear invariants
(Einstein--Hilbert, cosmological constant $\Lambda$, and the Holst
term~\cite{holst1995barbero} with Barbero--Immirzi parameter
$\gamma_\text{BI}$~\cite{barbero1995real,immirzi1997real}); $\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{R}^2}$ contains the
parity-even curvature-squared invariants\footnote{The
Riemann--Cartan curvature lacks the pair-exchange symmetry of the
torsion-free Riemann tensor ---
$\tilde{R}_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu}\neq\tilde{R}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$ in
general --- since the antisymmetric Ricci
$\tilde{R}_{[\mu\nu]}$ does not vanish~\cite{shapiro2002torsion,hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric,barker2024poincare}; the parity-even
curvature-squared sector therefore contains additional independent
contractions beyond the torsion-free Riemann-square count.};
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{T}^2}$ holds the parity-even torsion-squared
invariants; $\mathcal{L}_{F^2}$ is the Maxwell term and
$\mathcal{L}_{\tilde{R}F}$ the parity-even
curvature--electromagnetic cross-term; $\mathcal{L}_\text{odd}$
collects the parity-odd quadratic
invariants~\cite{karananas2014parity,blagojevic2018hamiltonian};
$\mathcal{L}_\partial$ the higher-derivative
extensions~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear}; $\mathcal{L}_\text{cubic}$
the cubic-order completions that contribute to the linearised
dynamics, and those as catalogued
in~\cite{bahamonde2025cubic};
and $\mathcal{L}_\text{CS}$ collects the Chern--Simons torsion--EM
couplings involving the gauge potential, motivated
by~\cite{hehl2000electromagnetic,itin2004maxwell,rubilar2003torsion}
and treated systematically
in~\cite{obukhov2024electrodynamics}. The explicit sector-by-sector
enumeration is collected in \cref{PGTEnumeration}, with
\cref{SectorSurvey} reporting the campaign survey across the
surveyed subsets of these sectors.

\paragraph*{Irreducible torsion decomposition}
The torsion tensor admits a unique decomposition into irreducible
$\mathrm{O}(3,1)$ representations~\cite{hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric,shapiro2002torsion,blagojevic2003lectures,obukhov2024electrodynamics,barker2024poincare}:
a traceless tensor part $\T[1]{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}$, a trace vector
$\T[2]{_\mu} \equiv \MAGT{^\lambda_\lambda_\mu}$, and an axial
pseudovector
$\T[3]{^\mu} \equiv \varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\mu}\MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\gamma}}$,
\begin{align}
  \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}
    = \tfrac{1}{3}\bigl(\T[2]{_\beta}\,\g{_{\alpha\mu}}
                       - \T[2]{_\mu}\,\g{_{\alpha\beta}}\bigr)
    - \tfrac{1}{6}\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\,\T[3]{^\nu}
    + \T[1]{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}.
  \label{IrreducibleTorsion}
\end{align}
The torsion-squared sector of \cref{QuadraticAction} is equivalently
written in the \emph{direct basis} of scalar contractions,
\begin{align}
  I_1 \equiv \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}\MAGT{^{\alpha\beta\mu}},
  \quad
  I_2 \equiv \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}\MAGT{^{\beta\alpha\mu}},
  \quad
  I_3 \equiv \MAGT{^\alpha_\alpha_\mu}\MAGT{^\beta_\beta^\mu},
  \label{TorsionDirectBasis}
\end{align}
with $\{I_1,I_2,I_3\}$ related to the irreducible squared-norms
$\{q^2,\,T^2,\,S^2\}$ by an invertible linear transformation collected
in \cref{PGTBackground}~\cite{shapiro2002torsion}. Coupling-constant
labels and basis orderings differ across the PGT literature ---
\cite{lin2019pgt} and~\cite{sezgin1980ghostfree} use distinct
conventions, and the explicit translation table is collected in
\cref{PGTEnumeration}. Throughout this work we use the direct basis
$\{I_1,I_2,I_3\}$ computationally and the irreducible decomposition
for naming the physical degrees of freedom that each interaction
term selects.

\subsection{Linearisation around the magnetic background}%
\label{Linearisation}
% Target ~350 w. Per genre_conventions_theory.md §(f) linearisation
% sub-movement.

\paragraph*{Background and perturbation ansatz}
We linearise about a flat metric carrying a uniform static magnetic
field $\Bzero$ along $\hat{x}$, with vanishing background torsion.
The metric, four-potential, and torsion are decomposed as
\begin{gather}
  g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu},
  \quad
  A_\mu = \bar{A}_\mu + \delta A_\mu,
  \quad
  \MAGT{^\sigma_\mu_\nu} = \delta\MAGT{^\sigma_\mu_\nu},
  \label{PerturbationAnsatz} \\
  \bar{A}_\mu\,\diff x^\mu = -\Bzero\,z\,\diff y, \nonumber
\end{gather}
with the torsion tensor $\MAGT{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ treated as
an inherently perturbative field. The Palatini connection
decomposes
as~\cite{hehl1976spin,shapiro2002torsion,hehlobukhov2007cartan,blagojevic2003lectures}
\begin{align}
  \MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}
    = \rCon{^\sigma_\mu_\nu} \!+ K^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu},
  \quad
  K^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}
    = \tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(
        \MAGT{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}
        \!\!+ \MAGT{_\mu^\sigma_\nu}
        \!+ \MAGT{_\nu^\sigma_\mu}\!
      \bigr),
  \label{PalatiniDecomposition}
\end{align}
the second term being the contortion tensor that encodes the
torsion contribution to the connection. The linearised connection
perturbation $\delta\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ therefore inherits
contributions from both the metric perturbation $h_{\mu\nu}$,
through the variation of the Levi--Civita connection
$\delta\rCon{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ obtained by varying the metric in
\cref{LeviCivita}, and the torsion perturbation
$\delta\MAGT{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$, through the contortion variation
$\delta K^\sigma{}_{\mu\nu}$. The metric
perturbation $h_{\mu\nu}$ enters without a $\kappa$ prefactor;
this choice is equivalent under field redefinition to the
canonically-normalised convention
$g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + \kappa\,\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}$ used
in~\cite{lella2024constraining}. A self-consistency bound on the
campaign parameter choices is in \cref{PerturbativeRegime}.

\paragraph*{Order-counting rule}
We work to linear order in $h_{\mu\nu}$, $\delta A_\mu$, and the
torsion fluctuation $\delta\MAGT{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$, and to first
order in $\Bzero$.

\paragraph*{Linearised kinetic matrix}
After expanding $\sqrt{-g}\,\lag$ to quadratic order in the
perturbation fields and reducing to plane-wave dependence
$\partial_x = \partial_y = 0$ along the propagation direction
$\hat{z}$, perpendicular to the background magnetic field
$\bm{B}$, which we take to be $\bm{B} = \Bzero\,\hat{x}$ --- the standard Gertsenshtein
geometry~\cite{gertsenshtein1962wave,boccaletti1970conversion,raffelt1988mixing,cillis1996graviton,ejlli2020graviton,palessandro2023simple,palessandro2024gertsenshtein,lella2024constraining,domcke2025scattering}%
% TODO: investigate WLOG vs not-WLOG status of this propagation
% direction for derivative / parity-odd PGT sectors (relates to
% issue #300 and the new "investigation: propagation-direction
% WLOG status" issue created with this revision).
---
with the photon perturbation in Lorenz gauge and the metric
perturbation evolved without a gauge constraint%
% TODO: cite TT-in-matter approach (await supervisor meeting
% 2026-05-08 outcome --- see docs/meetings/2026-05-08_supervisor.md
% for the question being raised).
(transverse-traceless-compatible initial conditions are imposed
in the vacuum region where the wavepacket is set up; the
evolution through the magnetic-field region is unconstrained,
allowing non-TT components to develop), the surviving degrees of freedom
assemble into a quadratic kinetic system
\begin{align}
  \lag^{(2)} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\xi^\mathsf{T}\,
    \mathcal{K}(\partial_t,\partial_z)\,\xi,
  \label{KineticMatrix}
\end{align}
for a vector $\xi$ of gauge-fixed metric, photon, and (where
propagating) torsion fluctuations. The detailed structure of
$\mathcal{K}$ depends on the Lagrangian chosen, and is reported
per surveyed theory class in \cref{ChannelStructure}.

\paragraph*{Electromagnetic-field conventions}
We work at the Lagrangian level throughout, expanding
$\sqrt{-g}\,\lag$ to quadratic order in the perturbation fields.
The electromagnetic sector is expressed in terms of the
four-potential $\FieldA{_\mu}$ and the gauge field strength
$\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}$, and background fields enter through the
background four-potential $\bar{A}_\mu$ rather than through any
explicit electric/magnetic decomposition. The $\sqrt{-g}$ expansion
automatically captures the metric-dependent contractions that
distinguish curved-spacetime EM from its naive flat-space
identification --- the issue addressed analytically
by~\cite{hwangnoh2023emdef,hwangnoh2023graviton} via a covariant
normal-frame decomposition. The corresponding gauge-consistent
equation-of-motion structure underlies the $\Bzero \to 0$
survey-coefficient framework set out in \cref{SurveyCoefficient}.

\paragraph*{Conversion probability}

% (non-perturbative comparator). The kappa-convention footnote attaches
% to this paragraph because it is where kappa first appears as a
% numerical coupling in a physical formula. The footnote is a
% Barker-style substantive technical aside (compare 2406.12826 footnote 2
% on the EC vs PGT distinction): it documents the two-kappa ambiguity,
% cites the published literature errors caused by confusion of the two
% (Palessandro-Rothman factor-of-sqrt(4pi) error; Hwang-Noh critique;
% Dandoy's corrected result), and points the specialist reader to
% \cref{sec:kappa-conventions} for the full disambiguation. This is the
% one place in the report where the kappa-convention story is told in
% full; the rest of the report uses the symbol freely.
The conversion probability --- defined as the energy fraction
transferred from the graviton sector to the photon sector over
the propagation distance $D$~\cite{raffelt1988mixing} --- for
a graviton traversing a region of uniform $\Bzero$ takes
the closed form~\cite{gertsenshtein1962wave,boccaletti1970conversion,lella2024constraining,domcke2025scattering}
\begin{align}
  \Pconv = \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\kappa\,\Bzero\,D}{2}\right),
  \label{GertsenshteinFormula}
\end{align}
which in the small-argument limit $\kappa\Bzero D \ll 1$ scales as
$\Pconv \propto \Bzero^2$, the characteristic Gertsenshtein
signature; the regime of validity of this linearised expression in
the torsionful background is set out in \cref{PerturbativeRegime}.

The quantitative reproduction of this kernel across our validation
grid is reported in \cref{ValidationResult}, with the full
simulation-to-analytic comparison archived in \cref{Validation}.

\paragraph*{Channel decomposition}
The linearised perturbation falls naturally into the two transverse
tensor polarisations $h_+$ and $h_\times$, the trace sector
$\{h_+, h_4, h_9, a_\perp\}$, and the irreducible torsion modes ---
trace vector $\T[2]{_\mu}$ and axial pseudovector $\T[3]{^\mu}$
where present. These are the kinematic sectors in which the
conversion question is posed; the standard Gertsenshtein channel
$h_\times \leftrightarrow a_\parallel$ is the canonical example~\cite{gertsenshtein1962wave,boccaletti1970conversion,raffelt1988mixing,lella2024constraining,domcke2025scattering}.
The empirical inter-channel structure observed across the surveyed
theory classes is reported in \cref{ChannelStructure}, with the
underlying linearised-coupling analysis collected in
\cref{SymbolicMechanism}.
