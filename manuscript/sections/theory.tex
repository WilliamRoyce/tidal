\section{Poincar\'{e} gauge theory and the Gertsenshtein kernel}%
\label{Theory}

% Conventions named paragraph (Step 0): signature, indices, units, M_P,
% kappa convention, Levi-Civita epsilon. The kappa note must reference
% \cref{sec:kappa-conventions} — the canonical disambiguation in
% docs/tex/gertsenshtein_formula.tex — to resolve the two-meanings-of-kappa
% ambiguity (issue #338). The label sec:kappa-conventions retains its
% prefix because it is defined in docs/tex/, not the manuscript scaffolding;
% see STYLE_GUIDE.md §(c).
\paragraph*{Conventions}
We work in the metric signature $(-,+,+,+)$ with natural units
$\hbar = c = 1$. Greek indices $\mu,\nu,\dots$ denote spacetime
coordinate components and Latin indices $a,b,\dots$ denote Lorentz-frame
components. The reduced Planck mass is $\MPl$ with
$\MPl^2 = (8\pi G)^{-1}$. Throughout we use the gravitational coupling
$\kappa$ defined by $\kappa^2 = 16\pi G$, which has dimension
$[\kappa] = [\mathrm{Mass}]^{-1}$ and satisfies $\kappa = \sqrt{2}/\MPl$;
this is the coupling that enters the Lagrangian prefactor
$\kappa^{-2}\,\MAGFt{}$ and the linearised graviton--photon vertex in
the Gertsenshtein conversion formula. The totally antisymmetric symbol
is normalised to $\varepsilon^{0123} = +1$, fixing the sign of the
axial torsion pseudovector below.

\subsection{Field content and quadratic action}%
\label{PGTframework}
% Pedagogical sequence per genre_conventions_theory.md §(d) Steps (a)–(e).
% Target ~350 w.

% Step (a): Riemannian baseline (notation, not pedagogy).
\paragraph*{Riemannian baseline notation}
We follow~\cite{barker2024poincare} in distinguishing the Riemannian
Levi--Civita connection
$\rCon{^\sigma_{\mu\nu}}
  = \tfrac{1}{2}\g{^{\sigma\rho}}\bigl(
      \PD{_\mu}\g{_{\nu\rho}} + \PD{_\nu}\g{_{\mu\rho}}
      - \PD{_\rho}\g{_{\mu\nu}}\bigr)$
(decorated with a ring) from the independent Riemann--Cartan
connection $\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ introduced below; the
corresponding covariant derivatives are denoted $\rD{_\mu}$ and
$\rcD{_\mu}$.

% Step (b): Promotion of connection to independent field. Palatini
% formulation declared explicitly.
\paragraph*{Independent connection in the Palatini formulation}
Poincar\'{e} gauge theory promotes the metric and the affine
connection $\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$ to independent dynamical fields,
gauging the Poincar\'{e} group on the tangent bundle and producing
torsion and curvature as the field strengths of spacetime translations
and rotations
respectively~\cite{utiyama1956invariant,kibble1961lorentz,sciama1962physical,trautman1972einstein,hehl1976spin}.
Modern reviews and the textbook presentations relevant here are given
in~\cite{hehl1995metric,blagojevic2002gravitation,blagojevic2003lectures,blagojevicHehl2013};
recent applications and computational frameworks at the level used
here are developed in~\cite{barker2024poincare,barker2024psalter}. The
full field-variable formalism, including the vierbein, the spin
connection, and the irreducible torsion components used in the
kinetic-matrix analysis below, is collected in \cref{PGTBackground}.

% Step (c): Paired curvature and torsion field-strength definitions.
\paragraph*{Field strengths: curvature and torsion}
The translational and rotational gauge field strengths take the
standard
form~\cite{kibble1961lorentz,hehl1976spin,hehl1995metric,blagojevic2003lectures,hehlobukhov2007cartan,barker2024poincare},
\begin{align}
  \MAGF{^\sigma_\mu_\nu_\rho}
    &\equiv 2\,\PD{_{[\mu}}\MAGA{^\sigma_{\nu]}_\rho}
       + 2\,\MAGA{^\sigma_{[\mu|\lambda|}}\MAGA{^\lambda_{\nu]}_\rho},
  \label{Curvature} \\
  \MAGT{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}
    &\equiv 2\,\MAGA{^\sigma_{[\mu\nu]}}.
  \label{Torsion}
\end{align}
Curvature reduces to its Riemannian limit when the antisymmetric part
of the connection vanishes; torsion is the obstruction to that limit.

% Step (d): General quadratic action with named couplings.
\paragraph*{General quadratic action}
The most general parity-preserving quadratic PGT action coupled to
electromagnetism contains eleven independent invariants --- six
$\MAGFt{}^2$, three $\mathcal{T}^2$, one $\FieldF{}^2$, and one
$\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}$ cross-term --- with parity-odd
extensions and cubic completions catalogued in~\cite{bahamonde2025cubic}
and the symbolic enumeration documented in
\cref{PGTBackground}~\cite{lin2019pgt,blagojevic2018hamiltonian,blagojevic2003lectures,barker2024poincare,sezgin1980ghostfree,aoki2020nonlinear}.
Schematically,
\begin{align}
  S = \int d^4x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\Bigl[
        \frac{1}{\kappa^2}\,\MAGFt{}
        - \tfrac{1}{4}\,\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
        + \sum_n \Alp{n}\,(\mathcal{T}^2)_n
        + \sum_n \Bet{n}\,(\mathcal{R}^2)_n
        + \Gam{1}\,\MAGFt{_{[\mu\nu]}}\FieldF{^{\mu\nu}}
      \Bigr],
  \label{QuadraticAction}
\end{align}
where the $\Alp{n}$ couple the torsion-squared invariants, the
$\Bet{n}$ couple the curvature-squared invariants, and $\Gam{1}$
couples the parity-even curvature--electromagnetic cross-term. This
assignment matches the convention used in our symbolic implementation
and in the BHL/Barker corpus; the constrained ghost-free subspace of
the $\Alp{n},\,\Bet{n}$ parameter space is the subject of the
classifications cited above, while the unrestricted enumeration we
employ is set out in \cref{PGTBackground}.

% Step (e): Irreducible torsion decomposition introduced AFTER the action.
% This is also the cross-reference target for App E.
\paragraph*{Irreducible torsion decomposition}
The torsion tensor admits a unique decomposition into irreducible
$\mathrm{O}(3,1)$ representations~\cite{hehl1976spin,shapiro2002torsion,blagojevic2003lectures,barker2024poincare}:
a traceless tensor part $\T[1]{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}$, a trace vector
$\T[2]{_\mu} \equiv \MAGT{^\lambda_\lambda_\mu}$, and an axial
pseudovector
$\T[3]{^\mu} \equiv \varepsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma\mu}\MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\gamma}}$,
\begin{align}
  \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}
    = \tfrac{1}{3}\bigl(\T[2]{_\beta}\,\g{_{\alpha\mu}}
                       - \T[2]{_\mu}\,\g{_{\alpha\beta}}\bigr)
    - \tfrac{1}{6}\,\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}\,\T[3]{^\nu}
    + \T[1]{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}.
  \label{IrreducibleTorsion}
\end{align}
The torsion-squared sector of \cref{QuadraticAction} is equivalently
written in the \emph{direct basis} of scalar contractions,
\begin{align}
  I_1 \equiv \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}\MAGT{^{\alpha\beta\mu}},
  \qquad
  I_2 \equiv \MAGT{_{\alpha\beta\mu}}\MAGT{^{\beta\alpha\mu}},
  \qquad
  I_3 \equiv \MAGT{^\alpha_\alpha_\mu}\MAGT{^\beta_\beta^\mu},
  \label{TorsionDirectBasis}
\end{align}
with $\{I_1,I_2,I_3\}$ related to the irreducible squared-norms
$\{q^2,\,T^2,\,S^2\}$ by an invertible linear transformation collected
in \cref{PGTBackground}~\cite{shapiro2002torsion}. Coupling-constant
labelling varies across the PGT literature; throughout this work we
use the direct basis $\{I_1,I_2,I_3\}$ computationally and the
irreducible decomposition for naming the physical degrees of freedom
that each interaction term selects.

\subsection{Linearisation around flat $+$ $B_0$ background}%
\label{Linearisation}
% Target ~350 w. Per genre_conventions_theory.md §(f) linearisation
% sub-movement.

\paragraph*{Background and perturbation ansatz}
We linearise about a flat metric carrying a uniform static magnetic
field $\Bzero$ along $\hat{z}$, with vanishing background torsion.
The metric and four-potential are decomposed as
\begin{gather}
  g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu},
  \quad
  A_\mu = \bar{A}_\mu + \delta A_\mu, \label{PerturbationAnsatz} \\
  \bar{A}_\mu\,dx^\mu = -\Bzero\,z\,dy, \nonumber
\end{gather}
with the connection treated as inherently perturbative,
$\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu} = \delta\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$. The metric
perturbation $h_{\mu\nu}$ enters without a $\kappa$ prefactor; this
choice is equivalent under field redefinition to the
canonically-normalised convention
$g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + \kappa\,\tilde{h}_{\mu\nu}$ used
in~\cite{lella2024constraining}, with the relation between the two
forms set out in \cref{sec:kappa-conventions}. A self-consistency
bound on the campaign parameter choices is in
\cref{PerturbativeRegime}.

\paragraph*{Order-counting rule}
We work to linear order in $h_{\mu\nu}$, $\delta A_\mu$, and the
torsion fluctuation $\delta\MAGA{^\sigma_\mu_\nu}$, and to first order
in $\Bzero$.

% Kinetic-matrix sub-movement (third move per §(f)). Equation label is the
% cross-reference target for §4 and App E.
\paragraph*{Linearised kinetic matrix}
After expanding $\sqrt{-g}\,\lag$ to quadratic order in the
perturbation fields and reducing to plane-wave dependence
$\partial_x = \partial_y = 0$ along the propagation direction
$\hat{z}$, the quadratic Lagrangian assembles into a block-structured
kinetic system,
\begin{align}
  \lag^{(2)} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\xi^\mathsf{T}\,
    \mathcal{K}(\partial_t,\partial_z)\,\xi,
  \qquad
  \mathcal{K}
    = \begin{pmatrix} \mathcal{K}_\times & 0 \\ 0 & \mathcal{K}_\text{trace} \end{pmatrix},
  \label{KineticMatrix}
\end{align}
where $\xi$ groups the surviving gauge-fixed degrees of freedom
into the cross-polarisation channel
$(h_\times,\,a_\parallel)^\mathsf{T}$ and the trace sector
containing $(h_+, h_4, h_9, a_\perp)^\mathsf{T}$ together with the
axial torsion mode when present. The upper $2\times 2$ block governs
the standard $h_\times$--photon Gertsenshtein channel; the lower
block carries the trace-sector fields, into which torsion
couplings --- where they appear --- enter through off-diagonal
entries.

\paragraph*{Electromagnetic-field conventions}
The electric and magnetic fields are defined via the covariant
decomposition of $\FieldF{_{\mu\nu}}$ in the normal-frame observer
four-vector~\cite{hwangnoh2023emdef,hwangnoh2023graviton}, which
entails metric-dependent corrections to the naive flat-space
identification (the explicit form is given in \cref{PGTBackground}).
Our pipeline derives equations from $\sqrt{-g}\,\lag$ at the Lagrangian
level, so these corrections enter automatically; the linearised
graviton mass coefficient $-\tfrac{1}{2}\kappa^2\Bzero^2$ agrees with
the potential-formulation result of~\cite{hwangnoh2023graviton}.

\paragraph*{Conversion probability}

The conversion probability for a graviton traversing a region of
uniform $\Bzero$ over distance $D$ takes the closed
form\footnote{%
    Two distinct objects appear under the symbol $\kappa$ in the
    recent Gertsenshtein literature: the linearised
    graviton--photon vertex coupling adopted here, with
    $\kappa^2 = 16\pi G$ and $[\kappa] = [\mathrm{Mass}]^{-1}$, and
    the Einstein-action constant $\kappa_\mathrm{E} = 8\pi G$,
    $[\kappa_\mathrm{E}] = [\mathrm{Mass}]^{-2}$, used as the
    prefactor of $\MAGFt{}$ in some recent
    work~\cite{barker2024poincare}. A non-canonical kinetic
    normalisation in the derivations
    of~\cite{palessandro2023simple,palessandro2024gertsenshtein}
    produces a factor-of-$\sqrt{4\pi}$ error in the conversion
    formula argument; \cite{hwangnoh2023graviton} identifies the
    compounding issue of electromagnetic-field identification in
    magnetic environments. The convention adopted
    in~\cite{lella2024constraining} agrees with ours. The full
    disambiguation, including dimensional analysis and the
    relation $\kappa = \sqrt{2}/\MPl$, is given in
    \cref{sec:kappa-conventions}.
  }
\begin{align}
  \Pconv = \sin^2\!\left(\frac{\kappa\,\Bzero\,D}{2}\right),
  \label{GertsenshteinFormula}
\end{align}
which in the small-argument limit $\kappa\Bzero D \ll 1$ scales as
$\Pconv \propto \Bzero^2$, the characteristic Gertsenshtein
signature; the regime of validity of this linearised expression in
the torsionful background is set out in \cref{PerturbativeRegime}.

\paragraph*{Channel decomposition}
The linearised perturbation falls naturally into the two transverse
tensor polarisations $h_+$ and $h_\times$, the trace sector
$\{h_+, h_4, h_9, a_\perp\}$, and the irreducible torsion modes ---
trace vector $\T[2]{_\mu}$ and axial pseudovector $\T[3]{^\mu}$
where present. These are the kinematic sectors in which the
conversion question is posed; the standard Gertsenshtein channel
$h_\times \leftrightarrow a_\parallel$ is the canonical example.
The empirical inter-channel structure observed across the surveyed
theory classes is reported in \cref{ChannelStructure}, with the
symbolic-pipeline outputs that motivate it archived in
\cref{SymbolicMechanism}.
