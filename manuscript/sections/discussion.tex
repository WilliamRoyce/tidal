\paragraph*{Interpretation of exponential mode growth}
The amplification ratios themselves are not the headline result of the
survey: they are produced by linearised exponential growth on the
unbounded plane-wave background, and so scale with the simulation
horizon --- running the modal solver longer would inflate the same
ratios arbitrarily. The structure --- which operators mediate
amplification and suppression, and how that mediation migrates across
the surveyed sectors --- is what the survey delivers; physical-magnitude
statements about conversion efficiency require finite-extent,
localised-$\Bzero$ simulations that bound the interaction time and
saturate the linearised growth without destroying the channel mixing.
The pipeline accepts these exponentially-growing modes through the
modal-solver evolution by design (\cref{InferenceArchitecture}):
prejudging which mode behaviours are unphysical for an input theory is
not the role of a theory-agnostic survey. The remaining interpretive
question is whether each growth represents a physical amplification, a
non-ghost tachyonic-instability artefact of the unbounded plane-wave
ansatz, or a wrong-sign kinetic-mode ghost. Several precedents in the
literature show that linearised exponential or tachyonic modes need
not be fatal pathologies. In the Standard Model the Higgs field
appears tachyonic about the unbroken vacuum but is perfectly unitary
about its symmetry-broken ground state~\cite{legner2025torc};
analogously, in the TorC model~\cite{legner2025torc} the linearised
tachyons around $T = 0$ are benign because the physical ground state
is a torsion-condensed phase at $T^2 \sim M_P$, with the
Einstein--Hilbert term itself emerging from a post-Riemannian
$R\,T^2$ cross-term in that condensed vacuum. The parallel is a
precedent that linearised exponential modes do not by themselves
disqualify a sector; we do not claim that our surveyed Lagrangians
necessarily realise such a condensation, only that the choice of
vacuum around which the modes are evaluated can change the unitarity
verdict.
Higher-derivative gravity theories that exhibit Ostrogradsky ghosts at
the linearised level can be non-linearly ghost-free when appropriate
torsion or higher-curvature terms are
present~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear,bahamonde2025cubic}.
Recent metric-affine catalogues~\cite{barker2025irfound1,barker2025irfound2}
argue that a no-ghost / no-tachyon condition that is imposed by hand
as an equality among couplings is not stable against quantum
corrections to those same couplings: the corrected coupling values
violate the tuning, and the ghost or tachyon reappears. By contrast,
when the absence of a ghost mode follows from a gauge symmetry of the
Lagrangian itself, no choice of coupling values can violate it, and
unitarity is structurally protected. The non-propagating-torsion
control of \cref{NonPropagatingControl} is a particularly clean
example of the latter: with $\xi$ pinned to zero there is no new
kinetic operator at all, so no wrong-sign coefficient can appear in
the first place. These
precedents do not prove that the linearised exponential growth we
observe is physical, but they establish that it is not automatically
fatal, and that resolution belongs to either finite-extent geometry or
a future \PSALTer{}-based particle-spectrum analysis.

\paragraph*{Parity-odd geometric incompatibility}
The same plane-wave embedding makes the parity-odd full and complete
sectors of \cref{ParityOddGeomIncomp} structurally incompatible with
finite-amplitude inference: the $\varepsilon$-tensor-mediated
contractions $\varepsilon\!\cdot\!\MAGT{}\!\cdot\!\FieldF{}$ and
$\varepsilon\!\cdot\!\MAGFt{}\!\cdot\!\nabla\MAGT{}$ produce linearised
eigenvalues so large that the matrix-exponential overflow ceiling is
breached at essentially any propagation time accessible to the modal
solver. A bounded interaction time, set by a finite-extent geometry,
would cap the growth at a finite envelope and restore a
finite-amplitude posterior --- which is the same hypothesis the
localised-geometry programme would test for the amplifying parity-even
sectors. The same finite-extent programme would also probe physics
inaccessible to the plane-wave ansatz, including $k$-mode interactions
across the bounded interaction region and realistic background
geometries that allow physical-magnitude amplification estimates for
actual astrophysical events.
