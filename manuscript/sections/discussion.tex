\subsection{Discussion and outlook}%
\label{Discussion}

\paragraph*{Interpretation of exponential mode growth}
The amplification ratios themselves grow with the simulation horizon,
because the modes are linearised on the unbounded plane-wave background
and the modal solver compounds exponential growth indefinitely.
Physical-magnitude statements about conversion efficiency require
finite-extent, localised-$\Bzero$ simulations that bound the
interaction time and saturate the linearised growth without destroying
the channel mixing. We accept these exponentially-growing modes by
design: prejudging which mode behaviours are unphysical for an input
theory is not the role of a theory-agnostic survey pipeline. The
remaining interpretive question is whether each growth represents a
physical amplification, an artifact of the uniform unbounded
background, or a pathology of the Lagrangian.

Several precedents in the
literature show that linearised exponential or tachyonic modes need
not be fatal pathologies. In the Standard Model the Higgs field
appears tachyonic about the unbroken vacuum but is perfectly unitary
about its symmetry-broken ground state~\cite{legner2025torc};
analogously, in the TorC model~\cite{legner2025torc} the linearised
tachyons around $\MAGT{} = 0$ are benign because the physical ground state
is a torsion-condensed phase at $\MAGT{^2} \sim \MPl$. These examples
show that linearised exponential modes do not by themselves disqualify
a sector; we do not claim that our surveyed Lagrangians
necessarily realise such a condensation, only that the choice of
vacuum around which the modes are evaluated can change the unitarity
verdict.

Higher-derivative gravity theories that exhibit Ostrogradsky ghosts at
the linearised level can be non-linearly ghost-free when appropriate
torsion or higher-curvature terms are
present~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear,bahamonde2025cubic}.
Recent infrared-foundations work~\cite{barker2025irfound1,barker2025irfound2}
argues that a no-ghost / no-tachyon condition that is imposed by hand
as an equality among couplings is not stable against quantum
corrections: the corrected coupling values
violate the tuning, and the ghost or tachyon reappears. By contrast,
when the absence of a ghost mode follows from a gauge symmetry of the
Lagrangian itself, no choice of coupling values can violate it, and
unitarity is structurally protected. The non-propagating-torsion
control of \cref{NonPropagatingControl} is a particularly clean
example of the latter: with $\xi$ pinned to zero there is no new
kinetic operator at all, so no wrong-sign coefficient can appear in
the first place. These
precedents do not prove that the linearised exponential growth we
observe is physical, but they establish that it is not automatically
fatal, and that resolution belongs to either finite-extent geometry or
a future \PSALTer{}-based particle-spectrum analysis.

\paragraph*{Sectors that overflow the modal solver}
The uniform unbounded background makes three of the largest sectors
in the survey --- the parity-odd full and complete parity-odd closure
of \cref{ParityOddGeomIncomp}, and the $\xi$-kinetic closure --- yield
linearised eigenvalues so large that the matrix-exponential overflow
ceiling is breached at essentially any propagation time accessible to
the modal solver. A bounded interaction time, set by a finite-extent
geometry, would cap the growth at a finite envelope and restore a
finite-amplitude posterior --- which is the same hypothesis the
localised-geometry programme would test for the amplifying sectors
that did resolve. The same finite-extent programme would also probe
physics inaccessible to the uniform background, including $k$-mode
interactions across the bounded interaction region and realistic
background geometries that allow physical-magnitude amplification
estimates for actual astrophysical events.

\paragraph*{The $\chi_1$ direction across the parity-even sectors}
$\chi_1\,\rcD{^\alpha}\MAGT{^{\beta\gamma\delta}}\,
\MAGFt{_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}}$ carries the dominant
$D_\mathrm{KL}(P_+\|P_-)$ contribution in all three parity-even
sectors that contain it, regardless of whether the torsion sector
propagates or the $\chi_i$ basis is closed. The reason this
contraction privileges itself over the other $\chi_{2\!-\!10}$
contractions and over the $\xi_i$ kinetic invariants is not visible
in the inference output and requires an analytic diagnosis of the
linearised conversion equations.

\paragraph*{Scope and limitations}
The results sit in the linearised regime at first order in $\Bzero$
on an unbounded plane-wave background with a graviton initial
condition; the per-coupling $D_\mathrm{KL}$ figures are read against
the prior choices and should not be compared across sectors that
adopt different priors. The largest theories in the survey produced
linearised eigenvalues too extreme for the uniform plane-wave setup
to resolve: a substantial fraction of sample points hit modal-solver
overflow in three sectors, and resolving them requires the
localised-geometry programme. The cubic-PGT sector has not been
surveyed.
