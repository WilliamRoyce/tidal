\subsection{Discussion and outlook}%
\label{Discussion}

\paragraph*{Interpretation of exponential mode growth}
The amplification ratios themselves grow with the simulation duration,
because the exponential growth compounds indefinitely
on the unbounded plane-wave background.
We accept the exponentially-growing modes: a theory-agnostic
framework must report whatever the input Lagrangian produces. The
remaining interpretive question is whether each growth represents a
physical amplification, an artifact of the uniform unbounded
background, or a pathology of the Lagrangian.

Several precedents in the
literature show that linearised exponential or tachyonic modes need
not be fatal pathologies. In the Standard Model the Higgs field
appears tachyonic about the unbroken vacuum but is perfectly unitary
about its symmetry-broken ground state~\cite{legner2025torc}.
Linearised exponential modes do not by themselves disqualify
a sector; we do not claim that our surveyed Lagrangians
necessarily realise such a vacuum structure, only that exponential
growth need not signal a Lagrangian pathology.

Higher-derivative gravity theories that exhibit Ostrogradsky ghosts at
the linearised level can be non-linearly ghost-free when appropriate
torsion or higher-curvature terms are
present~\cite{aoki2020nonlinear,bahamonde2025cubic}.
Recent analysis~\cite{barker2025irfound1,barker2025irfound2}
argues that a no-ghost-no-tachyon condition that is imposed by hand
on couplings is not stable against radiative
corrections. However,
when the absence of a ghost mode follows from a gauge symmetry of the
Lagrangian itself, unitarity is protected.
This supports the interpretation that the exponential growth we observe is not
automatically fatal.

\paragraph*{Eigenvalue overflow}
Some linearised theories predict eigenvalues with $\max|\Re\{\lambda\}|\sim 10^3$--$10^5$
for the survey setup.
Conversion amplitudes in those regions predict exponential growth by
many orders of magnitude over the propagation time: in a
finite-extent BGMF the growth would saturate at a
(possibly very large) amplitude whereas
on the unbounded plane-wave background the growth has no upper
bound, and standard floating-point precision cannot represent it
at the $t_\text{end}$ used in \cref{SectorSurvey}. Re-running these
theories at even smaller $t_\text{end}$ may contain
the growth further.

\paragraph*{The $\chi_1$ direction}

\paragraph*{Scope and limitations}
The results sit in the linearised regime at first order in $\Bzero$
on an unbounded BGMF with a plane-wave graviton initial
condition. A substantial fraction of sample points in the largest
sectors hit modal-solver overflow, leaving those parameter regions
unresolved. Three resolution paths remain open: a localised-geometry
programme, in which a finite-extent $\Bzero$ bounds the interaction
time and restores finite-amplitude posteriors in the overflow sectors
while also enabling physical-magnitude conversion estimates against
realistic astrophysical geometries; a \PSALTer{}~\cite{barker2024psalter,barker2025psalter2}
particle-spectrum analysis, to interpret the exponentially-growing modes
against the linearised unitarity verdict; and a survey of the
cubic-PGT sector~\cite{bahamonde2025cubic}, which lies beyond the
quadratic action that bounds the present work.
