\section{Conclusions}%
\label{Conclusions}

The extensions of general relativity that gravitational-wave
observatories will probe carry many additional operators, each with
couplings; the Gertsenshtein channel structure across
this coupling space has, until now, been mapped only model by model.
We have presented \TIDAL{}, a symbolic--numerical software package
that automates the linearised conversion analysis for any tensor
Lagrangian, and applied it to the quadratic Poincar\'{e}-gauge action
coupled to electromagnetism.

We find that the Gertsenshtein amplification verdict\wb{verdict?}, across the
surveyed operator space, is carried by a small set of Lagrangian
couplings, with the derivative-mixing coupling
\begin{equation}
    \lag \supset \rcD{^\alpha}\MAGT{^{\beta\gamma\delta}}\, \MAGF{_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}}
\end{equation}
as the
dominant amplifying direction. Significant suppression of conversion
also appears across most of the surveyed theories. The amplification
persists without propagating-torsion, so purely
ghost-driven growth cannot explain it.\wb{That is a nice result}

Whether the exponential mode growth that drives these amplification
verdicts represents physical amplification, a tachyonic instability,
or a ghost is not decided at the linearised level. A
particle-spectrum analysis via
\PSALTer{}~\cite{barker2024psalter,barker2025psalter2} would determine
which of the three applies for each amplifying direction identified
above.

Re-running the survey on a finite-extent magnetic background would
give the propagating wave only a finite interaction time,
bounding the exponential growth that otherwise compounds
indefinitely. The sectors that overflow
under that embedding would then re-enter the survey at finite
amplitude, and coupling-level verdicts could be replaced by magnitudes
comparable with detector sensitivities. A second extension is to
sample along directions in the coupling space rather than along the
bare-coupling axes the present survey uses; this would identify which
combinations of operators drive the amplification,
independently of the basis in which the action is written.

The non-minimal coupling sector of the quadratic Poincar\'{e}-gauge
action is, on the present evidence, the structural locus of the
Gertsenshtein channel amplification. Pending the proposed follow-up analyses,
the search for Gertsenshtein amplification in extensions of general
relativity moves from the propagating-torsion sector to the non-minimal
coupling content of the action. Whether each amplifying coupling identified here drives
physical amplification or a vacuum-dependent instability is a question
the linearised survey cannot, by construction, decide; what it does
decide is which operators of the quadratic gauge-gravity action are
candidates for that question at all --- and therefore which couplings
any high-frequency gravitational-wave search for the Gertsenshtein
signal will need to constrain.
