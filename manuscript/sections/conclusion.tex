\section{Conclusions}%
\label{Conclusions}

The extensions of general relativity that gravitational-wave
observatories will probe carry many additional operators, each with
its own coupling constant; the Gertsenshtein channel structure across
this coupling space has, until now, been mapped only model by model.
We present \TIDAL{}, a symbolic--numerical pipeline that automates the
linearised conversion analysis for any quadratic Lagrangian, and apply
it to the quadratic Poincar\'{e}-gauge action coupled to
electromagnetism.

We find that the Gertsenshtein amplification verdict, across the
surveyed operator space, is carried by a small set of Lagrangian
couplings, with the derivative-mixing coupling $\chi_1$ as the
dominant amplifying direction. Significant suppression of conversion
also appears across most of the surveyed theories. The amplification
persists in the non-propagating-torsion control, so purely
ghost-driven growth cannot explain it.

Whether the exponential mode growth that drives these amplification
verdicts represents physical amplification, a tachyonic instability,
or a kinetic ghost is not decided at the linearised level. A
particle-spectrum analysis via
\PSALTer{}~\cite{barker2024psalter,barker2025psalter2} would determine
which of the three applies for each amplifying direction identified
above.

Re-running the survey on a finite-extent magnetic background would
give the propagating wave only a finite proper time over which to
interact with the field, capping at a finite envelope the exponential
growth that under the unbounded plane-wave embedding compounds
indefinitely. The parity-odd full and complete sectors that overflow
under that embedding would then re-enter the survey at finite
amplitude, and coupling-level verdicts would be replaced by magnitudes
comparable with detector sensitivities. A second extension is to
sample along directions in the coupling space rather than along the
bare-coupling axes the present survey uses; this would identify which
linear combinations of operators drive the amplification and which
the suppression, independently of the basis in which the action is
written.

If the present locus survives the finite-extent re-run, the search
for Gertsenshtein channel structure in extensions of general
relativity moves from the propagating-torsion sector --- where it has
conventionally been sought --- to the non-minimal coupling content of
the action. Whether each amplifying coupling identified here drives
physical amplification or a vacuum-dependent instability is a question
the linearised survey cannot, by construction, decide; what it does
decide is which operators of the quadratic gauge-gravity action are
candidates for that question at all --- and therefore which couplings
any high-frequency gravitational-wave search for the Gertsenshtein
signal will need to constrain.
