\section{Conclusions}%
\label{Conclusions}

The extensions of general relativity that gravitational-wave observatories
will probe in the coming decade proliferate a multi-dimensional coupling
space whose Gertsenshtein channel structure has, until now, been
accessible only one Lagrangian at a time. This thesis develops
\TIDAL{}, a symbolic--numerical pipeline that automates the linearised
conversion analysis for any quadratic Lagrangian, and applies it to the
quadratic Poincar\'{e}-gauge action coupled to electromagnetism as a
Bayesian survey at the coupling level.

Across the surveyed quadratic operator space, a small set of Lagrangian
couplings carries the Gertsenshtein amplification verdict. The
non-minimal EM--Riemann--Cartan curvature coupling $\Del{1}$ and the
derivative-mixing coupling $\chi_1$ emerge as the dominant amplifying
directions across the propagating sector, its non-propagating-torsion
control, and the extended-kinetic-closure embedding. Significant
suppression of the Einstein--Maxwell conversion baseline also appears
across most of the surveyed theories. The non-propagating-torsion
control reproduces the amplification at the same coupling content with
the torsion kinetic operator removed: the structural ingredient is
therefore the non-minimal coupling, not the propagating-torsion kinetic
sector, and the absence of any kinetic operator in that control rules
out ghost-driven growth as the explanation, so the amplification cannot
be a ghost artefact of the embedding alone.

Whether the exponential mode growth that drives these amplification
verdicts represents physical amplification, a tachyonic instability of
the unbounded plane-wave embedding, or a wrong-sign kinetic ghost is
not decided at the linearised Bayesian level. A Hamiltonian
particle-spectrum analysis via
\PSALTer{}~\cite{barker2024psalter,barker2025psalter2}, applied at the
coupling-level identifications above, is what would fix the verdict for
each surviving amplifying direction.

Two extensions are needed to turn the structural verdicts into physical
predictions. Re-running the survey on a finite-extent magnetic
background --- already supported by the inference architecture ---
would bound the interaction time and saturate the linearised
exponential growth without removing the channel mixing; the parity-odd
full and complete sectors that overflow under the plane-wave embedding
would re-enter the survey, and coupling-level verdicts would be
replaced by magnitude estimates that admit comparison with detector
sensitivities. Atlas-based direction sampling, also already in place,
complements the axis-aligned coupling scans of the present survey by
sampling along directions in the coupling space rather than along the
arbitrarily-chosen named-coupling axes, identifying which linear
combinations of operators in the surveyed basis carry the amplification
and suppression verdicts.

The non-minimal coupling sector of the quadratic Poincar\'{e}-gauge
action is, on the present evidence, the structural carrier of the
linearised Gertsenshtein channel mixing --- a role the
propagating-torsion kinetic sector contributes to the spectrum of the
theory but does not, in itself, supply. Whether each amplifying
coupling identified here drives physical amplification or a
vacuum-dependent instability is a question the linearised survey
cannot, by construction, decide; what it does decide is which operators
of the quadratic gauge-gravity action are even candidates for that
question.
