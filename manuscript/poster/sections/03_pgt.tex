\begin{block}{1.\quad Gravity with torsion}
  General relativity curves spacetime but keeps it \emph{torsion-free}. Treating
  gravity as a gauge theory of the Poincar\'e group (\emph{PGT}) adds a second
  field strength --- \textbf{torsion} $\MAGT{}$, the gauge field of local
  translations --- alongside \textbf{curvature} $\MAGFt{}$.
  \vspace{0.6ex}
  \begin{center}
    \includegraphics[width=0.82\linewidth]{pgt_geometry.pdf}
  \end{center}
  \vspace{0.4ex}
  Its most general quadratic action holds \emph{many} independent couplings ---
  $\MAGT{}^2$, $\MAGFt{}^2$, $\MAGFt{}\!\times\!F$, $\MAGFt{}\!\times\!\rcD{}\MAGT{}$,
  $\dots$ --- far too many to work through by hand.
\end{block}
