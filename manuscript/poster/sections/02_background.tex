\begin{block}{Background}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Gravitational-wave astronomy spans twelve decades in frequency ---
          nanohertz pulsar-timing arrays, kilohertz LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA, millihertz
          LISA --- but the \textbf{mega-to-gigahertz band lies above any known
          source}, so a signal there would be primordial or beyond-the-Standard-Model.
    \item \textbf{Several proposed detection schemes} rest on the
          \emph{Gertsenshtein effect}~[1,2]: gravitational waves converting into
          photons in a magnetic field.
    \item Searches reinterpret dark-matter haloscopes~[4] and astrophysical
          fields~[3,5], but \textbf{no conversion has been confirmed}.
    \item In Einstein--Maxwell gravity the mixing length reaches ${\sim}10^{6}\,$km
          even at magnetar fields.
  \end{itemize}
