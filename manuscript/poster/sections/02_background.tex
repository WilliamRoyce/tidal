\begin{block}{Background}
  Gravitational-wave astronomy now spans twelve decades in frequency --- nanohertz
  pulsar-timing arrays, the kilohertz LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA network, and the
  millihertz LISA. But the \textbf{mega-to-gigahertz band lies above any known
  source}, so a signal there would be primordial or beyond-the-Standard-Model:
  inflationary preheating, phase transitions, or primordial black holes. The
  leading proposed probe is the \emph{Gertsenshtein effect} --- gravitational
  waves converting into photons in a magnetic field. Searches reinterpret
  dark-matter haloscopes and astrophysical fields, but \textbf{no conversion has
  been confirmed}: in Einstein--Maxwell gravity the mixing length reaches
  ${\sim}10^{6}\,$km even at magnetar fields, negligible without astrophysical
  baselines.
