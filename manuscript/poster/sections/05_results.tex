\begin{block}{3.\quad What we found}
  \centering

  \raggedright
  Scanning the coupling space, the conversion responds to the full content of
  each theory --- but a few operators are \textbf{robustly dominant}. The most
  consistent amplifier is the curvature--torsion derivative coupling
  \begin{exampleblock}{}
    \centering\large
    $\lag \;\supset\; \rcD{^{\alpha}}\MAGT{^{\beta\gamma\delta}}\,
                       \MAGF{_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}}$
  \end{exampleblock}
  \vspace{0.5ex}
  Both \textcolor{CamCherryDark}{\textbf{amplification}} and
  \textcolor{CamCrestDark}{\textbf{suppression}} occur, and the amplification is
  \textbf{ghost-free}. This maps out exactly which interactions a future bound on
  the Gertsenshtein signal would target.
\end{block}
